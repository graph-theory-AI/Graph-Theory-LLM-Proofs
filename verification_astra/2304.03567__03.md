## Interpretation

I checked the formalization against the primary source, not the catalog. I downloaded the
e-print TeX of arXiv:2304.03567v2 (`/tmp/src2304/body/request.tex`) and read the
definitions verbatim:

- RFCPP, `request.tex` line 3: *"the input is a strongly connected digraph D=(V,A) and a
  set R ⊆ (V choose 2) of requests, and the output is a vertex ordering of D maximizing the
  number of forward pairs {x,y} ∈ R, that is unordered pairs {x,y} such that either x,y or
  y,x is forward."*
- Problem 4, `request.tex` line 6, verbatim: *"Is there a polytime constant approximation
  algorithm for RFCPP?"*

The writeup's formalization matches exactly: `R` is a set of unordered pairs; a request is
realized if there is a forward directed path in **either** direction; the constructed
digraph is required to be strongly connected (Lemma 4 proves it, and I verified it
computationally for h ≤ 7 in both the YES and the NO case). A ρ-approximation is defined in
§1 as returning an enumeration realizing ≥ OPT/ρ requests — the standard convention for a
maximization problem.

This is **not** interpretation gaming and not a strawman reading. The writeup answers
Problem 4 in the negative under P≠NP, which is the form in which such a question is normally
settled. A reasonable author of the source paper would consider Problem 4 resolved by this
(modulo the standard complexity assumption). Two further points support the reading:

- The paper's own §6 speculates the answer might be *positive up to a log factor*
  (*"we could not find any set of requests R which is not satisfied within a ratio of
  1/log n"*, and Conjecture 1 *"would imply ... opening the possibility for polynomial time
  O(log n)-approximation"*). The writeup's Ω(log) lower bound therefore **complements**
  rather than contradicts Conjecture 1: if both hold, RFCPP's approximability is Θ(log n).
  This makes the claim a priori plausible rather than suspicious. The writeup does not make
  this observation; it should.
- The catalog's "lean disprove" triage tier is consistent with the outcome.

One labelling remark: the verdict token `partial` undersells the content. The writeup does
not partially progress Problem 4 — it settles it conditionally on P≠NP, which is what such a
resolution looks like. `disproved` (with the assumption stated) would be the honest token.

**The conclusion rests on exactly one assumption: P ≠ NP** (for the randomized variant,
RP ≠ NP). Everything else is a theorem: the NP-completeness of directed two-linkage
(Fortune–Hopcroft–Wyllie 1980) plus the construction. No graph-theoretic conjecture is used,
and the writeup states the assumption explicitly in §9 and in its verdict caveats.

- **Uncited prior art inside the source paper (the real attribution problem).**
  `request.tex`, Proposition 4, states: *"For all n, there exists an instance of RFCPP where

  realized as forward paths"*, and its proof uses the **bidirected complete binary tree**,
  requests defined by recursive matchings between the two children's leaf sets, with the
  authors writing *"With the right choice of matchings, the set of requests can correspond
  for instance to a hypercube of dimension n on the leaves."* Their induction uses
  `in(x)`, `out(x)`, `rf(x)` with the key bound `rf(x) ≤ rf(y)+rf(z)+min(in(y),out(z))`.

  same request family. So **Lemma 7 and R_h are a re-derivation of Proposition 4** — adapted
  from the bidirected tree (bound N) to the triangle cactus (bound 3N), which is a genuine
  but small extension needed because the compressed core is a 4-port tree, not an edge.
  The writeup's sentence *"The argument does not use Proposition 4 of the source paper"* is
  literally true but misleading, and Proposition 4 is never cited. **This must be fixed
  before publication.** It is not an ALREADY_KNOWN finding for the theorem, because
  Proposition 4 bounds OPT against |R|, not against OPT — on that instance OPT = 2^n is
  itself achievable, so Proposition 4 yields no approximation hardness whatsoever. All the
  hardness comes from the two-linkage gap, which is new.
- The catalog's two "internal references" (arXiv:2206.12335) are, as the catalog itself
  notes, false positives; nothing in the writeup depends on them.

## Caveats

1. **The conclusion is conditional on P ≠ NP** (RP ≠ NP for randomized algorithms). This is
   stated in the theorem, in §9, and in the verdict caveats. It is not a proof of P ≠ NP and
   the writeup does not pretend otherwise.
2. **Uncited prior art: Proposition 4 of the source paper.** Lemma 7 and the hypercube
   request family R_h are a re-derivation of the source paper's own Proposition 4 (same
   induction, same min(in,out) bound, same hypercube-on-recursive-binary-structure
   requests), extended from the bidirected binary tree to the triangle cactus. The writeup
   explicitly disclaims using it. This is an attribution defect, not a correctness defect,
   and it does not touch novelty of the theorem (Proposition 4 gives no approximation
   hardness at all: on its instance OPT = 2^n is achieved).
3. **Fortune–Hopcroft–Wyllie is never cited.** "The standard NP-completeness of directed
   two-linkage" is the single external input; it needs a reference.
4. **Unstated routine details.** (a) §2.1 does not say that one pass of the
   reachability pruning suffices; (b) Lemma 5 does not say where the non-path vertices are
   placed in the enumeration (after the path). Both are one-line repairs.
5. **Constants are not optimal.** Lemma 7 proves ≤ 3N; my DP suggests the truth is ≈ 2N, and
   the source paper gets N on the tree. Irrelevant to the asymptotics.
6. **Small-h vacuity.** The gap only bites for h > 6 (h/6 > 1), i.e. N ≥ 128. Exhaustive
   verification of OPT(B_h) is only feasible for h ≤ 3, where 3N exceeds |R_h| and the bound
   says nothing. The h ≥ 4 evidence is the (valid) DP relaxation plus the h=7 witness, not
   exhaustive enumeration. This is inherent — the instances are superpolynomially many
   enumerations — and each proof step that carries the bound was verified separately.
7. **Verdict token.** `partial` undersells the result; this settles Problem 4 negatively
   under the standard assumption. The `would_publish: true` flag is justified.
8. **Not observed by the writeup:** the result complements Conjecture 1 of the source paper
   rather than contradicting it. Together they would pin RFCPP's approximability at
   Θ(log n). Worth a sentence, and a useful consistency check that the theorem is not
   accidentally contradicting something the authors believe.
9. **Degenerate cases.** h = 0 gives R = ∅ (unused); the construction needs the four
   terminals distinct (given in the two-linkage problem statement); the fixed NO gadget
   covers instances failing the individual-reachability precondition.

## Referee summary

I set out to break this and could not. The reduction is a genuine gap reduction, not an
instance family with OPT = o(|R|) dressed up as hardness: the same request set R_h is worth
|R_h| = h2^{h-1} on YES inputs (Lemma 5, witnessed at h = 7 by an explicit Hamiltonian-style
path realizing 448/448) and at most 3·2^h on NO inputs (Lemmas 6+7), so the two sides do not
overlap once h > 6, and the instance is strongly connected and of polynomial size in **both**
cases (|V(F_h)| = (|V(G)|+4)2^h − (|V(G)|+1), verified exactly; O(q^2) for h = ⌈log_2 q⌉,
O(q) for fixed h). The four standard failure modes for this kind of claim are all closed: the
reduction is gap-producing rather than merely value-preserving; the YES and NO values are
separated by a factor h/6 that grows while the instance stays polynomial (no amplification
lemma is needed — a single two-linkage instance is reused 2^h − 1 times inside one gadget);
the complexity assumption P ≠ NP is stated in the conclusion; and the size is polynomial.
The one structural objection to any 2-linkage reduction for RFCPP — that Fortune–Hopcroft–
Wyllie's hard instances need not be strong, whereas RFCPP demands a strong digraph — is
handled explicitly by Lemma 4 and confirmed computationally up to h = 7. Every load-bearing
finite claim was machine-checked: Lemmas 2 and 3 exhaustively over all acyclic subgraphs of
the core for seven NO-instances (0 violations, with a control showing Lemma 2 genuinely fails
on a YES core); the Lemma 6 compression end to end on ~6 700 acyclic subgraphs at h = 2..5
covering all 24 achievable core port-relations (0 failures at any of four stages); Lemma 7
