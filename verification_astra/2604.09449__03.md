## Interpretation

**The interpretation is exactly the intended one.** I downloaded the TeX source of
arXiv:2604.09449v2 (the current version, posted 2026-07-11) and checked the problem
statement and every definition the writeup implicitly relies on.

* The paper defines, for a subgraph $G'\subseteq G$ and $\vec f\colon E(G)\to\mathbb R^k$,
  $$f_{\vec f}(G') = \Bigl\|\vec f(G') - \tfrac{e(G')}{e(G)}\vec f(G)\Bigr\|_1 ,$$
  under the hypothesis $\|\vec f(e)\|_1\le 1$. (Confirmed independently in the sibling
  report `verification/2604.09449__00.md`.)
* Problem 6.4 reads verbatim: *"Improve the bounds on $f_{\vec f}(H)$ for a Hamilton cycle
  $H$ in complete or complete bipartite graphs."* The surrounding paragraph fixes the
  baseline: $\Delta=2$ in `thm:bounded_deg_bound` ($f_{\vec f}=O(\Delta^2k^2\sqrt{\log 2\Delta})$)
  gives $O(k^2)$, and "our methods imply that this bound also holds for Hamilton cycles in
  complete bipartite graphs".
* The writeup's centring vectors are $\frac2n \vec f(K_{n,n})$ and $\frac2{N-1}\vec f(K_N)$.
  These are *exactly* $\frac{e(H)}{e(G)}\vec f(G)$: $2n/n^2 = 2/n$ and $N/\binom N2 = 2/(N-1)$.
  So Theorem 1, specialised to $X=(\mathbb R^k,\|\cdot\|_1)$, $d=k$, $L=1$, is literally a
  bound on the paper's $f_{\vec f}(H)$, with no reinterpretation and no weakening.
* The writeup is *more* general than the problem asks (arbitrary $d$-dimensional norm, not
  just $\ell_1$), and covers both hosts requested. No sub-class restriction: the divisibility
  hypotheses ($k\mid 2n$, $k\mid N$) appear only in the *recolouring* Corollary 2, where they
  are necessary for "colour-balanced" to be achievable at all, and are stated.

A reasonable author of Problem 6.4 would consider it answered (an improvement was asked for,
not the optimal order; the writeup is explicit that it does not determine the order).

**Understated claim.** The verdict line sells the result short. The substantive ingredient,
Proposition 6, bounds $f_{\vec f}(M)$ for perfect matchings of $K_{r,r}$ by $3kB_k=O(k\log k)$.
That is a direct improvement of the paper's **`thm:vec-bipartite`** ($O(k^2)$), which the
authors call "a central tool, which we use throughout the paper" and about which they write:
"any improvement to the general $k^2$ bound in \cref{thm:vec-bipartite} immediately gives an
equal improvement to the $k^2$ factor in the bounds of each of our other results." If correct,
the writeup therefore also improves Theorems 1.1, 1.2, 1.4, 1.5, 1.6 of the source paper.
This raises the stakes and is the main reason I audited every line rather than spot-checking.

## Caveats

1. **Novelty, not correctness, is the residual risk.** I could not find the result or its
   load-bearing lemmas in the literature, and the source paper's v2 still poses the problem —
   but Lemma 5 (approximate midpoint closure ⇒ $\log$-factor convexification) is a clean, general
   statement of the kind that could exist in discrepancy theory under other language. A human
   referee should search that literature before publication. The only web hit connecting this
   claim to the paper is this campaign's own repository (self-contamination, discounted).
2. **The announced claim understates what is proved.** Proposition 6 improves the source paper's
   `thm:vec-bipartite` from $O(k^2)$ to $O(k\log k)$; by the authors' own remark this propagates
   to *all* of their main theorems. The writeup mentions this only in passing ("It also
   establishes the corresponding $O(k\log(k+1))$ bipartite perfect-matching estimate").
3. **Order not determined.** The writeup is explicit that it does not rule out $O(k)$. In the
   general vector setting the truth is now pinned between $k$ (sibling counterexample) and
   $3kB_k$; for *colourings* the paper's lower bound is only $\sqrt{k/2}$ and its
   $O(\sqrt k)$ conjecture is untouched.
4. **Restricted Hamilton-cycle family.** The complete-graph construction only ever produces
   cycles that alternate between $A$ and $B$ (plus one $A$-internal edge when $N$ is odd). That is
   legitimate for an upper bound, but it means the bound is not tight by construction and the
   argument says nothing about which Hamilton cycles achieve it.
5. **Edge cases.** $n\ge2$ (bipartite) and $N\ge3$ (complete) are required and stated; Lemma 7
   needs $N\ge4$ (the $N-3$ denominator) and $N=3$ is handled separately; $d=1$ works with
   $B_1=2$. Disconnected/empty hosts do not arise. Corollary 2 additionally needs the host to be
   colour-balanced and $k\mid 2n$ resp. $k\mid N$ — necessary, and stated.
6. **Terse but repairable-as-written passages** (none rise to GAP): the dyadic-to-arbitrary
   limiting argument in Lemma 5; the "at most $4/N^2$" assertion in (22) (true for all $N\ge4$, I
   checked the algebra and the small cases); the one-line Auerbach basis construction.
7. **Constants are not optimised** and the $\ell_1$/general-norm constants differ by the factor
   $\tfrac12$ used in the recolouring translation; I checked that translation is applied
   consistently in (3) and (4).
