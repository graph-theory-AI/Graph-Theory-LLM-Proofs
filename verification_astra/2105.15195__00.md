## Interpretation

The interpretation is correct, and the writeup gets a subtle point right that the catalog
got wrong.

`c_r` is defined in the writeup as
`c_r = inf over partitions N = A_1 ⊔ … ⊔ A_r of max_i d̄_log(Σ(A_i))`.
I fetched the source paper (arXiv:2105.15195v3, downloaded and converted to text) and this
is verbatim CFP's definition ("let `c_r` be the minimum, taken over all partitions of N
into r parts, of the maximum over i of the upper logarithmic density of Σ(A_i)").
`Σ(A)` is sums of distinct elements, `d̄_log` is `limsup_x (1/log x) Σ_{n≤x, n∈S} 1/n`.
Both match.

**The catalog's `2^r b_0 − r` is a PDF-extraction garble.** CFP's Theorem 1 reads
`(1 − 1/(2b_0))(1 + 1/(2r b_0 − r))` with `b_0` the root `>1` of `b^r − 2rb + r − 1`.
The writeup's §6 diagnoses this correctly and proves the intended `2r` version; it also
correctly observes the two coincide at `r = 2`, which is why the r=2 sanity check hides the
error. This is exactly right and is confirmed against the source PDF (line 119 ff. of my
extraction). Marking the literal `2^r` reading as "false even as an upper bound" is a fair
call, not interpretation gaming: a reasonable author would regard the `2r` statement as the
conjecture.

So the target is CFP's **Conjecture 10** (§4 of the source paper): `c_r = b_0^{r-1}/(2r)`
for `r ≥ 3`. The writeup claims to prove it for all `r ≥ 2`. That is the intended problem.

**What the writeup gets wrong is not the statement but the diagnosis of what is open.**
The writeup asserts (intro and §6) that "the key addition … is an additive lemma for dense
sets of primes. It provides the required intervals of subset sums without requiring any
monochromatic interval of integers", and closes with "the gap in the previous attempt is
closed by Lemma 3". That step was never the gap — see the Reference check.

## Reference check

I downloaded and read the full source paper (`https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.15195v3`). The
writeup cites **nothing at all**, and the reference check is where it comes apart.

1. **CFP's Lemma 2 already is the writeup's Lemma 3, in stronger form.** Source paper §2:

   > *Lemma 2.* For every positive integer r, there are positive constants `C = C(r)` and
   > `C' = C'(r)` such that the following holds. For every `N > 0` and every partition
   > `N ∩ [N, eN) = A_1 ⊔ … ⊔ A_r` into r colour classes, there is some `i ∈ [r]` such that
   > `Σ(A_i)` contains all positive integers in `[CN, C'N²]`.

   That is precisely "at every scale, one colour's subset sums fill an interval from `~N` to
   `~N²`, with no monochromatic interval of integers required" — the exact thing the writeup
   advertises as its "key addition". CFP's version is **stronger**: the endpoints are
   `CN` and `C'N²` with constants, whereas the writeup loses `(log n)^{±3}` at both ends.
   CFP prove it from Theorem 6.1 of their companion paper arXiv:2104.14766 (with an
   alternative route via Szemerédi–Vu, Remark 5), using integers free of prime factors
   `≤ r²` instead of primes — the same "no small common divisor" trick, implemented better.
   The writeup's §4 (auxiliary colouring by dyadic scale, transfer to logarithmic density)
   is CFP's §3 and their Lemma 6.

2. **The actual open step is the writeup's Lemma 1, and the writeup never says so.** CFP's
   Lemma 7 is *exactly* the writeup's Lemma 1 specialised to `r = 2`, `λ = 2`: they prove
   that `S(φ,i) = ∪_{α(j)=i}[j,2j]` has upper density at least
   `f_2 = inf_{z∈[0,1)} (1−z/2)/(1−z²)`, which is `C_{2,2}` under `z = 1/q`. They prove it
   with a Brouwer fixed-point argument on the pair `(ā_n, b̄_n)` (source paper §3, Claims 8
   and 9), and their concluding remarks say:

   > "We have explored this conjecture in some detail ourselves, but were unable to establish
   > the optimality of our upper bound for `c_r` without additional assumptions. For instance,
   > it seems that our methods do apply if the auxiliary colouring α defined in Section 3 is
   > assumed to be **cyclic** … Since every two-colouring of the positive integers is
   > automatically cyclic in this sense, this restriction does not hamper us in that case."

   So the obstruction is entirely in the geometric lemma for non-cyclic colour words, and
   the writeup's Lemma 1 is a direct attack on it. The writeup does not mention Lemma 7,
   Brouwer, the cyclic restriction, or Conjecture 10 by name.

3. **Inheritance from the previous attempt.** Lemma 1 is *not new in this writeup*: it is
   the previous model's Theorem 2.1, with the same construction and the same
   telescoping/covering skeleton. The retry tightened the boundary accounting in (12) and
   the derivation of `η`, and dropped the previous attempt's unnecessary reduction to `s ≤ r`
   active colours. So the load-bearing novelty is inherited, and the part the writeup
   advertises as its contribution is the redundant one. Both models misdiagnosed which half
   of CFP's argument was missing: the previous attempt declared the additive reduction to be
   the gap (it is not — CFP did it in 2021) and reported `partial`; the retry "closed" that
   non-gap and reported `proved`.

4. Externally invoked results are all stated correctly: **Bertrand's postulate** (a prime in
   `(2n,4n)` — apply Bertrand to `2n`; valid for every `n ≥ 1`), the **prime number theorem**
   (`π(2n) − π(n) ≥ n/(2 log n)` for large n), **Fermat's little theorem**, and the
   **Combinatorial Nullstellensatz** (the coefficient form, sketched via Lagrange
   interpolation). Lemma 2 is the Dias da Silva–Hamidoune theorem (1994), whose polynomial
   proof is due to Alon–Nathanson–Ruzsa; the writeup labels it "standard" but names nobody.

5. **Priority (step 2b).** Nothing found. Searches over the citing literature and 2025–2026
   arXiv listings turned up no paper resolving Conjecture 10 for `r ≥ 3`; the catalog's own
   May-2026 review reports the same. The companion paper arXiv:2104.14766 (to appear in
   JEMS) develops the additive machinery but does not settle the tightness conjecture. So
   the *theorem* is not already known — but the writeup's self-declared key lemma is, and
   that is why I set `references_ok: false`.

6. **No self-contamination (step 2c).** The prior art above is not a web paraphrase. I
   downloaded `https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.15195v3` directly, converted it with `pdftotext`,
   and read Lemma 2, Lemma 7 and the concluding remarks in the original text; the paper has
   named authors (Conlon, Fox, Pham), an arXiv identifier, a v3 date of 22 Sep 2022 and a
   venue (Mathematika 68 (2022), 1292–1301). It predates this campaign by four years and is
   the very paper the prompt links as the source. Nothing I recorded as known traces back to
   this repository.

## Caveats

1. **Attribution / novelty (the main one).** Lemma 3 duplicates, more weakly, CFP's
   published Lemma 2; §4 duplicates their §3 and Lemma 6; §5 duplicates their Theorem 1.
   The one step that is actually new — Lemma 1 for `r ≥ 3` non-cyclic colour words — was
   inherited from the previous attempt and is nowhere flagged as the contribution. As
   written the paper would be rejected on scholarship grounds even if every line is right.
2. **Omitted determinant identity** in Lemma 2 (step #16). Routine; I checked it.
3. **Lemma 3 has no finite content**: the interval it produces is empty below `n ≈ 2.4×10⁷`
   and the proof's parameter inequalities only bite around `n ≈ 10¹⁵`. This is legitimate
   (everything is "for sufficiently large n") but it means no computational check can
   validate Lemma 3 directly; I could only verify its internal inequalities symbolically and
   its qualitative content on small dense prime sets.
4. **Prime-existence risk (flagged in my brief): clean.** Bertrand is used only to pick a
   prime in `(2n, 4n)`, which exists for every `n ≥ 1` (apply Bertrand to `2n`); no stronger
   prime-gap input is needed and nothing depends on a prime existing in a shrinking window.
   The PNT is used only as a lower bound `≥ n/(2 log n)` for large n. Every use is
   asymptotic, so there is no sub-threshold small-case obligation.
5. **Interlacing risk: not applicable.** "Interlacing" here means interlacing of the colour
   classes along N, not eigenvalue interlacing. There is no matrix, no submatrix, and no
   spectral argument anywhere in the writeup, so the principal-submatrix / symmetry failure
   mode cannot arise.
6. **`r = 1` is excluded** (the polynomial has no root `> 1`); the theorem is stated for
   `r ≥ 2`, which is the right range. Finitely many integers may be recoloured arbitrarily
   throughout — harmless for a limsup density, and correctly noted in §5.
7. **`λ ↑ 2` is a genuine limit, not an attained value.** For each `λ < 2` the threshold
   index `k_0(λ)` differs; this is fine because the conclusion is a limsup, but it means the
   proof never produces the intervals `[t_k, 2t_{k+1}]` directly. The writeup handles it
   correctly (the `(2−λ)k log 2` slack absorbs the `3 log log n` erosion).
8. **`would_publish: true` in the verdict block is over-claimed** relative to what the
   document is: the mathematics may well be publishable, the document is not.
9. My confidence is `medium`, not `high`, deliberately. I re-derived Lemma 1 from scratch
   line by line, verified its structural claims numerically, and found no counterexample to
   its conclusion in an exhaustive word search — but it is a short elementary argument
   settling a point that Conlon, Fox and Pham state they "explored in some detail" without
   success, and a single referee pass is not the same as the scrutiny such a claim deserves.
   The residual risk is concentrated entirely in Lemma 1's `O(1)` bookkeeping (steps 8–11).
