## Interpretation

**What the catalog asks.** Target `__00` is the source paper's *Question 1.1* ("Main
question, informal"): *"For which graphs `F` is there an `F`-counting lemma in `C4`-free
graphs?"* This is the full-characterization question. The writeup does **not** answer it.
It answers one named sub-case: the Petersen half of the paper's Open Problem 2.17. The
verdict block says `partial` and the closing section says so explicitly ("The full
characterization of countable graphs is not obtained, and the dodecahedral graph is not
decided here"). That self-assessment is accurate and not overclaimed.

**The target definition, retrieved verbatim.** I downloaded the arXiv TeX source of
2106.03261 (`https://arxiv.org/e-print/2106.03261`, single file `c4free-counting.tex`,
v1, 6 Jun 2021) and read Definition 1.3 (`\begin{definition}\label{def:CL}`) directly:

> A graph `F` is *countable* if, for every `ε > 0`, there exists `δ = δ(F,ε) > 0` such
> that if `G` is an `n`-vertex `C4`-free graph on vertex set `V` and `H ∈ [0,1]^{V×V}` is
> a symmetric matrix (i.e., an edge-weighted graph) satisfying
> `|e_G(A,B)/n^{3/2} − e_H(A,B)/n²| ≤ δ` for all `A,B ⊆ V`,
> (here `e_G(A,B) = {(x,y) ∈ A×B : xy ∈ E(G)}` and `e_H(A,B) = Σ_{x∈A,y∈B} H(x,y)`),
> then, for every `𝑨 = (A_v)_{v∈V(F)}` with `A_v ⊆ V`, one has
> `hom_𝑨(F,G) / n^{|V(F)|−|E(F)|/2} ≥ hom_𝑨(F,H) / n^{|V(F)|} − ε`.

The writeup's Theorem items 3 and 4 are exactly the negation of this with `F = P`,
`H = A_{D_q}` (a 0/1, hence `[0,1]`, symmetric matrix), and `A_v = V` for all `v`:
item 3 is the discrepancy hypothesis, and item 4 gives `hom(P,G_q)/n^{5/2} = 0` while
`hom(P,H)/n^{10} ≥ 10^{-10}`. The exponent `v(P) − e(P)/2 = 10 − 15/2 = 5/2` is the
definition's own normalisation. **The interpretation is the intended one**; it is not a
literal-reading strawman, and a reasonable author of the problem would regard the
Petersen half of Open Problem 2.17 as resolved in the negative.

Three points in the writeup's favour on interpretation:

1. **It proves more than the definition requires.** Definition 1.3 fails as soon as
   (1.2) fails for *one* family `𝑨`. The writeup proves `hom(P,G_q) = 0` outright
   (§4), so it fails for *every* `𝑨` simultaneously.
2. **It imposes extra self-restrictions the definition does not ask for.** Definition 1.3
   has no maximum-degree hypothesis and no lower bound on `e(G)`; the writeup additionally
   gives `Δ(G_q) ≤ (3/10)√n` (well inside the `2√n` that the paper's §3 trimming lemma
   shows is WLOG) and `e(G_q) = (3/20+o(1))n^{3/2}`, a constant fraction of the extremal
   `(1/2+o(1))n^{3/2}`. The normalisations match exactly: `e_{G_q}(V,V)/n^{3/2} → 0.3` and
   `e_{D_q}(V,V)/n² = 30m²/n² = 0.3`.
3. **The result is informative, not a technicality.** The paper's concluding remarks
   conjecture that 2-density `< 2` (`|E(F')| ≤ 2|V(F')|−4`) is also necessary. Petersen
   satisfies it (`15 ≤ 16`), so this shows the true conditions are strictly more
   stringent — which the authors themselves suspected.

not evaluate the earlier Heawood claim.

## Caveats

1. **Scope vs. the catalog question.** `__00` is the paper's *Question 1.1* — the full
   characterization. The writeup settles one named instance. `partial` is the right verdict;
   `would_publish: true` is defensible because the instance is a named open problem restated
   by a coauthor six weeks before the attack, but the headline "for which graphs `F`…"
   question is untouched, and so is the dodecahedron.
2. **Duplicate of the sibling target.** `attacks_retry/2106.03261__01` — same model, same
   batch — produces essentially the same construction (affine orthogonal-polarity host, ten
   classes, one twisted class pair, a Desargues-type forcing lemma, the Petersen-is-a-core
   reduction) and was independently refereed CONFIRMED. `__00` and `__01` are one result,
   not two. My verification here is independent of that report (my own scripts, my own
   retrieval of Definition 1.3), and it agrees.
3. **No citations whatsoever.** Definition 1.3 is used without attributing it to the source
   paper; Lemma 1 is not identified as a polarity form of Desargues' theorem; Lemma 3 (the
   Petersen graph is a core) is classical and proved from scratch without saying so; and
   Tait–Timmons (Petersen `⊆ ER_q` for all `q ≥ 3`) is the obvious related result and is not
   mentioned. None of this affects correctness, but all of it would have to be fixed before
   submission, and the last one matters because it is the closest prior art.
4. **Residual novelty risk.** Coxeter's *My graph* (1983) and the classical finite-geometry
   literature could not be full-text searched. The forcing statement is of a kind that could
   plausibly already appear there. The writeup itself declines to claim novelty, which is the
   right posture.
5. **Hypothesis stronger than needed.** Lemma 1 assumes `A_0, A_1` nonisotropic; exhaustive
   search says the conclusion survives without it. Harmless (the construction supplies it for
   free by deleting the isotropic points), but it means the excision `2y ≠ x²` may not be
   strictly necessary — it is, however, needed to make the diagonalisation argument as written
   go through, so I would not call it removable without rewriting the proof.
6. **Small-case degeneracies, all harmless.** At `q = 11` (`k = 1`) the stated lower degree
   bound `3k − 6 = −3` is vacuous and some vertices are isolated; the claims are asymptotic
   and the upper bounds still hold exactly. The sequence `n = (q−1)²` with `q = 11^r` is
   sparse in `N`, which is fine: Definition 1.3 needs only arbitrarily large admissible `n`.
7. **Presentation of (12).** The `30m√q` bound is crude; Cauchy–Schwarz would give
   `3√q·√(|S||T|)` and a bound of `3q^{-1/2}` rather than `3√q/(q−1) + 1/q`. Both tend to 0,
   so nothing depends on it.
8. **What is *not* claimed and should not be read in.** This does not show the untwisted
   polarity host is a counterexample (it is not — it has `≍ q^6/10^{10}` Petersen copies, as
   my table shows and as Tait–Timmons predicts); it does not give a new *necessary* condition
   in closed form; and it says nothing about the dodecahedral graph, whose analogous
   configuration would need its own forcing lemma.

## Referee summary

I set out to break this writeup and could not. The mathematical core is a single new
lemma — that among ten pairwise distinct points of `PG(2,K)`, `char K ≠ 2`, fourteen of the
fifteen Petersen adjacency-orthogonalities force the fifteenth — and it survives a symbolic
proof over `Z[α,β,γ,x,y,z,w,r,s,t,u]`, exhaustive verification over `PG(2,5)` and `PG(2,7)`
for two different nondegenerate forms (45240 configurations, zero violations), and the
operational test that matters: in the actual host graph, all 8733 canonical fourteen-edge
configurations found at `q = 61..191` satisfy the fifteenth orthogonality, so the twisted
graph has zero Petersen copies while the untwisted control has 4337 at `q = 191`. The
surrounding construction checks out exhaustively — `C4`-free (max codegree exactly 1),
triangle-free, `Δ = (3/10)√n`, `e = (3/20+o(1))n^{3/2}`, all fifteen pairs of density
`(1+o(1))n^{-1/2}`, spectral identity and mixing bound exact — and the conclusion is drawn
against Definition 1.3 of the source paper, which I retrieved verbatim from the arXiv TeX
and which the writeup restates faithfully and conservatively (it proves `hom(P,G_q) = 0`
outright, so the definition fails for *every* vertex-restriction family, and it respects the
paper's own degree-trimming reduction with room to spare). The result is not in the
literature: a CFSZ coauthor restated it as open in arXiv:2608.01525 on 2 August 2026, six
weeks before this attack ran. The honest qualifications are that this settles only the
Petersen half of Open Problem 2.17 and not the catalog's actual question (the full
characterization) nor the dodecahedron; that it is the same result the sibling target
`__01` produced in the same batch, so the two are one contribution; that the writeup cites
nothing at all, including the definition it refutes, the Desargues theorem its key lemma
restates, and Tait–Timmons' theorem that the Petersen graph embeds in every `ER_q`; and
that I could not full-text search the classical finite-geometry literature (Coxeter 1983 in
particular) where the forcing observation could conceivably already live.
