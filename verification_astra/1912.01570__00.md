## Interpretation

The conjecture under attack is, verbatim from the published version (EJC 28(4), P4.5, 2021,
Conjecture 14; identical to Conjecture 2 of arXiv:1912.01570v1):

> **Conjecture 14.** For every planar graph G, fvs(G) ⩽ 2 · fp(G), where fp(G) is the
> maximum size of a face-packing of G, i.e., a cycle-packing where, for some embedding of G,
> every cycle bounds a face.

and the same paper's Section 1 fixes the other two terms: "A cycle packing of a (multi)graph
G is a set of vertex disjoint cycles that appear in G as subgraphs"; "A feedback vertex set
of a (multi)graph G is a set S of vertices such that G − S is a forest."

The writeup uses exactly these definitions: vertex-disjoint cycles, all bounding faces in one
common embedding, maximised over embeddings. I checked both the arXiv v1 and the published
EJC text: **there is no connectivity, degree, simplicity or 2-connectivity hypothesis** —
the quantifier is literally "for every planar graph G". The counterexample G_1 is a simple,
connected, 2-connected planar graph on 12 vertices with maximum degree 4, so it is inside the
stated scope.

Three further points in favour of the interpretation being the intended one:

1. The writeup uses the *most generous* reading of fp — the maximum over **all** embeddings.
   Any stricter reading (fp of a fixed plane graph, as used e.g. in Definition 1.5 of
   arXiv:2401.07376) only makes fp smaller and the conjecture harder, so the refutation is
   robust to the convention. fvs(G_1) = 5 > 4 ≥ 2·fp(G_1) under either reading. It is even
   robust to the *looser-than-intended* reading in which each packed cycle may be facial in
   an embedding of its own rather than in one common embedding: my computation of the set of
   cycles that are facial in **some** embedding of G_1 found exactly 20 such cycles, and the
   largest pairwise vertex-disjoint subfamily of that whole union is still only 2. So no
   quantifier placement in the definition rescues the conjecture on this graph.
2. The authors' own sentence immediately before the conjecture — "the example of many nested
   disjoint cycles shows that the embedding cannot be fixed" — shows they were deliberately
   quantifying over embeddings and were consciously thinking about low-connectivity /
   disconnected configurations. So a 2-connected example exploiting 2-cuts is not an
   off-scope technicality that the authors implicitly excluded.
3. The conjecture is presented as a deliberately provocative strengthening ("To highlight how
   little we understand around Jones' Conjecture, we conclude by posing the following stronger
   conjecture"), i.e. a one-paragraph closing remark rather than a scrutinised central claim.
   A small counterexample is entirely plausible for such a statement.

There is **no interpretation gaming** here: this is a genuine refutation of the conjecture as
written and as published. I would expect the original authors to accept it.

The one legitimate scope reservation (see Caveats) is that the construction leans essentially
on 2-cuts, so the *interesting* question — whether fvs ≤ 2·fp survives for 3-connected planar
graphs, where Whitney makes the embedding essentially unique — is untouched. The writeup does
not raise this, but it also does not claim to.

## Reference check

The writeup invokes **no external theorem** — it is fully self-contained (only the Jordan curve
argument and fvs(K_4) = 2, both elementary). So there is nothing for it to have misquoted. What
I checked instead is the target statement itself and the surrounding literature:

* **arXiv:1912.01570v1** (fetched PDF, extracted text). Conjecture 2 reads exactly as the
  prompt quotes it. Section 1 defines cycle packing as vertex-disjoint cycles and fvs as the
  minimum feedback vertex set size. **Confirmed.**
* **Published version**, *Jones' Conjecture in subcubic graphs*, Electronic J. Combin. 28(4)
  P4.5 (2021), fetched from the QUB repository. The conjecture is renumbered **Conjecture 14**
  and is word-for-word identical, including "For every planar graph G" with no extra
  hypotheses. **Confirmed — the arXiv statement was not silently amended in the journal
  version.**
* **arXiv:2401.07376**, Bärnkopf & Győri, *Jones' conjecture for Halin graphs and a bit more*
  (the only related work named in the catalog). Fetched and read. Definition 1.5 defines face
  packing (with a fixed embedding), and **Remark 2.5** states "Every based planar graph G
  satisfies fvs(G) ⩽ 2·fp(G)". This is a published theorem that would *contradict* the
  writeup if G_1 happened to be a based planar graph, so I checked: across all 32 planar
  embeddings of G_1, **no face is adjacent to every other face**, so G_1 is not based planar
  and there is no conflict. **Confirmed consistent.**
* **Priority.** Semantic Scholar lists only 3 papers citing arXiv:1912.01570 (Bärnkopf–Győri
  2024; *Far-apart Erdős–Pósa property of long cycles* 2026; *Minimum maximal matchings in
  cubic graphs* 2020); none refutes Conjecture 14. Several targeted web searches for a
  counterexample to the face-packing conjecture returned nothing. The catalog's own 2026-05
  auto-review also found nothing. I therefore found **no evidence of prior publication**, but
  I cannot certify a negative — a short note of this kind could easily be unindexed or
  folklore. This is the one reason the verdict is CONFIRMED rather than provably novel.

## Computational check

Scripts under `verification_astra/scripts/1912.01570__00/` (Python 3, networkx 3.6.1, nauty
`geng`). All of them run in well under a minute except `minimality.py 9`.

**`build.py`** — builds G_t and brute-forces the invariants.

```

```

**`embeddings.py`** — *the decisive check*. Enumerates **every** rotation system of G_1 (all
cyclic orders at every vertex: 6^4 · 2^8 = 331 776), keeps those with V − E + F = 2 (i.e.
genus 0 = sphere embeddings, the most permissive reading of "some embedding"), traces the faces
and takes the maximum vertex-disjoint family of facial cycles.

```
enumerated 331776 rotation systems -> 32 planar ones
distinct cycles facial in SOME planar embedding: 20  (all 20 meet a gadget)
*** fp(G_1) = 2  (writeup claims 2) ***   witnessing packing: {p1,r1,s1}, {p0,q0,r0}
C_1 facial in some planar embedding: False
```

So **fvs(G_1) = 5 > 4 = 2·fp(G_1)**, established by two independent brute forces with no appeal
to the writeup's reasoning.

**`prose_check.py`** — guards against my having mis-transcribed the construction. I rebuilt

and two disjoint K_4's. Connect one K_4 to a,c, and the other to b,d, using distinct attachment

Conjecture 14 requires fvs <= 2*fp, i.e. 5 <= 4: False

**`sanity_and_t2.py`** — validates the enumerator and extends to larger t.

* Enumerator sanity (Whitney: a 3-connected planar graph has exactly 2 rotation systems of
  genus 0, a mirror pair): K_4 → 2, cube Q_3 → 2, octahedron → 2, C_5 → 1, K_5 → 0,
  K_{3,3} → 0. All as expected.
* A second, independent upper bound on fp: if C bounds a face in some embedding then a new
  apex vertex can be placed in that face and joined to all of V(C), so `G + apex(V(C))` is
  planar. Hence fp(G) ≤ max disjoint family among cycles passing this test — a *rigorous*
  upper bound. On G_1 the apex test selects exactly the same 20 cycles the exhaustive
  enumeration found to be facial (sets equal), which validates it.

```
t=1: 20 apex-candidate cycles, fp <= 2 (= t+1); no C_i passes the test
t=2: 36 apex-candidate cycles, fp <= 3 (= t+1); no C_i passes the test
t=3: 58 apex-candidate cycles, fp <= 4 (= t+1); no C_i passes the test
```
* The K_4-subdivision witnesses of S7 were printed and checked internally disjoint for
  t = 1, 2, 3, e.g. t=1: P = a1–p1–q1–c1, R = b1–p0–q0–d1.

**`lower_bound.py`** — exhibits a concrete planar embedding of G_t, traces its real face list

**`consistency_based.py`** — cross-check against the published Remark 2.5 of arXiv:2401.07376:
across all 32 planar embeddings of G_1, the number of embeddings in which some face is adjacent
to every other face is **0**, so G_1 is not a based planar graph and the counterexample does not
contradict that theorem.

**`small_search.py` / `minimality.py`** — independent search for counterexamples among small
planar graphs, using the rigorous apex upper bound (so any hit would be a genuine

connected one.) The n = 9 sweep (261 080 connected graphs) did not finish inside this review's

counterexample beyond "at least 9 vertices". The writeup itself explicitly disclaims minimality,
so nothing hinges on this.

## Caveats

1. **Only the literal statement falls.** The construction depends critically on the 2-cuts
   {p_j, q_j}: the whole force of Section 3 is that a gadget reachable by only two edges can
   host at most one packed cycle. G_t is 2-connected but never 3-connected (verified:
   `node_connectivity` = 2 for t = 1, 2). For 3-connected planar graphs Whitney's theorem makes
   the embedding unique up to reflection and fp becomes the dual's independence number, and the
   conjecture is untouched there. A referee should regard the natural repaired question —
   *does fvs ≤ 2·fp hold for 3-connected planar graphs?* — as still open. The writeup does not
   mention this, which is the main thing I would ask an author to add.
2. **The ratio-3 claim is an asymptotic supremum, not attained.** fvs/fp = 3 − 1/(t+1) < 3 for
   every t; no member of the family achieves ratio 3. The writeup states this correctly but a
   careless reader could take "tending to 3" as "reaching 3".
3. **No upper bound is proved.** The writeup is explicit about this: it shows c < 3 is
   impossible, not that c = 3 works. Whether fvs ≤ 3·fp holds is open (the known fvs ≤ 3·cp
   does not imply it, since fp ≤ cp — exactly the point the source paper itself makes).
4. **Minimality is unclaimed and unproven.** 12 vertices is not shown to be least; I verified
   exhaustively only that no counterexample exists on ≤ 8 vertices. Whether a 9-, 10- or
   11-vertex counterexample exists is open as far as this review goes.
5. **Small-t edge cases.** The theorem is stated for t ≥ 1 and that is necessary: "t = 0" would
   be a lone K_4 with fvs = 2, fp = 1, which satisfies the conjecture with equality. The
   degenerate inputs the conjecture might be probed with (k = 0, forests, empty graph,
   disconnected graphs) are all irrelevant here since a single explicit connected graph suffices.
6. **Convention robustness.** I checked the refutation under the *generous* reading (max over
   all sphere embeddings). Under a plane/fixed-outer-face reading, or the fixed-embedding
   reading of Definition 1.5 in arXiv:2401.07376, fp can only be smaller, so the refutation is
   a fortiori valid. The one reading under which it would fail — fp counting edge-disjoint
   rather than vertex-disjoint facial cycles — is excluded by the source paper's own definition
   of cycle packing ("vertex disjoint cycles").
7. **Priority not certifiable.** I found no publication of this counterexample, but absence of
   evidence in three targeted searches plus a 3-item citation list is weak evidence of absence.
   The writeup's own caveat ("no claim of ... literature priority") is appropriate.

## Referee summary

I set out to break this writeup and could not. The conjecture it attacks is quoted correctly
from both the arXiv and the published EJC version, with no hypothesis silently dropped: it
really does say "for every planar graph G". The 12-vertex graph G_1 — a 4-cycle with a K_4
hung on each of its two opposite-vertex pairs — is a genuine counterexample, and I verified
both of its invariants by brute force without using any of the writeup's reasoning:
fvs(G_1) = 5 by exhaustive subset search, and fp(G_1) = 2 by enumerating all 331 776 rotation
systems of the graph, isolating the 32 that are planar, and maximising disjoint facial cycles
over every one of them. A second, structurally independent method (the apex-planarity criterion)
reproduces the identical set of facial-realisable cycles and extends fp(G_t) = t+1 to t = 2, 3,
with fp ≥ t+1 confirmed by explicit traced embeddings up to t = 6. Every one of the twelve
steps I extracted from the writeup is VALID; the two combinatorial lemmas doing the real work
(at most one packed cycle per K_4 gadget; no C_i is facial in any embedding, via a K_4-subdivision
and a Jordan-curve parity argument) are both correct as written and both confirmed by exhaustive
computation. The result is also consistent with the published literature: the family has
cp(G_t) = 2t+1 so it does not touch Jones' Conjecture itself, and G_1 is not a based planar graph,
so it does not contradict Remark 2.5 of arXiv:2401.07376. The honest reservations are about
scope rather than correctness: the construction lives entirely on 2-cuts, so the interesting
3-connected case is untouched, no upper bound replaces the refuted one, minimality is unproven
(I checked exhaustively that no counterexample exists on 8 or fewer vertices), and I cannot certify that this
counterexample is unpublished, though nothing in the three papers citing the source suggests
otherwise. Subject to that last point, the claimed disproof stands.
