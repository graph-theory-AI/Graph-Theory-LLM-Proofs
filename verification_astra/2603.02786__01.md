# Referee report: 2603.02786__01 (leg `attacks_retry`)

**Target.** Conjecture 4 of Alon, Dębski, Grytczuk, Przybyło, *Packing arithmetic
progressions* (arXiv:2603.02786): $M_{\mathbb P(n)}(n)\sim\frac16\cdot\frac{n^3}{\ln n}$.
The writeup claims a full proof, via (i) an exact pairwise-avoidance criterion, (ii) an
exact packing lemma for blocks of prime differences that themselves form an arithmetic
progression with difference divisible by $\mathrm{lcm}(1,\dots,k-1)$, and (iii) the
dense-subset (Szemerédi-in-the-primes) form of the Green–Tao theorem to partition almost
all primes into such blocks.

**Bottom line.** I attacked this from every angle the task brief suggested — regime of
validity of the "exact" lemma, dropped error terms in the exact→asymptotic passage, and a
divisibility hypothesis failing for infinitely many parameters — and the proof survives all
three. The divisibility worry in particular is the one the writeup anticipates and defuses
correctly (the $Q\mid h$ condition is *imposed* inside Lemma 3 via a pigeonhole on residue
classes mod $Q$, not hoped for). The exact→asymptotic passage is the genuinely delicate
part and it is handled with the right order of quantifiers ($n\to\infty$ at fixed block
size $k$, then $k\to\infty$). I could not break it. However, the theorem is **not new**:
it is Theorem 1.2 of arXiv:2609.07487 (Hou, Liu, Zhao, 7 September 2026), posted ten days
before this attack ran. Hence ALREADY_KNOWN rather than CONFIRMED.

## Interpretation

I fetched the source paper (PDF **and** TeX source, `Packing_AP_23.02.tex`) and checked the
formalization line by line.

- **Objects.** Paper §1: $B_d=D_d\cap[nd]=\{d,2d,\dots,nd\}$ ($n$ terms); a packing of
  $\mathcal G_D=\{B_d:d\in D\}$ into $[m]$ is a choice of **integer** shifts $s_d$ with
  $s_d+B_d\subseteq[m]$ and the copies pairwise disjoint; $M_D(n)=m(\mathcal G_D)$ is the
  least such $m$. The writeup's $A_d(b)=\{b,b+d,\dots,b+(n-1)d\}$ is exactly a shifted copy
  of $B_d$, and "interval of length $L$ = $L$ consecutive integers" matches $[m]$. **Match.**
- **Conjecture 4.** Confirmed verbatim from the paper (p. 15, `\label{c64}`):
  $M_{\mathbb P(n)}(n)\sim\frac16\cdot\frac{n^3}{\ln n}$, with $\mathbb P(x)$ the primes
  $\le x$. So the difference set is *all* primes up to $n$ ($\pi(n)$ progressions, each of
  length $n$), not a truncation. The writeup packs exactly this family.
- **Strengthening, not gaming.** The writeup proves the sharper
  $M_{\mathbb P(n)}(n)=S(n)+o(n^3/\log n)$ with $S(n)=\sum_{p\le n}p(n-p)$, and derives the
  constant $1/6$ from the prime number theorem. This is strictly more informative than the
  conjecture; no hypothesis is weakened and no strawman reading is used. A reasonable author
  of the conjecture would consider it resolved.
- **Consistency with the paper's own results.** The paper proves
  $M(n)=M_{[n]}(n)\le(0.526+o(1))n^3/\ln n$ (Theorem 2) and its construction restricted to
  primes gives roughly $11/24\approx0.458$ (eq. (4.1): primes $\le n/2$ cost $p(n-p)$ each
  but primes in $(n/2,n]$ cost a full $np$ each). The prior attempt in the prompt got $1/4$.
  The new writeup gets $1/6$, i.e. it eliminates the $p>n/2$ loss entirely. That is a large
  claimed improvement, which is why I checked Lemma 2 by machine rather than by eye.
- **Cross-check against sibling `2603.02786__03`.** That target disproved Conjecture 6 by
  showing composite differences $2p,3p,5p$ force extra space. There is no tension: the
  mechanism there needs $\gcd>1$ between differences, and here all differences are distinct
  primes, so it cannot apply. The two results are compatible.

Lemma 1 then applies to **every** pair $i<j$, not merely consecutive ones. |
| S8. Total slack $\le 2Qkn$. Re-derived; correct (and slack, which is fine for an upper bound). |
| S9. The writeup's remark that mere existence of long prime APs would not suffice is correct and honest. |
| S10. **Lemma 3**: all but $o_{k,Q}(\pi(n))$ primes $\le n$ partition into $k$-APs with $Q\mid$ difference | VALID | Maximal disjoint collection; if the residue set $R_n$ has $\ge\delta\pi(n)$ elements, pigeonhole gives a class mod $Q$ with $\ge(\delta/Q)\pi(n)$ of them, and S9 (with the fixed density $\delta/Q$) yields a $k$-AP *inside that class*, whose difference is automatically divisible by $Q$ — contradicting maximality. | S11. Blocks satisfy Lemma 2's hypotheses | VALID | Distinct primes $\Rightarrow$ pairwise coprime; all $\le n$; AP with $Q\mid h$; nonconstant so strictly increasing after ordering. |
| S12. | S13. $\limsup_n\frac{M-S}{n^2\pi(n)}\le\frac1k$ for each fixed $k$, hence $\le0$ | VALID | $t_n\le\pi(n)/k$ gives $t_nn^2\le n^2\pi(n)/k$; $u_nn^2=o(n^2\pi(n))$ by S10; $2Qkn\,t_n\le2Qn\pi(n)=o(n^2\pi(n))$ with $Q,k$ fixed. **This is the exact→asymptotic passage and it is done correctly**: the limsup is a single number independent of $k$, so taking $k\to\infty$ afterwards is legitimate. ## Reference check

- **arXiv:2603.02786** (Alon, Dębski, Grytczuk, Przybyło). Fetched the PDF *and* the LaTeX
  source. Confirmed: the definition of $M_D(n)$ (integer shifts into $[m]$, $B_d$ of size
  $n$); **Conjecture 4** verbatim ($\texttt{\\label{c64}}$, p. 15); Theorem 5 and its
  lower-bound proof on p. 10 (the CRT bound $|s_{\pi(p_{i+1})}-s_{\pi(p_i)}|\ge
  (n-1)p_{\pi(i)}-p_{\pi(i)}p_{\pi(i+1)}$ — exactly the writeup's §5 inequality); the
  paper's own upper-bound construction (4.1), which pays a full $np$ for every prime in
  $(n/2,k]$. Nothing is misquoted; the writeup in fact cites the paper for nothing, proving
  both directions itself.
- **Dense-subset Green–Tao.** The headline statement in the literature (Green–Tao 2008,
  Annals 167, Thm 1.2; Conlon–Fox–Zhao exposition arXiv:1403.2957 §10) is infinitary:
  *every subset of the primes of positive relative upper density contains arbitrarily long
  APs.* The finitary form the writeup uses is the one the proof machinery produces and is
  stated and quantified explicitly by Rimanić–Wolf, *Szemerédi's theorem in the primes*
  (arXiv:1709.04719): with $\alpha_k(\mathcal P_N)$ the largest relative density of a
  $k$-AP-free subset of the primes up to $N$, they prove
  $\alpha_k(\mathcal P_N)\ll_k\alpha_k((\log\log N)^{c_k})\to0$, which is exactly "for every
  $\delta,k$ there is $N_0$ such that every $E\subseteq\mathbb P(N)$, $N\ge N_0$, with
  $|E|\ge\delta\pi(N)$ contains a $k$-AP". **Confirmed.** (Minor: the writeup calls this
  "the established dense-subset Green–Tao theorem" without a citation; the statement is
  correct, but a referee would ask for the pointer, since the *infinitary* statement alone
  does not obviously self-upgrade.)
- **Priority (step 2b) — the decisive finding.** Web search for the source paper's prime
  conjectures returned **arXiv:2609.07487**, *Asymptotically optimal packings of arithmetic
  progressions with prime differences*, by **Jianfeng Hou, Siyue Liu, Hongbin Zhao** (Center
  for Discrete Mathematics, Fuzhou University), **v1 posted 7 September 2026**, math.NT /
  math.CO. I downloaded the PDF and read it. Its **Theorem 1.2** is
  $M_{\mathcal P(n)}(n)=\left(\frac16+o(1)\right)\frac{n^3}{\ln n}$, and the introduction
  says explicitly: *"We prove both conjectures for prime differences [2, Conjectures 3 and
  4]"* (their Theorem 1.1 is Conjecture 3, their Theorem 1.2 is Conjecture 4). This attack
  ran on **2026-09-17**, i.e. **ten days after** that posting. The catalog's "open" status
  (auto-reviewed 2026-05-14) is stale.
  - Their proof is *different*: a cyclic phase-selection principle plus Banaszczyk's
    transference inequality for lattice covering radii, with almost all primes partitioned
    into random "regular ordered tuples" of fixed size. They do **not** use Green–Tao
    (grep for "Green"/"Szemerédi" in their text returns only the Green–Ruzsa Kakeya
    reference). The high-level architecture is nevertheless the same as the writeup's:
    fixed block size, $n\to\infty$ first, then block size $\to\infty$ so that the
    per-block boundary loss vanishes. So the writeup is an **independent rediscovery with a
    genuinely shorter proof**, not a reproduction.
  - **Self-contamination check (step 2c).** This is real, independent prior art, not our own
    artifact reflected back: three named authors with institutional affiliation, emails, an
    NSFC/National Key R&D grant number, a proper arXiv identifier and stamp
    (`arXiv:2609.07487v1 [math.NT] 7 Sep 2026`), a full 24-page paper with its own
    bibliography citing Alon et al. [2] and Mao–Wang–Wei–Yang [11]. Crucially it **predates**
    our writeup by ten days, so it cannot derive from this repository. I did not rely on any
    search-engine paraphrase: I read the paper's own PDF text.

## Computational check

Scripts in `verification_astra/scripts/2603.02786__01/` (run with `python3`; the repo
`.venv` lacks sympy).

1. **`check_lemma2.py` — exhaustive stress test of the packing lemma.** For $k\in\{3,4,5,6\}$
   I enumerated all $k$-term APs of primes with common difference divisible by
   $Q=\mathrm{lcm}(1,\dots,k-1)$ and all terms $\le400$, sampled 40 blocks per $k$, and for
   each ran $n\in\{d_{k-1},d_{k-1}+1,d_{k-1}+7,400,401,500\}$. For every instance the script
   (a) builds the $b_i$ by the lemma's recursion, (b) materializes all $kn$ points and tests
   **pairwise disjointness by brute force**, (c) checks the claimed decomposition
   $b_j-b_i=Ad_i+Bd_j$ for *every* pair $i<j$, (d) checks that each of the four displayed
   coefficients is an integer (i.e. $m\mid$ each numerator), (e) checks the Lemma 1
   hypotheses $A\ge n-d_j$ and $B\ge1$, (f) checks the exact length formula $\Lambda$ and the
   stated bound $\sum d_i(n-d_i)+n^2+2Qkn$.
   **Result: 540 instances, outcome counts `{'OK': 540}`, zero failures** — no collision, no
   non-integral coefficient, no violated hypothesis, no length overrun. The lemma's edge
   cases (blocks entirely above $n/2$, and $n=d_{k-1}$) are included; these are precisely the
   cases the paper's own construction and the prior attempt could not handle.
2. **`check_endtoend.py` — the whole construction.** For $(n,k)$ in
   $\{(60,3),(100,3),(200,3),(400,3),(1000,3),(2000,3),(5000,3),(200,4),(1000,4),(2000,4),(5000,4)\}$
   it greedily partitions **all** primes $\le n$ into $k$-APs with $Q\mid h$ (Lemma 3's
   greedy), packs each block by Lemma 2 and each leftover prime alone, lays the pieces out in
   consecutive intervals, and verifies **global pairwise disjointness of all $n\pi(n)$ points**.
   **Result: no collision in any of the 11 runs**, and the realized length is below the
   writeup's claimed bound $S(n)+(t_n+u_n)n^2+2Qkn\,t_n$ every time. Example ($n=5000$, $k=3$):
   669 primes, 218 blocks, 15 leftovers, realized length $4{,}602{,}672{,}957$ vs
   $S(n)=2{,}690{,}789{,}856$ and claimed bound $8{,}528{,}869{,}856$; ratio to $S(n)$ is
   $1.71$ and decreasing in $n$. (Small $k$ cannot exhibit the limit — the $1/k$ boundary
   overhead is real and only vanishes as $k\to\infty$ — but this confirms the construction is
   genuinely valid, which is the part a computer can settle.)
3. **`check_constants.py` — the asymptotics.** Sieve to $10^8$:
   $S(n)/(n^3/\ln n)=0.18253,\,0.17980,\,0.17763,\,0.17600,\,0.17476$ and
   $S(n)/(n^2\pi(n))=0.16125,\,0.16281,\,0.16379,\,0.16430,\,0.16467$ at

   constant $1/6$ in §6 is confirmed.

## Caveats

1. **Priority, not correctness, is what sinks this.** The theorem is Hou–Liu–Zhao,
   arXiv:2609.07487, Theorem 1.2 (7 Sep 2026); the attack ran 17 Sep 2026. The writeup's
   own caveat field ("novelty has not been checked against the full source paper") worried
   about the wrong thing — the danger was the *follow-up* literature, not the source paper.
2. **Citation precision.** The "dense-subset Green–Tao theorem" is stated without a
   reference and in a finitary form that the standard *headline* statement (relative upper
   density) does not literally give. The finitary form is correct and available
   (Rimanić–Wolf, arXiv:1709.04719; and it is what Green–Tao's transference argument
   produces), but a journal referee would require the pointer. Repairable in one line.
3. **No effective bounds.** Lemma 3 gives no rate for $|R_n|/\pi(n)\to0$, and $Q$ grows
   like $e^{(1+o(1))k}$, so the proof gives no explicit $n_0(\varepsilon)$. The writeup flags
   this correctly and the limit order makes it harmless. Hou–Liu–Zhao's route avoids
   Green–Tao and so is presumably effective; that is a real (if unstated) advantage of
   theirs.
4. **Unstated but harmless conventions.** (a) "Length" = number of integers in the interval;
   the paper's $[m]$ convention agrees, and the writeup's parenthetical about diameter vs
   length is correct and costs $O(1)$. (b) Primes $p=n$ (when $n$ is prime) contribute $0$ to
   $S(n)$ but span $\approx n^2$; this is absorbed by the $+n^2$ per block/singleton in S12
   and by the restriction to $p<n$ in S14 — both handled correctly. (c) Lemma 2 needs
   $k\ge2$ for $Q$ to be defined; only $k\ge3$ is used. (d) Small $n$, $k=0,1$, and empty
   families are vacuous in an asymptotic statement.
5. **The $S(n)$ form is a strengthening the conjecture did not ask for** and is where the
   proof is actually tight; the passage to $1/6$ is pure PNT and is the *least* interesting
   step, contrary to what the one-line verdict emphasizes.
6. **On the four claimed resolutions from this one paper.** The evidence says "genuinely
   tractable, and being actively resolved", not "one trick reapplied". The source paper is a
   March 2026 paper that closes with seven crisply stated asymptotic conjectures on an
   elementary-to-state packing problem, and independent groups have already settled several:
   Conjecture 7 by Mao–Wang–Wei–Yang (arXiv:2607.06113, July 2026) and Conjectures 3 and 4 by
   Hou–Liu–Zhao (arXiv:2609.07487, September 2026). The machinery the four attacks share is
   only the CRT overlap inequality $a_{i+1}-a_i>(n-1)p_i-p_ip_{i+1}$, which is the *source
   paper's own* Theorem 5 lower-bound argument; I re-derived it from scratch here (S14)
   rather than trusting sibling `__03`, and it is correct. Beyond that, the mechanisms
   differ completely (`__03`: weighted core multiplicity over $2p,3p,5p$; `__01`: Green–Tao
   AP-blocks). Note that sibling `__04` claims Conjecture 7, which is already
   arXiv:2607.06113 — it should be checked for the same priority problem.

## Referee summary

I tried hard to break this and could not: the interpretation matches the source paper
exactly (verified against its PDF and TeX source), every one of the sixteen steps
re-derived by hand checks out, and the load-bearing Lemma 2 — an *exact* simultaneous
packing of a block of prime differences in arithmetic progression, which is the claim most
likely to be exact only in a regime the asymptotic escapes — was verified by brute force on
540 instances (disjointness, coefficient integrality, Lemma 1 hypotheses, exact and bounded
length), and the whole construction end-to-end on all primes up to $n=5000$ with zero
collisions. The exact→asymptotic passage is the delicate point and it is handled correctly:
the per-block $n^2$ boundary overhead is *not* dropped, it is carried as $\pi(n)n^2/k$ and
killed by taking $n\to\infty$ at fixed block size $k$ and only then $k\to\infty$; and the
divisibility hypothesis $\mathrm{lcm}(1,\dots,k-1)\mid h$ is imposed by a pigeonhole inside
Lemma 3 rather than assumed, so it never fails. The only soft spot is bibliographic: the
finitary dense-subset form of Green–Tao is used without a citation, though it is genuine
(Green–Tao 2008 as quantified by Rimanić–Wolf, arXiv:1709.04719). The proof is, as far as I
can tell, correct and notably shorter than the published one. But it is not new: Conjecture
4 of arXiv:2603.02786 was proved as Theorem 1.2 of **Hou, Liu and Zhao,
arXiv:2609.07487 (7 September 2026)**, ten days before this attack ran, by an independent
lattice-covering/phase-selection route — a real paper with named authors, affiliation,
grant, and arXiv stamp, predating and therefore uncontaminated by this campaign. Verdict:
**ALREADY_KNOWN**.
