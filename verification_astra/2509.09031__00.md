## Interpretation

The target is Conjecture 1.2 of Nguyen–Scott–Seymour, *Asymptotic structure. II* (arXiv:2509.09031).
I fetched the paper and the statement in `prompt.md` is verbatim:

> "Let 𝒞 be a class of connected graphs, closed under contracting edges and subdividing edges.
> For all L,C there exists C′ such that if there is an (L,C)-quasi-isometry from a graph G to a
> member of 𝒞, then there is a (1,C′)-quasi-isometry from G to a member of 𝒞."

The same statement is Conjecture 8 of Davies–Hatzel–Hickingbotham (arXiv:2503.07448), where it is
explicitly posed as the surviving weakening after they refuted the fixed-target version.

Three interpretation points, all of which come out in the writeup's favour:

1. **Coarse surjectivity.** The writeup's caveat block flags that it "uses the standard coarsely
   onto definition of quasi-isometry". This is load-bearing and it is the right choice. NSS's own
   definition (Section 1 of 2509.09031) has three clauses, the third being "for every y ∈ V(H)
   there exists v ∈ V(G) such that dist_H(φ(v),y) ≤ C". So the intended reading is the one used.
   This matters enormously: **without** coarse surjectivity the counterexample dies outright, because
   one can attach, to the graph G_g itself, a thread of length 10^9 between every pair of its branch
   vertices that does not already have one; the result is a member of 𝒞 into which G_g embeds
   isometrically, i.e. by a (1,0)-quasi-isometric embedding. The writeup does not spell this out but
   it does flag the dependence.
2. **Quantifier form.** The writeup fixes (L,C) = (2,1) and produces, for each g, a graph G_g that is
   (2,1)-quasi-isometric to a member of 𝒞 but admits no (1,A)-quasi-isometry to any member of 𝒞 with
   A+1 ≤ g/10000. That is exactly the negation of Conjecture 1.2. The QI convention difference
   (NSS use `d_G ≤ L·d_H + C` where the writeup uses `L^{-1}d_G − C ≤ d_H`) is immaterial: for L = 1
   both say |d_G − d_H| ≤ C, and my computation verified the positive direction in the *stronger*
   form d_{H+} ≤ d_G and d_G ≤ 2 d_{H+} + 1, which satisfies both conventions at (2,1).
3. **Is 𝒞 a strawman class?** 𝒞 = {J : every two distinct vertices of degree ≥ 3 are joined by a path
   whose internal vertices have degree 2}. It is contrived but it is a legitimate instance: the
   conjecture is universally quantified over classes and imposes only the two closure properties.
   It is *not* closed under edge deletion, disjoint union, or minors, and the writeup says so. NSS's
   motivating remarks give no indication that they intended extra hypotheses, and DHH single out the
   planar case only as "of particular interest". I would call this a fair refutation of the
   conjecture as stated, though an author could reasonably respond by re-asking it for classes that
   are also deletion-closed (the writeup itself says this version is untouched).

**What could not be checked by computation.** The actual counterexample is not small: g ≥ 10 000,
and n = |Q_g| is the order of a subgroup of a product of symmetric groups indexed by all ~4·3^{g−2}

directly, and Lemma 3.1 (a statement about arbitrary continuous maps between metric graphs) admits
no brute-force analogue. Lemma 3.1 was checked by hand only, line by line; it is the single place
where a subtle error would still be possible.

## Caveats

1. **Coarse surjectivity is essential** and the counterexample is void without it. The writeup flags
   this but does not say why it is essential. NSS's own definition does require it, so the writeup is
   safe — but a reader using a "quasi-isometric embedding" convention would wrongly reject the proof.
2. **Missing attribution.** The turn-cost mechanism of §6.1–§6.4 is close in spirit to
   Davies–Hatzel–Hickingbotham's Theorem 4 (arXiv:2503.07448), which is exactly the fixed-target
   version of this obstruction, and the earlier attempt reproduced in `prompt.md` rediscovered that
   published theorem without noticing. The new writeup inherits the mechanism from that attempt and
   likewise cites nothing. The mechanisms are not identical (DHH orient a high-chromatic high-girth
   graph and use Burr's theorem; here one 2-colours a 4-regular high-girth graph and averages over
   monochromatic cycles), but in a submitted paper §6 would have to cite DHH. This is a
   presentational defect, not a correctness one; the genuinely new content — Lemma 3.1 and the class
   𝒞 — is not in the literature I could find.
3. **Unstated hypothesis checks.** n ≥ 4 in Lemma 3.1 is never verified in §6 (it holds); 𝒞 is
   defined without the word "connected" although the conjecture asks for a class of connected graphs
   (contraction and subdivision preserve connectivity, so restricting 𝒞 to connected graphs is
   harmless and should be stated); D ≥ 1 is implicitly used in Step 1 (10D+1 ≤ 12D).
4. **Scope.** The writeup itself notes that the variant of Conjecture 1.2 with the extra requirement
   of closure under edge deletion is untouched, as is the planar special case that DHH call out as
   the case of interest. 𝒞 is a fairly artificial class (it is not closed under deletion, disjoint
   union, or minors, and it does not even contain all trees — a path of three consecutive branch
   vertices is not in 𝒞). So the conjecture is refuted as stated, but the interesting instances are
   untouched. A referee of the source paper would likely accept the refutation and immediately
   re-ask the question for deletion-closed or minor-closed classes.
5. **Edge cases.** The argument never needs A ≥ 1 or g small; the hypothesis g ≥ 10 000 is used only
   through block (6.5), which I verified first holds at g = 80, so the constants are far from tight
   but are sound.

## Referee summary

I attacked this writeup expecting it to fail in one of the two ways the retry brief predicted —
either the class 𝒞 is not actually closed under the operations the conjecture requires, or the
prose under-determines the object — and neither happened. 𝒞 is well-defined and its closure under
contraction and subdivision (Lemma 1.1) survived exhaustive testing on every connected graph with at
most 7 vertices and 20 000 random larger members, with zero failures; the object is fully determined
(H_g by the Cayley construction of §4, S = g, M = 100g², G_g by vertex-splitting, H_g^+ as a
subdivision of K_n), and the positive half — p: G_g → H_g^+ ∈ 𝒞 is an onto (2,1)-quasi-isometry —
is exactly right and was verified exhaustively on three explicit instances. Every distance identity
Section 6 relies on reproduced numerically, the alternating-walk stationarity is exact, the
cyclic-window counting is exact, all nine inequalities of block (6.5) hold with large margins, and
when I recast the quantitative core as a linear program the optimum came out at exactly (s−3)/2,
matching the writeup's final inequality s−3 ≤ 2E to the digit; the same LP on an acyclic model
returns 0, which confirms the monochromatic-cycle averaging is doing real work rather than
decorating. The genuinely new step, the long-corridor rigidity Lemma 3.1 that converts an arbitrary
additive quasi-isometry to an arbitrary member of 𝒞 into edge lengths on a prescribed K_n skeleton,
is where the whole argument lives and is the one part no computation can touch; I checked it clause
by clause and every constant (12D, 14D, 20D, 34D, 50D, 63D, 25D, 30D) is correct under S ≥ 1000D,
but it is intricate enough that I record medium rather than high confidence. The defects I did find
are all repairable in a sentence each — the case analysis behind "choosing a simple path in the
image" in Lemma 1.1, the observation that the bad cycle in Lemma 2.2 is a whole component, the
unstated derivations of the three distortion constants in Lemma 2.1, and the unchecked n ≥ 4
hypothesis — so MINOR_GAPS, not MAJOR_GAP. Two things a journal would insist on: the writeup must
cite Davies–Hatzel–Hickingbotham (arXiv:2503.07448) for the fixed-target version of the turn-cost
obstruction, which it inherited from the previous attempt (that attempt had rediscovered their
Theorem 4 without noticing), and it must state prominently that the coarsely-onto definition of
quasi-isometry is not a convenience but a necessity — attach a length-10⁹ thread between every pair
of G_g's branch vertices that lacks one and G_g embeds isometrically into a member of 𝒞, so the
counterexample evaporates under the quasi-isometric-embedding convention. NSS's own definition does
require coarse surjectivity, so the writeup is on safe ground. As for why the authors missed this: the
object is not small — n = |Q_g| for g ≥ 10 000 is astronomically large — the class 𝒞 is artificial
enough that nobody would test the conjecture against it, and the rigidity lemma is the real work.
The conjecture is refuted as literally and correctly stated; the versions people actually care about
(deletion-closed classes, planar graphs) remain open, and the writeup says so.
