## Interpretation

The problem is Question 6.4 of Christoph–Janzer–Petrova–Steiner, *Extending Thomassen's
conjecture to directed graphs* (arXiv:2510.11311): "Is every orientation of `C_4`
Eulerian-avoidable?". I fetched the source paper's HTML and confirmed both the question
and the definition verbatim (Section 6, just before Question 6.4):

> "We say that a digraph `F` is *Eulerian-avoidable* if there exists `d_F : N -> N` such
> that every Eulerian digraph with minimum out-degree `d` contains an `F`-free subdigraph
> with minimum out-degree `k`."

Three interpretation points, all of which the writeup gets right.

* **Which orientation.** The writeup attacks `F = K⃗_{2,2}` (two sources, two sinks:
  `u->x, u->y, v->x, v->y`). That is indeed the anti-directed orientation of `C_4`, and it
  is the one the catalog page names. It is *not* the orientation `C_4^{(2,2)}`
  (`a->b->c`, `a->d->c`), which is a different isomorphism type; the writeup does not
  conflate them.
* **The subdigraph is not required to be spanning or Eulerian.** The source definition
  asks only for an `F`-free *subdigraph* of minimum out-degree `>= k`. The writeup proves
  the strongest form: *no nonempty subdigraph at all* (vertex deletion allowed) has
  `delta^+ >= 2` and is `F`-free. This is the correct, non-strawman reading, and it is
  exactly the point on which the previous `gpt-5.6-sol` attempt stalled — that attempt's
  Section 8 correctly observed that its own blow-up candidate `D_{a,b}` *does* admit a
  small `F`-free 2-out core (a projective-plane/affine-line core), so the previous attempt
  is not reproduced here; the new writeup discards it and builds a host in which no such
  core exists. That is a genuine advance over the lead.
* **Answering "no" to a "for all" question.** The question asks whether *every*
  orientation is Eulerian-avoidable; exhibiting one that is not answers it negatively. The
  writeup's caveat ("this does not classify the other orientations of `C_4`") is honest
  about the remaining scope.

A reasonable author of Question 6.4 would consider the question resolved (negatively) by
this argument. Interpretation is fair.

### Priority check (step 2b) — the result is already published

`C_4`*, by **Hui Lei, Xiaoyi Wang, Zhijun Xu, Zhenyu Yang** (Nankai University),
**submitted 13 September 2026**, math.CO. The attack ran on **2026-09-17T01:09:10Z** — four

Its **Theorem 1.3** is the writeup's theorem plus the writeup's corollary, in one statement:

> "A one-directed complete bipartite graph is not Eulerian-avoidable. More precisely, if
> `s,t >= 2`, then for every integer `r >= t` there is an Eulerian oriented graph with
> minimum out-degree exactly `r` such that every subdigraph of minimum out-degree at least
> `t` contains `K⃗_{s,t}`."

This is not a partial overlap: it is the same proof, including the two-part
"bottleneck bounds the last layer / girth forces strict decrease backwards" architecture,
which the paper even announces in its introduction ("Theorem 1.3 uses a cyclic sequence of
large-girth bipartite graphs and a final complete bipartite interface"). The only
difference is the proof of the auxiliary existence statement: their Lemma 2.5 uses a
`(Z/2Z)^{E(B)}` graph lift, the writeup uses involutions on reduced words; both are
standard, and the statement itself is classical.

The paper covers **all** of the writeup: its Theorem 1.3 with `s=t=2` is the writeup's

also handling `min(s,t) = 1`. It also does what the writeup explicitly does not: its

of `C_4` is Eulerian-avoidable if and only if it is not isomorphic to `K⃗_{2,2}`",

settled in the literature, and the writeup recovers only the negative half.

## Computational check

Scripts are in `verification_astra/scripts/2510.11311__04/`. Python 3 with
networkx 3.6.1 and scipy 1.11.3 (the repo `.venv` lacks networkx; I used the system
`python3`).

**`check_construction.py`** — builds `D_d` exactly as specified and decides *exactly*
whether a nonempty `K⃗_{2,2}`-free subdigraph with `delta^+ >= 2` exists. Two independent
decision procedures: (A) enumerate all `2^{\|V\|}` vertex subsets and test for a system of
distinct representatives (each surviving vertex needs a 2-subset of its retained
out-neighbourhood, all distinct — an equivalence I re-derived and confirm is correct);
(B) a MILP feasibility model over vertex and arc indicators with an explicit
`x_{ua}+x_{ub}+x_{va}+x_{vb} <= 3` constraint for every pair of vertices with two common
out-neighbours (this encodes `F`-freeness directly, without using the SDR reduction).

Results (A and B agree wherever both were run):

| instance | structure | `F`-free 2-out core exists? |
|---|---|---|
| `D_2`, `Q = C_8` (girth 8), `L = M+1 = 2` | 10 v, 24 a, 0 digons, Eulerian, strongly connected, `delta^+=2`, `Delta^+=4` | **no** (MILP infeasible, 12 `F`-free constraints; exhaustive over `2^10` subsets agrees) |
| `D_2`, `Q = C_10` | 12 v, 30 a, Eulerian, s.c., `delta^+=2` | **no** (both methods; exhaustive over `2^12`) |

## Caveats

1. **Priority.** The decisive one: the theorem and its construction were published four
   days earlier (arXiv:2609.14368, 13 Sep 2026). The writeup is a correct independent
   rediscovery, not a new result.
2. **Scope versus the question asked.** Question 6.4 asks about *all four* orientations.
   The writeup settles only `K⃗_{2,2}`; it does not show the other three are
   Eulerian-avoidable, so it answers "no" to the quantified question but does not produce
   the classification (which Lei et al. do). The `would_publish: true` flag in the verdict
   block should be read against that.
3. **Corollary is terser than the theorem.** In Section 5 the writeup redefines `M` but
   does not restate that `Q` must now have girth `> 2M` for the new, larger `M`, and it
   does not treat `s = 1` or `t = 1` (both trivial: `K⃗_{1,t}` sits in any digraph of
   `delta^+ >= t`, and `K⃗_{s,1}` follows from the degree-sum argument). Routine.
4. **Size.** The host is astronomically large — `n = |W|!` with
   `|W| = 1 + d·sum_{r<d(d-1)} (d-1)^r`. The writeup says so ("deliberately
   unoptimized"). Lei et al.'s lift construction is similarly non-explicit in size. No
   mathematical consequence.
5. **Convention dependence.** The theorem quantifies over *nonempty* subdigraphs; the
   empty digraph vacuously has infinite minimum out-degree under some conventions. The
   writeup states nonemptiness explicitly and the source paper clearly intends it, so this
   is not a loophole. The proof also does not need `H` spanning, induced, or Eulerian, and
   it holds under the stricter convention that Eulerian digraphs be connected (the host is
   strongly connected).
6. **No inherited error.** The previous `gpt-5.6-sol` attempt is used only as a lead for
   the pair-counting bound (3.1), which I checked from scratch; its blow-up family
   `D_{a,b}`, which it itself refuted as a counterexample, is correctly discarded.
