# Referee report: 2603.02786__04 (retry leg)

**Target claim.** Conjecture 7 of arXiv:2603.02786 (Alon, Dębski, Grytczuk, Przybyło,
*Packing arithmetic progressions*): for every fixed $n$, $M_k(n)=(1+o(1))nk$ as
$k\to\infty$. The writeup claims a full proof, via (i) an exact fractional tiling of
$[nK]$ built from a random composition of $K$, and (ii) a rounding step using the
Pippenger–Spencer edge-colouring theorem plus a reservoir and a greedy completion.

**Headline finding.** The mathematics holds up under a line-by-line check and under
independent computation. But the result is **not new**: it is Theorem 1.2 of
arXiv:2607.06113, posted 7 July 2026, i.e. **two months before this attack ran**
(2026-09-17). Worse for novelty, the *only* genuinely new ingredient of the writeup —
the fractional packing of Lemma 1 — is the same mechanism as that paper's Lemma 2.2
(a random partition drawn from the cycle structure of a uniform random permutation,
with $\mathbb E N_d\propto 1/d$), and the identity behind it is the classical fact
that a uniform random permutation of $[K]$ has $1/d$ cycles of length $d$ in
expectation. The rounding half is the source paper's own machinery (its Theorem 3
already packs almost everything by a near-perfect hypergraph matching and mops up the
rest greedily).

## Interpretation

- **Definition.** The writeup takes $A(d,a)=\{a,a+d,\dots,a+(n-1)d\}$ and $M_k(n)$ =
  least $m$ with pairwise disjoint $A(d,a_d)\subseteq[m]$ for all $d\in[k]$. I
  confirmed against the source paper (case (2): $B_d=D_d\cap\{1,\dots,nd\}=
  \{d,2d,\dots,nd\}$, integer shifts, minimum interval length) and against the
  independent solution paper, which states the same definition verbatim. **Match.**
- **Conjecture 7, verbatim from the source paper:** "For every fixed $n$ and $k$
  tending to infinity, we have $M_k(n)=(1+o(1))nk$." The writeup proves
  $M_k(n)=nk+o(k)$ for fixed $n$, which is the same statement (since $n$ is fixed),
  with the lower bound $M_k(n)\ge nk$ supplied by counting. No strawman, no literal
  reading trick, no silent weakening: the quantifier order ("fix $\eta$, then
  $k\to\infty$, then $\eta\downarrow0$") is exactly what the conjecture asks for.
- **What is *not* claimed** (correctly flagged in the writeup): no uniformity in $n$,
  no rate. Fine — the conjecture is stated for fixed $n$.
- **Retry discipline.** The prompt contained a prior `gpt-5.6-sol` attempt that got
  only $\limsup M_k(n)/(nk)\to 4/e$ and explicitly identified its own missing piece
  ("no construction of the required non-uniform fractional weights is supplied"). The
  new writeup supplies exactly that piece and does *not* inherit its errors; it also
  repairs a genuine hand-wave in the old text (the old Proposition 1 asserted the
  max-degree form of Pippenger–Spencer "by embedding $H$ in an almost $\Delta$-regular
  $r$-graph" with no construction; the new §2 gives an explicit regularization). I
  re-verified the inherited reservoir/greedy skeleton myself rather than crediting it.

## Reference check

- **arXiv:2603.02786** (Alon, Dębski, Grytczuk, Przybyło, 3 Mar 2026). Conjecture 7
  confirmed verbatim: "For every fixed $n$ and $k$ tending to infinity, we have
  $M_k(n)=(1+o(1))nk$." Definition of $M_k(n)$ in case (2) matches the writeup.
  Theorem 3 confirmed: "For every $n\in\mathbb N$ there exists $k_0=k_0(n)$ such that,
  for all $k\ge k_0$, we have $M_k(n)\le 3nk$." Its proof already uses a near-perfect
  matching in a nearly-regular uniform hypergraph with small codegrees, plus a greedy
  packing lemma for the leftovers — i.e. **the writeup's §2–§4 rounding scaffold is
  the source paper's own method**, not new.
- **Pippenger–Spencer** (*Asymptotic behavior of the chromatic index for hypergraphs*,
  JCTA 51 (1989) 24–42). Confirmed: the theorem is stated for $r$-uniform hypergraphs
  with min degree $\sim$ max degree and codegree $o(D)$, concluding $\chi'=(1+o(1))D$.
  The almost-regularity hypothesis is real, and the writeup is right to supply a
  regularization rather than cite the max-degree form directly. Correctly invoked.
- **PRIORITY — arXiv:2607.06113**, Yaping Mao, Zhao Wang, Meiqin Wei, Gang Yang,
  *Solution to a conjecture of Alon, Dębski, Grytczuk and Przybyło on
  fixed-cardinality arithmetic progressions*, math.CO, submitted **7 July 2026**,
  supported by NSFC grants 12471329, 12401461, 12061059. Its **Theorem 1.2**: "For
  every fixed positive integer $n$ and every $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, there exists
  $k_0=k_0(n,\varepsilon)$ such that $M_k(n)\le n\lceil(1+\varepsilon)k\rceil$ for
  every $k\ge k_0$. Consequently, $M_k(n)=(1+o(1))nk$ as $k\to\infty$." That is
  precisely the writeup's theorem, with the same $M_k(n)$, and it is *stronger* in
  form (the writeup pays an extra $(n^2+2n-1)\eta k$ for the small differences it
  exiles to a second interval; 2607.06113 needs no such second interval).
  Its **Lemma 2.2**: "For every pair of integers $L\ge k\ge1$, there exists a random
  partition $\Pi_{L,k}$ of $L$ into parts of size at most $k$ such that, if $N_d$
  denotes the number of parts of size $d$, then $\mathbb E[N_d]=L/(kd)$ for every
  $d\in[k]$", constructed from "a uniformly random permutation of $[L]$, conditioned
  on not being a single $L$-cycle", keeping each cycle of length $\le k$ as a part.
  This is the same $1/d$ mechanism as the writeup's Lemma 1 (whose stick-breaking
  composition *is* the permutation cycle-discovery process). The rounding differs:
  2607.06113 rounds with the Delcourt–Postle full-rainbow-matching theorem and needs
  no reservoir; the writeup rounds with Pippenger–Spencer plus a reservoir.
- **arXiv:2609.07487** (Sept 2026), *Asymptotically optimal packings of arithmetic
  progressions with prime differences*, settles two further conjectures of the same
  paper. The source paper's conjectures are being cleared out quickly by the
  community; note for the batch-level question below.

## Caveats

- **Novelty, not correctness, is the problem.** Everything below is secondary to the
  fact that the theorem is already in the literature.
- The concentration step (14)–(16) is compressed to "the bounded-differences
  inequality, with deviation $k^{2/3}$" plus a sentence on Lipschitz constants. I
  re-derived the variable counts and Lipschitz constants and they work, but as written
  a referee would ask for the two lines I wrote above. This is the only place in the
  writeup where a reader must supply arithmetic.
- The writeup never states that Lemma 1's identity $\mathbb E C_d=1/d$ is the classical
  permutation-cycle fact, presenting it as a fresh observation ("the cancellation
  behind the construction is worth emphasizing"). Not an error, but it overstates the
  originality of the one ingredient the argument turns on.
- The bound obtained is strictly weaker in shape than the published one: $nk+
  (n^2+2n-1)\eta k+n$ versus $n\lceil(1+\varepsilon)k\rceil$. The $\Theta(n^2\eta k)$
  overhead comes from exiling the differences $\le\eta k$ to a separate interval,
  which the published proof avoids entirely. Also, $k_0(n,\eta)$ is not extracted, and
  nothing is uniform in $n$ — both acknowledged.
- Edge cases are clean: $n=1$ is disposed of separately; $\eta<1/2$ guarantees
  $K\le2k$, which Lemma 1(4) needs; $q=\lfloor\eta k\rfloor$ can be $0$ for small $k$,
  harmlessly. No convention mismatch on interval endpoints or on the number of terms
  ($n$ terms, $n-1$ gaps) anywhere.
- The verdict block's `would_publish: true` is the one claim I would reject outright:
  the result is four months stale relative to arXiv:2607.06113.

## Referee summary

I set out to break this at the rounding step, as instructed, and could not. The
fractional solution is genuinely *constructed*, not assumed from an LP whose
feasibility is asserted: Lemma 1 exhibits an explicit random exact tiling of $[nK]$
whose weights I verified to the last rational for $n\le5$, $K\le10$ — label sums
exactly $1$, point loads exactly $1$, spread $\le1/d$, and a *constant* slack
$\frac{K-k}{2K}$ at every point once the top differences are discarded. The rounding
preserves the property being tiled for free (each hyperedge carries exactly one label
vertex, so a matching is literally a packing with distinct differences), and the loss
is $o(k)$ leftover labels against a constant-fraction reservoir, i.e. genuinely lower
order than the $\delta k$ slack being spent; I re-derived the McDiarmid budget
($\sum c_i^2=O(k)$, deviation $k^{2/3}$, $D=\Theta(k)$) rather than take the writeup's
word for it, and I implemented §2's Pippenger–Spencer regularization and confirmed it
really does produce an exactly $\Delta$-regular simple hypergraph with codegree
$\le\max\{\Delta_2,1\}$ — a step the earlier `gpt-5.6-sol` attempt had waved through.
So on correctness this is the strongest writeup I have refereed in this batch. It is
nonetheless **ALREADY_KNOWN**: Conjecture 7 was proved by Mao, Wang, Wei and Yang,
arXiv:2607.06113 (7 July 2026), Theorem 1.2, in a stronger form
($M_k(n)\le n\lceil(1+\varepsilon)k\rceil$, no second interval), two months before
this attack ran — and their Lemma 2.2 is the *same* $\mathbb E N_d\propto1/d$
random-permutation-cycle fractional packing, differing only in rounding
(Delcourt–Postle rainbow matchings instead of Pippenger–Spencer plus a reservoir).
That source is independent prior art by every test in step 2c and does not trace back
to this repository. On the batch-level question: this is not one trick reapplied — the
four `2603.02786` attacks use quite different machinery (CRT-generic prime blocks for
`__00`, relative-density Green–Tao for `__01`, weighted-core counting for the
confirmed `__03`, random compositions here) — it is that a five-month-old 18KB note
by Alon et al. left behind a batch of accessible conjectures, several of which the
human literature has also cleared in the same window (arXiv:2607.06113 in July,
arXiv:2609.07487 in September settling two more). The right reading is "genuinely
easy", with the corollary that the catalog's `status: open` is the weakest link in
this pipeline, not the model's reasoning.
