## Interpretation

The writeup proves exactly Conjecture 5 of Bonamy–Knor–Lužar–Pinlou–Škrekovski
(arXiv:1602.05184, Appl. Math. Comput. 312 (2017) 202–213), with no weakening or
literal-reading gamesmanship. I verified against the ar5iv full text of the source
paper:

* Source definition: "*let K_n^t be the graph obtained from K_{n−1} by adding one new
  vertex adjacent to t of the n−1 old vertices*" — identical to the writeup's §1.
* Source Conjecture 5: "*Let G be a 2-connected graph of order n ≥ 10 not isomorphic to
  K_n, K_n^2, or K_n^{n−2}. Then η(G) ≥ 2n*" — identical to what is proved.
* η(G) = Sz(G) − W(G) with the standard Sz(G) = Σ_{uv∈E} n_uv(u)·n_uv(v) — identical.

The writeup in fact proves something strictly stronger and for all n ≥ 4:
η(G) ≥ min{2n, 3n−10}, i.e. η ≥ 3n−10 for n ≤ 10 and η ≥ 2n for n ≥ 10. My exhaustive
computation shows this stronger form is *exactly* tight at every order 5 ≤ n ≤ 11
(minima 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 22), so the formulation is not accidentally weak, and the
n = 10 transition in the conjecture is genuine. A reasonable author of the original
problem would consider the conjecture resolved (modulo the two writing gaps below).

This is an `attacks_retry` target. The previous attempt (`gpt-5.6-sol`, verdict
`partial`) proved the universal-vertex case only. The new writeup reproduces that part
(as its §5, essentially verbatim) and adds the genuinely new content: the
dominated-vertex reduction of §§2–4. I checked the reproduced §5 independently rather
than crediting it; it is correct (one exposition improvement over the original: the
distinctness of the counted induced subgraphs is now justified by "unique vertex of
maximum layer"). No error flagged in the earlier attempt is reproduced — the earlier
attempt's own honest statement of what remained open ("no step above proves that a graph
with η(G) < 2n must belong to either treated class") is precisely what §§2–4 now supply.

## Reference check

The writeup is deliberately self-contained and cites nothing, so there is no miscitation
to flag. I nevertheless checked everything it implicitly leans on, via the ar5iv full
text of arXiv:1602.05184:

* **Conjecture 5** and the definition of K_n^t: quoted above, identical to the writeup's
  reading. ✔
* **Proposition 6** of the source paper is exactly the writeup's identity (2)
  (η = Σ_{pairs}(g − d)); the source attributes the "good edge" notion to Simić et al.
  [15] (Math. Slovaca 50 (2000) 1–15). The writeup should cite this rather than derive
  it anonymously. This is an attribution gap, not a correctness problem. ✔/flag
* **Theorem 2** (η ≥ 2n−6 for 2-connected non-complete) and **Theorem 4** (η ≥ 2n−5 after
  excluding K_n^2, K_n^{n−2}) of the source paper are both implied by, and consistent
  with, the writeup's min{2n, 3n−10} (e.g. n = 5: 5 ≥ 5; n = 6: 8 ≥ 7). ✔
* **Lemma 7** of the source paper ("if G−u is 2-connected, not complete, and N[u] ⊆ N[v],
  then η(G) − η(G−u) ≥ 2") is the weaker ancestor of the writeup's Lemma 2.1(3); the
  writeup's exact version (increment = c + q) is stronger and is what makes the ≥ 4
  increment possible. Not cited, again an attribution matter. ✔/flag

**Priority check (step 2b/2c).** A dedicated literature sweep (OpenAlex citing set of the
source paper — 13 works, Semantic Scholar — 15, arXiv API for all "Szeged" abstracts since
2024, zbMATH, and the Knor–Škrekovski–Tepeh survey arXiv:2303.11405, whose full text I had
grepped) found **no published proof or disproof of Conjecture 5**. It remains open in the
refereed and arXiv literature as of 2026-09-17. One item deserves explicit mention:

* **Zhijie He, "The Strengthened Szeged–Wiener Conjecture"**, a manuscript posted
  2026-09-08 (nine days before this attack ran) at
  `github.com/FDmd233/strengthened-szeged-wiener-conjecture` and submitted as a solution
  to MathDB problem 332514. Its Theorem 1 is verbatim Conjecture 5, and its method
  (minimum counterexample + dominated-vertex induction, the K_n^t formula, exceptional
  2-connected deletions, plus a computer certificate at order 10) overlaps the writeup's
  strategy. **However**: it has no arXiv identifier, no DOI, no venue, is indexed nowhere,
  and MathDB's own raw status line still reads *"Open. A reader-submitted proof claims to
  settle the strengthened inequality, but it is incomplete and unverified, so the problem
  remains open."* It is not this campaign's own artifact (different author, different
  repository, predates the attack), so it is not self-contamination — but by the standard
  of step 2c (an independent paper with authors, a date **and a venue or arXiv id**) it
  does not qualify as prior art. I therefore do **not** mark ALREADY_KNOWN; I flag it as a
  live priority risk that a human should check before any submission, and note that if
  that manuscript is correct, the writeup's contribution is independent rediscovery, not
  priority.
* No published source contains the diameter-≤2 identity of Lemma 5.1 in the p₃/p₄/c₄ form
  (the nearest published diameter-2 Szeged formulas, Ilić arXiv:1103.2513 Prop. 3.1/3.3,
  are Zagreb-index expressions under an extra "every edge in exactly t triangles"
  hypothesis — a different statement). So Lemma 5.1 appears to be new, and true.

**F. A caution about a contrary claim.** My literature sub-search reported, from random
sampling, that the true minimum for n ≥ 10 is 4n−16 (attained by K_n³) and that 2n is
therefore not tight. That is **wrong**, and my exhaustive data refutes it: at n = 11 the
minimum over all 900,969,091 2-connected graphs is 22 = 2n, whereas 4n−16 = 28. Random
sampling simply never hits the sparse extremal family (a clique K_{n−2} plus an edge xy
joined to it by a 2-matching), which attains exactly 2n for every n ≥ 5. The writeup's
sharpness section is correct and the conjectured constant 2n is best possible.

## Caveats

1. **Two one-line justification gaps** (steps 15 and 20 above): the L₂ = {z} argument in
   Lemma 3.1 Case 2 does not treat z's own predecessor class, and "a tree with ≥ 3
   vertices has a leaf outside {p,q}" is false in general and needs the observation that
   the spanning tree was chosen to contain the edge pq. Both are repaired in one sentence
   each; I verified both repairs.
2. **Missing attribution.** Identity (2) is Proposition 6 of the very paper that poses the
   conjecture, and the "good edge" concept is due to Simić–Gutman–Baltić; Lemma 2.1 is a
   sharpened form of the source paper's Lemma 7. A submitted version must cite these. The
   writeup's closing claim "no computational enumeration or unproved external assertion is
   used" is literally true and is a real strength — the proof is fully self-contained —
   but it should not be read as a claim that the framework is original.
3. **Priority risk.** An unrefereed manuscript claiming exactly this theorem by a similar
   route was posted publicly nine days before this attack (Zhijie He, 2026-09-08, GitHub +
   MathDB 332514). Not indexed, no venue, and marked "incomplete and unverified" by the
   host, so I do not treat it as prior art — but it must be checked by a human before any
   submission.
4. **Scope statements.** The theorem is proved for n ≥ 4; the conjecture's hypothesis
   n ≥ 10 is only used at the very end (3n−10 ≥ 2n). The three exceptional graphs are
   excluded exactly as in the source. Edge cases are handled: n = 4 base case, C₅ as the
   one sporadic obstruction in Lemma 4.1/Prop 4.3, m = 4 folded into Case 3 of §6, and
   t ≥ 2 in Case 4 needing m ≥ 5 (which is exactly what Case 3 leaves open). The empty and
   disconnected cases do not arise since G is 2-connected throughout.
5. **What is *not* claimed.** The writeup does not characterise the extremal graphs for
   η = 2n (my searches suggest the clique K_{n−2} + 2-matching family is the only one, but

6. **Residual verification limits.** The lemma-level machine check is exhaustive only to
   n = 9 (plus an 8.4% sample at n = 10); the theorem is exhaustive to n = 11; beyond that
   the evidence is a very large but not exhaustive search. The correctness argument at
   large n rests on the hand audit above, which found no error.
