## Interpretation

The prompt asks for Conjecture 1.2 of Alon–Defant (arXiv:1904.02595): if `H_1,...,H_n` are
complete multipartite graphs and `G = prod H_i` (direct/tensor product), then `alpha(G) = IR(G)`.
The writeup proves exactly this statement, for arbitrary (unbalanced) complete multipartite
factors and arbitrary `n >= 1`.

Definitions were checked against the source, not taken on trust:

* The source defines `S` irredundant by `N[S \ {v}] != N[S]` for all `v in S`, and `IR(G)` as the
  maximum size of an irredundant set. The writeup uses "for each `v in S` there is `p` with
  `N[p] cap S = {v}`". These are equivalent: `N[S\{v}] != N[S]` iff some `p in N[v]` lies outside
  `N[S\{v}]`, i.e. `N[p] cap S = {v}`. No silent weakening.
* The writeup's reduction to "external" private neighbours is legitimate: if `p in S` then
  `p in N[p] cap S = {v}` forces `p = v`, which is the lonely case.
* Adjacency convention for the direct product (adjacent in **every** coordinate) matches the source.

No interpretation gaming: the theorem proved is the intended conjecture, with no extra hypotheses,
no restriction to balanced factors, no "as literally stated" dodge. A reasonable author of the
problem would regard Conjecture 1.2 as resolved by this argument (and, as a by-product, Burcroff's
Conjecture 1.1 including the 37 cases the source paper leaves open, and the Defant–Iyer conjecture).

This is a retry target. The prompt contained an earlier `gpt-5.6-sol` attempt (verdict `partial`)
but **no referee report on it**. The new writeup reuses none of the earlier attempt's load-bearing
machinery: it discards the blocker inequality (Lemma 3.2/7.1 there) and the low-dimensional
classifications, and reproves the bipartite lemma from scratch. The earlier attempt's stated
obstruction (its section 8: in `K_3^4`, `A = {(0,0,0,0),(1,0,0,0)}` has `|A| + |B(A)| = 59 > 54 = 2*alpha`)
is simply irrelevant to the new route; I checked that family explicitly — it has `rho(A) = 2`, so
Lemma 4 demands only `27 - 2 >= 16 - 4`, which holds with room to spare. So the new writeup does
not reproduce the earlier stall, and inherits nothing unchecked.

## Reference check

* **arXiv:1904.02595 (Alon–Defant), fetched via ar5iv.** Confirmed verbatim: irredundant is
  `N[S\{v}] != N[S]`; `IR(G)` is the maximum size of an irredundant set; **Conjecture 1.1**
  (Burcroff, balanced factors `K[u_i,t_i]`) and **Conjecture 1.2** ("If `H_1,...,H_n` are complete
  multipartite graphs and `G = prod H_i`, then `alpha(G) = IR(G)`") are as quoted in the prompt.
  The target statement is therefore correctly transcribed.
* **Theorem 1.3 of the source**: "there exist `Lon(S), Soc(S) subset S` such that `Lon(S)` is
  independent and `|Soc(S)| <= 2^n`. In particular `IR(G) <= alpha(G) + 2^n`." It is proved by the
  polynomial method: `f_v(x_1,...,x_n) = prod_i (x_i - c_v(i))` inside the `2^n`-dimensional space
  of multilinear polynomials. This is the *dual* formulation of the writeup's tensor model, and the
  writeup's Lemma 2 is a strict refinement of it (the `rho(A) = 0` case). The writeup does not cite
  the source for this; it proves it afresh, which is legitimate, but see Caveats on attribution.
* **The 37 exceptional cases** of the source (fetched): `K_3^4`, `K[2,3] x K_3^3`, `K_4 x K_3^3`,
  `K_5 x K_4 x K_3^2`, ..., `K_3^5`, `K_3^6`, `K_4 x K_3^5`. The new argument covers all of them;
  I ran the load-bearing inequality on `K_3^4` and `K_3^5` specifically (below).
* **External results invoked**: essentially none. The only classical fact is "a maximal intersecting
  family on `[n]` has size `2^{n-1}`", which the writeup proves inline (correctly). Everything else
  is self-contained linear algebra. So there is no misquoted-citation failure mode available here.
* **Priority search** (web): no paper resolving Conjecture 1.2 was found (searches for follow-ups on
  Burcroff's conjecture / upper irredundance in direct products, 2019–2026 returned only the source
  paper, its journal version in Discrete Math. 343 (2020), and unrelated domination work). The
  catalog's May-2026 review reached the same conclusion. The result therefore appears to be new,

## Caveats

1. **Attribution/priority, not correctness.** Lemma 2 with `rho(A) = 0` *is* Theorem 1.3 of the
   source paper (proved there by the polynomial method). The writeup states "the proof below is
   self-contained" and "literature priority is unchecked"; for publication it must cite Alon–Defant
   Theorem 1.3 and present Lemma 2 as a refinement of it. The genuinely new ingredients are the
   `2*rho(A)` term and Lemma 4.
2. **`rho` depends on the labelling.** `rho(A)` is defined through the specific embedding
   `a |-> (1,a)` with parts indexed in *decreasing size order*. Lemmas 2 and 4 use the same fixed
   labelling, so the combination in §6 is legitimate — but the writeup never remarks that `rho` is
   not a labelling-invariant of `A`, and the sorted-order convention (fixed in §2) is silently
   re-used in Lemma 3(2). Both are expository, not mathematical, gaps.
3. **Implicit step in Lemma 2**: distinct social vertices lie in distinct cells (false twins cannot
   both be social). This is forced by the displayed relations (3.2), so nothing breaks, but the
   writeup should say it.
4. **`q_i >= 3` scope.** Lemma 4 and §7.1/§7.3 assume every factor has at least three parts. This is
   justified only by the earlier reduction (a factor with `<= 2` parts makes the whole product
   edgeless or bipartite). Note the reduction does not delete the small factor, it disposes of the
   whole product — which is correct, but means Lemma 4 is genuinely unavailable for products with a
   bipartite factor; those are covered by Lemma 1 alone.
5. **Edge cases.** `n = 0` (empty product) and factors with no vertices are dismissed in one line;
   both are trivially fine (`alpha = IR = 0` or `1`). `n = 1` and `n = 2` need the separate §7.2/§7.3
   arguments, and `K_3 x K_3` genuinely is the unique hard lonely-free case — the writeup finds it.
6. **Strength of the claim.** The theorem, if correct, resolves Alon–Defant Conjecture 1.2, hence
   also Burcroff's Conjecture 1.1 *including the 37 cases the source paper could not settle*, and the
   Defant–Iyer conjecture, by an argument of three pages that needs no isoperimetry or stability.
   I could not find an error and every load-bearing inequality survives exhaustive computational
   attack, including in the exceptional cases themselves; but a result that supersedes a published
   Alon paper's main theorem this cheaply should get independent human verification before submission.
7. The verdict block's one-liner ("a tensor-rank bound retaining the lonely vertices, combined with
   rank-nonincreasing coordinate compression") accurately describes what is proved; there is no gap
   between the announced claim and the theorem.

## Referee summary

I set out to break this writeup and could not. The argument is short and entirely self-contained,
and it survives a line-by-line re-derivation: the quotient reduction, the tensor/determinant-form
model, the refinement `t + 2 rho(A) <= 2^n` of Alon–Defant's Theorem 1.3 (whose proof correctly uses
that `M(A)` is isotropic and that private neighbours annihilate it), the three compression claims
(independence, weight, rank — the rank part via `rho >= dim T + dim E` and the exact identity after
compression), the down-set/intersecting-family identification `rho(D) = |D cap {1,2}^n| = |F|`, and
the weighted rank-defect inequality `alpha_w - W(A) >= 2^n - 2 rho(A)` obtained by extending `F` to
a maximal intersecting family and charging at least 2 to each new pattern class. The two inequalities
combine exactly, and the residual lonely-free cases (`n = 1`, `K_3 x K_3`, `n >= 3` via
`3^{n-1} >= 2^n`) are handled correctly, the `K_3 x K_3` case analysis being the only fiddly point —
and brute force confirms `IR(K_3 x K_3) = 3`. Computationally, `IR = alpha` held in 80 exhaustively
searched products (up to 36 vertices and 4.2M irredundant sets), Lemma 2 held over all 207,244
irredundant sets of `K_3^3` and over adversarially constructed quotient configurations, and Lemma 4
held in every exhaustive and randomized sweep, tight exactly where the proof predicts — including in
`K_3^4` and `K_3^5`, two of the 37 cases the source paper leaves open. The only defects I found are
expository (an implicit "distinct social vertices occupy distinct cells", no remark that `rho` is
labelling-dependent) and one of scholarship: Lemma 2 at `rho = 0` is the published Theorem 1.3 of
arXiv:1904.02595 and must be cited. Verdict: CONFIRMED.
