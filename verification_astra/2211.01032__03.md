## Interpretation

**Source statement, verbatim.** I downloaded the arXiv v3 TeX source
(`https://arxiv.org/e-print/2211.01032v3`, file `RE-arxiv3.tex`, line 2604, third
conjecture of the closing "Open Problems" discussion):

> The expected number of faces in a non-orientable random embedding of the complete
> graph $K_n$ is at most $\ln(n) + O(1)$.

preceded (line 2601) by the definition of the model:

> One natural way to define a random embedding of a graph on a non-orientable surface
> is to randomly choose a rotation system, and randomly choose a signature for all the
> edges in the graph, with probability $1/2$ of being either sign. From data, we
> expect a similar result to hold for non-orientable random embeddings of $K_n$ under
> this definition.

This matches the catalog text exactly, and it is exactly the model the writeup uses:
uniform local rotation at each vertex (a uniform cyclic order, $(d-1)!$ choices,
$d=n-1$) together with i.i.d. fair edge signs, **unconditioned**. The source does not
condition on the resulting surface being non-orientable; the writeup nevertheless
disposes of that alternative reading in its last section (the orientable signatures
are the $2^{n-1}$ coboundaries, so they have probability $2^{-(n-1)(n-2)/2}$ and
conditioning changes nothing). So both readings are covered and the interpretation is
the intended one; a reasonable author of the conjecture would regard it as resolved by
this theorem. No interpretation gaming: the writeup proves the upper bound the
conjecture asserts, in the stronger explicit form $\ln n+\tfrac14+\tfrac\gamma2+o(1)$.

**Retry-leg discipline.** The prompt contains an earlier `gpt-5.6-sol` attempt
(verdict `partial`) and no referee report on it. The new writeup inherits only the
flag/ribbon-graph representation and the exposure lemma, and it re-derives both
(§1); I checked both from scratch rather than crediting them. It explicitly discards
the earlier short-face enumeration, and it does **not** reproduce the earlier
attempt's actual weakness (the abstract-stub relaxation of its §5, which is
exponentially lossy for long faces). The route is genuinely different: a
fixed-vertex split plus a per-trace congestion supermartingale.

## Caveats

1. **(11)'s conditioning is asserted, not proved.** "To estimate a good-face event,
   discard continuations whose histories cease to satisfy the load restriction" is the
   whole argument. The repair is routine (given in S14 above) but has one step that is
   not purely mechanical: applying the $1/(2D)$ bound at the last query needs
   $r_{w_0}\le R$ *after* query $k-1$, which holds only because the trace's active
   vertex changes at every step, so query $k-1$ loads $u\ne w_0$. A referee should
   require this paragraph to be written out.
2. **No explicit $n_0$.** "For sufficiently large $n$" in (7) hides $D=n-2-\lceil
   4\log_2n\rceil>0$, which fails up to $n\approx32$ ($n=20$ gives $D=0$, $n=32$ gives
   $D=10$ and $C(n)=68$). Harmless — the conjecture is asymptotic and $F\le n(n-1)$
   always — but the writeup should name the threshold.
3. **(10) is exactly at the boundary.** $n^32^{-R}\le1/n$ holds only because $R$ is a
   *ceiling* of $4\log_2 n$; with $R=\lfloor4\log_2 n\rfloor$ the chain would fail.
4. **Simplicity of $K_n$ is load-bearing and unstated as a hypothesis.** (4) and the
   "at most two flags to $w_0$" step in (11) use that there is one edge between any two
   vertices; the argument does not transfer verbatim to multigraphs (where the source's
   companion conjecture $\mathbb E[F^-]\le\mathbb E[F]$ also lives).
5. **The model-reduction sentence** "with an immaterial fixed adjustment if the
   opposite sign convention is used" is hand-waved. It is correct (verified here to TV
   distance $\le0.0026$ at $n=4,5,6$), but a reader cannot check it as written.
6. **In §2 the exposure bound is applied to a flag identified by a different path
   structure** ($P\cup Q_o$) than the one the lemma forbids ($J_o\cup Q_o$). The word
   "at most" saves it, but the writeup does not point this out.
7. **The boxed theorem is asymptotic.** At $n=50$ the argument actually proves only
   $\mathbb EF_{50}\le7.98$ (against the display's $4.45$); the constants
   $\frac14+\frac\gamma2$ are limits. This is consistent with the claim as stated
   ("As $n\to\infty$") but the verdict block's one-line phrasing could be read as a
   finite-$n$ statement.
8. **Edge cases.** $n=1,2$: $\mathbb EF_2=1$, trivial; $k=0,1,2$ face lengths handled
   (S15); disconnected graphs do not arise. $d=1$ ($n=2$) breaks $\mathcal H_d$'s
   no-proper-cycle argument, which is why S15 assumes $n\ge3$ — correctly stated.
9. **Not independently refereed by a human.** The claim resolves a conjecture of
   Campion Loth–Halasz–Masařík–Mohar–Šámal that is about 1.5 years old; the argument
   is short and, by the measurements above, asymptotically tight in its avoiding-face
   half. That combination is exactly what one would want an expert pass on before
   treating it as settled.
