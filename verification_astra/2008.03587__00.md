## Interpretation

The target is Question 4.1 (`qu:poiluszombius`) of arXiv:2008.03587. I downloaded
the v3 TeX source (`https://arxiv.org/e-print/2008.03587`, file `main.tex`,
lines 420-423) and the statement is verbatim:

```
\begin{question}\label{qu:poiluszombius}
For every graph $G$, and for a graph $G'$ obtained from $G$ by successively
adding vertices of degree $1$, does it always hold that $z(G')= z(G)$?
\end{question}
```

The catalog transcription in `prompt.md` is faithful — **no garbling this time**,
in contrast to the sibling target `2008.03587__01`, where the catalog's `>` for
`\geq` in Question 4.2 was the entire source of that attempt's FATAL_ERROR
(see `verification/2008.03587__01.md`). I checked this first precisely because of
that history. The new writeup does **not** reproduce that failure mode: it attacks
the genuine statement.

The writeup exhibits a 59-vertex graph `G` and `H = G + ` one pendant vertex, and
proves `z(H) = 2 < 3 <= z(G)`. A single such pair refutes a universally quantified
"does it always hold" question, so this is a complete resolution, not a literal-reading
strawman. It is moreover the *intended* mechanism: the paper's own gloss immediately
after the question reads "Question~\ref{qu:poiluszombius} can be interpreted as: is
there any advantage for zombies to individually wait for some pre-announced time at
the beginning of the game (and then activate and follow the standard rules)?" — and
the writeup's Section 6 identifies its mechanism as exactly that ("the new leaf delays
the rear zombie by one move"). An author of the source paper would unambiguously
regard Question 4.1 as answered in the negative by this example.

**Convention audit.** The paper's Section 1 quotes [fitzpatrick2016deterministic]:
"each zombie must move along some shortest path, or geodesic, joining itself and a
nearest survivor. We say that the zombies capture a survivor if one of the zombies
moves onto the same vertex as a survivor", plus "In this version, zombies may have a
choice as to which shortest path to follow"; graphs are connected and there is a
single survivor; `z(G)` is "the minimum number of zombies to place around a graph G
so as to ensure that the survivor will eventually be captured" (zombies placed first).
The proof of Theorem 2.1 in the paper has zombies actively *selecting* a favourable
geodesic ("choosing to keep the same coordinate in G. Note that this is a valid
move"), confirming that the zombie team exercises free will in its own favour, while
survivor lower bounds must hold against *all* zombie choices. The writeup's stated
conventions (Section 1 preamble) match this exactly, and so does my solver
(existential over joint zombie moves, universal over survivor responses). No
convention mismatch.

Nothing is inherited uncritically from the previous attempt reproduced in
`prompt.md`: the new writeup shares only the standing rules, and every object it
uses is constructed and argued from scratch. (Consistency note: the previous
attempt proved `z(H) <= z(G)` and that any counterexample needs `z(G) >= 3`,
`c(G) < z(G)` and a mixed active/delayed configuration. The new counterexample has
`z(G) = 3`, and `c(G) = 2` for this cactus-with-a-path, so it sits exactly in the
window that argument left open — a mild independent consistency check, not evidence.)

## Caveats

- **C1 (unstated, immediate).** The rotation lemma tacitly uses that each attached
  cycle is an isometric subgraph and that its attachment vertex is a cut vertex. Both
  hold in `G` and `H` by construction (a cactus with cycles hung on distinct path
  vertices), but the lemma is stated for a general host graph `Q` where "attached ...
  only at its vertex 0" is what supplies this. Not a gap in the application.
- **C2 (loose wording).** §5's "This shrinking interval cannot support evasion
  indefinitely" is the only genuinely hand-waved sentence. The sharp statement is: the
  zombies stand on `v_t` and `v_{11-t}` and at `t=5` on `v5, v6`, so by round 5 either
  capture or a cycle-root contact has occurred. Routine; machine-confirmed.
- **C3 (asserted, easy).** §5's "this first contact is made by the zombie coming from
  `v0`" is asserted without proof; it follows because the rear zombie sits at
  `v_{11-t}` with `11-t ≥ 6` for `t ≤ 5` and no root has index `> 4`.
- **C4 (understatement, harmless).** The verdict says "requires at least three
  zombies". The true value is `z(G) = 3`. The writeup explicitly (and correctly) says
  determining this is unnecessary.
- **C5 (conventions).** The writeup's caveat field flags dependence on compulsory moves
  and zombies-before-survivor. I tested both orderings and both survivor stay-rules; the
  separation survives all of them. Compulsory zombie motion is of course essential —
  under the pass-permitting variant `z_pass` of the previous attempt the mechanism
  would collapse — but compulsory motion is the definition of the game, so this is not
  a weakness.
- **C6 (survivor stepping onto a zombie).** The paper defines capture as a zombie moving
  onto the survivor; my solver additionally treats the survivor moving onto a zombie as
  capture (standard). This never binds here — the "survivor must move" variant, where
  that rule could matter most, gives the same answers.
- **C7 (scope).** Both graphs are connected and finite, as the source paper requires.
  No `k=0/1`, empty-graph or disconnected edge case arises.
- **C8 (attribution).** The rotation argument is essentially the source paper's own
  "Busan strategy"; a submitted version should cite it and Liu-Miller (FUN 2026).
  Scholarly, not mathematical.
- **C9 (what is and is not settled).** This kills the equality `z(G')=z(G)` but leaves
  the natural refined question (how far below `z(G)` can `z(G')` drop? is the drop
  bounded?) wide open, and it does not touch Question 4.2. The verdict block does not
  overclaim on this point.
