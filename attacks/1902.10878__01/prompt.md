 The question of whether $\psi(x,y) = \psi(y,x)$ for all $x,y \in (0,1]$ remains open. The companion paper arXiv:1908.07453 (Hompe, 2019), which extended the results to additional parameter ranges for $\phi$ and $\psi$, was withdrawn in June 2022 and its content incorporated back into arXiv:1902.10878; neither version appears to resolve the symmetry question for $\psi$. No other follow-up addressing this specific open question was found in the literature.

Context

The authors prove Theorem 2.3: $\phi(x,y) = \phi(y,x)$ for all $x,y \in (0,1]$, via a linear-programming argument. They remark immediately after that they have been unable to establish the analogous symmetry for the biconstrained function $\psi$, even though they have no counterexample.

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:Let $x,y\in(0,1]$ and let $A,B,C$ be disjoint nonempty subsets of a graph $G$, where every vertex in $A$ has at least $x|B|$ neighbours in $B$, and every vertex in $B$ has at least $y|C|$ neighbours in $C$. We denote by $\phi(x,y)$ the maximum $z$ such that, in all such graphs $G$, there is a vertex $v$ in $C$ that is joined to at least $z|A|$ vertices in $A$ by two-edge paths. The function $\phi$ is interesting, and we investigate some of its properties. For instance, we show that it is symmetric in $x$ and $y$, and that it has a discontinuity at $x=y=1/k$ for all integers $k>1$. We raise a number of questions and conjectures.
