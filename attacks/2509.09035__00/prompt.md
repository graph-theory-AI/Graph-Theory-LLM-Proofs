Georgakopoulos–Papasoglu conjecture for trees and planar graphs — Graph-theory open problems (arXiv)

 
 Status
 open
 high confidence
 

 Conjecture 1.2 of Georgakopoulos and Papasoglu — that fat-$H$-minor-free graphs are quasi-isometric to $H$-minor-free graphs — was disproved in general by Davies, Hickingbotham, Illingworth, and McCarty with a counterexample $H$ containing a clique of size 15. The source paper (arXiv:2509.09035) proves a weaker result for trees: fat-tree-minor-free graphs admit quasi-isometries to graphs of bounded line-width, but the full conjecture for $H$ a tree or planar graph remains open. No follow-up resolving the tree or planar special case was found in the indexed literature as of May 2026.

 Reviewer notes. The question is raised informally in arXiv:2509.09035 (September 2025). The paper's own main result handles trees but with a strictly weaker conclusion (quasi-isometry to bounded line-width rather than to H-minor-free), so Conjecture 1.2 for H a tree or H planar remains open. A related April 2026 paper (arXiv:2604.11318, Bonnet–Le–Pilipczuk–Pilipczuk) on coarse balanced separators in fat-minor-free graphs was found but its abstract does not address this specific conjecture.

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:Robertson and Seymour proved that for every finite tree $H$, there exists $k$ such that every finite graph $G$ with no $H$ minor has path-width at most $k$; and conversely, for every integer $k$, there is a finite tree $H$ such that every finite graph $G$ with an $H$ minor has path-width more than $k$. If we (twice) replace ``path-width'' by ``line-width'', the same is true for infinite graphs $G$.
We prove a ``coarse graph theory'' analogue, as follows. For every finite tree $H$ and every $c$, there exist $k,L,C$ such that every graph that does not contain $H$ as a $c$-fat minor admits an $(L,C)$-quasi-isonetry to a graph with line-width at most $k$; and conversely, for all $k,L,C$ there exist $c$ and a finite tree $H$ such that every graph that contains $H$ as a $c$-fat minor admits no $(L,C)$-quasi-isometry to a graph with line-width at most $k$.
