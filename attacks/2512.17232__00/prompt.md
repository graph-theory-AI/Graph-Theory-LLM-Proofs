 Conjecture 6 from arXiv:2512.17232 posits an optimal induced Menger-type theorem: for any $k$, $G$, and $X,Y\subseteq V(G)$, either $G$ contains $k$ pairwise anti-complete $(X,Y)$-paths or there is a set $Z$ of size at most $k-1$ whose closed neighbourhood hits all $(X,Y)$-paths. The source paper itself proves a weaker form with separator bound $78(k-1)$ (improvable to $4(k-1)$ using radius-4 balls), leaving the optimal $k-1$ bound as an open conjecture. No follow-up paper resolving or partially resolving Conjecture 6 was found in the five months since the preprint appeared.

 Reviewer notes. The conjecture, if proved, would imply an Induced Grid Minor Theorem for $K_{1,t}$-induced-subgraph-free graphs conjectured by Dallard et al. The paper is only ~5 months old; no follow-up was found in indexed literature. Conjecture 6 is closely related to but distinct from Conjecture 2 in the same paper (the $A$-path version), for which Albrechtsen, Knappe, and Wollan independently announced a partial solution ($d=2$ case). No such concurrent progress on Conjecture 6 (the $(X,Y)$-path variant) was identified.

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:Given a graph $G$ and $\mathcal{A}\subseteq V(G)$, a classical theorem of Gallai (1964) states that for every positive integer $k$, the graph $G$ contains $k$ pairwise vertex-disjoint $\mathcal{A}$-paths, or a set $Z\subseteq V(G)$ of size at most $2(k-1)$ such that $G-Z$ contains no $\mathcal{A}$-paths. We generalise Gallai's theorem to the induced setting: We prove that $G$ contains $k$ pairwise anti-complete $\mathcal{A}$-paths, or a set $Z$ of size at most $78(k-1)$ such that, after removing the closed neighbourhood of $Z$, the resulting graph has no $\mathcal{A}$-path. Here, two paths are anti-complete if they are vertex disjoint and there is no edge in $G$ having one endpoint in each of them.
We further show that the bound $78(k-1)$ on the size of $Z$ can be reduced to $4(k-1)$ if one removes the balls of radius $4$ around the vertices of $Z$ (instead of radius $1$), which is within a factor $2$ of optimal. We also establish analogous results for long induced $\mathcal{A}$-paths.
