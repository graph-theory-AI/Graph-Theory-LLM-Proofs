Question. What is the infimum $c$ such that for any large enough $t$, there is a graph that admits a quasi-$K_{t}$-minor but no $K_{ct}$-minor?

Context

Follows from the strong disproof of Conjecture 1.4. The paper establishes the bounds $\frac{1}{2}\leqslant c\leqslant\frac{2}{3}$; the trivial upper bound $c\leqslant 1$ comes from the fact that every quasi-$K_{2t}$-minor-free graph admits a $K_t$-minor.

 On a recolouring version of Hadwiger's conjecture
 Marthe Bonamy, Marc Heinrich, Clément Legrand-Duchesne, Jonathan Narboni · 2025-03-13
 https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10684

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:We prove that for any $\varepsilon>0$, for any large enough $t$, there is a graph $G$ that admits no $K_t$-minor but admits a $(\frac32-\varepsilon)t$-colouring that is "frozen" with respect to Kempe changes, i.e. any two colour classes induce a connected component. This disproves three conjectures of Las Vergnas and Meyniel from 1981.
