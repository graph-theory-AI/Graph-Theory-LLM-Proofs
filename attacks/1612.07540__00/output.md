## 1. Statement and present status

For \(h\ge 1\), let
\[
D(h)=\sup\{\dim(P): \operatorname{height}(P)\le h,\ \operatorname{cover}(P)\text{ is planar}\}.
\]

There are two distinct questions in the extracted statement:

1. Is \(D(h)\) bounded by a polynomial in \(h\)?
2. Is \(D(h)=O(h)\)?

According to the supplied literature, Kozik–Micek–Trotter first proved \(D(h)=O(h^6)\), and Gorsky–Seweryn improved this to
\[
D(h)=O(h^3).
\]
Thus the polynomial question is settled. The reported lower construction gives
\[
D(h)\ge 2h-2
\]
for the relevant values of \(h\). Consequently, the current gap is
\[
2h-O(1)\le D(h)\le O(h^3).
\]
No superlinear lower bound, and no linear upper bound, is obtained here.

The remainder gives a self-contained reduction explaining how the \(O(h^3)\) bound follows from the \(O(k^3)\) theorem for \(k\)-outerplanar cover graphs, and isolates a sufficient route to a linear bound.

## 2. Reversible sets and min–max pairs

Write \(\operatorname{Inc}(P)\) for the ordered incomparable pairs of \(P\). A set
\(S\subseteq\operatorname{Inc}(P)\) is **reversible** if there is a linear extension \(L\) of \(P\) such that
\[
y<_L x\qquad\text{for every }(x,y)\in S.
\]

An **alternating cycle** in \(S\) is a cyclic sequence
\[
(x_1,y_1),\ldots,(x_m,y_m)\in S
\]
such that
\[
x_i\le_P y_{i+1}
\]
for all \(i\), with indices modulo \(m\).

### Lemma 2.1
A set \(S\subseteq\operatorname{Inc}(P)\) is reversible if and only if it contains no alternating cycle.

#### Proof
If a linear extension reverses every \((x_i,y_i)\), then
\[
y_i<_L x_i\le_L y_{i+1}
\]
for every \(i\), which is impossible cyclically.

Conversely, add an arc \(y\to x\) for every \((x,y)\in S\) to the order digraph of \(P\). If the resulting digraph has a directed cycle, compressing each maximal segment consisting of order arcs gives an alternating cycle in \(S\). Hence, in the absence of an alternating cycle, the augmented digraph is acyclic and has a topological ordering reversing every pair in \(S\). ∎

It follows that \(\dim(P)\) is the minimum number of reversible sets needed to cover all ordered incomparable pairs.

Let
\[
\operatorname{MM}(P)=
\{(a,b):a\in\min(P),\ b\in\max(P),\ a\parallel b\}.
\]
Let \(\mu(P)\) be the minimum number of reversible sets covering \(\operatorname{MM}(P)\).

### Lemma 2.2: reduction to min–max pairs
Let \(P\) have height \(h\) and planar cover graph. There is a poset \(P^\pm\) of height at most \(h+2\), again with planar cover graph, such that
\[
\dim(P)\le \mu(P^\pm).
\]

#### Proof
For every \(x\in P\), add two new elements \(x^-\) and \(x^+\), with relations generated by
\[
x^-<x<x^+.
\]
The cover graph is obtained from the cover graph of \(P\) by attaching two pendant vertices at each old vertex, so it remains planar. Every \(x^-\) is minimal and every \(x^+\) is maximal.

If \(x\parallel y\) in \(P\), then \(x^-\parallel y^+\) in \(P^\pm\). A min–max realizer of \(P^\pm\) contains a linear extension with
\[
y^+<x^-.
\]
Restricting it to \(P\) gives
\[
y<y^+<x^-<x.
\]
Thus every ordered incomparable pair \((x,y)\) of \(P\) is reversed by one of the restricted extensions. ∎

If
\[
M(h)=\sup\{\mu(P):\operatorname{height}(P)\le h,\ \operatorname{cover}(P)\text{ planar}\},
\]
then
\[
M(h)\le D(h)\le M(h+2).
\]
Thus the linear and polynomial growth questions are unchanged by restricting attention to min–max incomparable pairs.

## 3. A radial reduction to \(k\)-outerplanar cover graphs

Define
\[
\Phi(k)=\max\left(2,\sup\{\dim(Q):\operatorname{cover}(Q)
\text{ is }k\text{-outerplanar}\}\right).
\]
The supplied Gorsky–Seweryn result is
\[
\Phi(k)=O(k^3).
\]

The following reduction is included with proof; no claim of novelty is made.

### Proposition 3.1
If \(P\) has height \(h\ge2\) and planar cover graph, then
\[
\mu(P)\le 2+3\Phi(3h-3).
\]
Consequently,
\[
D(h)\le 2+3\Phi(3h+3).
\]

#### Proof

It suffices to consider a connected cover graph. Indeed, min–max realizers of the components can be padded to the same size and concatenated. Ordering the components in opposite orders in two of the extensions handles all min–max pairs belonging to different components.

Fix a plane embedding of the cover graph \(G\), choose a root \(r\) on the outer face, and put
\[
\ell(v)=\operatorname{dist}_G(r,v).
\]
Set \(H=h-1\). If \(x\le_P y\), a saturated chain from \(x\) to \(y\) has at most \(H\) cover edges. Hence
\[
|\ell(x)-\ell(y)|\le H. \tag{3.1}
\]

### Far pairs

Partition the min–max pairs into
\[
S^+=\{(a,b):\ell(b)>\ell(a)+H\},
\]
\[
S^-=\{(a,b):\ell(a)>\ell(b)+H\},
\]
and the remaining near pairs, for which
\[
|\ell(a)-\ell(b)|\le H.
\]

The set \(S^+\) is reversible. Otherwise, let
\((a_i,b_i)\) be an alternating cycle in \(S^+\). Since
\(a_{i-1}\le b_i\), equation (3.1) gives
\[
\ell(a_{i-1})\ge \ell(b_i)-H>\ell(a_i),
\]
for every \(i\), an impossible cyclic strict decrease. The proof for \(S^-\) is symmetric. Thus the far pairs require only two reversible classes.

### An annulus lemma

For integers \(s\le t\), let \(G[s,t]\) be the subgraph induced by vertices whose levels lie between \(s\) and \(t\).

#### Lemma 3.2
Every \(G[s,t]\) is \((t-s+1)\)-outerplanar.

#### Proof
For \(s>0\), the ball induced by levels at most \(s-1\) is connected. Contract it to a vertex \(z\). Every level-\(s\) vertex has a neighbor at level \(s-1\), and hence is adjacent to \(z\) after contraction. Delete \(z\) and take the resulting face as the outer face. All level-\(s\) vertices are then on the outer face.

After deleting the level-\(s\) vertices, every level-\((s+1)\) vertex becomes incident with the outer face because it has a neighbor at level \(s\). Continuing in this manner removes all vertices in at most \(t-s+1\) rounds. The case \(s=0\) starts directly with the root. ∎

### Near pairs

For a near pair \((a,b)\), define
\[
q(a)=\left\lfloor \frac{\ell(a)}{H}\right\rfloor.
\]
If \(q=q(a)\), then
\[
qH\le \ell(a)\le(q+1)H-1
\]
and
\[
(q-1)H\le\ell(b)\le(q+2)H-1.
\]
Let
\[
X_q=\{v:(q-1)H\le\ell(v)\le(q+2)H-1\},
\]
with negative levels omitted.

Let \(Q_q\) be the poset whose order is generated only by cover relations of \(P\) having both endpoints in \(X_q\). The cover graph of \(Q_q\) is a subgraph of \(G[X_q]\): indeed, a cover relation in the reachability order of a DAG must already be an arc of that DAG. By Lemma 3.2, this graph is \(3H\)-outerplanar. Therefore
\[
\dim(Q_q)\le\Phi(3H).
\]

Every near pair assigned to \(q\) remains an incomparable min–max pair in \(Q_q\). Choose a realizer of \(Q_q\), and assign the pair a color
\[
(q\bmod 3,c),
\qquad 1\le c\le\Phi(3H),
\]
where the \(c\)-th local extension reverses it.

We claim every fixed color \((r,c)\) is reversible in \(P\). Suppose it contained an alternating cycle
\[
(a_1,b_1),\ldots,(a_m,b_m).
\]
Write \(q_i=q(a_i)\). All the \(q_i\) are congruent modulo \(3\). If \(q_i\ne q_{i+1}\), then \(|q_i-q_{i+1}|\ge3\). Using the intervals above, this would imply
\[
|\ell(a_i)-\ell(b_{i+1})|\ge H+1,
\]
contradicting \(a_i\le b_{i+1}\) and (3.1). Hence
\[
q_1=\cdots=q_m=q.
\]

For every relation \(a_i\le b_{i+1}\), choose a saturated chain. It has at most \(H\) edges. Since \(a_i\) lies between levels \(qH\) and \((q+1)H-1\), every vertex of the chain lies in \(X_q\). Thus
\[
a_i\le_{Q_q} b_{i+1}.
\]
The pairs would therefore form an alternating cycle in the set reversed by the \(c\)-th linear extension of \(Q_q\), a contradiction.

Thus the near pairs are covered by \(3\Phi(3H)\) reversible classes. Together with the two far classes,
\[
\mu(P)\le2+3\Phi(3H)=2+3\Phi(3h-3).
\]

Finally, apply Lemma 2.2 to \(P^\pm\), whose height is at most \(h+2\):
\[
\dim(P)\le\mu(P^\pm)
 \le 2+3\Phi(3((h+2)-1))
 =2+3\Phi(3h+3).
\]
∎

### Consequences

Using the reported theorem \(\Phi(k)=O(k^3)\), Proposition 3.1 gives
\[
D(h)=O(h^3).
\]

More importantly, it isolates a sufficient strengthening:

> If every poset with a \(k\)-outerplanar cover graph had dimension \(O(k)\), then every height-\(h\) poset with planar cover graph would have dimension \(O(h)\).

Indeed, if \(\Phi(k)\le Ak+B\), then
\[
D(h)\le 9A(h+1)+3B+2.
\]
This is only a sufficient route, not a resolution: no linear bound for \(\Phi(k)\) is proved here.

The known cubic \(k\)-outerplanar theorem also gives a genuine linear special regime: any family satisfying
\[
k(P)=O(h(P)^{1/3})
\]
has \(\dim(P)=O(h(P))\).

## 4. Another elementary linear special case

Let \(S\subseteq P\) be such that \(P-S\) is a planar poset in the source paper's stronger sense.

### Lemma 4.1
For any \(x\in P\),
\[
\dim(P)\le \dim(P-x)+2.
\]

#### Proof
Let \(Q=P-x\) and extend each member of a realizer of \(Q\) to a linear extension of \(P\). This is possible while retaining its restriction to \(Q\), since every predecessor of \(x\) precedes every successor of \(x\).

Add two further linear extensions. In the first, place all strict predecessors of \(x\), then \(x\), then all remaining elements; this puts \(x\) below every element incomparable with it. In the second, place all elements not strictly above \(x\), then \(x\), then all strict successors; this puts every element incomparable with \(x\) below \(x\). These two extensions handle both orientations of every incomparable pair involving \(x\). ∎

Iterating and applying the source theorem gives
\[
\dim(P)
 \le \dim(P-S)+2|S|
 \le 192h+96+2|S|.
\]
Therefore the desired linear bound holds for every family in which deleting \(O(h)\) vertices makes the poset planar in the stronger, upward-planar sense. No bound of this form on the deletion number is known for all planar-cover-graph posets.

## 5. Obstacles to straightforward improvements

### Height does not control graph width

Let \(G_n\) be the \(n\times n\) grid with bipartition \(A\cup B\). Define a height-two poset by declaring
\[
a<b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
a\in A,\ b\in B,\text{ and }ab\in E(G_n).
\]
There are no chains with two strict relations, so this is a height-two poset, and its cover graph is exactly \(G_n\).

Thus fixed height does not bound treewidth, radius, or outerplanarity. In particular, one cannot apply the \(k\)-outerplanar theorem to the whole cover graph with \(k=O(h)\). The annular localization in Proposition 3.1 is essential.

### Pairwise conflicts are insufficient

For \(t\ge3\), take elements \(a_1,\dots,a_t,b_1,\dots,b_t\) and relations
\[
a_i<b_{i+1}
\]
with indices modulo \(t\), and no other strict relations. The cover graph is merely a matching. Nevertheless,
\[
\{(a_i,b_i):1\le i\le t\}
\]
is a strict alternating cycle and hence is not reversible. No two of these pairs form an alternating \(2\)-cycle. Thus reversibility cannot be recognized solely through a graph of pairwise conflicts; alternating cycles of arbitrary length must be controlled.

## 6. Exact gap

The polynomial portion of the original question is resolved by the known \(O(h^3)\) theorem. The unresolved task is to replace the local \(O(k^3)\) control in Proposition 3.1 by \(O(k)\), or to exploit additional structure of the particular annular posets \(Q_q\) to obtain \(O(h)\) reversible classes directly.

I do not have such a coloring, nor a construction with dimension \(\omega(h)\). Hence the linear conjecture remains open.
