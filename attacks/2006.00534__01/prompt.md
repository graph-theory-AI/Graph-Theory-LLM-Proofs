 Question 8 asks whether T(n) behaves differently when n is prime and how much T(G) depends on group structure beyond just the order. The source paper itself notes that the best known upper bound for T(p) (prime p) is only O(p^{3/4+epsilon}), which may not be tight, since the constructions achieving better bounds for composite n rely on proper subgroups unavailable in Z/pZ. No follow-up paper resolving either part of this question was found in a wide web search across arXiv, Journal of Number Theory, and the Ramanujan Journal.

 Reviewer notes. No follow-up paper specifically addressing Question 8 was found. A Ramanujan Journal paper (DOI: 10.1007/s11139-025-01281-6) on minimal additive complements appeared in a 2025 search result but was inaccessible due to authentication redirect. The gap between the O(p^{3/4+epsilon}) bound for prime p and the better O(sqrt(n)) bound achievable when n has divisors of suitable size is the core open issue; the structural question (T(G) vs |G|) is related but broader.

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:Given a subset $W$ of an abelian group $G$, a subset $C$ is called an additive complement for $W$ if $W+C=G$; if, moreover, no proper subset of $C$ has this property, then we say that $C$ is a minimal complement for $W$. It is natural to ask which subsets $C$ can arise as minimal complements for some $W$. We show that in a finite abelian group $G$, every non-empty subset $C$ of size $|C| \leq 2^{2/3}|G|^{1/3}/((3e \log |G|)^{2/3}$ is a minimal complement for some $W$. As a corollary, we deduce that every finite non-empty subset of an infinite abelian group is a minimal complement. We also derive several analogous results for ``dual'' problems about maximal supplements.
