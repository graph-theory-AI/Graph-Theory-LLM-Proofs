 The specific regime of fixed $s$ and $t \to \infty$ for Woodall's conjecture (that every $K_{s,t}$-minor-free graph is $(s+t-1)$-choosable) remains open as of 2026. Steiner's 2022 disproof (arXiv:2201.09115) only applies when $s$ and $t$ are of comparable size ($s \le t \le Cs$), and a 2025 follow-up by Vanderbush (Graphs Combin. 41, 2025) improves the disproof to much smaller comparable-size parameters (e.g., $t \ge 48$ for the $(t,t)$ case), but still entirely within the comparable-size regime. No analogous list-chromatic result to Kostochka's chromatic-number theorem is known for fixed $s$ with $t \to \infty$.

 Reviewer notes. No follow-up resolving the fixed-$s$ case was found. A related follow-up, Vanderbush, 'New Counterexamples to a Conjecture by Woodall on Graph Minors and List Coloring', Graphs and Combinatorics 41, 103 (2025), DOI:10.1007/s00373-025-02957-y, improves Steiner's counterexample parameters (e.g., provides explicit counterexamples for $(s,t)=(t,t)$ with $t \ge 48$, $t \ne 49$, and $(s,t)=(t,t+1)$ with $t \ge 54$, $t \ne 55$) but remains in the comparable-size regime and does not address fixed $s$ with $t \to \infty$. === Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:In 2001, Woodall conjectured that for every pair of integers $s,t \ge 1$, all graphs without a $K_{s,t}$-minor are $(s+t-1)$-choosable. In this note we refute this conjecture in a strong form: We prove that for every choice of constants $\varepsilon>0$ and $C \ge 1$ there exists $N=N(\varepsilon,C) \in \mathbb{N}$ such that for all integers $s,t $ with $N \le s \le t \le Cs$ there exists a graph without a $K_{s,t}$-minor and list chromatic number greater than $(1-\varepsilon)(2s+t)$.
