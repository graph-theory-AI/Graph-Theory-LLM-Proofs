 The open question of estimating s((Z/kZ)^n) for fixed k and large n remains unresolved. For prime k=p, Fox–Sauermann (2018) established s(F_p^n) ≤ 2p·r(F_p^n), giving an exponential upper bound via Ellenberg–Gijswijt, but a gap between this and Elsholtz's exponential lower bound (base ~2.08 for odd k≥3) remains open. For composite k the Alon–Dubiner bound (cn log n)^n·k remains the state of the art, far exceeding the exponential lower bound. No subsequent verified paper closes this gap.

 Reviewer notes. No verified post-2018 paper found that resolves or substantially narrows the gap between the exponential lower bound (base ~2.08 for odd k≥3, Elsholtz) and the upper bounds for s((Z/kZ)^n) with fixed k and large n. Naslund (arXiv:1701.04942) proved exponential upper bounds for prime-order groups before the Fox–Sauermann paper; Hegedüs (arXiv:1712.00228, 2017) extended these to arbitrary finite abelian groups; both arXiv versions predate Fox–Sauermann and are not post-2018 results. The question for composite fixed k remains fully open, with a large gap between polynomial-exponential upper bounds and exponential lower bounds.

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:For a finite abelian group $G$, the Erdős-Ginzburg-Ziv constant $\mathfrak{s}(G)$ is the smallest $s$ such that every sequence of $s$ (not necessarily distinct) elements of $G$ has a zero-sum subsequence of length $\operatorname{exp}(G)$. For a prime $p$, let $r(\mathbb{F}_p^n)$ denote the size of the largest subset of $\mathbb{F}_p^n$ without a three-term arithmetic progression. Although similar methods have been used to study $\mathfrak{s}(G)$ and $r(\mathbb{F}_p^n)$, no direct connection between these quantities has previously been established. We give an upper bound for $\mathfrak{s}(G)$ in terms of $r(\mathbb{F}_p^n)$ for the prime divisors $p$ of $\operatorname{exp}(G)$. For the special case $G=\mathbb{F}_p^n$, we prove $\mathfrak{s}(\mathbb{F}_p^n)\leq 2p\cdot r(\mathbb{F}_p^n)$. Using the upper bounds for $r(\mathbb{F}_p^n)$ of Ellenberg and Gijswijt, this result improves the previously best known upper bounds for $\mathfrak{s}(\mathbb{F}_p^n)$ given by Naslund.
