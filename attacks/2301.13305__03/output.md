# 1. Statement of the partial result

Let \(M(n)\) be the maximum size of a family \(\mathcal F\subseteq\mathbb F_2^n\) such that no difference of two distinct members is the indicator of an interval of length \(k^3\), where \(k\geq 1\).

Put \(N=n+1\), and define the cube-distance graph \(D_N\) on
\[
\{0,1,\dots,N-1\}=\{0,1,\dots,n\}
\]
by joining \(i\) and \(j\) when
\[
|i-j|=k^3
\]
for some positive integer \(k\). Let
\[
r_3(N)=\alpha(D_N).
\]

The following bounds are proved below:
\[
\boxed{
2^{\,n-\lceil\log_2\chi(D_N)\rceil}
\leq M(n)
\leq \frac{2^n}{\chi_f(D_N)}
\leq \frac{r_3(N)}{N}\,2^n .
}
\tag{1}
\]

Moreover, the leftmost quantity is exactly the maximum size of an admissible binary linear subspace.

There is an explicit 7-adic coloring of \(D_N\) giving
\[
\chi(D_N)\leq \frac{49}{12}N^\beta,
\qquad
\beta=\log_{343}\!\left(\frac73\right)
=\frac{\log(7/3)}{3\log 7}
=0.145141\ldots.
\tag{2}
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
M(n)\geq \frac6{49}\,\frac{2^n}{(n+1)^\beta}.
}
\tag{3}
\]
The family in (3) can be chosen to be a binary linear code.

The supplied polynomial-difference theorem gives \(r_3(N)=o(N)\), so (1) recovers
\[
M(n)=o(2^n).
\]
Thus the present quantitative information leaves the large gap
\[
\frac{2^n}{n^{0.145142}}\ \lesssim\ M(n)\ =\ o(2^n).
\]

# 2. The cube-distance graph and the Cayley graph

For \(0\leq j\leq n\), let
\[
p_j=\mathbf 1_{\{1,\dots,j\}}\in\mathbb F_2^n,
\qquad p_0=0.
\]
If \(i<j\), then
\[
p_i+p_j=\mathbf 1_{\{i+1,\dots,j\}},
\]
which is an interval vector of length \(j-i\). Hence the map
\[
j\longmapsto p_j
\]
is a graph homomorphism from \(D_N\) into the forbidden-difference Cayley graph
\[
\Gamma_n=\operatorname{Cay}\!\left(
\mathbb F_2^n,\,
\{\mathbf 1_I: I\text{ is an interval of cube length}\}
\right).
\]
By definition,
\[
M(n)=\alpha(\Gamma_n).
\]

Equivalently, under the boundary map
\[
\partial(x_1,\dots,x_n)
=(x_1,x_1+x_2,\dots,x_{n-1}+x_n,x_n),
\]
\(\mathbb F_2^n\) is identified with the even-weight subspace of
\(\mathbb F_2^{N}\), and the indicator of an interval \([a,b]\) maps to
\[
e_{a-1}+e_b.
\]
Thus \(\Gamma_n\) is the cubelike graph generated by the edge-vectors of \(D_N\).

This also explains the triangle-free observation: a triangle in \(D_N\), with ordered vertices \(a<b<c\), would give
\[
(b-a)+(c-b)=c-a
\]
with all three quantities positive cubes, contradicting Fermat's theorem for exponent \(3\).

# 3. Exact determination for linear families

Let \(M_{\mathrm{lin}}(n)\) denote the maximum size of an admissible linear subspace of \(\mathbb F_2^n\).

## Proposition
\[
\boxed{
M_{\mathrm{lin}}(n)
=
2^{\,n-\lceil\log_2\chi(D_N)\rceil}.
}
\tag{4}
\]

## Proof: upper bound on a linear subspace

Let \(C\leq\mathbb F_2^n\) be admissible and have codimension \(d\). Consider the quotient map
\[
\pi:\mathbb F_2^n\longrightarrow \mathbb F_2^n/C\cong\mathbb F_2^d.
\]
Color vertex \(j\in V(D_N)\) by \(\pi(p_j)\).

If \(|i-j|\) is a cube, then \(p_i+p_j\) is a forbidden interval vector. It cannot belong to \(C\), since otherwise \(0\) and \(p_i+p_j\) would be two members of \(C\) with forbidden difference. Therefore
\[
\pi(p_i)\neq\pi(p_j).
\]
This is a proper coloring of \(D_N\) with at most \(2^d\) colors. Hence
\[
\chi(D_N)\leq 2^d,
\]
so
\[
d\geq \lceil\log_2\chi(D_N)\rceil.
\]

## Proof: construction from a coloring

Set
\[
d=\lceil\log_2\chi(D_N)\rceil.
\]
Take a proper coloring of \(D_N\), and assign its colors distinct labels
\[
c_0,c_1,\dots,c_n\in\mathbb F_2^d.
\]
Define a linear map \(T:\mathbb F_2^n\to\mathbb F_2^d\) by
\[
T(e_j)=c_{j-1}+c_j,\qquad 1\leq j\leq n.
\]
For an interval \([a,b]\),
\[
T(\mathbf 1_{[a,b]})
=\sum_{j=a}^b(c_{j-1}+c_j)
=c_{a-1}+c_b.
\]
If the interval length \(b-a+1\) is a cube, then \(a-1\) and \(b\) are adjacent in \(D_N\), so \(c_{a-1}\neq c_b\). Therefore
\[
T(\mathbf 1_{[a,b]})\neq0.
\]
It follows that \(\ker T\) is admissible. Its codimension is at most \(d\), while the preceding paragraph shows that every admissible linear subspace has codimension at least \(d\). Thus \(\ker T\) has codimension exactly \(d\), proving (4). \(\square\)

The same construction also gives
\[
M(n)\geq M_{\mathrm{lin}}(n).
\]

# 4. Fractional-chromatic and additive upper bounds

Because \(D_N\to\Gamma_n\), fractional chromatic number is monotone under graph homomorphisms:
\[
\chi_f(D_N)\leq\chi_f(\Gamma_n).
\]
The graph \(\Gamma_n\) is a Cayley graph and hence vertex-transitive. For every finite vertex-transitive graph \(G\),
\[
\chi_f(G)=\frac{|V(G)|}{\alpha(G)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\chi_f(D_N)
\leq \frac{2^n}{M(n)},
\]
which proves
\[
M(n)\leq \frac{2^n}{\chi_f(D_N)}.
\]

Since every independent set of \(D_N\) has size at most \(r_3(N)\), the defining linear program for fractional coloring gives
\[
\chi_f(D_N)\geq \frac{N}{r_3(N)}.
\]
Thus
\[
M(n)\leq \frac{r_3(N)}{N}\,2^n.
\]

There is also a direct averaging proof of the last inequality. Given
\(\mathcal F\subseteq\mathbb F_2^n\), define for each \(y\in\mathbb F_2^n\)
\[
J_y=\{j\in\{0,\dots,n\}:y+p_j\in\mathcal F\}.
\]
If \(i,j\in J_y\) and \(|i-j|\) is a cube, then the two corresponding members of \(\mathcal F\) differ by the forbidden interval \(p_i+p_j\). Hence \(J_y\) is independent in \(D_N\), so \(|J_y|\leq r_3(N)\). On the other hand,
\[
\sum_{y\in\mathbb F_2^n}|J_y|
=\sum_{j=0}^n\sum_y \mathbf 1_{\mathcal F}(y+p_j)
=N|\mathcal F|.
\]
Therefore
\[
N|\mathcal F|\leq 2^n r_3(N).
\]

# 5. A 7-adic coloring with \(O(N^{0.145142})\) colors

Choose
\[
t=\left\lceil\log_{343}N\right\rceil,
\]
so \(N\leq7^{3t}\). Write each \(x\in\{0,\dots,N-1\}\) in base \(7\):
\[
x=\sum_{\nu=0}^{3t-1}d_\nu(x)7^\nu.
\]
Retain only every third digit:
\[
\pi_t(x)=\bigl(d_0(x),d_3(x),d_6(x),\dots,d_{3t-3}(x)\bigr)
\in\mathbb Z_7^t.
\]

Define a graph \(L_t\) on \(\mathbb Z_7^t\) as follows. Two distinct strings are adjacent when, at their first differing coordinate read from low to high, the two digits differ by \(1\) or \(-1\) modulo \(7\).

The nonzero cubes modulo \(7\) are precisely
\[
\{1,-1\}.
\]

Suppose \(y-x=k^3\), and write \(s=v_7(k)\). Then
\[
v_7(y-x)=3s.
\]
Hence the base-\(7\) digits of \(x\) and \(y\) agree below position \(3s\), while at position \(3s\) their difference modulo \(7\) is
\[
\left(\frac{k}{7^s}\right)^3\equiv\pm1\pmod7.
\]
Thus \(\pi_t(x)\) and \(\pi_t(y)\) are adjacent in \(L_t\). Consequently,
\[
D_N\longrightarrow L_t,
\qquad
\chi(D_N)\leq\chi(L_t).
\tag{5}
\]

## Chromatic number of \(L_t\)

Let \(C_7\) be the 7-cycle. By construction,
\[
L_t=C_7[L_{t-1}],
\]
the lexicographic product, with \(L_0\) a single vertex.

For an integer \(h\), let \(\chi_h(C_7)\) be the \(h\)-fold chromatic number: each vertex receives \(h\) colors and adjacent vertices receive disjoint color sets. Then
\[
\chi_h(C_7)=\left\lceil\frac{7h}{3}\right\rceil.
\tag{6}
\]

Indeed, each color class has at most \(\alpha(C_7)=3\) vertices, so at least
\(\lceil7h/3\rceil\) colors are necessary. For the matching upper bound:

- \(C_7\) is ordinarily 3-colorable.
- The seven independent sets
  \[
  I_j=\{j,j+2,j+4\}\pmod7
  \]
  form a 3-fold coloring with seven colors.
- Omitting the disjoint sets \(I_0\) and \(I_1\) leaves five independent sets covering every vertex at least twice, giving a 2-fold coloring with five colors.
- Decomposing \(h=3q+r\), \(r\in\{0,1,2\}\), gives (6).

If \(h_{t-1}=\chi(L_{t-1})\), coloring the seven copies of \(L_{t-1}\) requires an \(h_{t-1}\)-fold coloring of the outer \(C_7\). Therefore
\[
h_0=1,\qquad
h_t=\left\lceil\frac73h_{t-1}\right\rceil.
\]
It follows inductively that
\[
h_t
\leq \left(\frac73\right)^t
+\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\left(\frac73\right)^j
<\frac74\left(\frac73\right)^t.
\]
Set
\[
\beta=\log_{343}\left(\frac73\right).
\]
Since \(t\leq\log_{343}N+1\),
\[
\left(\frac73\right)^t
\leq \frac73 N^\beta.
\]
Combining this with (5),
\[
\chi(D_N)
\leq h_t
\leq\frac{49}{12}N^\beta.
\]

Finally, by the exact linear formula (4),
\[
M(n)\geq
2^{n-\lceil\log_2\chi(D_N)\rceil}
\geq \frac{2^n}{2\chi(D_N)}
\geq
\frac6{49}\,\frac{2^n}{N^\beta}.
\]
This proves (3).

# 6. Small exact cases and a finite optimization route

Because \(1=1^3\), every pair differing in one coordinate is forbidden. Hence
\[
M(n)\leq2^{n-1}
\]
for every \(n\).

For \(1\leq n\leq7\), length \(1\) is the only available cube length. The even-weight vectors form an admissible family of size \(2^{n-1}\). Therefore
\[
\boxed{M(n)=2^{n-1}\qquad(1\leq n\leq7).}
\]

The linear problem is also explicit for the next range. If \(8\leq n\leq26\), then \(D_N\) has allowed distances \(1\) and \(8\). The vertices \(0,\dots,8\) induce a \(9\)-cycle, so \(\chi(D_N)\geq3\), while coloring \(j\) by \(j\bmod3\) is proper. Hence
\[
\chi(D_N)=3,\qquad
M_{\mathrm{lin}}(n)=2^{n-2}
\quad(8\leq n\leq26).
\]
This does not determine the unrestricted \(M(n)\) in that range.

More generally, for a prime \(p\), let
\[
R_p=\{u^3:u\in\mathbb Z_p^\times\}
\]
and let
\[
G_p=\operatorname{Cay}(\mathbb Z_p,R_p),\qquad a_p=\alpha(G_p).
\]
The same digit argument, together with the translates of a maximum independent set of \(G_p\), gives
\[
\chi(D_N)=O_p\!\left(
N^{\beta_p}
\right),
\qquad
\beta_p=\frac{\log(p/a_p)}{3\log p}.
\]
Thus finding a better finite pair \((p,a_p)\) would improve the exponent. The required finite computation is the explicit ILP
\[
\max\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z_p}z_x,
\qquad
z_x+z_{x+r}\leq1
\quad(x\in\mathbb Z_p,\ r\in R_p),
\qquad
z_x\in\{0,1\}.
\]
For \(p=7\), \(G_p=C_7\) and \(a_p=3\), yielding the exponent above. I have not established that \(p=7\) is optimal among such constructions.

# 7. Remaining gap

The argument gives:

- an exact characterization of the best linear family;
- a self-contained linear construction of size
  \[
  \Omega\!\left(2^n n^{-0.145142}\right);
  \]
- the general upper bound
  \[
  M(n)\leq 2^n r_3(n+1)/(n+1)=o(2^n).
  \]

It does not provide a quantitative upper bound comparable to the polynomial lower bound. In particular, it does not decide whether the true density is \(n^{-\Theta(1)}\), \(n^{-o(1)}\), or of another order. The exact asymptotic maximum therefore remains open.
