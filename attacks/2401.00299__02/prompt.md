2-subcube partition asymptotics in hypercube Q_d — Graph-theory open problems (arXiv)

 
 Status
 open
 high confidence
 

 Problem 1.9 asks to determine the asymptotic behaviour of f_2(d), the number of partitions of the d-dimensional hypercube Q_d into 2-dimensional subcubes, with the authors conjecturing the same asymptotic as Proposition 1.7 (for general r) specialised to r=2. The source paper provides an upper bound f_2(d) ≤ (d+1)^n (Proposition 1.6) and a related lower bound for f_{0,2}(d) (Proposition 1.10), but the precise asymptotic of f_2(d) remains open. No follow-up paper resolving this problem was found in a wide web search as of May 2026.

 Reviewer notes. No follow-up paper resolving Problem 1.9 was found. The related paper arXiv:2411.04479 (Potapov, November 2024) studies partitions of Z_q^n into large subcubes of dimension n-m (fixed m, growing n) and proves an asymptotic of n^((q^m-1)/(q-1)); this addresses a different regime (large-dimensional subcubes in a q-ary hypercube) rather than 2-dimensional subcubes of the binary hypercube, and does not appear to resolve the conjecture. The source paper was subsequently published in the Illinois Journal of Mathematics, vol. 69, issue 1 (2025), DOI 10.1215/00192082-11792788.

Problem. Determine the asymptotic behaviour of the number $f_{2}(d).$

Context

It is natural to ask what happens if only $2$-dimensional subcubes are allowed in a partition of $Q_d$. The authors believe the same asymptotic bound as given by Proposition 1.7 (for general $r$) holds with $r=2$.

 Partitioning the hypercube into smaller hypercubes
 Noga Alon, Jozsef Balogh, Vladimir N. Potapov · 2024-11-07
 https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00299

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:Denote by Q_d the d-dimensional hypercube. Addressing a recent question we estimate the number of ways the vertex set of Q_d can be partitioned into vertex disjoint smaller cubes. Among other results, we prove that the asymptotic order of this function is not much larger than the number of perfect matchings of Q_d. We also describe several new (and old) questions.
