 The open problem of characterizing (non-hereditary) convex drawings in terms of pseudocircular drawings remains unresolved. Arroyo, Richter, and Sunohara (arXiv:2001.06053) established that hereditary-convex drawings coincide precisely with pseudospherical drawings (pseudocircular drawings satisfying two additional properties), but the analogous characterization for general convex drawings has not appeared in the subsequent literature. No paper resolving this structural question was found across searches through May 2026.

Recent papers by subsets of the same authors (holes in convex drawings, separable drawings, plane Hamiltonian cycles) address related questions but do not resolve this one.

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:In this article we discuss classical theorems from Convex Geometry in the context of topological drawings and beyond. In a simple topological drawing of the complete graph $K_n$, any two edges share at most one point: either a common vertex or a point where they cross. Triangles of simple topological drawings can be viewed as convex sets. This gives a link to convex geometry.
As our main result, we present a generalization of Kirchberger's Theorem that is of purely combinatorial nature. It turned out that this classical theorem also applies to "generalized signotopes" - a combinatorial generalization of simple topological drawings, which we introduce and investigate in the course of this article. As indicated by the name they are a generalization of signotopes, a structure studied in the context of encodings for arrangements of pseudolines.
We also present a family of simple topological drawings with arbitrarily large Helly number, and a new proof of a topological generalization of Carathéodory's Theorem in the plane and discuss further classical theorems from Convex Geometry in the context of simple topological drawings.
