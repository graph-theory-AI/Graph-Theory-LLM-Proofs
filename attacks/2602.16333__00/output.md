## Statement

Let \(\operatorname{circ}(D)\) denote the maximum length of a directed cycle in \(D\), and let
\[
\operatorname{gap}(D)=|V(D)|-\operatorname{circ}(D).
\]

We prove the following stronger statement.

**Theorem.** For every integer \(m\ge 3\), there is a strongly connected, vertex-transitive oriented digraph \(\Gamma_m\) such that
\[
|V(\Gamma_m)|=12m
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\operatorname{circ}(\Gamma_m)\le 11m.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\operatorname{gap}(\Gamma_m)\ge m=\frac1{12}|V(\Gamma_m)|,
\]
so the conjecture holds with \(\varepsilon=1/12\).

## 1. Construction

Let \(H=A_4\), and, using the usual right-to-left convention for permutation multiplication, set
\[
a=(12)(34),\qquad b=(243).
\]
Then
\[
a^2=e,\qquad b^3=e,\qquad ba^{-1}=ba=(142),
\]
so \(ba^{-1}\) has order \(3\). Moreover,
\[
ab=(123),
\]
and \(a\) together with \((123)\) generates \(A_4\); hence
\[
\langle a,b\rangle=A_4.
\]

For \(m\ge3\), let
\[
G_m=A_4\times \mathbb Z_m
\]
and define the directed Cayley graph
\[
\Gamma_m=\operatorname{Cay}^{\rightarrow}
 \bigl(G_m,\{u,v\}\bigr),
\qquad
u=(a,\overline1),\quad v=(b,\overline1).
\]
Thus its arcs are
\[
(x,\overline i)\longrightarrow (xa,\overline{i+1})
\quad\text{and}\quad
(x,\overline i)\longrightarrow (xb,\overline{i+1}).
\]

Left multiplication by \(G_m\) acts transitively on the vertices, so \(\Gamma_m\) is vertex-transitive.

### Strong connectivity

We have
\[
u^2=(e,\overline2),\qquad v^3=(e,\overline3),
\]
and therefore
\[
v^3(u^2)^{-1}=(e,\overline1).
\]
It follows that the group generated by \(u,v\) contains
\[
u(e,\overline{-1})=(a,\overline0),
\qquad
v(e,\overline{-1})=(b,\overline0).
\]
Since \(a,b\) generate \(A_4\), the elements \(u,v\) generate all of
\(A_4\times\mathbb Z_m\). A generating set of a finite group also generates it as a semigroup, so the directed Cayley graph is strongly connected.

For \(m\ge3\), neither generator is the identity, and every inverse generator has second coordinate \(\overline{-1}\ne\overline1\). Thus the construction is in fact loopless and has no antiparallel pair of arcs.

## 2. The parity obstruction

We need the following property of the two possible transitions in the \(A_4\)-coordinate.

**Lemma.** If \(f:A_4\to A_4\) is a bijection satisfying
\[
f(x)\in\{xa,xb\}
\qquad\text{for every }x\in A_4,
\]
then \(f\), regarded as a permutation of the twelve elements of \(A_4\), is even.

**Proof.**
Let \(P\) be right multiplication by \(a\):
\[
P(x)=xa.
\]
Since \(a\) is an involution, \(P\) is the product of six disjoint transpositions, and is therefore even.

Put
\[
\psi=P^{-1}\circ f.
\]
For each \(x\in A_4\),
\[
\psi(x)\in\{x,xba^{-1}\}.
\]
Write \(d=ba^{-1}\). Since \(d\) has order \(3\), right multiplication by \(d\) partitions \(A_4\) into four orbits of size \(3\).

Consider one such orbit
\[
O=\{x,xd,xd^2\}.
\]
At every point \(y\in O\), the map \(\psi\) either fixes \(y\) or sends it to \(yd\). For this restriction to be bijective, all three choices must agree: either every point is fixed, or every point is advanced by \(d\). Indeed, any cyclic occurrence of “advance” followed by “fix” would give two points with the same image.

Thus, on each of the four \(d\)-orbits, \(\psi\) is either the identity or a \(3\)-cycle. Hence \(\psi\) is even. Since \(f=P\circ\psi\) and \(P\) is even, \(f\) is even. \(\square\)

## 3. There is no Hamilton cycle

Suppose for contradiction that \(\Gamma_m\) has a Hamilton directed cycle. Let \(\sigma\) be its successor permutation.

Partition the vertices into the layers
\[
L_i=A_4\times\{\overline i\},
\qquad i\in\mathbb Z_m.
\]
Every arc goes from \(L_i\) to \(L_{i+1}\). Consequently there are bijections
\[
f_i:A_4\to A_4
\]
such that
\[
\sigma(x,\overline i)
   =(f_i(x),\overline{i+1})
\]
and
\[
f_i(x)\in\{xa,xb\}.
\]
The maps \(f_i\) are bijections because \(\sigma\) is a bijection and all predecessors of \(L_{i+1}\) lie in \(L_i\).

By the lemma, every \(f_i\) is even. Therefore
\[
F=f_{m-1}\circ f_{m-2}\circ\cdots\circ f_0
\]
is even.

On the other hand, \(F\) is the action of \(\sigma^m\) on \(L_0\). If \(\sigma\) is a single cycle of length \(12m\), then sampling that cycle every \(m\) steps visits the twelve vertices of \(L_0\) in one cycle. Thus \(F\) must be a \(12\)-cycle. Every \(12\)-cycle is odd, a contradiction.

Hence \(\Gamma_m\) is not Hamiltonian.

## 4. Circumference bound

Projection onto the second coordinate maps every arc to
\[
\overline i\longrightarrow\overline{i+1}.
\]
Therefore every directed cycle in \(\Gamma_m\) has length divisible by \(m\).

The graph has \(12m\) vertices, and a directed cycle of length \(12m\) would be Hamiltonian, which has just been excluded. Hence every directed cycle has length at most
\[
11m.
\]
Thus
\[
\operatorname{circ}(\Gamma_m)\le11m
\]
and
\[
|V(\Gamma_m)|-\operatorname{circ}(\Gamma_m)
 \ge 12m-11m
 =m
 =\frac1{12}|V(\Gamma_m)|.
\]

Taking \(n=12m\) for \(m\ge3\) gives infinitely many orders, proving the conjecture with \(\varepsilon=1/12\).

## Gaps and status

There is no mathematical gap apparent in the argument: connectivity, vertex-transitivity, non-Hamiltonicity, and the circumference bound are all established directly. The only unresolved issue is bibliographic priority—this particular \(A_4\times\mathbb Z_m\) construction may conceivably already occur in literature not covered by the supplied review.
