## 1. Definitions and interpretation

Let \(G\) be a finite connected simple graph with at least two vertices. For distinct \(x,y\in V(G)\), its metric line is

\[
\overline{xy}_G=\left\{z\in V(G):
\begin{array}{l}
d(x,y)=d(x,z)+d(z,y),\ \text{or}\\
d(x,z)=d(x,y)+d(y,z),\ \text{or}\\
d(y,z)=d(y,x)+d(x,z)
\end{array}\right\}.
\]

Let \(\ell(G)\) be the number of distinct such sets, and let \(\operatorname{br}(G)\) be the number of graph-theoretic bridges. A counterexample is therefore a graph satisfying

\[
\ell(G)+\operatorname{br}(G)<|V(G)|.
\]

I interpret “obtained by replacing a bridge by a path” as closure under finitely many such replacements, equivalently arbitrary subdivision of bridge edges.

The main progress is a reduction showing that all genuine infinitude must already occur among bridgeless counterexamples.

## 2. Subdivision of a bridge preserves the number of lines

### Lemma 2.1

Let \(uv\) be a bridge of \(G\), and let \(G_r\) be obtained by replacing \(uv\) with

\[
u=p_0,p_1,\ldots,p_r=v,\qquad r\geq 1.
\]

Then

\[
\ell(G_r)=\ell(G).
\]

Consequently,

\[
|G_r|-\operatorname{br}(G_r)-\ell(G_r)
=
|G|-\operatorname{br}(G)-\ell(G).
\]

### Proof

Let \(A,B\) be the vertex sets of the components of \(G-uv\), with \(u\in A\) and \(v\in B\).

For triples of old vertices, changing the bridge length from \(1\) to \(r\) preserves collinearity. Indeed, if all three vertices lie on one side, no relevant distance changes. If two lie in \(A\) and one in \(B\), the two cross-distances both increase by \(r-1\), so either of the two possible “same-side vertex lies between” equalities is preserved. The vertex in \(B\) cannot lie between two vertices in \(A\), since that would require crossing the bridge twice. The other distribution is symmetric.

Thus a line of \(G_r\) generated by two old vertices restricts on \(V(G)\) to their line in \(G\).

Lines involving internal path vertices also give no new restrictions:

- If \(1\leq i<j\leq r-1\), then
  \[
  \overline{p_i p_j}_{G_r}=V(G_r),
  \]
  whose restriction is the universal line \(\overline{uv}_G=V(G)\).

- If \(x\in A\) and \(1\leq i\leq r-1\), then a direct distance check gives
  \[
  \overline{x p_i}_{G_r}\cap V(G)=\overline{xv}_G.
  \]
  Symmetrically, for \(y\in B\),
  \[
  \overline{p_i y}_{G_r}\cap V(G)=\overline{uy}_G.
  \]

Hence restriction to \(V(G)\) maps every line of \(G_r\) to a line of \(G\), and it is surjective because every old generating pair remains available.

It is also injective. Every line of \(G_r\) contains either all internal vertices \(p_1,\ldots,p_{r-1}\) or none of them. Moreover, it contains them exactly when its restriction meets both \(A\) and \(B\). Thus the old restriction uniquely determines the whole line.

Therefore restriction is a bijection between the line sets of \(G_r\) and \(G\), proving \(\ell(G_r)=\ell(G)\). Replacing one bridge by \(r\) bridges adds \(r-1\) vertices and \(r-1\) bridges, proving the second assertion. ∎

## 3. Contracting a bridge cannot increase the number of lines

This is the key reduction in the other direction.

### Lemma 3.1

Let \(e=uv\) be a bridge of \(G\), and let \(H=G/e\), where \(u,v\) are identified to a vertex \(w\). Then

\[
\ell(H)\leq \ell(G).
\]

### Proof

Again let \(A,B\) be the components of \(G-e\), with \(u\in A\) and \(v\in B\), and let

\[
\pi:V(G)\longrightarrow V(H)
\]

be the contraction map.

We show that every line \(L\) of \(H\) has a line \(L'\) of \(G\) satisfying

\[
\pi(L')=L.
\]

This gives an injection from the distinct lines of \(H\) into those of \(G\).

First suppose \(L=\overline{xy}_H\) with \(x,y\neq w\). Lift \(x,y\) to the corresponding vertices of \(G\). For a third vertex \(z\neq w\):

- if \(x,y,z\) are all on one side, all distances are unchanged;
- if the triple meets both sides, exactly two of the three distances increase by \(1\).

As in Lemma 2.1, all possible collinearity equalities are preserved. If \(x,y\) lie on the same side, \(w\in\overline{xy}_H\) precisely when the corresponding bridge endpoint lies in \(\overline{xy}_G\). If \(x,y\) lie on opposite sides, \(w\) lies between them in \(H\), while both \(u\) and \(v\) lie on every \(x\)-\(y\) geodesic in \(G\). Hence

\[
\pi\bigl(\overline{xy}_G\bigr)=\overline{xy}_H.
\]

Now suppose one generator is \(w\), say \(L=\overline{wy}_H\), where \(y\) lies on the \(A\)-side. Use the pair \(u,y\) in \(G\). On the \(A\)-side, the relevant distances are unchanged. Every vertex of \(B\) belongs to both lines: in \(H\), \(w\) lies between \(y\) and every vertex of \(B\), while in \(G\), \(u\) lies between them. Therefore

\[
\pi\bigl(\overline{uy}_G\bigr)=\overline{wy}_H.
\]

The \(B\)-side case is symmetric. Thus every line of \(H\) has a lift, and distinct lines of \(H\) have distinct lifts because contraction of the lift recovers the original line. ∎

### Corollary 3.2

Let \(C(G)\) be obtained by contracting all bridges of \(G\). Then

\[
|C(G)|=|G|-\operatorname{br}(G),\qquad
\operatorname{br}(C(G))=0,\qquad
\ell(C(G))\leq \ell(G).
\]

In particular, if \(G\) is a counterexample, then \(C(G)\) is a bridgeless counterexample.

### Proof

Contracting a bridge reduces both the vertex count and the bridge count by one. Every nonbridge lies on a cycle not using the contracted bridge and therefore remains a nonbridge. Repeating gives the first two assertions. Lemma 3.1 gives the line inequality. Thus, writing \(q=|G|-\operatorname{br}(G)\),

\[
\ell(C(G))\leq\ell(G)<q=|C(G)|.
\]

Hence \(C(G)\) is bridgeless and violates the inequality. ∎

Equivalently, the deficit

\[
\delta(G)=|G|-\operatorname{br}(G)-\ell(G)
\]

is unchanged by bridge subdivision and cannot decrease when bridges are contracted.

## 4. There are only finitely many reduced bridged counterexamples over a bounded bridgeless core

Call a vertex suppressible if it has degree two and both incident edges are bridges. Suppressing it replaces its two bridge edges by a single bridge. By Lemma 2.1 this does not change \(\ell(G)\) or \(|G|-\operatorname{br}(G)\).

A graph with no suppressible vertex will be called bridge-reduced.

### Proposition 4.1

Let \(R\) be a bridge-reduced counterexample, and put

\[
q=|R|-\operatorname{br}(R).
\]

Define

\[
\tau(q)=\left\lfloor\frac{1+\sqrt{8q-7}}2\right\rfloor .
\]

If \(R\) has at least one bridge, then

\[
|R|
\leq
q+2\left\lfloor\frac{q-1}{2}\right\rfloor+2\tau(q)-3.
\]

In particular, for fixed \(q\), there are only finitely many bridge-reduced counterexamples.

### Proof

Delete all bridges of \(R\). The resulting components form the vertices of a tree \(T\), with the bridges of \(R\) corresponding to the edges of \(T\).

Let \(R_1,\dots,R_r\) be the components containing at least one edge, with \(s_i=|R_i|\). All edges in such a component are nonbridges, hence lie on cycles. Since the graph is simple,

\[
s_i\geq 3.
\]

All remaining bridge-deletion components are singleton vertices. Since \(R\) is bridge-reduced, a singleton node of \(T\) has degree either \(1\) or at least \(3\), never degree \(2\).

Since \(T\) is a tree,

\[
q=|R|-\operatorname{br}(R)
  =1+\sum_{i=1}^r(s_i-1).
\]

Therefore

\[
r\leq \left\lfloor\frac{q-1}{2}\right\rfloor
\]

and the total number of vertices lying in the nontrivial components is

\[
\sum_{i=1}^r s_i=q-1+r.
\]

Let \(t\) be the number of singleton leaves of \(T\); these are precisely degree-one vertices of \(R\). For any two distinct such leaves \(a,b\),

\[
\overline{ab}_R
\]

contains no third degree-one vertex. Indeed, if a third leaf were in the line, one of the three leaves would be an internal vertex of a geodesic between the other two, which is impossible for a degree-one vertex. Hence lines generated by different unordered pairs of leaves are distinct:

\[
\binom{t}{2}\leq \ell(R).
\]

As \(R\) is a counterexample,

\[
\ell(R)\leq q-1,
\]

so \(t\leq\tau(q)\).

Let \(h\) be the number of singleton nodes of \(T\) having degree at least three. A tree has at most \(L-2\) vertices of degree at least three, where \(L\) is its number of leaves. Every leaf of \(T\) is either one of the \(t\) singleton leaves or one of the \(r\) nontrivial components. Thus

\[
h\leq t+r-2.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\begin{aligned}
|R|
 &= (q-1+r)+t+h\\
 &\leq(q-1+r)+t+(t+r-2)\\
 &=q+2r+2t-3\\
 &\leq q+2\left\lfloor\frac{q-1}{2}\right\rfloor+2\tau(q)-3.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is the asserted bound. ∎

## 5. Exact reformulation of the open question

### Theorem 5.1

Under the repeated-subdivision interpretation, the following are equivalent:

1. Every counterexample is obtained from one of finitely many graphs by subdividing bridges.
2. There are only finitely many bridgeless counterexamples.
3. The orders of bridgeless counterexamples are bounded.

### Proof

The equivalence of 2 and 3 follows because there are only finitely many finite simple graphs of bounded order.

If 1 holds, a bridgeless counterexample cannot arise by a nontrivial subdivision of a bridge: every edge of the replacement path remains a bridge. Thus every bridgeless counterexample must itself occur among the finite bases. Hence 2 holds.

Conversely, suppose all bridgeless counterexamples have order at most \(Q\). Let \(G\) be any counterexample, and contract all bridges. By Corollary 3.2, \(C(G)\) is a bridgeless counterexample, and hence

\[
q:=|G|-\operatorname{br}(G)=|C(G)|\leq Q.
\]

Suppress every degree-two vertex incident with two bridges, obtaining a bridge-reduced graph \(R\). Lemma 2.1 shows that \(R\) is still a counterexample and has the same value of \(q\). Proposition 4.1 bounds \(|R|\) solely in terms of \(Q\). Hence only finitely many isomorphism classes of such \(R\) exist. Every original \(G\) is obtained from one of them by subdividing bridges. ∎

Thus an explicit disproof of the catalog question can always be chosen as an unbounded family of **bridgeless** counterexamples. Complicated bridge trees cannot by themselves produce a negative answer while the contracted order remains bounded.

## 6. An exact special case: complete multipartite graphs

As a check and a genuine restricted result, all complete multipartite counterexamples can be classified.

### Proposition 6.1

Up to permutation of parts, the connected complete multipartite counterexamples are exactly

\[
K_{2,2},\qquad
K_{2,3},\qquad
K_{1,2,2},\qquad
K_{2,2,2},\qquad
K_{2,2,2,2}.
\]

They are all bridgeless.

### Proof

Let \(G=K_{s_1,\dots,s_k}\), \(k\geq2\), with parts \(P_1,\dots,P_k\).

If \(x\in P_i\), \(y\in P_j\), \(i\neq j\), then

\[
\overline{xy}=P_i\cup P_j.
\]

If \(x,y\in P_i\), then

\[
\overline{xy}=(V(G)\setminus P_i)\cup\{x,y\}.
\]

For \(k=2\), all cross-part pairs generate the universal line. A part of size two also generates that same universal line, while a part of size at least three contributes one distinct line for each pair. Thus

\[
\ell(G)=1+\sum_{i:s_i\geq3}\binom{s_i}{2}.
\]

If one part has size one, the graph is a star and \(\ell(G)+\operatorname{br}(G)\geq |G|\). If both parts have size at least two, the strict inequality occurs only for \((s_1,s_2)=(2,2)\) and \((2,3)\).

Now suppose \(k\geq3\). Lines from different unordered pairs of parts are distinct, and there are \(\binom{k}{2}\) of them. All size-two parts generate the same additional universal line. Hence

\[
\ell(G)=
\binom{k}{2}
+\mathbf 1_{\{\text{some }s_i=2\}}
+\sum_{i:s_i\geq3}\binom{s_i}{2}.
\]

Let \(a\) and \(b\) be the numbers of parts of sizes one and two. Then

\[
\ell(G)-|G|
=
\binom{k}{2}
+\mathbf 1_{\{b>0\}}
-a-2b
+\sum_{s_i\geq3}\left(\binom{s_i}{2}-s_i\right).
\]

The final sum is nonnegative. For \(k\geq5\),

\[
\binom{k}{2}\geq 2k\geq a+2b,
\]

so there is no counterexample. Direct enumeration for \(k=3,4\) gives respectively

\[
(1,2,2),\ (2,2,2),\quad\text{and}\quad(2,2,2,2).
\]

Their parameters are:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
G & |G| & \ell(G) & \operatorname{br}(G)\\ \hline
K_{2,2} &4&1&0\\
K_{2,3} &5&4&0\\
K_{1,2,2}&5&4&0\\
K_{2,2,2}&6&4&0\\
K_{2,2,2,2}&8&7&0
\end{array}
\]

as claimed. ∎

## 7. Another elementary special case: maximum degree at most two

For a path \(P_n\), every metric line is universal, so

\[
\ell(P_n)=1,\qquad \operatorname{br}(P_n)=n-1,
\]

and equality holds.

For an odd cycle \(C_{2m+1}\), the line generated by an edge is the whole cycle except for the unique vertex equidistant from its endpoints. The omitted vertices are different for the \(2m+1\) edges, yielding at least \(2m+1\) lines.

For \(C_{2m}\), \(m\geq3\), label the vertices cyclically. Then

\[
\overline{v_{i-1}v_{i+1}}
=
V(C_{2m})\setminus\{v_{i+m}\}.
\]

These \(2m\) lines are distinct. In \(C_4\), by contrast, every pair generates the universal line, so \(\ell(C_4)=1\).

Hence among connected graphs of maximum degree at most two, the only counterexample is \(C_4\).

## 8. Remaining gap

The reduction theorem isolates the unresolved assertion precisely:

> Is there a constant \(Q\) such that every bridgeless graph \(H\) with  
> \(\ell(H)<|H|\) has \(|H|\leq Q\)?

Nothing above proves such a bound. In particular, contracting bridges only gives the one-sided inequality

\[
\ell(C(G))\leq\ell(G);
\]

it does not provide a lower bound on the number of lines in an arbitrary bridgeless graph. Thus the original conjecture is neither proved nor disproved here.

The useful consequence is that searches for a counterexample to finite generation may be restricted entirely to bridgeless graphs: if the catalog conjecture is false, then there is an unbounded family of bridgeless counterexamples.
