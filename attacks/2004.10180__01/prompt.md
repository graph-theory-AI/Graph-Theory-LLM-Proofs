Polynomial bound for x₁+x₂+2x₃=x₄+3x₅ sets — Graph-theory open problems (arXiv)

 No follow-up work has been found that improves the bound from $o(\sqrt{n})$ to $n^{1/2-\varepsilon}$ for subsets of $[n]$ without a nontrivial solution to $x_1+x_2+2x_3=x_4+3x_5$. Prendiville (arXiv:2005.03484, 2020) gave an alternative proof of the $o(\sqrt{n})$ result of Conlon--Fox--Sudakov--Zhao with effective (quantitative) bounds and extensions to equations in more variables, but this work predates the 2021 publication and does not establish a polynomial gain. The open question of whether $n^{1/2-\varepsilon}$ holds for some $\varepsilon>0$ remains unresolved.

 Reviewer notes. Prendiville (arXiv:2005.03484, 2020, published Math. Proc. Cambridge Phil. Soc. 2022) provides an alternative proof of the o(sqrt(n)) result and effective bounds, but predates the paper's formal 2021 publication and does not address the polynomial improvement question. No papers after 2021 found that resolve or make progress on the n^{1/2-epsilon} question.

Informal. Can the bound $o(\sqrt{n})$ for subsets of $[n]$ without a nontrivial solution to $x_1+x_2+2x_3=x_4+3x_5$ be improved to $n^{1/2-\varepsilon}$ for some $\varepsilon>0$?

Context

Theorem 1.12 shows that every subset of $[n]$ without a nontrivial solution to $x_1+x_2+2x_3=x_4+3x_5$ has size $o(\sqrt{n})$, improving on the trivial Sidon-set upper bound of $(1+o(1))\sqrt{n}$. The authors explicitly identify as an open problem whether the bound can be further strengthened to $n^{1/2-\varepsilon}$.

 David Conlon, Jacob Fox, Benny Sudakov, Yufei Zhao · 2021-09-25
 https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.10180
 PDF source

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:We develop a sparse graph regularity method that applies to graphs with few 4-cycles, including new counting and removal lemmas for 5-cycles in such graphs. Some applications include:
* Every $n$-vertex graph with no 5-cycle can be made triangle-free by deleting $o(n^{3/2})$ edges.
* For $r \geq 3$, every $n$-vertex $r$-graph with girth greater than $5$ has $o(n^{3/2})$ edges.
* Every subset of $[n]$ without a nontrivial solution to the equation $x_1 + x_2 + 2x_3 = x_4 + 3x_5$ has size $o(\sqrt{n})$.
