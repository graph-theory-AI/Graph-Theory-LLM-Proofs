# 1. Definitions and results

For vertices \(x,y,z\) of a connected graph, write
\[
[xyz]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d(x,y)+d(y,z)=d(x,z).
\]
The metric line generated by \(x,y\) is
\[
\overline{xy}
 =\{x,y\}\cup\{z:[zxy]\text{ or }[xzy]\text{ or }[xyz]\}.
\]
Let \(\ell(G)\) be the number of distinct metric lines and \(\operatorname{br}(G)\) the number of graph-theoretic bridges.

I prove three partial results.

1. Subdividing a bridge preserves \(\ell(G)\) and increases both \(|G|\) and \(\operatorname{br}(G)\) by one. Consequently, a single counterexample having a bridge but no pendant edge would disprove the conjecture by producing infinitely many such counterexamples.

2. Every connected cactus graph \(G\neq C_4\) satisfies
   \[
   \ell(G)+\operatorname{br}(G)\ge |G|.
   \]
   Thus among cacti without pendant edges, \(C_4\) is the only exception.

3. Among connected complete multipartite graphs without pendant edges, the strict counterexamples are exactly
   \[
   K_{2,2},\quad K_{2,3},\quad K_{1,2,2},\quad
   K_{2,2,2},\quad K_{2,2,2,2}.
   \]

These results do not settle the conjecture for general graphs.

# 2. Subdivision of a bridge

## Lemma 2.1

Let \(ab\) be a bridge of \(G\), and let \(G'\) be obtained by replacing \(ab\) with the path
\[
a-w-b.
\]
Then
\[
\ell(G')=\ell(G),\qquad
\operatorname{br}(G')=\operatorname{br}(G)+1,\qquad
|G'|=|G|+1.
\]

### Proof

Let \(A,B\) be the components of \(G-ab\), with \(a\in A\) and \(b\in B\).

For a line \(L\) of \(G\), define
\[
\widehat L=
\begin{cases}
L\cup\{w\},&a,b\in L,\\
L,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

We claim that the lines of \(G'\) are exactly the sets \(\widehat L\), where \(L\) ranges over the lines of \(G\).

For a pair \(x,y\in A\), distances within \(A\) are unchanged. For \(z\in B\), the distances from \(z\) to both \(x\) and \(y\) increase by one, so the relevant distance differences, and hence collinearity with \(x,y\), are unchanged. Moreover, \(w\) has the same distances to vertices of \(A\) that \(b\) had in \(G\). Therefore
\[
\overline{xy}^{\,G'}=\widehat{\overline{xy}^{\,G}}.
\]
The analogous assertion holds for \(x,y\in B\).

If \(x\in A\) and \(y\in B\), then the distance \(d(x,y)\), as well as the distance from any vertex on one side to a vertex on the other side, increases by one. The collinearity equations on the old vertex set remain unchanged, while \(w\) lies between \(x\) and \(y\). Hence again
\[
\overline{xy}^{\,G'}=\widehat{\overline{xy}^{\,G}}.
\]

It remains to consider pairs involving \(w\). If \(x\in A\), direct substitution in the distance equations gives
\[
\overline{xw}^{\,G'}=\widehat{\overline{xb}^{\,G}},
\]
and if \(y\in B\), then
\[
\overline{wy}^{\,G'}=\widehat{\overline{ay}^{\,G}}.
\]
Thus no new line outside the family \(\{\widehat L\}\) arises. Since restriction to \(V(G)\) recovers \(L\), the map \(L\mapsto\widehat L\) is injective. Therefore \(\ell(G')=\ell(G)\).

The bridge \(ab\) is replaced by the two bridges \(aw\) and \(wb\), while bridge status of every other edge is unchanged. This proves the other two equalities. ∎

## Consequence

The deficit
\[
|G|-\ell(G)-\operatorname{br}(G)
\]
is invariant under subdivision of a bridge.

If \(G\) has no pendant edge, subdividing one of its bridges also creates no pendant edge: all old degrees are unchanged and the new vertex has degree two. Thus:

> The finite-exception conjecture implies that every connected graph with no pendant edge and at least one bridge must satisfy the inequality.

Indeed, one such counterexample would have infinitely many pairwise nonisomorphic bridge subdivisions.

# 3. Cactus graphs

A cactus is a connected graph in which every block is either an edge or a cycle. Equivalently, any two cycles have at most one common vertex.

For a cactus \(G\), let
\[
\sigma(G)=\sum_{C}(|C|-1),
\]
where the sum ranges over its cycle blocks.

## Theorem 3.1

If \(G\) is a connected cactus with at least two vertices and \(G\neq C_4\), then
\[
\ell(G)\ge 1+\sigma(G).
\]
Consequently,
\[
\ell(G)+\operatorname{br}(G)\ge |G|.
\]

### The order identity

Every bridge block adds one new vertex, while a cycle block \(C\) adds \(|C|-1\) new vertices when blocks are assembled along cutvertices. Hence
\[
|G|=1+\operatorname{br}(G)+\sigma(G).
\]
Thus the stated lower bound on \(\ell(G)\) immediately implies the desired inequality.

We first record the needed facts about cycles.

## Lemma 3.2: proper lines in cycles

For every \(m\neq4\), the cycle \(C_m\) has at least \(m\) distinct proper metric lines.

### Proof

If \(m=2k+1\) is odd, consider the \(m\) edges. For an edge \(uv\), every vertex except the unique vertex equidistant from \(u\) and \(v\) belongs to \(\overline{uv}\). Thus these edge-lines are the \(m\) distinct sets
\[
V(C_m)\setminus\{z\}.
\]

Now let \(m=2k\ge6\). For each vertex \(i\), take its two cyclic neighbors \(i-1,i+1\). They are at distance two. Every vertex other than the antipode \(i+k\) is collinear with this pair, while \(i+k\) has equal distance \(k-1\) from both endpoints and is not between them. Hence
\[
\overline{(i-1)(i+1)}
   =V(C_m)\setminus\{i+k\}.
\]
Again these are \(m\) distinct proper lines.

For \(C_3\), this is just the assertion that its three edges generate the three two-element lines. ∎

For \(C_4\), in contrast, every pair generates the universal line, so
\[
\ell(C_4)=1.
\]

## Lemma 3.3: attaching a leaf block

Suppose \(G=H\cup C\), where \(C\) is a cycle and
\[
V(H)\cap V(C)=\{r\},
\]
with no other edges between \(H\) and \(C\).

1. Lines generated by pairs in \(H\) remain distinct in \(G\).
2. If \(u,v\in H\), then \(\overline{uv}^{\,G}\cap(C-\{r\})\) is either all of \(C-\{r\}\) or empty.
3. Every proper line of \(C\) is therefore a new line of \(G\), distinct from all lines generated wholly in \(H\).

### Proof

The subgraph \(H\) is isometric in \(G\), so
\[
\overline{uv}^{\,G}\cap V(H)=\overline{uv}^{\,H}.
\]
This proves the first assertion.

For \(z\in C-\{r\}\),
\[
d(z,u)=d_C(z,r)+d_H(r,u),\qquad
d(z,v)=d_C(z,r)+d_H(r,v).
\]
In each possible external collinearity equation, the term \(d_C(z,r)\) either cancels or appears twice. The latter case would require
\[
d_H(u,r)+2d_C(z,r)+d_H(r,v)=d_H(u,v),
\]
which is impossible because \(d_H(u,v)\le d_H(u,r)+d_H(r,v)\). Hence membership of \(z\) is independent of \(z\): either every non-root vertex of \(C\) belongs to the line or none does.

A line generated within \(C\) has the same intersection with \(C\) as in the isolated cycle. Therefore a proper line of \(C\) cannot coincide with a line generated wholly in \(H\). ∎

## The special \(C_4\) attachment

Suppose a leaf \(C_4\) is attached to \(H\) at \(r\), and write it as
\[
r-p-q-s-r.
\]
If \(H\) has a vertex \(y\neq r\), then:

- \(\overline{py}\cap C_4=C_4-\{s\}\);
- \(\overline{sy}\cap C_4=C_4-\{p\}\);
- \(\overline{ps}=V(C_4)\).

Moreover, \(\overline{ps}\) contains no vertex of \(H-\{r\}\), since every such vertex is equidistant from \(p\) and \(s\). These are three distinct lines not generated wholly in \(H\). Thus attaching a leaf \(C_4\) creates at least three new lines, exactly the increment \(|C_4|-1\) needed in \(\sigma\).

## Proof of Theorem 3.1

Proceed by induction on the number of blocks.

If \(G\) has one block, then \(G=K_2\) or \(G=C_m\). The assertion is immediate for \(K_2\), and follows from Lemma 3.2 for \(C_m\neq C_4\).

Choose a leaf block and remove all of its vertices except its cutvertex, obtaining \(H\).

- If the leaf block is an edge, lines generated in \(H\) remain distinct, while \(\sigma\) is unchanged.
- If the leaf block is a cycle \(C_m\neq C_4\), Lemmas 3.2 and 3.3 give at least \(m\), hence at least \(m-1\), new lines.
- If the leaf block is \(C_4\) and \(H\neq K_1\), the preceding special argument gives three new lines.

These steps prove the induction unless \(H=C_4\), since the induction hypothesis is unavailable for that graph.

There are two exceptional extensions of \(H=C_4\).

### A pendant edge attached to \(C_4\)

Let the pendant vertex be \(y\), adjacent at \(r\), and write the square as \(r-p-q-s-r\). The four lines
\[
V(G),\qquad V(C_4),\qquad \overline{py},\qquad \overline{sy}
\]
are distinct; the last two have square traces \(C_4-\{s\}\) and \(C_4-\{p\}\). Hence
\[
\ell(G)\ge4=1+\sigma(G).
\]

### A cycle \(C_m\) attached to \(C_4\)

If \(m\neq4\), take the \(m\) proper cycle lines supplied by Lemma 3.2. Add:

- the line \(V(C_4)=\overline{ps}\);
- for any \(y\in C_m-\{r\}\), the two cross-lines \(\overline{py}\) and \(\overline{sy}\).

Their traces on the original square are respectively
\[
C_4,\qquad C_4-\{s\},\qquad C_4-\{p\},
\]
whereas a line generated wholly in the attached cycle has square trace either all of \(C_4\), only \(r\), or empty. The listed \(m+3\) lines are therefore distinct, and
\[
m+3=1+(4-1)+(m-1)=1+\sigma(G).
\]

If \(m=4\), each non-root vertex of either square gives one of three distinct rooted traces:
\[
C_4,\quad C_4-\{p\},\quad C_4-\{s\}.
\]
The nine cross-pairs obtained by choosing a non-root vertex in each square consequently give nine distinct lines. Since \(1+\sigma(G)=7\), this is more than enough.

The induction is complete. ∎

## Corollary 3.4

Every cactus graph without a pendant edge satisfies the conjectured inequality except \(C_4\). In particular, no cactus can provide the dangerous no-pendant counterexample with a bridge described in Section 2.

# 4. Complete multipartite graphs

Let
\[
G=K_{s_1,\ldots,s_k},\qquad k\ge2,
\]
with nonempty parts \(P_1,\ldots,P_k\).

For \(x\in P_i\), \(y\in P_j\), \(i\neq j\),
\[
\overline{xy}=P_i\cup P_j.
\]
Indeed, a vertex in either endpoint part is collinear with \(x,y\), while a vertex in a third part is adjacent to both endpoints and is not collinear with them.

For distinct \(x,y\in P_i\),
\[
\overline{xy}=(V(G)\setminus P_i)\cup\{x,y\}.
\]

Consequently, for \(k\ge3\),
\[
\ell(G)
 =\binom{k}{2}
  +\mathbf 1_{\{\exists i:s_i=2\}}
  +\sum_{i:s_i\ge3}\binom{s_i}{2}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
The indicator term accounts for the universal line generated inside every part of size two.

For \(k=2\),
\[
\ell(K_{p,q})
 =1+\mathbf 1_{\{p\ge3\}}\binom p2
    +\mathbf 1_{\{q\ge3\}}\binom q2.
\tag{4.2}
\]

## Proposition 4.1

The complete multipartite graphs without a pendant edge for which
\[
\ell(G)<|G|
\]
are exactly
\[
K_{2,2},\quad K_{2,3},\quad K_{1,2,2},\quad
K_{2,2,2},\quad K_{2,2,2,2}.
\]

### Proof

First suppose \(k\ge3\). For fixed \(k\), the expression \(\ell(G)-|G|\) is minimized when every part has size two. In that case
\[
\ell(G)-|G|
 =\binom{k}{2}+1-2k.
\]
This is nonnegative for \(k\ge5\), so only \(k=3,4\) need consideration.

For \(k=4\), the all-size-two graph has deficit one:
\[
\ell(K_{2,2,2,2})=7<8.
\]
Replacing any size-two part by a singleton raises \(\ell-|G|\) to zero, and increasing a part to size at least three raises it still further. Thus this is the sole \(k=4\) exception.

For \(k=3\), formula (4.1) gives strict inequality only for part-size vectors
\[
(2,2,1)\quad\text{and}\quad(2,2,2).
\]

Now let \(k=2\). Absence of pendant edges requires \(p,q\ge2\). Assuming \(p\le q\), formula (4.2) gives strict inequality exactly for
\[
(p,q)=(2,2),(2,3).
\]
This proves the classification. ∎

The corresponding values are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
G&|G|&\ell(G)\\ \hline
K_{2,2}&4&1\\
K_{2,3}&5&4\\
K_{1,2,2}&5&4\\
K_{2,2,2}&6&4\\
K_{2,2,2,2}&8&7
\end{array}
\]
and all five graphs are bridgeless.

# 5. Remaining gap

The subdivision lemma shows that the bridge case is especially sharp: the conjecture cannot tolerate even one no-pendant counterexample with a bridge. The cactus theorem rules out all constructions assembled from edge and cycle blocks, including arbitrarily long barbell graphs, chains of cycles, and trees of cycles.

What remains uncontrolled is the behavior of lines in blocks with substantially overlapping cycles. The complete multipartite calculation gives a finite list in one dense class, but does not extend to arbitrary 2-connected graphs or to arbitrary bridge components. No explicit infinite family violating the conjecture has been found here, so the global verdict is partial rather than proved or disproved.
