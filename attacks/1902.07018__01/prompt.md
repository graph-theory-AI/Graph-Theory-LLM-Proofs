List vs ordinary Ramsey number equality for Kᵣ — Graph-theory open problems (arXiv)

 The question of whether the 2-colour list Ramsey number $R_\ell(K_r, 2)$ equals the ordinary Ramsey number $R(K_r, 2)$ for $r > 3$ remains entirely open. The base case $R_\ell(K_3, 2) = 6 = R(K_3, 2)$ is verified, but the source paper itself states that for larger complete graphs the problem is unresolved. No follow-up work settling this equality question in either direction was found in the indexed literature through May 2026.

Informal. For $r > 3$ we cannot even decide the equality of the two Ramsey numbers of $K_r$ when $k = 2$.

Context

The classic probabilistic lower bound gives $2^{r/2} < R_\ell(K_r, 2) \leq R(K_r, 2) < 2^{2r}$. The equality $R_\ell(K_3, 2) = 6 = R(K_3, 2)$ is easily verified, but for larger complete graphs the question of whether the list and ordinary 2-colour Ramsey numbers coincide is entirely open.

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:We introduce the list colouring extension of classical Ramsey numbers. We investigate when the two Ramsey numbers are equal, and in general, how far apart they can be from each other. We find graph sequences where the two are equal and where they are far apart. For $\ell$-uniform cliques we prove that the list Ramsey number is bounded by an exponential function, while it is well-known that the Ramsey number is super-exponential for uniformity at least $3$. This is in great contrast to the graph case where we cannot even decide the question of equality for cliques.
