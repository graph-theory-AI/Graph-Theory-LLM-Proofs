## 2. Arbitrary finite abelian groups below doubling \(2\)

### Proposition 1

Let \(G\) be a finite abelian group of order \(n\), let \(A\subseteq G\) have \(|A|=s\), and let \(0<\delta\le 1\). If
\[
|A+A|\le (2-\delta)s,
\]
then \(A+A\) contains a coset of a subgroup of size at least \(\delta s\).

Moreover, for fixed \(G,s,\delta\), all such \(A\) are covered by a family
\[
\mathcal C_{\delta,s}
 =\{G\}\cup
 \{x+H:H\le G,\ \delta s\le |H|\le 2s,\ x\in G\}
\]
satisfying
\[
\min_{F\in\mathcal C_{\delta,s}}|F|\ge \delta s
\]
and
\[
\log |\mathcal C_{\delta,s}|
 =O\bigl((1+\log s)\log(2n)\bigr).
\]

### Proof

Put \(S=A+A\), and let
\[
H=\operatorname{Stab}(S)=\{g\in G:S+g=S\}.
\]
Write \(h=|H|\) and
\[
t=\frac{|A+H|}{h}.
\]
Kneser’s theorem, in the form
\[
|A+A|\ge 2|A+H|-|H|,
\]
gives
\[
|S|\ge (2t-1)h.
\]
Since \(A\subseteq A+H\), we also have \(s\le th\). Consequently,
\[
(2t-1)h
 \le |S|
 \le (2-\delta)s
 \le (2-\delta)th.
\]
After cancelling \(h\),
\[
2t-1\le (2-\delta)t,
\]
so
\[
\delta t\le 1.
\]
It follows that
\[
h\ge \frac{s}{t}\ge \delta s.
\]

The set \(S\) is \(H\)-periodic. Thus for every \(z\in S\), the whole coset \(z+H\) is contained in \(S\). Its size is \(h\ge\delta s\), while
\[
h\le |S|\le (2-\delta)s<2s.
\]
Hence \(z+H\in\mathcal C_{\delta,s}\).

It remains to count the family. Every subgroup \(H\) with \(|H|\le 2s\) can be generated by at most
\[
r=\left\lceil\log_2(2s)\right\rceil
\]
elements: in a minimal generating sequence, each new generator at least doubles the generated subgroup. Therefore there are at most \(n^r\) possibilities for \(H\), using padded ordered generating sequences. Each subgroup has at most \(n\) cosets. Hence
\[
|\mathcal C_{\delta,s}|\le 1+n^{r+1},
\]
which proves the asserted logarithmic bound. ∎

### Consequences

- Taking \(\delta=1\) gives the exact doubling-one case.
- Taking \(\delta=\tfrac12\) covers every \(A\) with
  \[
  |A+A|\le \frac32|A|
  \]
  by a family of logarithmic complexity \(O(\log s\log n)\), with members of size at least \(s/2\).
- More generally, if the intended \(\widetilde\Omega(s)\) permits a loss \(L(n,s)\), taking \(\delta=1/L(n,s)\) proves the natural level-zero assertion whenever
  \[
  |A+A|\le \left(2-\frac1{L(n,s)}\right)|A|.
  \]

The argument does not handle the narrow near-critical range where \(2-|A+A|/|A|\) is smaller than the permitted polylogarithmic loss.

## 3. The complete subcritical-doubling case in prime cyclic groups

The near-critical obstruction above can be handled in \(\mathbb Z_p\).

### Proposition 2

Let \(p\) be prime, \(G=\mathbb Z_p\), and \(A\subseteq G\) with \(|A|=s\). If
\[
|A+A|<2s,
\]
then \(A+A\) belongs to a family \(\mathcal P_{p,s}\) with
\[
|\mathcal P_{p,s}|\le 1+p(p-1)
\]
and every member of \(\mathcal P_{p,s}\) has size at least \(s\).

Thus
\[
\log|\mathcal P_{p,s}|=O(\log p),
\]
which is stronger than the schematic level-zero target.

### Proof

Define
\[
\mathcal P_{p,s}
 =\{\mathbb Z_p\}
 \cup
 \left\{
 \{x+jd:0\le j\le 2s-2\}:
 x\in\mathbb Z_p,\ d\ne0
 \right\},
\]
where the arithmetic-progression part is included only when \(2s-1<p\).

By Cauchy–Davenport,
\[
|A+A|\ge \min(p,2s-1).
\]

If \(2s-1\ge p\), then \(A+A=\mathbb Z_p\), which belongs to the family.

Suppose \(2s-1<p\). Since \(|A+A|<2s\) and cardinalities are integral,
\[
|A+A|\le 2s-1.
\]
Cauchy–Davenport therefore gives equality:
\[
|A+A|=2s-1.
\]
For \(s=1\), the conclusion is immediate. For \(s\ge2\), the strict inequality \(2s-1<p\) implies \(2s-1\le p-2\) except for the already trivial \(p=2\) case. Vosper’s theorem then shows that \(A\) is an arithmetic progression:
\[
A=\{a+jd:0\le j\le s-1\}
\]
for some \(d\ne0\). Consequently,
\[
A+A=\{2a+jd:0\le j\le 2s-2\},
\]
which is a member of \(\mathcal P_{p,s}\).

There are at most \(p(p-1)\) represented progressions, and all have size \(2s-1\ge s\); the full group also has size at least \(s\). ∎

In particular, if the intended conjecture has levels indexed so that \(K<2\) corresponds to \(\ell=0\), then its level-zero conclusion holds completely for prime cyclic groups.

## 4. A higher-level structured special case

There is also an elementary linear-complexity covering for sets that are themselves low-rank coset progressions.

### Proposition 3

Fix \(r\ge0\), \(q\ge1\), \(s\), and a finite abelian group \(G\) of order \(n\). Consider sets of the form
\[
P=x+H+
 \left\{
 \sum_{i=1}^{r}j_i d_i:
 0\le j_i<L_i
 \right\},
\]
with \(|P|=s\). There is a family \(\mathcal Q_{r,q,s}\) such that:

1. every \(F\in\mathcal Q_{r,q,s}\) has \(|F|\ge qs\);
2. whenever \(A=P\) has \(|A+A|\ge qs\), one has \(A+A\in\mathcal Q_{r,q,s}\);
3.
   \[
   \log |\mathcal Q_{r,q,s}|
   \le
   \bigl(\lceil\log_2s\rceil+2r+1\bigr)\log n.
   \]

### Proof

Let \(\mathcal Q_{r,q,s}\) consist of \(P+P\) over all parameter choices for which \(|P|=s\) and \(|P+P|\ge qs\).

Because \(P\) contains an \(H\)-coset, \(|H|\le s\); hence \(H\) can be generated by at most \(\lceil\log_2s\rceil\) elements. There are at most \(n^{\lceil\log_2s\rceil}\) choices for \(H\), at most \(n^{r+1}\) choices for \(x,d_1,\ldots,d_r\), and the lengths may be normalized to \(1\le L_i\le n\), giving at most \(n^r\) choices. The stated bound follows. ∎

Taking \(q=2^\ell\), this has the conjectured schematic complexity whenever the rank satisfies
\[
r=\widetilde O(2^\ell).
\]
The crucial limitation is that a general small-doubling set may merely be contained in a progression. Containment gives \(A+A\subseteq P+P\), whereas the conjecture needs a large set contained in \(A+A\); thus this observation cannot be promoted using an ordinary inverse theorem alone.

## 5. Remaining gap

These arguments leave untouched the principal problem:

- for arbitrary finite abelian groups near doubling \(2\), Kneser’s subgroup can be too small, while the prime cyclic argument uses the strong critical-pair conclusion supplied by Vosper;
- for levels \(\ell\ge1\), no argument here converts arbitrary small doubling into a contained set whose description uses only \(\widetilde O(2^\ell)\) parameters;
- consequently, there is no global improvement from the paper’s reported \(2^{2\ell}\) complexity to \(2^\ell\).

The propositions above are complete, but they are standard structural consequences and may already be subsumed by the source paper’s weaker theorem. A definitive assessment requires the exact text of Conjecture 15.
