# Mathematical writeup

## 1. Precise statement for matrix-represented oriented matroids

The phrase “given an oriented matroid” does not specify an input encoding. Consider the standard explicit realizable model:

\[
\textsc{Even-Directed-Circuit}_{\mathbb Q}:
\]

- **Input:** A rational matrix \(A\), whose columns represent an oriented matroid \(\vec M(A)\).
- **Question:** Does \(\ker A\) contain a support-minimal nonzero vector whose nonzero entries all have the same sign and whose support has even cardinality?

Such a support is precisely a directed circuit of \(\vec M(A)\).

### Theorem

\(\textsc{Even-Directed-Circuit}_{\mathbb Q}\) is NP-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions. Hardness already holds for full-row-rank integer matrices of the form

\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
I_N&I_N&-\mathbf 1\\
w^\top&0^\top&-b
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad N\ \text{odd}.
\]

On the instances constructed below, every directed circuit has size either \(N+1\) or \(N+2\), and the directed circuits of size \(N+1\) are in bijection with solutions of a SUBSET SUM instance.

---

## 2. Positive circuits and extreme rays

For a real matrix \(A\), define

\[
K(A)=\{z\in \mathbb R_{\ge 0}^{m}:Az=0\}.
\]

### Lemma 2.1

The directed circuits of \(\vec M(A)\), up to positive scaling, are exactly the extreme rays of \(K(A)\).

#### Proof

Let \(c\in K(A)\setminus\{0\}\), and put \(S=\operatorname{supp}(c)\).

Suppose first that \(S\) is a circuit. Then \(\ker A_S\) is one-dimensional and is generated by the full-support vector \(c_S\). If \(c=u+v\) with \(u,v\in K(A)\), nonnegativity and \(c_i=0\) outside \(S\) imply that \(u\) and \(v\) are both supported on \(S\). Hence \(u_S,v_S\in\ker A_S\), so both are nonnegative scalar multiples of \(c_S\). Thus \(c\) spans an extreme ray.

Conversely, suppose \(c\) spans an extreme ray. If \(\dim\ker A_S\ge 2\), choose \(d\in\ker A_S\) not proportional to \(c_S\). Since every coordinate of \(c_S\) is strictly positive, for sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\),

\[
c_S+\varepsilon d,\qquad c_S-\varepsilon d
\]

are both nonnegative. Extending them by zero outside \(S\) gives two nonproportional vectors of \(K(A)\) whose average is \(c\), contradicting extremality. Therefore \(\dim\ker A_S=1\). No proper subset of \(S\) can be dependent, since a dependence on a proper subset would give a second kernel vector not proportional to the full-support vector \(c_S\). Hence \(S\) is a circuit, and it is directed because \(c_S>0\). ∎

---

## 3. Membership in NP

A certificate is an even-cardinality subset \(S\) of the columns.

Using exact rational Gaussian elimination, one checks:

1. \(\operatorname{rank}(A_S)=|S|-1\);
2. the one-dimensional nullspace of \(A_S\) has a generator with every coordinate nonzero;
3. all coordinates of that generator have the same sign.

The full-support condition ensures minimal dependence. All calculations have polynomial bit complexity. Thus the problem belongs to NP.

---

## 4. Reduction from SUBSET SUM

Use the usual positive-integer version of SUBSET SUM:

\[
a_1,\ldots,a_n,b>0,\qquad
\text{does some }I\subseteq[n]\text{ satisfy }\sum_{i\in I}a_i=b?
\]

We may restrict to

\[
0<b<\sum_{i=1}^n a_i.
\]

Indeed, instances outside this range have an immediately known answer and can be mapped to fixed yes- or no-instances inside the range.

We first make the number of weights odd. If \(n\) is odd, let

\[
N=n,\qquad w_i=a_i.
\]

If \(n\) is even, append the dummy weight \(b+1\):

\[
N=n+1,\qquad
(w_1,\ldots,w_N)=(a_1,\ldots,a_n,b+1).
\]

Then \(N\) is odd, and the dummy weight cannot belong to any subset summing to \(b\). Thus

\[
\exists I\subseteq[n]\ \sum_{i\in I}a_i=b
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists x\in\{0,1\}^{N}\ \ w^\top x=b.
\]

Construct the \((N+1)\times(2N+1)\) integer matrix

\[
A(w,b)=
\begin{pmatrix}
I_N&I_N&-\mathbf 1_N\\
w^\top&0^\top&-b
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Order its columns as

\[
p_1,\ldots,p_N,q_1,\ldots,q_N,h.
\]

For a coefficient vector \(z=(x,y,t)\), the equation \(Az=0\) is

\[
x_i+y_i=t\quad (i=1,\ldots,N),
\qquad
w^\top x=bt.
\]

The matrix has full row rank: in a linear relation among its rows, inspection of the \(q_i\)-columns first forces all coefficients of the first \(N\) rows to vanish, and then any \(p_i\)-column forces the last coefficient to vanish.

### 4.1 The positive kernel as a homogenized sliced cube

Let

\[
K=\{(x,y,t)\ge 0:A(x,y,t)=0\}.
\]

If \(t=0\), then \(x_i+y_i=0\) for all \(i\), and hence \(x=y=0\). Thus every nonzero vector of \(K\) has \(t>0\). After scaling to \(t=1\), we obtain

\[
y_i=1-x_i,\qquad 0\le x_i\le 1,\qquad w^\top x=b.
\]

Therefore the section \(K\cap\{t=1\}\) is affinely isomorphic to

\[
P(w,b)=\{x\in[0,1]^N:w^\top x=b\}.
\]

This polytope is nonempty because \(0<b<\sum_iw_i\); for example,

\[
x_i=\frac{b}{\sum_jw_j}
\]

is feasible.

Since every nonzero vector in \(K\) has positive \(t\), the extreme rays of \(K\) correspond exactly to the vertices of \(P(w,b)\).

### 4.2 Vertices of the sliced cube

#### Lemma 4.1

A point \(x\in P(w,b)\) is a vertex if and only if at most one coordinate of \(x\) lies strictly between \(0\) and \(1\).

#### Proof

If two coordinates \(x_i,x_j\) are fractional, define a direction \(d\) by

\[
d_i=w_j,\qquad d_j=-w_i,\qquad d_k=0\quad(k\ne i,j).
\]

Then \(w^\top d=0\). For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), both \(x+\varepsilon d\) and \(x-\varepsilon d\) remain in \([0,1]^N\), so \(x\) is not a vertex.

Conversely, suppose at most one coordinate is fractional and

\[
x=\lambda u+(1-\lambda)v,\qquad 0<\lambda<1,\qquad u,v\in P(w,b).
\]

Every coordinate of \(x\) equal to \(0\) or \(1\) forces the corresponding coordinates of \(u\) and \(v\) to equal \(x_i\). If there is one remaining coordinate, the equality \(w^\top u=w^\top v=b\), together with \(w_i>0\), forces that coordinate to agree as well. Thus \(u=v=x\), proving that \(x\) is a vertex. ∎

### 4.3 Parity of the corresponding circuits

At a normalized point \((x,1-x,1)\):

- If \(x_i\in\{0,1\}\), exactly one of \(x_i\) and \(1-x_i\) is positive.
- If \(0<x_i<1\), both are positive.
- The coordinate \(t\) is always positive.

Hence an integral vertex has support size

\[
N+1,
\]

while a vertex with exactly one fractional coordinate has support size

\[
N+2.
\]

By Lemma 2.1 these supports are precisely the directed circuits. Since \(N\) is odd,

\[
N+1\ \text{is even},\qquad N+2\ \text{is odd}.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\vec M(A(w,b))\text{ has an even directed circuit}
\]

if and only if \(P(w,b)\) has an integral vertex, which is equivalent to

\[
\exists x\in\{0,1\}^{N}:w^\top x=b.
\]

The dummy weight \(b+1\), when present, cannot be selected, so this is equivalent to the original SUBSET SUM instance being positive. The matrix has polynomial dimensions and polynomial binary encoding length. This proves NP-hardness, and together with membership in NP proves the theorem. ∎

## 5. Small sanity check

Take the SUBSET SUM instance

\[
(a_1,a_2)=(2,3),\qquad b=3.
\]

Since there are two weights, append \(b+1=4\), giving \(w=(2,3,4)\) and \(N=3\). The integral solution \(x=(0,1,0)\) gives

\[
(x,y,t)=((0,1,0),(1,0,1),1).
\]

Its positive support is

\[
\{q_1,p_2,q_3,h\},
\]

of size \(4=N+1\), and indeed the corresponding four columns sum to zero. Thus they form an even directed circuit.

---

## 6. Scope and remaining gap

This gives a complete complexity classification for rationally represented, hence realizable, oriented matroids:

\[
\boxed{\text{matrix-represented even directed circuit is NP-complete}.}
\]

It follows that a polynomial-time algorithm in the matrix bit length would imply \(P=NP\). The reduction proves ordinary, not strong, NP-hardness.

It does not settle a representation-independent version of Problem 1.4:

- If all signed circuits are explicitly listed, the problem is solved by scanning the list.
- A signed-circuit or chirotope oracle is a different query model.
- Nonrealizable oriented matroids are not covered by rational matrices.
- The construction is not claimed to produce regular oriented matroids.

Thus the original wording remains encoding-dependent, but the catalog’s explicit rational-matrix interpretation has an NP-completeness obstruction. I have not verified whether this reduction or an equivalent result already appears in the literature, so I make no novelty claim.
