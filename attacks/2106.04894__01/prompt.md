In Theorem 1.1 we have $\Pr(X \in S) \leq C_S/\sqrt{n}$, for some $C_S$ depending only on $S$.

Context:
Theorem 1.1 establishes $\Pr(X \in S) \leq n^{-1/2+\alpha}$ for any $\alpha > 0$ when $S \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ is definable with respect to an o-minimal structure and contains no line segment; the conjecture posits that the optimal $O(1/\sqrt{n})$ bound from the classical Erd\H{o}s–Littlewood–Offord theorem also holds in this geometric setting. The authors prove it when $S$ satisfies the generic intersection property, which covers convex or irreducible algebraic plane curves in $\mathbb{R}^2$, irreducible complex algebraic curves, and the boundary of a 3-dimensional ball. === Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:The classical Erdős-Littlewood-Offord theorem says that for nonzero vectors $a_1,\dots,a_n\in \mathbb{R}^d$, any $x\in \mathbb{R}^d$, and uniformly random $(\xi_1,\dots,\xi_n)\in\{-1,1\}^n$, we have $\Pr(a_1\xi_1+\dots+a_n\xi_n=x)=O(n^{-1/2})$. In this paper we show that $\Pr(a_1\xi_1+\dots+a_n\xi_n\in S)\le n^{-1/2+o(1)}$ whenever $S$ is definable with respect to an o-minimal structure (for example, this holds when $S$ is any algebraic hypersurface), under the necessary condition that it does not contain a line segment. We also obtain an inverse theorem in this setting.
