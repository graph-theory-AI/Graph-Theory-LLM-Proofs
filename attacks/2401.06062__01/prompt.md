 Question 4.28 asks when $X_R$ is prime, where $X_R$ is the Cayley graph on a finite ring $R$ built from its group of units. The source paper itself reduces the question to the semisimple case (non-semisimple $R$ yields non-prime $X_R$) and further to a combinatorial question about primality of tensor products of complete graphs. A March 2024 follow-up (arXiv:2403.05635) establishes necessary and sufficient conditions for primality in the related $p$-unitary Cayley graph setting, partially addressing the broader question for certain classes of finite commutative rings, but the full general question for arbitrary finite rings appears to remain open.

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:The decomposition of complex networks into smaller, interconnected components is a central challenge in network theory with a wide range of potential applications. In this paper, we utilize tools from group theory and ring theory to study this problem when the network is a Cayley graph. In particular, we answer the following question: Which Cayley graphs are prime?
