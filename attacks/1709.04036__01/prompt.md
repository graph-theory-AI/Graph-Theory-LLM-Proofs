 Dvořák and Kelly prove $\alpha_2(G) \ge \frac{4}{5}n$ for triangle-free planar graphs and informally believe the same argument can be pushed to $\frac{5}{6}$, but they do not pursue it because it would not suffice to prove the full $\frac{7}{8}$ conjecture. No subsequent paper has been found that establishes the $\frac{5}{6}$ bound or otherwise resolves this informal belief; a 2025 survey of degenerate induced subgraphs in sparse graphs (arXiv:2511.13693) cites the $\frac{4}{5}$ result without improvement.

 Reviewer notes. The 5/6 claim is an informal belief stated in the paper, not a formally labelled conjecture. A 2025 paper (arXiv:2511.13693, 'A Note on Large Degenerate Induced Subgraphs in Sparse Graphs') cites the Dvořák–Kelly 4/5 result but does not improve upon it for the triangle-free planar case. The full 7/8 conjecture (Conjecture 1.1 of the source paper) also remains open.

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:A graph is $k$-degenerate if every subgraph has minimum degree at most $k$. We provide lower bounds on the size of a maximum induced 2-degenerate subgraph in a triangle-free planar graph. We denote the size of a maximum induced 2-degenerate subgraph of a graph $G$ by $\alpha_2(G)$. We prove that if $G$ is a connected triangle-free planar graph with $n$ vertices and $m$ edges, then $\alpha_2(G) \geq \frac{6n - m - 1}{5}$. By Euler's Formula, this implies $\alpha_2(G) \geq \frac{4}{5}n$. We also prove that if $G$ is a triangle-free planar graph on $n$ vertices with at most $n_3$ vertices of degree at most three, then $\alpha_2(G) \geq \frac{7}{8}n - 18 n_3$.
