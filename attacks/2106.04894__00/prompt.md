 Question 1 from arXiv:2106.04894 asks, for arbitrary nonzero vectors $a_i$, which geometric constraints on $S \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ yield non-trivial uniform upper bounds on $\Pr(X \in S)$. The source paper itself answers this for o-minimal sets (those definable in an o-minimal structure) not containing a line segment, giving a bound of $n^{-1/2+o(1)}$. A 2025 paper by Grebennikov and Kwan (arXiv:2505.24699) extends this programme substantially, proving that sets in convex position and algebraic varieties of prescribed dimension and degree also yield such bounds, thereby resolving Conjecture 10.1 of Fox-Kwan-Spink as well as a conjecture of Kwan-Sauermann; the general question of characterising all admissible geometric constraints remains an open research direction.

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:The classical Erdős-Littlewood-Offord theorem says that for nonzero vectors $a_1,\dots,a_n\in \mathbb{R}^d$, any $x\in \mathbb{R}^d$, and uniformly random $(\xi_1,\dots,\xi_n)\in\{-1,1\}^n$, we have $\Pr(a_1\xi_1+\dots+a_n\xi_n=x)=O(n^{-1/2})$. In this paper we show that $\Pr(a_1\xi_1+\dots+a_n\xi_n\in S)\le n^{-1/2+o(1)}$ whenever $S$ is definable with respect to an o-minimal structure (for example, this holds when $S$ is any algebraic hypersurface), under the necessary condition that it does not contain a line segment. We also obtain an inverse theorem in this setting.
