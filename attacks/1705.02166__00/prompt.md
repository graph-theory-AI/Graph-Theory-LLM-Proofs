Title: Question 1.1
For which subsets $K \subset E^n$ does every red/blue-coloring of $E^n$ contain a red pair of points of distance one or a blue isometric copy of $K$?

Context:
This is the central question studied throughout the paper. The notation $E^n \to (\ell_2, K)$ means every red/blue-coloring of $E^n$ contains a red copy of $\ell_2$ or a blue copy of $K$, and $E^n \not\to (\ell_2, K)$ means there is a coloring avoiding both. Euclidean Ramsey unit-distance vs isometric copy — Graph-theory open problems (arXiv)

 
 Status

 medium confidence
 

 Question 1.1 asks for which K every red/blue-coloring of E^n contains a red unit-distance pair or a blue isometric copy of K; the source paper resolves this for K = ℓ_m (m collinear unit-spaced points) up to the constant in the exponent 2^(cn). Conlon–Wu (2022) extends the analysis to asymmetric variants involving ℓ_3 vs. ℓ_m colorings, and Führer–Tóth (2024) sharpens those explicit bounds from m = 10^50 down to m = 1177. The question for general K beyond collinear configurations remains open.

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:Let $\ell_m$ be a sequence of $m$ points on a line with consecutive points of distance one. For every natural number $n$, we prove the existence of a red/blue-coloring of $\mathbb{E}^n$ containing no red copy of $\ell_2$ and no blue copy of $\ell_m$ for any $m \geq 2^{cn}$. This is best possible up to the constant $c$ in the exponent. It also answers a question of Erdős, Graham, Montgomery, Rothschild, Spencer and Straus from 1973. They asked if, for every natural number $n$, there is a set $K \subset \mathbb{E}^1$ and a red/blue-coloring of $\mathbb{E}^n$ containing no red copy of $\ell_2$ and no blue copy of $K$.
