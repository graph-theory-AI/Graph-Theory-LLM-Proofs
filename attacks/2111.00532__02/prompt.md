 The question of which graphs H have the strong transversal property — i.e., for which H there exists epsilon > 0 guaranteeing a B-transversal copy of H in every graph with an (epsilon W, epsilon W)-cohesive blockade of local degree less than epsilon W — is posed as an open problem in Section 2 of the source paper. The paper establishes that H must be a forest, proves the property for paths and certain caterpillar-like trees, and shows it fails for trees containing a vertex of degree d with 2^{d-1} >= |H|. No complete characterization has been found, and no follow-up paper resolving or substantially extending this question was found in five targeted web searches covering arXiv and the published literature through May 2026.

 Reviewer notes. Two superficially related arXiv papers were checked: arXiv:2305.05713 studies density thresholds for connected/Hamiltonian transversals in multipartite graphs (a different setting, not blockades/local-degree conditions), and arXiv:2310.06354 studies rainbow transversals in families of trees — neither addresses the strong transversal property as defined in 2111.00532. The source paper was published in SIAM J. Discrete Math. 38(1), 2024, pp. 645–667. No follow-up found.

Informal. Which graphs $H$ have the strong transversal property, i.e., for which $H$ does there exist $\varepsilon > 0$ such that every graph $G$ with an $(\varepsilon W, \varepsilon W)$-cohesive blockade $\mathcal{B} = (B_1, \ldots, B_{|H|})$ of local degree less than $\varepsilon W$ contain a $\mathcal{B}$-transversal copy of $H$?

Context

The question opens Section 2. The authors note that $H$ must be a forest, and give partial results in Theorem 2.1: paths have the property; certain caterpillar-like trees do; trees with a vertex of degree $d$ satisfying $2^{d-1} \geq |H|$ do not. No complete characterization is given, and the converse of the component-wise criterion fails.

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:Fix k>0, and let G be a graph, with vertex set partitioned into k subsets (`blocks') of approximately equal size. An induced subgraph of G is transversal (with respect to this partition) if it has exactly one vertex in each block (and therefore it has exactly k vertices). A pure pair in G is a pair X,Y of disjoint subsets of V(G) such that either all edges between X,Y are present or none are; and in the present context we are interested in pure pairs (X,Y) where each of X,Y is a subset of one of the blocks, and not the same block. This paper collects several results and open questions concerning how large a pure pair must be present if various types of transversal subgraphs are excluded.
