Title: Conjecture 4
For every integer $k\geq 3$, there exists a set $X$ of vertices and a set $C$ of $k$-colorings of $X$ such that no $X$-rooted-$K_{k}$-minor-free graph $G$ realizes $C$.

Context:
The conjecture asserts that Theorem 3 (the generalization of the DeVos–Seymour result to $X$-rooted-$K_{k+1}$-minor-free $K_{k+2}$-minor-free graphs) does not further extend to $X$-rooted-$K_{k}$-minor-free graphs, i.e., reducible configurations cannot exploit the full global structure when the number of colors falls below what Hadwiger's conjecture postulates. Holroyd's conjecture — that if $G$ is $k$-colorable and every $k$-coloring uses all $k$ colors on $X$ then $K_k$ is an $X$-rooted minor of $G$ — would imply this conjecture if true in general.

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:DeVos and Seymour (2003) proved that for every set $C$ of 3-colorings of a set $X$ of vertices, there exists a plane graph $G$ with vertices of $X$ incident with the outer face such that a 3-coloring of $X$ extends to a 3-coloring of $G$ if and only if it belongs to $C$. We prove a generalization of this claim for $k$-colorings of $X$-rooted-$K_{k+1}$-minor-free $K_{k+2}$-minor-free graphs.
