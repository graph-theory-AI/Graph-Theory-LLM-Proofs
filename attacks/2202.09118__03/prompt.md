Title: Open problem (disjoint union of good trees)
It is not known that if trees $H_1$, $H_2$ are good (i.e., the class of $H_i$-free graphs is polynomially $\chi$-bounded for $i=1,2$), then the disjoint union of $H_1$ and $H_2$ is also good.

Context:
Trees of diameter three are good [14], but whether disjoint unions of diameter-three trees are good is unknown. Self-isolation provides a sufficient condition: if $H_1$ and $H_2$ are good forests and one of them is self-isolating, their disjoint union is good.

Closure of good trees under disjoint union — Graph-theory open problems (arXiv)

 The general question of whether the disjoint union of two good trees is always good remains open as of 2026. Partial progress exists: arXiv:2202.10412 (submitted three days after the source paper) proves that if $H$ is a good forest then $H \cup P_4$ is also good, and the source paper itself notes that the disjoint union of two good forests is good whenever one is self-isolating. No paper resolving the full conjecture was found in the indexed literature.

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:A hole in a graph $G$ is an induced cycle of length at least four, and a $k$-multihole in $G$ is a set of pairwise disjoint and nonadjacent holes. It is well known that if $G$ does not contain any holes then its chromatic number is equal to its clique number. In this paper we show that, for any $k$, if $G$ does not contain a $k$-multihole, then its chromatic number is at most a polynomial function of its clique number. We show that the same result holds if we ask for all the holes to be odd or of length four; and if we ask for the holes to be longer than any fixed constant or of length four. This is part of a broader study of graph classes that are polynomially $\chi$-bounded.
