Maximum symmetric intersecting family in [k]^n — Graph-theory open problems (arXiv)

 Eberhard, Kahn, Narayanan, and Spirkl (arXiv:1909.11578) proved that any symmetric intersecting $A \subset [k]^n$ has size $o(k^n)$ for fixed $k \geq 3$, but the precise asymptotics remain open. The best known constructions, based on set-intersecting families derived from projective planes $PG(2,q)$, achieve size roughly $k^{n-\sqrt{n}}$. No matching upper bound or improvement to the construction is known, and no follow-up resolving the question was found in the indexed literature.

 Reviewer notes. No follow-up resolving the precise asymptotics of the maximum size of a symmetric intersecting family in $[k]^n$ was found. The gap between the known upper bound $o(k^n)$ and the best construction $k^{n-\sqrt{n}}$ remains wide. The Keevash 2023 paper on 'forbidden intersections for codes' appeared in search results but was behind a paywall; it may or may not address this specific question.

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:A family of vectors $A \subset [k]^n$ is said to be intersecting if any two elements of $A$ agree on at least one coordinate. We prove, for fixed $k \ge 3$, that the size of a symmetric intersecting subfamily of $[k]^n$ is $o(k^n)$, which is in stark contrast to the case of the Boolean hypercube (where $k =2$). Our main contribution addresses limitations of existing technology: while there is now some spectral machinery, developed by Ellis and the third author, to tackle extremal problems in set theory involving symmetry, this machinery relies crucially on the interplay between up-sets and biased product measures on the Boolean hypercube, features that are notably absent in the problem at hand; here, we describe a method for circumventing these barriers.
