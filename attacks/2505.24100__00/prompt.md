 Question 1.7 from arXiv:2505.24100 asks whether the edge-addition half of the induced saturation condition can be satisfied for even cycles $C_{2t-2}$ with $t \geq 6$: specifically, whether there exists a graph $G$ that is $C_{2t-2}$-free but such that adding any non-edge creates an induced $C_{2t-2}$. The paper proves Theorem 1.6 (the edge-deletion half) and notes that small cases ($C_4, C_6, C_8, C_{10}$) have known induced-saturated graphs (icosahedron, Cartesian product of two 5-cycles, dodecahedron, and a 28-vertex computer-found graph), but the full edge-addition problem for $t \geq 6$ ($C_{10}$ and beyond) remains wide open. No follow-up resolving this question was found in a wide literature search.

 Reviewer notes. Paper submitted May 30 / revised June 2, 2025. The conjecture is less than one year old; absence of follow-up in the indexed literature is expected and consistent with open status. The edge-deletion half (Theorem 1.6) is the main result of the source paper; the edge-addition half (Question 1.7) is explicitly left open. Known induced-saturated examples exist only for small even cycles (C_4 through C_10), and the paper states 'it remains wide open whether induced-saturated graphs exist for even cycles on 12 or more vertices.'

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:For graphs $G$ and $H$, we say that $G$ is $H$-free if no induced subgraph of $G$ is isomorphic to $H$, and that $G$ is $H$-induced-saturated if $G$ is $H$-free but removing or adding any edge in $G$ creates an induced copy of $H$. A full characterization of graphs $H$ for which $H$-induced-saturated graphs exist remains elusive. Even the case where $H$ is a path -- now settled by the collective results of Martin and Smith, Bonamy et al., and Dvoŕǎk -- was already quite challenging.
What if $H$ is a cycle? The complete answer for odd cycles was given by Behren et al., leaving the case of even cycles (except for the $4$-cycle) wide open. Our main result is the first step toward closing this gap: We prove that for every even cycle $H$, there is a graph $G$ with at least one edge such that $G$ is $H$-free but removing any edge from $G$ creates an induced copy of $H$ (in fact, we construct $H$-induced-saturated graphs for every even cycle $H$ on at most 10 vertices).
