Local limit of random wooded triangulations — Graph-theory open problems (arXiv)

 
 Status

 The conjecture, posed in the November 2025 paper arXiv:2511.07601, asks whether uniformly random wooded triangulations (triangulations paired with an arbitrary Schnyder wood) possess a local limit as the boundary length tends to infinity, and if so, whether it differs from the maximal-Schnyder-wood half-plane triangulation constructed in the same work. The paper notes that tree heights in uniformly random wooded triangulations are Theta(sqrt(n)), whereas the maximal-Schnyder-wood setting yields order n^{3/4}, indicating a fundamentally different combinatorial regime. No follow-up work addressing this question was found in the literature as of May 2026.

Informal. It would be interesting to understand the local limit of uniformly random wooded triangulations as the boundary length tends to infinity, and whether such wooded triangulations have a local limit, which would be a random wooded triangulation of the half-plane different from the one constructed in this work.

Context

The authors remark that uniformly random wooded triangulations (triangulations paired with an arbitrary Schnyder wood) are quite different from maximal Schnyder woods of uniformly random triangulations: tree heights in the former are $\Theta(\sqrt{n})$ in probability, while the expected height of a uniformly random vertex in the latter is of order $n^{3/4}$. Whether these wooded triangulations possess a local limit as the boundary length grows is left open.

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:It is well-known that any finite triangulation possesses a unique maximal Schnyder wood. We introduce Schnyder woods of infinite triangulations, and prove there exists a unique maximal Schnyder wood of any infinite triangulation with finite boundary, and of the uniform infinite half-planar triangulation. Furthermore, the maximal Schnyder wood of the uniform infinite planar triangulation is the limit of maximal Schnyder woods of large finite random triangulations. Several structural properties of infinite Schnyder woods are also described.
