 The question asks whether, for all fixed $s$ and $\kappa$, every graph $G$ with $\omega(G)\leq\kappa$ and sufficiently large $\chi(G)$ must contain, in every colouring, a hole with some $s$ consecutive rainbow vertices. The source paper (1702.01094) shows that the stronger property — a fully rainbow hole, or even one in which every three consecutive vertices are rainbow — fails via a shift-graph construction, leaving the weaker $s$-consecutive variant open. A 2026 preprint (arXiv:2601.00602) improves quantitative bounds on induced rainbow paths in triangle-free graphs but does not address holes. No resolution of the hole question was found in the indexed literature.

=== Source paper abstract / header ===
Abstract:We prove that for all nonnegative integers k,s there exists c with the following property. Let G be a graph with clique number at most k and chromatic number more than c. Then for every vertex-colouring (not necessarily optimal) of G, some induced subgraph of G is an s-vertex path, and all its vertices have different colours. This extends a recent result of Gyarfas and Sarkozy, who proved the same (when k=2) for graphs G with girth at least five.
