# Referee report: 1907.06019__00

## Interpretation

The catalog question (Scott-Wilmer, arXiv:1907.06019, prose after Theorem 2.3) asks:
for $r < n/2$, is every self-annihilating subspace $W \subseteq \bigwedge^r V$
($V = \mathbb{R}^n$) with $\dim W = \binom{n-1}{r-1}$ of the form
$v \wedge \bigwedge^{r-1} V$?

I fetched the source paper (Scott's copy at people.maths.ox.ac.uk, identical content
to arXiv:1907.06019v2) and confirmed against the actual text:

- **Definition matches.** The paper defines $W$ self-annihilating iff $v \wedge w = 0$
  for all $v, w \in W$ (Section 2.2, p. 4) — exactly the writeup's hypothesis.
- **Theorem 2.3 matches.** "Let $V=\mathbb{R}^n$ and let $W$ be a self-annihilating
  subspace of $\bigwedge V$. Then $\dim W \le 2^{n-1}$. Furthermore, if
  $W \subseteq \bigwedge^r V$, where $r \le n/2$, then $\dim W \le \binom{n-1}{r-1}$."
- **Question matches.** On p. 5 the paper says: "For $r < n/2$, the extremal cases in
  Theorem 2.2 have a nice characterization... It is an interesting question to
  describe the extremal examples for Theorem 2.3. This is trivially true for $r=1$;
  it is also true for $r=2$... Could all extremal examples be of this form?" The
  paper itself frames the characterization question in the strict range $r < n/2$,
  which is exactly the range the writeup proves. The writeup's Section 4 additionally
  exhibits the (correct, and known) boundary failure $W = \bigwedge^r H$ at $n = 2r$.

The formalization is the intended one; a reasonable author of the original paper
would consider the question resolved by the writeup's statement. No interpretation
gaming. The field is $\mathbb{R}$, matching the paper's setting.

## Step-by-step findings

I numbered every claim and re-derived each in full. $k = r-1$, $U = \mathrm{span}(e_1,\dots,e_{n-1})$, $m = \dim U$.

| step | label | note |
|---|---|---|
| S1. Echelon setup; $\lvert\mathcal F\rvert = \dim W$ | VALID | Binary weights $\omega(A)=\sum_{i\in A}2^i$ are injective on subsets; row echelon form in decreasing-weight order gives distinct leading (max-weight) supports, one per basis vector. |
| S2. $\mathcal F$ is intersecting | VALID | If $A,B\in\mathcal F$ disjoint: any pair $(P,Q)$ from the supports of $w_A, w_B$ contributing to $e_{A\cup B}$ has $P\cap Q=\emptyset$, $P\cup Q=A\cup B$, so $\omega(P)+\omega(Q)=\omega(A)+\omega(B)$; echelon gives $\omega(P)\le\omega(A)$, $\omega(Q)\le\omega(B)$ with equality iff $P=A$, $Q=B$. So the $e_{A\cup B}$-coefficient of $w_A\wedge w_B$ is $\pm\alpha_A\beta_B\neq 0$, contradicting $W\wedge W=0$. (This mirrors Scott-Wilmer's own proof of Theorem 2.3, which shows the initial hypergraph is intersecting.) Verified computationally on scrambled stars (check D). |
| S3. EKR equality case: $\mathcal F$ a full star | VALID | Classical: for $n > 2r$ an intersecting $r$-uniform family of size $\binom{n-1}{r-1}$ is a star (follows from Hilton-Milner). $r<n/2 \iff n>2r$ for integers. The uniqueness statement is quoted verbatim in BGW's Theorem 1.1 ("Moreover, if $k<n/2$ and $\lvert\mathcal F\rvert$ achieves the upper bound, then $\mathcal F$ consists of all the $k$-subsets containing some fixed element"). |
| S4. Graph form (1): $W=\{e_0\wedge x+f(x)\}$ | VALID | Pivot columns are exactly the $\binom{n-1}{r-1}$ star monomials; the pivot submatrix of the echelon basis is triangular with nonzero diagonal, so the coordinate projection $W\to E$ is injective, hence an isomorphism of equal-dimensional spaces. The non-star monomials span $\bigwedge^{k+1}U$, giving the linear map $f$. (Relabelling for the star center does not disturb this: only invertibility of the pivot submatrix is used, not the ordering.) |
| S5. Rigidity lemma statement ($m\ge 2k+2$, $k\ge1$) | VALID | Statement consistent; hypothesis is exactly what is available ($m=n-1\ge 2r=2k+2$). |
| S6. Claim (4): $c_{J,L}\neq0\Rightarrow L\subseteq J$ | VALID | With $q\in L\setminus J$, a $k$-set $I\subseteq[m]\setminus(J\cup\{q\})$ exists since $m\ge 2k+2$. Re-derived; correct. |
| S7. Form (5) | VALID | Immediate from S6: $J\supseteq L$, $\lvert J\rvert=k+1$ means $J=L\cup\{a\}$, and $e_J=\pm e_a\wedge e_L$. |
| S8. Claim (6): $\mu_{a,L}=\mu_{a,I}$ for disjoint $I,L$ avoiding $a$ | VALID | The sign identity $e_L\wedge e_a\wedge e_I=(-1)^k e_I\wedge e_a\wedge e_L$ is correct: both sides equal $e_a\wedge e_I\wedge e_L$ up to the stated sign ($(-1)^{k+k^2}=1$). Comparing the coefficient of support $I\cup L\cup\{a\}$ in (2) gives exactly $\mu_{a,L}=\mu_{a,I}$. |
| S9. Connectivity of the disjointness (Kneser) graph on $k$-subsets of $[m]\setminus\{a\}$ | VALID | Johnson graph is connected; a Johnson edge $A\sim B$ has $\lvert A\cup B\rvert=k+1$, and a common disjoint neighbor $C$ exists since $(m-1)-(k+1)\ge k$, i.e., $m\ge2k+2$. Hence all $\mu_{a,L}$ equal. |
| S10. $f(x)=a_0\wedge x$, uniqueness of $a_0$ | VALID | $e_a\wedge e_L=0$ for $a\in L$ closes the sum; uniqueness since the $\mu_a$ are read off coefficients ($b\wedge e_L=0$ for all $k$-sets $L$ forces $b=0$ as $m-1\ge k$). |
| S11. Direct sum $\bigwedge^{2k+2}V=e_0\wedge\bigwedge^{2k+1}U\oplus\bigwedge^{2k+2}U$ is correct ($2k+1\le n-1$), so both components vanish, giving (2). Verified exactly by computer for $(n,r)\in\{(5,2),(7,3),(9,4)\}$ (check A). Note the argument correctly only needs the first component; $f=a\wedge\cdot$ then automatically kills $f(x)\wedge f(y)$. |
| S12. Lemma applies: $\dim U=n-1\ge2k+2$ | VALID | $n\ge 2r+1$ from $r<n/2$; $2k+2=2r$. Also $k\ge1$ since $r\ge2$ here ($r=1$ handled in S14). |
| S13. $W=(e_0+a)\wedge\bigwedge^kU=v\wedge\bigwedge^{r-1}V$ | VALID | $V=\mathbb{R}v\oplus U$ and the $v$-component of any $z\in\bigwedge^kV$ dies under $v\wedge\cdot$, so $v\wedge\bigwedge^kV=v\wedge\bigwedge^kU$. |
| S14. $r=1$ case | VALID | $\dim W=\binom{n-1}{0}=1$, and every line in $V$ is of the required form. |
| S15. Uniqueness of the line $\mathbb{R}v$ | VALID | If $u,v$ independent with $u\wedge W=v\wedge W=0$, extend $\{u,v\}$ by $r-1$ vectors ($n\ge r+2$) to get $u\wedge v\wedge z\neq0$ with $v\wedge z\in W$, contradiction. |
| S16. Boundary counterexample $n=2r$: $W=\bigwedge^rH$ | VALID | $\binom{2r-1}{r}=\binom{2r-1}{r-1}$, $W\wedge W\subseteq\bigwedge^{2r}H=0$, and no nonzero $v$ annihilates $W$ for $r\ge2$. Verified by computer for $r=2,3,4$ (check B). This example is consistent with the source paper's own optimality remarks. |

## Reference check

1. **Scott-Wilmer, arXiv:1907.06019 (Theorem 2.3 and the question).** Fetched the
   full PDF. Theorem 2.3, the definition of self-annihilating, and the exact prose
   question ("Could all extremal examples be of this form?", asked in the context
   $r < n/2$) all check out as the writeup and catalog state. The writeup's Section 1
   technique (initial supports intersecting) is the paper's own proof method for
   Theorem 2.3, correctly rederived.
2. **Erdős-Ko-Rado equality case** (intersecting $r$-family of size
   $\binom{n-1}{r-1}$ with $n>2r$ is a star). Classical; consequence of
   Hilton-Milner (1967); stated verbatim as the "Moreover" clause of Theorem 1.1 in
   arXiv:2406.17857. Confirmed.
3. **FATAL for novelty — the question is no longer open.** Denys Bulavka, Francesca
   Gandini, and Russ Woodroofe, *A Hilton-Milner theorem for exterior algebras*,
   arXiv:2406.17857 (submitted 25 June 2024; v3 14 October 2025), published in
   **Transactions of the London Mathematical Society 12 (2025), e70022**
   (doi:10.1112/tlm3.70022). I read the paper (pages 1-3). Its introduction states
   explicitly: "Scott and Wilmer asked in [19, Section 2.2] whether the
   characterization of the maximal families in Theorem 1.1 extends to the exterior
   algebra setting... We will answer both questions in the affirmative." Their
   **Theorem 1.5** proves the Hilton-Milner bound
   $\dim L \le \binom{n-1}{k-1}-\binom{n-k-1}{k-1}+1$ for nontrivially
   self-annihilating subspaces, and their **Corollary 1.6** states: "Let $k < n/2$.
   If $L$ is a self-annihilating subspace of $\bigwedge^k V$ such that $\dim L$
   achieves the upper bound of $\binom{n-1}{k-1}$, then $L$ is annihilated by a
   1-form." Combined with their Lemma 2.1 ($v\wedge x=0 \iff x=v\wedge x'$), this is
   precisely the claimed characterization — proved over an arbitrary field (with
   characteristic-independent techniques), strictly stronger than the writeup's
   result over $\mathbb{R}$. The catalog's auto-review of 2026-05-15 missed this
   paper; it predates the attack by roughly two years.

## Caveats

- **Priority (decisive).** The writeup's own caveat "publication priority has not
  been independently checked" turns out to be the fatal one: the question was
  resolved in June 2024 by Bulavka-Gandini-Woodroofe (arXiv:2406.17857; Trans.
  London Math. Soc. 2025), in stronger form (arbitrary field, full Hilton-Milner
  bound for nontrivial self-annihilating subspaces). The catalog entry (auto-review
  2026-05-15, "medium confidence") missed it, exactly as its own confidence note
  feared.
- **Field.** The writeup works over $\mathbb{R}$ (the source paper's setting). Its
  argument in fact never divides except by pivot entries and uses no order or
  topology, so it would work over any field; but as written it claims only
  $\mathbb{R}$. BGW's published result covers arbitrary fields.
- **Range.** The proof covers exactly $1\le r<n/2$; the characterization fails at
  $n=2r$ (correctly noted and proved in Section 4). $r=1$ is handled trivially and
  separately (the rigidity lemma needs $k\ge1$). The case $r=0$ is degenerate and
  not part of the question.
- **Novelty of proof method.** The writeup's proof route (initial-monomial star +
  graph-map rigidity) is different from and considerably shorter than BGW's
  (limit/shifting actions on Grassmannians), but Section 1 is essentially
  Scott-Wilmer's own Theorem 2.3 argument plus classical EKR uniqueness. The proof
  might have minor expository value; it establishes nothing new.
- No other unstated assumptions found; disconnectedness/small-$n$ edge cases do not
  arise (the hypothesis $r<n/2$ forces $n\ge2r+1$, which is all the argument uses).

## Referee summary

The writeup gives a complete and, as far as I can determine, entirely correct proof
of the Scott-Wilmer extremal characterization for $r<n/2$ over $\mathbb{R}$: every
step verified by hand, the rigidity lemma and the boundary counterexample verified
exactly by computer, and every cited result confirmed against the sources. However,
the problem was not open at the time of the attack: Bulavka, Gandini, and Woodroofe
proved the same characterization (Corollary 1.6 of arXiv:2406.17857, June 2024,
published in Trans. London Math. Soc. 12 (2025), e70022) as a corollary of a
stronger exterior-algebra Hilton-Milner theorem, over an arbitrary field. The
correct verdict is ALREADY_KNOWN, not a new resolution of an open problem; the
catalog entry should be updated to closed.
