# Referee report: 2308.15387__00 (Conjecture 4.2 of "The power of many colours")

## Interpretation

The catalog statement is a verbatim copy of the conjecture in the paper. I downloaded
the arXiv v2 TeX source (`main.tex`, 1156 lines); the conjecture environment in
Section 4 (Concluding remarks), i.e. Conjecture 4.2, reads exactly:

> For any $1 \le s \le \frac{\sqrt{r}}{\log r}$ and $n$ large enough we have
> $f(n,r,s) = \frac{s^2(\log r)^{1-o_r(1)}}{r} \cdot n$.

The writeup's formalization of $f(n,r,s)$ (min over $r$-edge-colourings of $K_n$,
max over colour sets $C$ with $|C|\le s$, of the largest component of the graph of
$C$-coloured edges) matches the paper's definition exactly. So far so good.

However, the *same paper* contains, three paragraphs earlier in the same section,
the summary Theorem 4.1 (`thm:f-main`), whose first case states

> $f(n,r,s) = \Theta\!\left(\frac{2^s}{r}\right)\cdot n$ for $s \le (1-o_r(1))\log\log r$,

restating Theorem 1.1 of the introduction ("Let $s \ge 1$, $r$ large enough and
$n \ge \Omega(r)$ then $f(n,r,s) = \frac{\Theta(2^s)}{r}\cdot n$"). At $s = 1$ this
gives $f(n,r,1) = \Theta(n/r)$ (consistent with the classical Gyárfás–Füredi results
the paper cites), whereas the literal Conjecture 4.2 at $s=1$ would demand
$f(n,r,1) = (\log r)^{1-o_r(1)} n/r \to \infty \cdot n/r$. So the literal statement,
with range starting at $s = 1$, is contradicted **by the paper itself, on the same
page**. Moreover the paper explicitly locates the crossover: "Theorem 1.1 holds for
$s \le (1-o_r(1))\log\log r$, and this is essentially tight since a more precise
version of Theorem 1.2 shows it can not hold for $s \ge (1+o_r(1))\log\log r$." And
the sentence introducing Conjecture 4.2 states its intent: "Perhaps the most
immediate question is to determine whether the $\log r$ term is necessary in the
second part of Theorem 4.1 ... our lower bound for the first part shows that a
$(\log r)^{1-o_r(1)}$ factor is necessary when $s \approx \log\log r$."

The intended conjecture is therefore unambiguous: the upper bound
$f(n,r,s) \le \frac{8s(s+1)\log r}{r} n$ (Prop. `prop:upr-bnd-f-medium-s`) is
essentially tight, i.e. $f(n,r,s) = s^2(\log r)^{1-o_r(1)} n/r$ in the regime where
this is not already excluded by the paper's own first-regime theorem — effectively
$(1+o(1))\log\log r \lesssim s \le \sqrt r/\log r$. The "$1 \le s$" endpoint is an
authors' slip in the stated range, not mathematical content.

The writeup refutes exactly and only this slip. Its two counterexample regimes are
$s = 1$ and $s_d = \lfloor\frac12\log_2 d\rfloor \approx \frac12\log_2\log_2 r$ —
**both strictly inside** the regime $s \le (1-\varepsilon)\log\log r$ where the paper
already proved $f = \Theta(2^s/r)\cdot n$. Worse, the construction it uses is
literally the paper's own Proposition 3.1 (`prop:f-uppr-bound-small-s`, quoted there
from Liu–Morris–Prince 2007): the $\mathbb{F}_2^d$ coset colouring giving
$f(n, 2^d-1, s) \le 2^s\lceil n/(r+1)\rceil$. The authors stated Conjecture 4.2 in
full knowledge of this proposition — it is printed 160 lines earlier in the same
file. No reasonable author of the paper would consider Conjecture 4.2 resolved by
this writeup; they would consider it a restatement of their own Proposition 3.1.
The writeup itself half-concedes this in its caveats field ("a version with a lower
restriction on $s$ ... is not resolved here") yet still stamps "disproved" with high
confidence. This is the interpretation-gaming failure mode: a strawman literal
reading is refuted while the intended conjecture is untouched. **interpretation_ok:
false.**

## Caveats

1. **The refutation has no force against the intended conjecture.** Both
   counterexample regimes ($s=1$ and $s_d\approx\frac12\log_2\log_2 r$) lie in
   $s\le(1-\varepsilon)\log\log r$, where the source paper itself proved
   $f=\Theta(2^s/r)n$. The substantive open problem — whether
   $f = s^2(\log r)^{1-o_r(1)}n/r$ for $(1+o(1))\log\log r\lesssim s\le\sqrt r/\log r$,
   equivalently whether the $\log r$ factor in Theorem 1.2 is necessary — remains
   open. The writeup proves nothing for any $s\ge\log_2\log_2 r$.
2. **The literal statement was never consistent.** Conjecture 4.2 as printed
   contradicts Theorem 4.1(first case) of the same paper; "disproving" it therefore
   conveys no information the authors did not already publish alongside it. The
   correct catalog action is to repair the extracted range (e.g. to
   $C\log\log r\le s\le\sqrt r/\log r$), not to mark the conjecture refuted.
3. **Priority.** The Lemma is Proposition 3.1 of the source paper = LMP 2007
   (Discrete Math. 308 (2008) 5096–5121); the writeup presents it without
   attribution as its own construction.
4. Minor: $s_d\ge 1$ requires $d\ge 4$ (covered by "sufficiently large $d$");
   the Lemma needs $2^d\mid n$, but since the conjecture asserts the formula for
   *all* large $n$, exhibiting arbitrarily large multiples of $2^d$ suffices, and
   the writeup's general $\lceil\cdot\rceil$ version covers all $n$ anyway.
5. The writeup's own caveats field acknowledges the possible range slip, which sits
   uneasily with its "high confidence, disproved" verdict block.

## Referee summary

Every individual mathematical step in the writeup is correct — I re-derived each
inequality and verified the finite construction exhaustively by computer — but the
writeup does not disprove Conjecture 4.2 in any sense its authors would recognise.
Its counterexample is, verbatim, Proposition 3.1 of the very paper posing the
conjecture (there credited to Liu–Morris–Prince 2007), and the incompatibility of
the conjectured formula with small $s$ is likewise stated in that paper (Theorem 1.1
holds for $s\le(1-o_r(1))\log\log r$, where $f=\Theta(2^s/r)n$). What the writeup
exposes is a range slip in the printed conjecture ("$1\le s$" instead of something
like "$\log\log r\lesssim s$"), i.e. a defect of transcription/statement, not of the
mathematical conjecture, whose content — a $(\log r)^{1-o(1)}$ factor in
$f(n,r,s)\cdot r/(s^2 n)$ throughout $\log\log r\lesssim s\le\sqrt r/\log r$ —
remains wide open. Verdict: ALREADY_KNOWN (correct mathematics, entirely contained
in LMP 2007 and in arXiv:2308.15387 itself), with interpretation_ok: false; the
claimed resolution of the open problem should be rejected.
