# Referee report — 1802.05582__01

**Target claim.** The writeup answers the open question of Aboulker–Bonamy–Bousquet–Esperet
(arXiv:1802.05582, remark after Corollary 2.1) affirmatively: it presents a randomized
LOCAL algorithm that Δ-list-colors any n-vertex graph with maximum degree Δ ≥ 3 and no
K_{Δ+1} component in O(log² n) rounds w.h.p., i.e. with **no** multiplicative poly(Δ)
factor (and O(log² n) ≤ O(log³ n / log Δ), the Panconesi–Srinivasan benchmark).

## Interpretation

The source paper's Corollary 2.1 (fetched from arXiv, quoted verbatim):

> "There is a deterministic distributed algorithm of round complexity O(Δ²log³n) that
> given any n-vertex graph of maximum degree Δ≥3, and any Δ-list-assignment L for the
> vertices of G, either finds an L-list-coloring of G, or finds that no such coloring
> exists."

and the open question, verbatim from the paper:

> "Note that Panconesi and Srinivasan also gave a randomized variant of their algorithm
> working in O(log³n/logΔ) rounds. In our case, it is not clear whether we can similarly
> avoid the multiplicative factor polynomial in Δ in a randomized version of our
> algorithm."

The writeup's formalization is the intended one, with two deviations, both fair:

1. **Hypothesis shift.** Corollary 2.1 says "either finds an L-coloring or reports that
   none exists"; the writeup instead *excludes* components isomorphic to K_{Δ+1}
   (whether or not their lists make them colorable). This is a slightly narrower promise
   than Corollary 2.1, but the repair is trivial: a K_{Δ+1} component has diameter 1, so
   one vertex can gather it, and either color it or report infeasibility, in O(1) rounds.
   No poly(Δ) round cost is hidden here (LOCAL messages are unbounded). I therefore do
   not consider this interpretation gaming, but it is a genuine (if routine) gap between
   what is proven and Corollary 2.1's exact "either/or" form.
2. **"A randomized version of our algorithm."** The writeup's algorithm is not a
   randomization of the paper's algorithm (which iteratively removes low-degree vertices);
   it is a Panconesi–Srinivasan-style layered algorithm. A reasonable author would still
   consider the question resolved: the question is about achievable round complexity for
   the same problem in the same (randomized LOCAL) model, and the analogy the authors
   draw is precisely to Panconesi–Srinivasan's O(log³n/log Δ) bound, which O(log² n)
   meets (log²n = log³n/log n ≤ log³n/log Δ since Δ ≤ n).

The writeup honestly flags the literal-reading obstruction (K_{Δ+1} with identical
Δ-lists is uncolorable), the LOCAL-vs-CONGEST caveat, Δ ≥ 3 (also assumed by
Corollary 2.1), and unverified novelty. `interpretation_ok: true`.

- **Degree-choosability theorem (Borodin 1977; Erdős–Rubin–Taylor 1979)** — classical;
  confirmed against independent sources (e.g. Dvořák's lecture notes "List coloring and

  exactly the forward direction with the correct hypotheses (connected, lists ≥ degree).
- **Luby-type randomized MIS in O(log n) rounds whp** — classical (Luby 1986 /

  arXiv:2405.09975) — CONGEST Δ-coloring, common palette. I found no published
  polylog(n) randomized algorithm for Δ-list-coloring (lists of size exactly Δ), in
  agreement with the catalog's 2026 auto-review. The claimed result appears new, though
  scientifically modest: it is a direct assembly of classical techniques, and the
  non-list problem now has poly(log log n) bounds.

## Caveats

1. **Corollary 2.1's exact form not matched.** The writeup proves the "no K_{Δ+1}
   component" promise version, not "either color or report infeasibility", and it also
   skips K_{Δ+1} components that *are* colorable (non-identical lists). Repairable in
   O(1) rounds (such components have diameter 1), but the repair is not written.
2. **Δ = 2 excluded** — consistent with Corollary 2.1's own Δ ≥ 3 hypothesis; not a
   defect, but the one-line claim should carry it (the writeup's caveat block does).
3. **Global knowledge of n and Δ** is silently assumed (n enters k and R; Δ enters the
   low-degree test). Standard in this literature (and needed already by Corollary 2.1),
   but unstated.
4. **LOCAL only, unbounded messages and computation**: radius-Θ(log n) gathering and
   exhaustive core coloring are legitimate in LOCAL but hopeless in CONGEST. The writeup
   states this.
5. **Monte Carlo**: with probability n^{-c} the output can be improper/partial; this
   matches the Panconesi–Srinivasan benchmark's guarantee type.
6. **Lemma 2 bookkeeping (S14)**: initial states at the root and the
   generation-vs-distance accounting are left implicit; the repair is routine (done in
   this review).
7. **Novelty**: not established by the writeup and only negatively checked here (no
   prior source found); the result is plausible enough that experts may consider it
   folklore-adjacent, since it assembles only classical ingredients. That affects
   significance, not correctness.

## Referee summary

I set out assuming the writeup was wrong and failed to break it. The algorithm is a
Panconesi–Srinivasan-style layered scheme adapted to lists: every vertex is within
O(log n) of an "escape" (a sub-Δ-degree vertex or a non-Gallai-tree ball — Lemma 2, the
one genuinely load-bearing structural claim, whose branching argument I re-derived case
by case and stress-tested computationally, including on a near-worst-case Gallai family
where the bound is close to tight); well-separated escapes are selected by an MIS of a
power graph; layers are colored inward with a standard randomized deg+1-slack subroutine
(Lemma 1, re-derived and simulated); the escape cores finish via the classical
Borodin/Erdős–Rubin–Taylor degree-choosability theorem (verified exhaustively on small
graphs). All arithmetic (radii, separation, domination, list-slack inequalities, round
counts) re-derives correctly, and 384 end-to-end simulated runs on adversarial
Δ-list instances all produced proper colorings. The claimed verdict "proved" is
justified under the writeup's stated formalization; what keeps this from CONFIRMED is a
minor informality in Lemma 2's counting and the (trivially repairable, O(1)-round)
mismatch with Corollary 2.1's "either color or report infeasible" statement. No prior
publication of the Δ-list variant was found, so ALREADY_KNOWN does not apply as far as I
could determine.
