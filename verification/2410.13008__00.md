# Referee report: 2410.13008__00 (Seymour, Conjecture 1.2 — annular crossing-free drawing)

## Interpretation

The catalog statement matches arXiv:2410.13008v2 verbatim (checked against the arXiv
HTML): *"Let G be a strongly 2-connected digraph drawn (possibly with crossings) in an
annulus Σ, such that every directed cycle has winding number one around the annulus in
the natural sense. Then G can be drawn in Σ without crossings, such that every directed
cycle has winding number one."*

The writeup interprets (a) "strongly 2-connected" as "D − v strongly connected for every
vertex v", and (b) "winding number one" as signed winding number +1, the same for every
directed cycle. Both are the intended readings:

- (a) is exactly Seymour's definition ("strongly 2-connected" = remains strongly
  connected after deleting any single vertex, confirmed from the paper's introduction).
- (b) is confirmed by Seymour's own revised version (Princeton website, April 16, 2025),
  which explains that the hypothesis of 1.2 is equivalent to *weightability*: a real
  edge-weighting w with Σ_{e∈C} w(e) = 1 for **every** directed cycle C (weights are the
  fraction of a full turn subtended around the origin, i.e. signed winding, all cycles
  winding the same way once). This is precisely the writeup's "advance/12" mechanism.

The writeup's own caveat ("This uses signed winding number") turns out to be moot: my
exhaustive computation (below) shows the counterexample also kills the *unsigned*
reading — no crossing-free annular drawing of D gives every directed cycle winding
number ±1 either. There is no interpretation gaming here; a reasonable author — indeed
Seymour himself, faced with Carter's analogous counterexample — considers the conjecture
resolved (refuted) by exactly this kind of example.

## Reference check

The writeup cites no external literature explicitly; the only external result it leans
on is Whitney's uniqueness theorem for embeddings of 3-connected planar graphs
("By uniqueness of the sphere embedding of a 3-connected planar graph"), which is
standard and correctly applied (hypothesis verified: connectivity 4).

Checks on the problem's own sources, however, uncovered the decisive fact:

- **arXiv:2410.13008v2** (Feb 2025): Conjecture 1.2 stated exactly as in the catalog;
  discussion "This would be a pretty extension of Thomassen's theorem, but we cannot
  prove it." Definitions of strongly 2-connected and the rectangle→annulus gluing match
  the writeup's usage. Confirmed via https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13008v2.
- **Revised version, Princeton website** (https://web.math.princeton.edu/~pds/papers/tricycles/paper.pdf,
  "September 17, 2024; revised April 16, 2025" — postdating arXiv v2): statement 1.2 is
  now headed "**1.2 False conjecture:**" and the text reads: *"This would have been a
  pretty extension of Thomassen's theorem, and was some of the motivation for the
  research in this paper, but it has very recently been shown to be false, by Daniel
  Carter [1]; see Figure 1."* Figure 1 ("Carter's counterexample. The four thick edges
  have weight one, and the others have zero.") shows a digon-free, strongly 2-connected,
  weakly 3-connected planar counterexample. The acknowledgements thank Carter, Codsi and
  Steiner "for extensive work on 1.2 during a recent conference in Barbados".
- **Reference [1]** = Daniel Carter, problem 1, in
  https://web.math.princeton.edu/~pds/barbados25/problems.pdf (Open Problems for the
  2025 Barbados Graph Theory Workshop, collected by Julien Codsi). Read directly: Carter
  (with Seymour) answers the question "does G have to come from a planar drawing in this
  way?" *no*, first with an 8-vertex digon example (red edges weight 1/4, blue digons
  weight 1/2), then with a vector-field construction (Poincaré–Hopf) producing
  digon-free, strongly 2-connected, weakly 3-connected counterexamples.
- **Follow-up**: Berger–Carter–Seymour, "When all directed cycles have the same weight",
  arXiv:2601.12746 (Oct 2025, rev. Jan 2026), develops the weightable-digraph theory
  (weightable ⟺ annular-drawable with all windings one).

So the conjecture attacked here had already been refuted, roughly a year before this
writeup, by Daniel Carter. The catalog's "open, high confidence" status (auto-review of
May 2026) is wrong: the auto-reviewer fetched arXiv HTML (v2, Feb 2025, which predates
the revision and does not mention Carter) and dismissed a search-result claim of
Carter's disproof as "likely a hallucination" — that claim was in fact correct; the
refutation lives in the Princeton-hosted revision, not on arXiv.

  disks, up to reflection (a global sign flip on all windings). All 16×15 = 240 ordered
  face pairs were tested against all 332 directed cycles, computing exact signed winding
  numbers geometrically from a straight-line planar drawing:
  - pairs giving every directed cycle winding identically +1 (or identically −1): **0**;
  - pairs giving every directed cycle winding in {+1, −1} (unsigned reading): **0**.
  With p in face (0,2,4) and q in face (6,8,10): winding(0→2→4→0) = +1,

The finite construction is therefore fully verified: D satisfies the hypothesis of
Conjecture 1.2 and violates its conclusion — under both the signed and the unsigned
reading of "winding number one".

## Caveats

- **Priority, not correctness, is the issue.** The disproof is correct, but Daniel
  Carter refuted the conjecture in early 2025 (Barbados workshop; acknowledged in
  Seymour's own April 2025 revision). The writeup shows no awareness of this.
- The writeup's 12-vertex circulant example appears to be a **different construction**
  from Carter's (Carter: an 8-vertex weighted digon example, plus digon-free grid-like
  examples from a Poincaré–Hopf vector-field construction). At 12 vertices, digon-free,
  planar, with a fully self-contained one-page impossibility argument, it is arguably a
  cleaner certificate — of possible expository value, but not a new resolution.
- The writeup's caveat about signed winding is unnecessary: computation shows no annular
  placement gives even all-±1 windings, so the counterexample is interpretation-robust.
- The example is planar, hence consistent with (and no threat to) Carter's still-open
  follow-up conjecture that every strongly 2-connected weightable digraph is planar.
- Definition dependence is benign: "strongly 2-connected" matches Seymour's definition;
  D has no digons, so the paper's allowance of directed 2-cycles is irrelevant here.
- The exhaustive check relies on Whitney's theorem to enumerate all annular embeddings;
  connectivity 4 was machine-verified, so this is airtight.

## Referee summary

I set out to break this writeup and could not. Every one of its fourteen steps checks
out under re-derivation; the finite construction was verified exhaustively by computer
(332 directed cycles, all of advance 12; strong 2-connectivity; forced facial triangles
with coherent orientation; and an exhaustive scan of all 240 annular placements of the
unique sphere embedding finding none with all windings +1, or even all ±1). The
interpretation is the one Seymour intended, as his own later "weightable" reformulation
confirms. However, the conjecture was already refuted: Daniel Carter disproved it at the
2025 Barbados Graph Theory Workshop, and the April 16, 2025 revision of Seymour's paper
(Princeton website) explicitly relabels 1.2 "False conjecture" and presents Carter's
counterexample. The correct verdict is therefore ALREADY_KNOWN — a correct, independently
constructed, and arguably simpler counterexample to a conjecture that had been refuted
about a year earlier, and whose catalog "open" status was itself erroneous.
