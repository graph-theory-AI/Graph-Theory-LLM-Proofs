## Interpretation

The writeup formalizes the problem as: find a polynomial f such that for every
t >= 3 there is a graph G on at most f(t) vertices with E(G) nonempty, G has no
induced C_{2t-2}, and G - e has an induced C_{2t-2} for every e in E(G).

I fetched the source paper (arXiv:2505.24100v2, HTML full text). Question 1.8
there reads verbatim: "Does there exist a polynomial f such that for every
t >= 3, there is a graph G on at most f(t) vertices with E(G) != emptyset, such
that G is H-free but G - e has an induced subgraph isomorphic to C_{2t-2} for
every e in E(G)?" (H = C_{2t-2} from context). This is exactly the writeup's
formalization: the deletion-only ("halfway") property, not full induced
saturation. The writeup explicitly notes at the end that it addresses only edge
deletion, "exactly as in the question" — which is correct. No interpretation
gaming: a reasonable author of arXiv:2505.24100 would consider Question 1.8
answered affirmatively (and optimally, up to the constant factor: linear in t,
matching their remark that they "do not even know a rationale for expecting the
number of vertices of G_t to be more than linear in t").

**interpretation_ok: true.**

## Reference check

1. **Goedgebeur & Zamfirescu, "On hypohamiltonian snarks and a theorem of
   Fiorini"** (arXiv:1608.07164, Ars Mathematica Contemporanea). Fetched the
   full PDF. Theorem 3.6 states verbatim: "There exists a hypohamiltonian snark
   of order n iff n in {10, 18, 20, 22} or n is even and n >= 26." Table 1 of
   that paper reports 95 hypohamiltonian snarks of order 26. Snark = cubic,
   cyclically 4-edge-connected, chromatic index 4, girth >= 5 — in particular a
   simple cubic hypohamiltonian graph. Exactly what the writeup uses (it only
   needs the "even n >= 26" direction). **Checks out.**

2. **Theorem 1.6 of the source paper** (arXiv:2505.24100v2, fetched HTML full
   text). Exists and gives the deletion-only property for C_{2t-2} — but for
   t >= 5, not t >= 3 as the writeup's Section 4 uses. **Mis-cited at t = 3, 4**
   (the reason for references_ok: false). Repairable via the same paper's
   Observation 1.5: "The icosahedron is C_4-induced-saturated, the Cartesian
   product of two 5-cycles is C_6-induced-saturated, and the dodecahedron is
   C_8-induced-saturated" (plus a computer-verified C_10 example), which is
   stronger than what is needed.

3. **Novelty / prior-art search.** The source paper itself nowhere mentions
   hypohamiltonian graphs (checked in the full text); its own construction is
   doubly exponential. A web search for the combination induced-saturated /
   even cycle / line graph / hypohamiltonian found no paper containing this
   construction. However, it found **Ilkyoo Choi, "Induced-saturated graphs
   exist for even cycles" (arXiv:2608.24202, submitted 2026-08-25)**, which
   constructs, for every q >= 3, a C_{2q+2}-induced-saturated graph on 8q
   vertices by a block construction (no line graphs, no hypohamiltonian
   graphs, and no mention of Question 1.8 or of size bounds as a goal). Since
   full induced saturation implies the deletion-only property, Choi's result
   makes the affirmative answer to Question 1.8 an immediate corollary of the
   very recent literature (linear: 8q = 8t - 16 vertices for t >= 5). The
   writeup's construction remains different, self-contained, deletion-only,
   and smaller (3t - 3 < 8t - 16 for all t >= 4).

## Caveats

1. **The t = 3, 4 citation slip** (step 8): "Theorem 1.6 ... for 3 <= t <= 13"
   is false as written for t = 3, 4 (Theorem 1.6 requires t >= 5). Fully
   repaired above via Observation 1.5 of the same source paper and verified by
   machine; this is the only reason the verdict is MINOR_GAPS rather than
   CONFIRMED.

2. **Concurrent literature.** Choi (arXiv:2608.24202, 2026-08-25, nine days
   before this review) proves the strictly stronger full induced-saturation
   statement for every even cycle C_{2q+2}, q >= 3, on 8q vertices. The
   affirmative answer to Question 1.8 is therefore now derivable from the
   literature, though Choi does not state Question 1.8, does not discuss size
   as a goal, and uses an entirely different construction. Depending on the
   ledger's convention for near-simultaneous subsuming work, ALREADY_KNOWN
   could be argued for the yes/no answer; the specific 3(t-1)-vertex
   deletion-only construction via line graphs of hypohamiltonian cubic graphs
   appears to be new and is smaller than Choi's for every t >= 4.

3. **Deletion only.** The construction resolves exactly the deletion half asked
   by Question 1.8; L(F) is generally NOT C_m-induced-saturated (adding a
   nonedge need not create an induced C_m). The writeup states this
   limitation explicitly, so nothing is over-claimed.

4. **Parity.** The argument silently uses that m = 2t-2 is even (a cubic graph
   on an odd number of vertices does not exist, and 3m/2 must be an integer);
   this is automatic here but worth noting as a convention dependence.

5. **The constant C is astronomically large** if instantiated via Theorem 1.6
   for 7 <= t <= 13 (those A_t are doubly exponential in the fixed t), so
   f(t) = 3t + C is linear with an enormous additive constant. This is
   irrelevant to the truth of the claim (any fixed C is a constant), and in
   fact C can be made small: cubic hypohamiltonian graphs also exist at
   m = 10, 18, 20, 22 (covering t = 6, 10, 11, 12), and Observation 1.5 covers
   t = 3, 4, 5, 6 with graphs of at most 25 vertices, leaving only
   t in {7, 8, 9, 13} to Theorem 1.6. The writeup did not optimize this; it
   did not need to.

6. **Boundary case m = 26 (t = 14)** was verified through the literature
   (Goedgebeur–Zamfirescu Theorem 3.6 and their Table 1 listing 95
   hypohamiltonian snarks of order 26), not by machine; my machine checks
   instantiate the construction at m = 10, 20, 28. The proof of Lemma 2 is
   uniform in m, so this affects nothing.

## Referee summary

I set out to break this proof and could not. The construction — take the line
graph of any simple cubic hypohamiltonian graph on m = 2t-2 vertices — is
short, correct, and verifiably answers Question 1.8 of arXiv:2505.24100
affirmatively with a linear polynomial, giving graphs on exactly 3t-3 vertices
for every t >= 14 (and the general statement for all t >= 3 after absorbing
finitely many small cases into a constant). Every step re-derives cleanly: the
classical correspondence between induced cycles of length >= 4 in a line graph
and cycles of the root graph, the degree-2 forcing of the two root edges
through c in F - d, and the single-chord structure of the witness set. Every
claimed property was independently confirmed by brute-force computation on
three concrete cubic hypohamiltonian graphs (Petersen, J_5, J_7), including
exhaustive chordless-cycle enumeration and per-edge deletion checks, and the
one external citation (Goedgebeur–Zamfirescu's spectrum theorem for
hypohamiltonian snarks) states verbatim what is used. The single defect is a
citation slip: Theorem 1.6 of the source paper is stated for t >= 5, not
t >= 3, so the cases t = 3, 4 need Observation 1.5 of the same paper instead —
a one-line repair, machine-verified here. Separately, note that Choi
(arXiv:2608.24202, Aug 25, 2026) has just published a stronger full
induced-saturation construction (8q vertices) that independently implies the
affirmative answer, though without addressing Question 1.8 or size; the
writeup's construction is different and smaller.
