# Referee report: 1610.00239__00 (Alon-Klartag Conjecture 2.4, bounded-norm bipartite JL)

## Interpretation

The catalog statement is Conjecture 2.4 of Alon-Klartag, arXiv:1610.00239: "Under
the assumptions of Theorem 1.2, the conclusion holds together with the further
requirement that ||x_i|| <= O(1) and ||y_i|| <= O(1) for all 1 <= i <= n." I fetched
the source paper (ar5iv rendering of v4) and confirmed Theorem 1.2 verbatim: for
a_1..a_n, b_1..b_n in the Euclidean unit ball and 0 < eps < 1, with
t = floor(C log(2+eps^2 n)/eps^2), there exist x_1..x_n, y_1..y_n in R^t with
|<x_i,y_j> - <a_i,b_j>| <= eps for all i,j. The conjecture asks for the same with
all output norms bounded by an absolute constant.

The writeup's Theorem is exactly this statement (with the paper's (a_i,b_j)/(x_i,y_j)
renamed to (x_i,y_j)/(p_i,q_j)), and is in fact strictly stronger in two harmless
ways: it allows two families of different sizes n and m, and it produces, after the
p_i are fixed, a valid q(y) for *every* y in the unit ball simultaneously. Setting
m = n recovers the conjecture. There is no interpretation gaming: this is the
intended reading, the same one Jain's 2026 paper describes when it says its main
theorem "confirms their conjecture". The floor in t, the range 0 < eps < 1, and the
O(1) norm bound (writeup: ||p_i|| <= 2, ||q_j|| <= 4) are all handled. A reasonable
author of the original paper would consider this statement a resolution of
Conjecture 2.4. interpretation_ok: true.

crux inequality (1) is, in the orthonormal special case x_i = e_i, precisely the
standard lower restricted-isometry property of Gaussian matrices over effectively
sparse vectors {||z||_1 <= ||z||_2/eps}, at the textbook sample complexity
t ~ s log(2+n/s) with s = 1/eps^2 — a well-established fact, here reproved
correctly from scratch.

## Reference check

1. **Theorem 1.2, Conjecture 2.4, Theorem 2.5 of arXiv:1610.00239 (Alon-Klartag).**
   Fetched via ar5iv. All three match the writeup's and the catalog's usage
   verbatim (Theorem 1.2 as quoted under Interpretation; Conjecture 2.4 word for
   word; Theorem 2.5 is the eps = Theta(1/sqrt(n)) case with the bound
   |X_i| + |Y_i| <= C_3). The paper's own proof of Theorem 1.2 uses Khatri-Sidak
   plus the low-M* estimate and produces y_j = sqrt(t) P(U^{-1} b_j) with norms up
   to ~sqrt(t); the paper's Section 7 explains why iterating their dimension-halving
   (Theorem 2.5) fails to give Conjecture 2.4. The writeup's route is different
   from both. CONFIRMED.

2. **Gordon's Gaussian min-max / escape through the mesh** (used in Lemma 2).
   Standard: for S a subset of the unit sphere and Gamma a t x d standard Gaussian
   matrix, P(inf_{x in S} ||Gamma x|| > a_t - w(S) - s) >= 1 - e^{-s^2/2}, with
   a_t = E||g_t|| >= t/sqrt(t+1) >= sqrt(t)-1 (Gordon 1988; see e.g. the MIT
   18.S096 notes "Johnson-Lindenstrauss Lemma and Gordon's Theorem" and
   Foucart-Rauhut Thm 9.21). Exactly the form the writeup uses. CONFIRMED.

3. **Prior resolution — FATAL for novelty, not for correctness.** Vishesh Jain,
   "The sharp dimension bound in the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma",
   arXiv:2608.13782 (v1 2026-08-13, v2 2026-08-21), resolves the Larsen-Nelson
   conjecture: every n-point set in R^d embeds with distortion 1+eps into dimension
   r <= C min{d, n-1, log(2+eps^2 n)/eps^2}, attained by a linear map. I read the
   PDF directly; its Remark (iii) states verbatim that Alon-Klartag "further
   conjectured [2, Conjecture 2.4] that the output vectors ... may be chosen to
   have norm bounded by a universal constant ... We note that our main result
   Theorem 1.1, applied to {0, a_1, ..., a_n, b_1, ..., b_n}, confirms their
   conjecture." (Including 0 preserves norms up to 1+eps; polarization converts
   multiplicative distance distortion on unit-ball points into O(eps) additive
   inner-product error.) The attack artifacts were committed 2026-08-31, i.e.,
   after Jain's paper was public; the catalog's "open" status was auto-reviewed
   2026-05-15 and was stale. The writeup does not cite Jain and its proof is
   independent and far simpler (it proves only the bipartite statement, which is
   what the conjecture asks).

## Caveats

- **Constants are existential.** C_1, C_2, c, c', C, K are never pinned down; I
  chased them and they close comfortably (e.g. C_2 = 64 works in Lemma 2), but a
  fully formal writeup would need this arithmetic spelled out. Routine.
- **Lemma 2 hypothesis "S = -S"** is stated but not needed by Gordon's theorem;
  harmless (S_R is symmetric).
- **Empty S_R / h = 0 edge cases** in step 3 are handled implicitly (inf over the
  empty set, trivial case h = 0); both are genuinely trivial.
- The proof covers **all 0 < eps < 1**, including eps < 2/sqrt(n) (below the range
  where the conjecture matters for Theorem 2.2's bit bounds) and the writeup even
  handles eps >= 1 by zero vectors; no range restriction is hidden.
- The theorem is **existential/probabilistic** (positive probability of a good G);
  the source's Theorem 1.2 is likewise a randomized construction, and Section 5's
  SOCP remark matches the paper's "randomized polynomial time" framing. No mismatch.
- The verdict block's one-line claim ("Gaussian quotient map ... gives the target
  dimension and uniformly bounded output norms") matches what is actually proven;
  the "slightly stronger one-sided form" (all y simultaneously; m independent of n)
  is indeed stronger, not weaker.
- **The one substantive caveat is priority, not correctness**: as of the attack
  date (2026-08-31) the conjecture had already been confirmed by Jain
  (arXiv:2608.13782, 2026-08-13), as a corollary of a much stronger theorem proved
  by an entirely different method (discrepancy-style matrix walk). The catalog's
  "open" status (auto-review 2026-05-15) predates Jain.

## Referee summary

I set out to break this proof and could not. Every lemma re-derives correctly: the
Maurey-type empirical-approximation bound on the Gaussian width of RK cap B_2 with
its quadratic soft-max penalty (Lemma 1) is airtight and matches the known sharp
width of effectively sparse vectors; the escape-through-the-mesh application
(Lemma 2) uses Gordon's inequality in its standard form; the case analysis behind
the key inequality ||T*z|| <= 4||P*z|| + eps||z||_1 is exhaustive; and the
support-function/separation duality converting that inequality into feasibility of
all right-hand vectors q(y) with ||q|| <= 4 is exactly right. Numerical
verification of all four quantitative ingredients (width constant, escape lower
bound, output norms, feasibility, inequality (1) under adversarial search) passed
with large margins. The interpretation is the intended one — the writeup proves
precisely Conjecture 2.4, in slightly stronger form. However, the result was
already in the literature when the attack ran: Vishesh Jain's resolution of the
Larsen-Nelson conjecture (arXiv:2608.13782, 13 Aug 2026) explicitly notes that it
confirms Alon-Klartag Conjecture 2.4. Hence ALREADY_KNOWN rather than CONFIRMED,
with the notable qualification that the writeup's proof is independent, self-
contained, restricted to the bipartite statement, and dramatically simpler than
Jain's; if it holds up under expert human scrutiny it may be of independent
expository interest, and the only reason for any residual doubt is the a priori
implausibility of a two-page argument for a nine-year-old conjecture of Alon and
Klartag — a doubt this review actively tried and failed to substantiate.
