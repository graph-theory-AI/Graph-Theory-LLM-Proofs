# Referee report: 2603.02786__03

**Target claim.** The writeup claims to *disprove* Conjecture 6 of arXiv:2603.02786
(Alon, Dębski, Grytczuk, Przybyło, "Packing arithmetic progressions"), which asserts
that if $k\ll n$ then $M_k(n)\sim \frac{k^2}{2\ln k}\,n$ (and otherwise
$M_k(n)\sim \frac{k^2}{2\ln k}\,n-\frac{k^3}{3\ln k}$). The writeup proves the lower
bound $M_k(n)\ge(\tfrac{6619}{10800}-o(1))\frac{nk^2}{\ln k}$ for $k\to\infty$,
$k=o(n)$, and $6619/10800=0.61287\ldots>1/2$, contradicting the first clause.

## Interpretation

I fetched the full source paper (17 pp.) and checked the writeup's formalization
against it line by line.

- **Definition of $M_k(n)$.** Paper, Sections 1 and 4: $B_d=\{d,2d,\ldots,nd\}$ for
  $d=1,\ldots,k$ (case (2): all progressions of size $n$); a packing is a choice of
  **integer** shifts $s_d$ with $s_d+B_d\subseteq[m]=\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and the shifted
  copies pairwise disjoint; $M_k(n)=m(\mathcal G_{[k]})$ is the least such $m$. The
  writeup uses exactly this: integral translates $t_d+B_d$ pairwise disjoint in one
  interval, $M_k(n)$ = minimum interval length. **Match.** The writeup's remark that
  real translations would trivialize the problem is correct and moot — the paper's
  shifts are integers by definition ("where $s$ is an arbitrary integer", p.1).
- **Conjecture 6.** Verified verbatim from the paper, p.16 — identical to the catalog
  statement. It is stated for $M_k(n)$ "with $k<n$, in view of Theorem 5", whose
  $o_k(1)$ error terms fix the intended asymptotic regime: the first clause means
  $k\to\infty$ with $k=o(n)$. The writeup's formalization ($k\to\infty$, $k=o(n)$,
  e.g. $n=k^4$) is the intended reading, not a strawman.
- **Would the authors consider the conjecture resolved?** Yes. The conjecture is a
  conjunction of two clauses; the writeup refutes the first (main) clause under
  exactly the paper's definitions and regime. Note the disproof only shows the
  constant $1/2$ is too small; it does not determine the true asymptotics (which
  remain between $6619/10800$ and $\zeta(2)/2\approx0.8225$ by the paper's Theorem 5
  upper bound). That is the correct meaning of "disproved" for a "$\sim$" conjecture.
- **Consistency sanity check.** The claimed lower-bound constant $0.6129$ lies
  strictly *between* the paper's proven lower bound $1/2$ and proven upper bound
  $\zeta(2)/2\approx0.8225$ (Theorem 5, p.9), so the writeup contradicts no theorem of
  the paper — only the conjecture. The paper's own lower-bound proof (p.10) uses
  *prime* differences only; the writeup's gain comes from the families $2p,3p,5p$,
  a mechanism the paper never considers (its p.12 remark even flags the prime-packing
  constant as the key open issue).

No step is labeled GAP or ERROR. The underlying mechanism is transparent: the hull
of $B_{2p}$ has length $\approx 2pn$ but, by CRT, its core can overlap prime cores
only inside $C_p\cup C_2$ (length $\approx pn$), and the system $\{C_{2p}\}_p$ is at
most $2$-fold overlapping — so at least $\approx\frac12\sum_{p\le k/2}pn\sim
\frac{nk^2}{16\ln k}$ of extra space beyond the prime baseline $\frac{nk^2}{2\ln k}$
is unavoidable. The families $3p,5p$ with the stated weights refine $1/16$ to
$\frac{1219}{10800}$.

## Caveats

1. **Regime formalization.** "$k\ll n$" is formalized as $k\to\infty$, $k=o(n)$. This
   matches Theorem 5's $o_k(1)$ convention and is the only reading under which the
   conjecture's $\sim$ is meaningful; for bounded $k$ the statement is vacuous.
2. **Only the first clause is refuted.** The second clause ($k\asymp n$ regime,
   with the $-k^3/(3\ln k)$ correction) is not analyzed; the writeup says so
   explicitly. Since the leading constant $1/2$ is common to both clauses and the
   conjecture is stated as a conjunction, Conjecture 6 as a whole is false; a
   corrected conjecture with a larger constant is not supplied (and the true constant
   remains open, in $[\,6619/10800,\ \zeta(2)/2\,]$).
3. **Length conventions.** The paper counts integer points in an interval; the
   writeup's core/hull accounting is by real length. Differences are $O(1)$ per core,
   $O(\pi(k))$ in total, absorbed by the stated error terms (the writeup flags this).
4. **Vacuity at small $k$.** The families $\{2p,3p,5p:p>5\}$ are empty for $k<14$,
   and cores are empty unless $(n-1)d>2k^2$; both are irrelevant in the asymptotic
   regime (and the empty-core convention $\lvert C_d\rvert\ge(n-1)d-2L$ "even when
   negative" is used correctly, only in lower bounds).
5. **Not optimized.** The weight scheme over multipliers $\{2,3,5\}$ is optimal for
   this three-family argument, but deeper hierarchies (e.g. $4p$, $6p$, larger prime
   multipliers) would push the constant further; the writeup does not pursue this —
   harmless for a disproof.
6. **Sociological caveat (not mathematical).** The refuted conjecture is by a strong
   author team ~6 months before this review; however, the paper's lower bound uses
   primes only, its conjecture extrapolates tightness of that bound by analogy with
   the case-(1) parameter $m_k(n)$ (where composite hulls are short and genuinely
   negligible — Theorem 4 proves constant 1 there), and in case (2) the hull of
   $B_{2p}$ is twice that of $B_p$, which is exactly the loophole the writeup
   exploits. I found no error after independent re-derivation of every inequality and
   machine verification of both lemmas; the disproof survives.

## Referee summary

I set out to break this proof and could not. The writeup's formalization matches the
source paper exactly (integer shifts, $B_d=\{d,\ldots,nd\}$, packing into $[m]$,
Conjecture 6 quoted verbatim from p.16). The argument is short, elementary, and
self-contained: a CRT core-overlap lemma (identical in mechanism to the one the paper
itself uses for its own lower bound), a weighted multiplicity bound for the families
$2p,3p,5p$, and a two-line measure count. Every step re-derived by hand checks out;
both lemmas were verified by exhaustive computation (3,893 configurations; 149 phase
patterns; max multiplicity exactly 1) and end-to-end on six explicit tight packings
of $B_1,\ldots,B_{35}$ with $n=500$; the constant $6619/5400$ is exact rational
arithmetic. The resulting bound $M_k(n)\ge(\frac{6619}{10800}-o(1))\frac{nk^2}{\ln k}$
strictly exceeds the conjectured $\frac{nk^2}{2\ln k}$ yet stays below the paper's
proven upper bound $\frac{\zeta(2)}{2}\frac{nk^2}{\ln k}$, so it is consistent with
everything proven while refuting the conjectured constant $1/2$. No prior resolution
of Conjecture 6 exists in the literature (the July 2026 follow-up arXiv:2607.06113
settles Conjecture 7, fixed $n$, only). Verdict: **CONFIRMED** — Conjecture 6 of
arXiv:2603.02786 is disproved in its first (and hence overall) clause.
