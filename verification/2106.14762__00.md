# Referee report: 2106.14762__00 (horizontal uniformity of the runsort permuton density)

## Interpretation

The open problem (Alon–Defant–Kravitz, arXiv:2106.14762, remark after Theorem 1.1) is
not a conjecture about an unknown truth: horizontal uniformity — that the absolutely
continuous density of the runsort permuton $R$ on $C^+=\{x<ye^{1-y}\}$ equals
$e^{y-1}$, independent of $x$ — is already proven in the source paper. The authors'
exact words (verified against the ar5iv full text) are:

> "More surprising is that the pointwise density of **R** in C⁺ depends only on the
> vertical coordinate y. This 'horizontal uniformity' is not combinatorially obvious,
> and we do not know how to establish it directly (that is, without recourse to the
> explicit characterization of **R**)."

So the problem is one of *proof technique*, and the authors' own parenthetical defines
"directly" as "without recourse to the explicit characterization of $R$". The writeup
formalizes exactly this target and honestly discusses the scope of "direct" in its
final section. Its mechanism: mark every entry by the first value $z$ of its ascending
run; the limiting per-position intensity of (starter $z$, value $y$) pairs off the
diagonal is $(1-z)e^{y-z}$; the size-biased run-starter density (which is the local
horizontal measure after sorting) is $r(z)=(1-z)e^{1-z}$; and the two-line identity
$(1-z)e^{y-z}=r(z)\,e^{y-1}$ makes the interior vertical profile $e^{y-1}$ regardless
of the starter, *before* the explicit position function $H(z)=ze^{1-z}$ is ever
computed. I verified against the paper (see Reference check) that this is **not** the
paper's own route: there, the enveloping curve $x=ye^{1-y}$ is established first
(Lemma/Prop 3.1 via expected positions $nye^{1-y}+O(\log n)$), and the density
$e^{y-1}$ falls out afterwards from a recursion for placement probabilities
$\tilde p_n(i,j)$ (Section 4) — that is the "indirect" derivation the authors wanted
to avoid. The writeup's derivation of uniformity (its Section 4, eqs. (14)–(16))
genuinely uses no explicit formula for $H$ or for $R$; $H(z)=ze^{1-z}$ is evaluated
only afterwards to identify the boundary.

Two interpretation caveats, neither disqualifying but both real:

1. **"Combinatorial."** The remark says horizontal uniformity is "not combinatorially
   obvious". A reasonable reading of the wish is for a combinatorial/bijective
   finite-$n$ explanation. The writeup delivers a probabilistic local-limit
   computation. It satisfies the parenthetical definition of "directly" to the letter,
   and the factorization "run profile = (size-biased starter density) × $e^{y-1}$" is
   a genuine structural explanation of *why* the density is flat in $x$ (the run
   profile above starter $z$ is $e^{y-z}$, the expected run length — i.e. the local
   horizontal scale — is $e^{1-z}$, and their ratio kills $z$). But whether
   Alon–Defant–Kravitz would consider their remark fully answered by an asymptotic
   probabilistic argument, rather than a bijection, is a judgment call. The writeup
   itself flags exactly this ("probabilistic-combinatorial local-limit proof rather
   than a finite-$n$ bijection") and sets `would_publish: false`.
2. **Reverse-engineering suspicion.** Identity (14) is equivalent to knowing the
   answer $e^{y-1}$, so one may suspect the argument was reverse-engineered from the
   known density. That does not affect validity: the proof as written is
   self-contained and never invokes the paper's characterization of $R$.

On balance the interpretation is the intended one; `interpretation_ok: true`.

## Reference check

- **arXiv:2106.14762, Theorem 1.1** (Alon, Defant, Kravitz, *The runsort permuton*;
  also published in Adv. Appl. Math.): fetched via ar5iv. States
  $R(B)=\int_{B\cap C^+}e^{y-1}\,dx\,dy+\int_{B\cap C}(1-y)\,dy$ with support
  $\{x\le ye^{1-y}\}$ — exactly the writeup's formula (1). Confirmed.
- **The open remark**: quoted verbatim above from the paper; matches the catalog
  statement, including the parenthetical defining "directly". Confirmed.
- **Novelty relative to the source paper's proof**: the paper's route is a martingale
  concentration (Azuma) plus expected-position analysis giving the enveloping curve
  $nye^{1-y}+O(\log n)$ first (Section 3), then a recursion for placement
  probabilities yielding $e^{y-1}$ (Section 4). The paper nowhere states the
  size-biased factorization $(1-z)e^{y-z}=[(1-z)e^{1-z}]\cdot e^{y-1}$, the starter
  intensity $(1-z)$, or the expected run length $e^{1-z}$ as such. The writeup's route
  is materially different. Confirmed via targeted ar5iv fetch.
- **No prior direct proof in the literature**: web search (Sept 2026) for a direct
  proof of the horizontal uniformity found only the original paper and its mirrors;
  consistent with the catalog's May 2026 review of 15 citing papers. No citation the
  writeup makes is unconfirmed (it cites nothing beyond the source paper).

## Caveats

1. **"Direct combinatorial" is a judgment call.** The proof satisfies the authors'
   parenthetical definition of "directly" (no recourse to the explicit
   characterization of $R$; $H(z)=ze^{1-z}$ is derived only afterwards, and
   uniformity — eq. (16) — is complete before it). But it is an asymptotic
   probabilistic argument, not a finite-$n$ bijection; a purist reading of
   "combinatorial" may want more. The writeup states this caveat itself.
2. **Step 3 is an outline.** Mean convergence of the truncated window statistic and
   the null-discontinuity-set point are omitted; both are routine.
3. **Convergence in probability only**, vs. a.s. in the paper; harmless for the
   stated goal, and the writeup says so, but the phrase "this identifies that limit
   with $R$" implicitly uses the paper's existence theorem.
4. **The underlying fact is already known** (proven indirectly in the source paper);
   the writeup's claim of "proved" refers to the proof-technique problem, which is the
   correct reading of the catalog entry. No prior direct proof was found in the
   literature, so this is not ALREADY_KNOWN in the relevant sense — but novelty of the
   factorization observation has not been exhaustively verified beyond the searches
   described.
5. **Edge cases** are unproblematic: position 1 is always a run starter (an $O(1/n)$
   boundary effect absorbed by (9)); $H'(1)=0$ does not break strict monotonicity of
   $H$; ties never occur in a permutation.
6. The identity (14) being "equivalent to the answer" means the proof explains the
   uniformity but was plausibly found knowing the target density; this affects taste,
   not validity.

## Referee summary

I set out to break this proof and could not. The argument is short, self-contained,
and correct: the limiting per-position law of (run-starter value, own value) is
$(1-z)dz\,\delta_z(dy)+\mathbf 1_{z<y}(1-z)e^{y-z}\,dz\,dy$; the elementary
factorization $(1-z)e^{y-z}=[(1-z)e^{1-z}]\cdot e^{y-1}$ says that off-diagonal mass
is (size-biased starter measure) $\times$ ($e^{y-1}\,dy$), and since runsort's
horizontal coordinate is exactly the size-biased starter CDF, the interior density is
$e^{y-1}$ independent of $x$ — established before any explicit formula for the
position function or for $R$ is computed. Every calculus identity and the full
limiting measure were verified by independent simulation at $n=10^6$ (interior density
flat in $x$ to within $2\%$ per bin and matching $e^{y-1}$ to $<0.5\%$; boundary atom
mass $(1-y)$ confirmed). The paper's own proof is materially different (curve first
via concentration, density second via a recursion), and no prior direct proof exists
in the literature searched. The verdict is MINOR_GAPS rather than CONFIRMED for two
reasons only: the concentration step (writeup Section 3) is a correct but compressed
outline whose routine details a journal would require, and the ultimate question —
whether a probabilistic local-limit argument fulfills the authors' wish for a
"direct" (and implicitly combinatorial) explanation — is a matter of judgment that
the writeup itself concedes, though it does meet the authors' own parenthetical
definition of "directly".
