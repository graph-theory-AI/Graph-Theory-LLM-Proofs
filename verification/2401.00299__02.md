## Interpretation

**Problem as posed.** Problem `2dim` of arXiv:2401.00299 (Problem 1.11 in v1/v2, Problem 1.9
in the catalog's numbering of the published version) reads: *"Determine the asymptotic
behaviour of the number f_2(d)"*, preceded by: *"It is natural to ask what happens if only
2-dimensional subcubes are allowed to be in a partition. We believe that the same bound as in
Proposition [zero2] holds."* Proposition `zero2` (v2 Proposition 1.10; folded into
Proposition 1.7 with r=2 in v3) states f_{0,2}(d) = N^{1+o(1)}, where n := 2^{d-1} and
N := (d/e)^n. So the authors' explicitly conjectured asymptotic is

    N^{1-o(1)} <= f_2(d) <= N^{1+o(1)},   i.e.   log_2 f_2(d) = (1+o(1)) n log_2 d.

**Writeup's formalization.** The writeup proves log f_2(d) = (1+o(1)) 2^{d-1} log d,
equivalently f_2(d) = d^{(1+o(1))2^{d-1}}. Since N^{1±o(1)} = 2^{(1±o(1)) n (log_2 d - log_2 e)}
and (log_2 e)/log_2 d -> 0, the statement "f_2(d) = d^{(1±o(1))n}" is *identical* to
"f_2(d) = N^{1±o(1)}" — the constant-per-vertex factor is absorbed by the o(1) in the
exponent in both directions. So the writeup proves exactly the bound the authors said they
believe, at exactly the precision at which Proposition `zero2` itself is stated. No
interpretation gaming: the writeup does not weaken the target, and it explicitly discloses
that second-order (2^{Theta(n)}) factors are not determined.

Two definitional points I checked against the paper: (i) the paper counts unordered
partitions whose classes "span a subhypercube"; for dimension 2 the vertex sets inducing a
Q_2 (an induced C_4) in Q_d are precisely the coordinate 2-faces, so the writeup's
"coordinate 2-dimensional subcubes" is the same object; (ii) f_2 = f_{{2}} and
f_{0,2} = f_{{0,2}} per the paper's f_S notation — matches the writeup's usage.

**Would the original authors consider it resolved?** At the precision they conjectured, yes:
the writeup establishes the stated belief. The literal phrase "determine the asymptotic
behaviour" is open-ended; a commented-out line in the paper's TeX ("and that even proving
2^{-(1/2+o(1))n} N may be possible, if true") shows the authors were also curious about the
finer exponential-in-n second-order term, which remains open and which the writeup honestly
disclaims. This affects scope, not correctness.

## Reference check

- **Source paper obtained directly**: I downloaded the TeX sources of all three arXiv
  versions of 2401.00299 (v1, v2, v3) and read the statements and proofs verbatim
  (scratchpad copies; the checks below quote the actual TeX).
- **"Proposition 1.10" (lower bound for f_{0,2})**: In v1/v2 the environment
  `zero2` is numbered **1.10** and states `f_{0,2}(d) = N^{1+o(1)}` (v2; v1 has the typo
  `N^{1-o(1)}`), with proof `f_{0,2}(d) >= binom(d,2)^{(1/2-o(1))n} = N^{1-o(1)}` via a
  counting nibble theorem. In v3 this is subsumed into Proposition 1.7 (`new`), now stated
  for every fixed r >= 2: `N^{r/2^{r-1}-o(1)} <= f_{0,r}(d) <= N^{r/2^{r-1}+o(1)}`; r=2
  gives the identical bound. **The writeup's quoted content is exactly what the paper
  proves**; the numbering "1.10" matches v1/v2 (and the catalog page itself cites
  "Proposition 1.10" for this bound), while v3 renumbers it. Confirmed.
- **"Proposition 1.6" (upper bound)**: Proposition `main` is numbered 1.6 in v2 and v3 and
  contains `f(d) <= (d+1)^n` (with n = 2^{d-1}); since a 2-face partition is a subcube
  partition, f_2(d) <= (d+1)^{2^{d-1}} as used. Confirmed. (In v1 this was Theorem 1.6 with
  the weaker `f(d) <= (5e)^n N` — still sufficient for the same asymptotic.)
- **One level deeper (not cited by the writeup but load-bearing for Prop. 1.10)**: the
  paper's proof uses a Frankl–Rödl-nibble counting theorem attributed to Asratian–Kuzjurin;
  that paper exists: "On the number of nearly perfect matchings in almost regular uniform
  hypergraphs", Discrete Mathematics 207 (1999), 1–8. The source paper is refereed
  (Illinois J. Math. 69(1), 109–122, 2025). I did not re-verify the nibble theorem's proof;
  per the catalog framing, the source paper's propositions are taken as ground truth.
- **Already-known check**: Semantic Scholar citations of 2401.00299 contain no paper
  resolving f_2(d); the only related follow-up is arXiv:2411.04479 (Tarannikov/Potapov
  line: partitions of Z_q^n into subcubes of dimension n-m, fixed m — a different regime).
  The bent-function paper arXiv:2108.00232 counts partitions of F_2^m into 2-dimensional
  **affine subspaces** (log_2 count ~ (m/2)2^m, a strictly larger family and much larger
  count), so it does not resolve f_2 either. I found no publication of the writeup's result;
  ALREADY_KNOWN does not apply.

## Caveats

1. **Precision of "asymptotic behaviour"**: the writeup determines the first-order
   asymptotic of log f_2(d) (equivalently f_2(d) = N^{1±o(1)}), which is precisely the
   bound the authors conjecture in the sentence introducing the Problem. It does not
   determine second-order 2^{Theta(n)} factors (cf. the commented-out remark in the paper's
   TeX about 2^{-(1/2+o(1))n} N); under a maximalist reading of "determine the asymptotic
   behaviour" that finer question remains open. The writeup states this limitation itself.
2. **Conditionality**: the lower bound inherits the source paper's Proposition 1.10
   (nibble-based, via Asratian–Kuzjurin 1999); the upper bound inherits Proposition 1.6.
   Both are in a refereed journal paper and were verified verbatim against the TeX, but were
   not re-proved from scratch here (the writeup also supplies its own valid elementary
   upper bound, so only the lower bound is genuinely external).
3. **Citation numbering is version-dependent**: "Proposition 1.10" is correct for arXiv
   v1/v2 (and the catalog page); in v3 the same statement is Proposition 1.7 with r=2. A
   published version of the writeup should cite by content or by the journal version.
4. **Small-d edge cases**: the lifting needs m = d-2 >= 1 and the bounds are asymptotic;
   inequality (2) is vacuous (RHS < 1) for small m, which is harmless. f_2(d) >= 1 for all
   d >= 2, so the liminf statement is well-posed.
5. **Convention**: logs must be read in a fixed base throughout (the paper uses base 2); the
   chain in the "Lower bound" section is base-independent as written. The identification
   Q_{m+2} = Q_m x Q_2 fixes the last two coordinates as the Q_2 factor; the decoding
   argument depends on this and is used consistently.
6. **Sociological note, not a finding**: the argument is strikingly simple for a problem
   left open by Alon, Balogh and Potapov; the most plausible explanation is that the authors
   regarded the finer (second-order) asymptotics as the real content of "determine the
   asymptotic behaviour". I searched for, and failed to find, any mathematical flaw, and
   found no prior publication of the observation.

## Referee summary

The writeup's single new ingredient is a lifting lemma: pigeonhole the {0,2}-partitions of
Q_{d-2} by their singleton set L (cost at most 2^{2^{d-2}} per layer, i.e. 2^d log 2 in total
after taking fourth powers — asymptotically negligible against 2^{d-1} log d), pick four
partitions with the same L, lay them on the four layers of Q_{d-2} x Q_2, and replace the
four aligned copies of each singleton by one vertical square. Every step of this argument is
correct as written; the decoding argument makes the count injective, so
f_2(d) >= (f_{0,2}(d-2)/2^{2^{d-2}})^4, and the source paper's Proposition 1.10
(f_{0,2}(m) >= N_m^{1-o(1)}, verified verbatim in the paper's TeX) then gives
log f_2(d) >= (1-o(1)) 2^{d-1} log d. The matching upper bound is the paper's Proposition
1.6 (also verified; the writeup's independent encoding bound is likewise valid). I re-derived
every inequality, confirmed both cited propositions against all three arXiv versions,
machine-verified the construction exhaustively (all 810,000 lifted 4-tuples at m=4 give
distinct valid square partitions of Q_6; exhaustive censuses f_2(2..5) = 1, 3, 30, 4380 agree
with all claims), and found no resolving prior work. The result proves exactly the asymptotic
the authors conjectured (f_2(d) = N^{1±o(1)}); only finer second-order asymptotics remain
open, as the writeup itself states. CONFIRMED.
