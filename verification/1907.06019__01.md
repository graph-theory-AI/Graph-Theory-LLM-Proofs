# Referee report: 1907.06019__01

Target: `attacks/1907.06019__01/output.md`, claiming to prove that for
$1 \le r, s < n/2$, every mutually annihilating pair $U \subseteq \bigwedge^r V$,
$W \subseteq \bigwedge^s V$ ($V = \mathbb{R}^n$) with
$\dim U \cdot \dim W = \binom{n-1}{r-1}\binom{n-1}{s-1}$ is of the form
$U = v \wedge \bigwedge^{r-1}V$, $W = v \wedge \bigwedge^{s-1}V$ for a common
nonzero $v \in V$.

## Interpretation

I downloaded the arXiv source of Scott–Wilmer (1907.06019v2) and checked the writeup
against it directly.

- Theorem 2.4 of the source (`prop:crossintspaces`) states exactly: $V=\mathbb{R}^n$,
  $1 \le r,s \le n/2$, $U \subseteq \bigwedge^r V$, $W \subseteq \bigwedge^s V$ with
  $u \wedge w = 0$ for all $u \in U$, $w \in W$ implies
  $\dim U \dim W \le \binom{n-1}{r-1}\binom{n-1}{s-1}$. This matches the bound the
  writeup takes as input.
- "Mutually annihilating" is defined in the source as $u\wedge w = 0$ for all
  $u \in U, w \in W$ — the writeup's hypothesis is identical.
- The open question is stated in the source, verbatim: "it would be interesting to
  characterize the extremal examples when $r,s<n/2$", immediately after exhibiting the
  equality example $U=\{v\wedge z : z \in \bigwedge^{r-1}V\}$,
  $W=\{v\wedge z: z \in \bigwedge^{s-1}V\}$.

The writeup answers exactly this question, in exactly the range asked ($r,s < n/2$
strict), with exactly the expected answer (the exhibited examples are the only ones).
No interpretation gaming: a reasonable author of the source paper would consider this
question resolved by the claimed theorem. The strict range is the right one to ask
about: at the boundary $r=s=n/2$ there are genuinely different extremizers (e.g.
$n=4$, $\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{B}=\binom{\{1,2,3\}}{2}$ gives $9 = \binom{3}{1}^2$
without being a star — verified computationally), so the exclusion is not an evasion.

The writeup restricts to $r,s \ge 1$; $r=0$ or $s=0$ makes the problem degenerate
(the bound involves $\binom{n-1}{-1}=0$) and is clearly outside the intended question.

   Note the monomial-span derivation is sound: spans of cross-intersecting families
   are mutually annihilating by the monomial multiplication rule.

   the extremal cross-intersecting pair for $n\ge 2r,2s$ except $n=2r=2s$; so Lemma 1

   mathematical, issue; the writeup's own proof is correct).

## Caveats

- **Range.** The theorem covers $1\le r,s<n/2$ only — precisely the range the source
  paper asks about. The boundary $r=n/2$ or $s=n/2$ (where Theorem 2.4's bound still
  holds but extra extremizers exist, e.g. $\bigwedge^{n/2}T$ with $\dim T=n-1$ when
  $r=s=n/2$) is explicitly not characterized, and the writeup says so.
- **Degenerate degrees.** $r=0$ or $s=0$ excluded by the statement ($1\le r,s$);
  the question is vacuous there.
- **Field.** Stated over $\mathbb{R}$, matching the source paper. (Nothing in the
  argument appears char-dependent, but the claim is only made over $\mathbb{R}$.)
- **Unstated routine facts** (all true, all one-liners): sections of shifted families
  are shifted (needed to apply the induction in Lemma 1); the compression process
  terminates; base case of the induction on $n$ is absorbed by the $r=1$/$s=1$ case.
- **Priority.** Lemma 1 duplicates Matsumoto–Tokushige (1989) without citation; the
  diagonal case ($U=W$, $r=s$) of the main theorem follows from Bulavka 2024. The
  writeup itself flags that it has not checked literature priority, and its verdict
  block honestly sets `would_publish: false`. The pair characterization itself
  appears to be new, per my searches and the catalog's review.
- The verdict block's one-line claim ("two full exterior stars generated by the same
  one-dimensional subspace") matches exactly what is proven — no over-claim.
