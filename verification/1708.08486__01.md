## Interpretation

The catalog statement reads "The bounds on the tower height of $n_p(\alpha,\beta)$ given in
Theorem 2 should also hold for all primes $p < 19$", extracted from the sentence in
arXiv:1708.08486 (v2, before Theorem 2): *"We conjecture these bounds should also hold for
p < 19. The upper bounds still hold when p < 19, as well as the lower bound when ε is small.
The only issue is the case ε is large. In this case, the lower bound construction fails as it
uses bounds on the largest subset of $\mathbb F_p^n$ without a three-term arithmetic
progression, and the known bounds for this are not good enough..."* (verified verbatim from
the fetched PDF).

The writeup refutes the conjecture **only for p = 2**, under the degenerate reading in which
the 3-AP $(x, x+d, x+2d)$ collapses to $(x, x+d, x)$ because $2d = 0$ in characteristic 2.
This is textbook interpretation gaming. Three independent lines of evidence show p = 2 is
outside the intended scope:

1. **The authors' own adjacent sentence excludes p = 2.** They assert that for $p < 19$ "the
   lower bound [holds] when ε is small", i.e. $n_p(\alpha,\beta)$ is a tower of $p$'s of
   height $\Theta(\log(1/\varepsilon))$ for fixed $\alpha$ as $\varepsilon\to 0$. By the
   writeup's *own* Proposition, $n_2(\alpha,\beta) \le \lceil\log_2\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\alpha^2-\beta}\rceil
   \le \log_2(2/\alpha)+1$ stays **bounded** as $\varepsilon\to0$ with $\alpha$ fixed — no
   tower. So if p = 2 were included, the authors' factual claim accompanying the conjecture
   would already be false; they evidently meant the odd primes $3,5,7,11,13,17$.
2. **Companion paper I is explicitly odd-prime.** The small-ε lower bound the conjecture
   sentence refers to is the main theorem of arXiv:1708.08482 ("Popular progression
   differences in vector spaces"), whose abstract states *"For a fixed odd prime p, we prove
   that the required dimension grows as an exponential tower of p's of height
   Θ(log(1/ε))"* (fetched and confirmed).
3. **The whole framework degenerates at p = 2.** The obstruction named in the conjecture
   (bounds on the largest 3-AP-free subset of $\mathbb F_p^n$ — cap sets) concerns $p\ge3$;
   the paper cites Alon–Shpilka–Umans "for $p \ge 3$ fixed"; and the paper notes Green's
   generalization of Theorem 1 to abelian groups requires **odd order**. Under the
   alternative "three distinct terms" convention, $n_2(\alpha,\beta)$ does not even exist
   (the writeup concedes this), contradicting Theorem 1's "there is a least positive
   integer" — again showing p = 2 was never part of the intended setting.

A reasonable author of arXiv:1708.08486 would emphatically **not** consider their conjecture
resolved by this writeup, and the writeup itself effectively admits this
(`"would_publish": false`; Section 4: "the substantive conjecture remains unresolved here").
The only literal-reading loophole is the single word "primes" instead of "odd primes" in an
informal prose sentence.

## Caveats

- **The decisive caveat is stated by the writeup itself** ("If the source has a standing
  odd-prime convention, only the catalog wording is refuted") — and the evidence above shows
  this caveat is the actual situation, yet the verdict block still announces
  `"disproved"` with `"confidence": "high"` for the catalog conjecture.
- The conjecture is informal prose ("We conjecture these bounds should also hold for
  p < 19"), with no formal environment; "these bounds" are Theorem 2's, stated only for
  $p\ge19$ with hypotheses ($0<\alpha\le1/2$, $\beta>\alpha^{3+e^{-133}}$), so any
  "extension" already requires interpretation. The natural one is the one the authors
  describe in the very next sentences — odd primes, with the large-ε cap-set obstruction.
- Under Theorem 2's literal hypothesis $\beta>\alpha^{3+e^{-133}}$, the pair
  $(\alpha_k,\alpha_k^3/2)$ enters the middle regime only for $k>e^{133}$; this does not
  affect the asymptotic contradiction (and Corollary 3 carries no such restriction), but the
  refutation is intrinsically asymptotic — no explicit finite counterexample dimension is
  produced (none is needed for a Θ-statement).
- Under the "three distinct terms" convention, $n_2(\alpha,\beta)=\infty$ and Green's
  Theorem 1 as quoted ("there is a least positive integer") would already be false at p = 2 —
  further evidence p = 2 was never in scope, as the writeup notes.
- Exhaustive computer verification was feasible only for $n\le4$ (and exact-fraction checks
  for $k\le60$); the Proposition's general proof was re-derived line by line by hand and is
  elementary.
- The odd primes below 19 are exactly $3,5,7,11,13,17$, as the writeup says; for these the
  problem remains open (consistent with the catalog's May 2026 literature review).

## Referee summary

The writeup is internally flawless: its Proposition ($n_2(\alpha,\beta)=\log_2(1/\alpha)+O(1)$
for the degenerate characteristic-2 reading of 3-APs), the exact evaluation
$n_2(2^{-k},2^{-3k-1})=k+1$, and the resulting contradiction with a p = 2 extension of
Theorem 2's tower-height lower bound all check out, by hand and by exhaustive computation.
But it resolves nothing the authors of arXiv:1708.08486 actually conjectured. The
conjecture's own surrounding sentences (the small-ε lower bound claimed to hold for p < 19
is false at p = 2 by the writeup's own Proposition), the explicitly odd-prime companion
paper arXiv:1708.08482, and the odd-order requirement in Green's framework all show the
intended scope is the odd primes $3,5,7,11,13,17$ — precisely the cases the writeup admits
it leaves open. The claimed verdict "disproved" therefore rests entirely on reading the
single word "primes" hyper-literally to smuggle in a degenerate case, i.e. interpretation
gaming; as a resolution of the catalog problem it is irreparably wrong, whence FATAL_ERROR
(with the internal mathematics itself being correct and even mildly interesting as a
footnote on why p = 2 is excluded from this literature).
