# Referee report: 1601.01886__00 — sharpness of the factor 2 in Lemma 4 of arXiv:1601.01886

Target: `attacks/1601.01886__00/output.md`
(claim: **proved** — the factor 2 in "every tree of pathwidth $k$ has a path-partition
of height at most $2k$" is unavoidable, via an explicit extremal family).

## Interpretation

**Source-paper conventions** (checked against arXiv:1601.01886v2, downloaded and read in full
for the relevant sections):

* Definitions section (p. 4): *"The height of a rooted tree $T$ is the maximum length of a
  path from the root to a leaf of $T$. (Thus $T$ has height 0 if it consists of a unique
  vertex.)"* — i.e. the paper counts **edges**, root at height 0.
* Section 4 (p. 6): a *path-partition* of a tree $T$ is a pair $(\mathcal T,\mathcal P)$
  where $\mathcal T$ is a rooted tree and $\mathcal P=\{P_x : x\in V(\mathcal T)\}$ is a
  collection of vertex-disjoint paths of $T$ partitioning $V(T)$ such that
  $xy\in E(\mathcal T)$ iff an edge of $T$ joins $P_x$ and $P_y$. This is exactly the
  writeup's rooted quotient tree, and the paper's "height of the path-partition" equals the
  writeup's $\lambda$ (edge-height), not the part-counting $H=\lambda+1$.
* Lemma 4 (p. 6): *"Every tree of pathwidth $k$ has a path-partition of height at most
  $2k$."* Immediately after its proof (pp. 7–8): *"…we made no effort to optimize the bound
  in Lemma 4 and we do not know whether the factor 2 is unavoidable."* This matches the
  catalog statement verbatim.

**Is the writeup answering the intended question?** Yes. It proves, for every $k\ge 1$:
(i) every tree of pathwidth $k$ has $\lambda\le 2k-1$ (slightly improving Lemma 4 by an
additive 1 in the paper's convention), and (ii) an explicit tree $T_k=D_{2k-1}$ with
$\mathrm{pw}(T_k)=k$ and $\lambda(T_k)=2k-1$. So in the paper's own (edge-counting)
convention the exact optimum is $2k-1$: the bound $2k$ of Lemma 4 can be lowered by exactly 1
and no further, and in particular no bound of the form $ck+O(1)$ with $c<2$ is possible.
That is an affirmative answer to "is the factor 2 unavoidable", together with the exact
extremal value; a reasonable author of the source paper would consider the question resolved.

One convention slip, honestly flagged by the writeup itself: the writeup's front-matter
one-liner ("minimum part-counting path-partition height $2k$") uses the part-counting
convention and guesses that this is "the usual formulation around Lemma 4". That guess is
wrong — the paper counts quotient edges — but the writeup's caveat field states exactly the
correct alternative ("if height counts quotient edges, the exact sharp value is $2k-1$ rather
than $2k$"), and the body proves both statements. No interpretation gaming: the two
normalizations differ by exactly 1 everywhere and the factor-2 question is resolved in
either. `interpretation_ok: true`.

## Computational check

Pathwidth was computed by three mutually independent methods: (a) an exact exponential
subset-DP computing the vertex-separation number (= pathwidth) for $n\le 22$; (b) a solver
based on the Ellis–Sudborough–Turner 3-branch characterization of tree pathwidth (memoized on

*certificates*: an explicitly constructed path-decomposition of the claimed width, validated
from first principles (interval property, edge coverage, width), plus a concrete 3-branch
lower-bound certificate (a vertex whose deletion leaves $\ge3$ components each containing a
canonical copy of the previous-level tree), grounded at machine-checked base cases.

## Caveats

1. **Convention (the writeup's own caveat, confirmed here against the source):** the paper's
   height counts quotient edges (root at height 0). In that convention the sharp value is
   $2k-1$, not $2k$; the writeup's headline "$H(T_k)=2k$" refers to the part-counting
   convention, which is *not* the paper's. Either way the factor 2 is unavoidable and the
   writeup states both normalizations correctly; but a published version should be phrased in
   the paper's convention: "Lemma 4's bound $2k$ can be improved to $2k-1$, and $2k-1$ is
   attained for every $k\ge1$."
2. Because the true optimum is $2k-1$ rather than $2k$ (paper convention), the literal bound
   "$2k$" of Lemma 4 is *not* attained; what is proven is that the multiplicative factor 2 is
   necessary (any valid bound is $\ge 2k-1$). This matches the intended question ("factor 2").
3. Edge cases are handled: $k\ge1$ assumed throughout (pathwidth-0 trees are single vertices,
   $\lambda=0$); $D_0=K_2$ noted; disconnected graphs are irrelevant (statement is about trees).
4. The claim $\mathrm{pw}(T_k)=k$ was machine-certified for $k\le5$ ($n\le3068$), the
   exhaustive path-deletion facts up to 764 vertices, and the exact $\lambda$ computation
   (all path-partitions) only up to $D_2$ ($2^{19}$ partitions); for general $k$ the result
   rests on the writeup's inductions, each of which I checked by hand and whose key
   combinatorial cases were verified exhaustively at these sizes. I consider the general
   proofs sound.
5. Novelty is not fully settled (see Reference check): the construction is close to classical
   complete-binary-tree examples for tree pathwidth, and the result could be folklore-adjacent,
   but no prior source stating it was found. This affects publishability, not correctness —
   consistent with the writeup's own `would_publish: false`.
6. The EST 3-branch characterization used by the fast pathwidth solver is itself a known
   theorem taken from the literature, but the solver was validated against a
   first-principles exact DP on all trees with $\le10$ vertices and on the small $R_h/D_h$
   instances, so no unverified theorem is load-bearing for the computational claims.
