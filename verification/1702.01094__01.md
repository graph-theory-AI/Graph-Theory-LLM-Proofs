## Interpretation

The catalog statement was checked against the actual arXiv source. I downloaded the
TeX source of arXiv:1702.01094 (Scott & Seymour, "Induced subgraphs of graphs with
large chromatic number. IX. Rainbow paths", v2) and located the question verbatim in
the Conclusion (source lines 249-253):

> "Let us also mention another question to which we do not know the answer. Let $G$
> be a triangle-free graph with very large chromatic number, and let $\mathcal{A}$
> be a set of stable subsets (not necessarily pairwise disjoint) of $V(G)$ with
> union $V(G)$. Does there necessarily exist an $s$-vertex induced path $P$ of $G$
> such that for each $v\in V(P)$, some $X\in \mathcal{A}$ satisfies
> $X\cap V(P)=\{v\}$?"

The extraction is word-for-word identical to the paper; no hypothesis was dropped.
The natural quantification (for each $s$, does some threshold on $\chi$ suffice?) is
exactly what the writeup negates: for every $s\ge 3$ and every $C$ it exhibits a
triangle-free (indeed huge-girth) connected $G$ with $\chi(G)>C$ and a stable-set
cover $\mathcal{A}$ admitting no such path of any length $\ge 3$.

This is **not** interpretation gaming. The parenthetical "(not necessarily pairwise
disjoint)" is the authors' own emphasis: the entire content of the question is
whether the rainbow-path theorem (the partition/proper-coloring case, which is the
paper's main theorem and is true) survives when overlap is allowed. The
counterexample exploits precisely the overlap the authors explicitly permitted, and
its cover is far from a partition, so it is consistent with the main theorem. A
reasonable author of the source paper would consider their question answered (in the
negative). Interpretation: fair and intended.

## Computational check

Scripts in `verification/scripts/1702.01094__01/`
(run with Python 3.14 + networkx 3.x in a venv):

- **`check_lemma1.py`** implements Lemma 1 exactly as written ($D(G)$, DSATUR
  proper coloring, color-increasing orientation, principal upsets) and brute-forces
  its claims on eight graphs satisfying the hypothesis odd-girth $>4q-3$: odd
  cycles $C_{13},C_{17},C_{21},C_{25}$ (all with $q=3$, $4q-3=9$), $P_{20}$,
  $C_{24}$, the Petersen graph with every edge subdivided into a 7-edge path
  ($n=100$, odd girth $35$, $q=3$), and a random 40-vertex tree ($q=6$,
  $4q-3=21$). On every graph: all $A_x$ stable (0 violations), union $=V(G)$,
  domination holds on every $D(G)$-edge (up to 120 edges), **every** induced path
  with $s=3,4,5,6$ vertices fails the private-cover property (e.g. $C_{25}$: 25
  induced paths per length, 0 pass; subdivided Petersen: 120/135/150 induced paths
  for $s=3/4/5$, 0 pass), while all $s=1,2$ paths pass, matching the writeup's
  trivial cases. A control run with a proper-coloring **partition** cover produced
  rainbow induced $P_3$'s wherever $\chi\ge3$ (e.g. 3 on each odd cycle, 16 on
  subdivided Petersen), confirming consistency with the Scott-Seymour theorem.
- **`check_lemma2_arith.py`** machine-checks the arithmetic of Lemma 2 for
  $d\in\{9,12,20,50,100,1000\}$: $(e/4)^d\le1/16$ (e.g. $3.09\times10^{-2}$ at
  $d=9$), $\sum_{\ell=3}^{g}d^\ell\le g d^g$, the $r$-bounds
  $5n\log d/d\le r-1<r\le7n\log d/d$, $\log(en/r)\le\log d$, the direct exponent
  evaluation confirming $r\log\frac{en}{r}-\frac{pr(r-1)}2\le-8n(\log d)^2/d$
  (e.g. $-7.87\times10^{10}\le-3.86\times10^{10}$ at $d=9$, $n=9\times10^9$), the
  identity $7-\tfrac12\cdot6\cdot5=-8$ in exact rationals, $4q-3<20d^2$ for
  $q=2d(2d-1)+1$, and $\chi$-bound algebra $\frac{n/2}{7n\log d/d}=\frac{d}{14\log d}$.

Both scripts print `ALL ... CHECKS PASSED`. Note the final counterexample graphs
themselves ($n\ge16\cdot20d^2\cdot d^{20d^2}$) are astronomically large and
existential, so they cannot be built; what the code verifies is the deterministic
core (Lemma 1, the load-bearing novelty) end-to-end on real graphs, plus every
inequality in the probabilistic part.

## Caveats

1. **Existential, not explicit.** The high-chromatic counterexamples come from the
   probabilistic method; no explicit family is given. This does not affect
   correctness (the writeup says so itself).
2. **Priority not fully excluded.** I found no prior resolution in searches or in
   the catalog's literature review, but a human check (e.g. asking Scott/Seymour or
   scanning all citing papers) is the only way to be certain the negative answer is
   new. The construction is simple enough that independent prior discovery is
   conceivable.
3. **Chernoff mean.** The degree is $\mathrm{Bin}(n-1,d/n)$ with mean slightly
   below $d$; the writeup's "stochastically dominated by a binomial of mean $d$"
   ($\mathrm{Bin}(n,d/n)$) is the correct and standard fix — cosmetic only.
4. **What survives of the question.** The counterexample cover has heavily nested
   members (chains of length up to $q\approx4d^2$), and each vertex can lie in many
   members. Restricted variants — covers of bounded multiplicity, or with a bound
   on the number of members containing each vertex — are untouched and remain open,
   as does the partition case (true, by the paper's main theorem).
5. **Strength of the refutation.** The failure is total: a single cover blocks all
   paths (induced or not) with $\ge3$ vertices, for graphs of arbitrarily large
   girth and chromatic number, so no weakening by fixing $s$ small (beyond $s\le2$)
   or imposing girth conditions on $G$ rescues the question.
6. **Quantifier reading.** "Very large chromatic number" is informal in the source;
   under any reasonable reading (threshold depending on $s$, or $\chi\to\infty$
   with $s$ fixed) the writeup's theorem negates it for every $s\ge3$.

## Referee summary

I set out to find the error and could not. The deterministic heart of the disproof
— Lemma 1's construction of a stable-set cover by principal upsets of a
color-increasing orientation of the distance-two graph, under the hypothesis
odd-girth $>4q-3$ — is correct, and I verified it exhaustively by computer on eight
graphs, including a 100-vertex subdivided Petersen graph: the cover is always
stable, always covering, and never admits a privately-covered induced path on 3 or
more vertices, exactly as claimed. The probabilistic Lemma 2 is a standard
Erdős-type argument with generous slack; every inequality was re-derived by hand
and machine-checked numerically. The question was confirmed verbatim against the
arXiv TeX source, including the crucial "(not necessarily pairwise disjoint)"
clause, so the interpretation is the authors' intended one and the negative answer
resolves it fairly — the counterexample cover is not a partition, so the paper's
main theorem is untouched. No prior resolution surfaced in literature searches.
Verdict: CONFIRMED (subject to a human priority check).
