## Interpretation

The writeup formalizes Problem 3.21 of arXiv:1812.02420 (Hochstättler, Schröder,
Steiner, DMTCS 22:1, 2020) exactly as the paper does. I downloaded the arXiv v4
LaTeX source (identical to the DMTCS version) and checked:

- **Digons are in scope.** The paper's conventions section states verbatim:
  "Graphs and digraphs in this paper are considered loopless, but are allowed to
  have multiple parallel and anti-parallel edges/arcs between vertices … a
  directed cycle of length two (a *digon*) is a pair of anti-parallel arcs in a
  digraph." Problem 3.21 takes "A (multi-)digraph D" as instance. So a
  reduction whose output contains digons is squarely within the intended
  problem — this is not interpretation gaming.
- **The definition of χ⃗_f matches.** The paper defines χ⃗_f(D) as the optimal
  value of the covering LP: min Σ_{A∈𝒜(D)} x_A subject to Σ_{A∋v} x_A ≥ 1 for
  all v, x ≥ 0, where 𝒜(D) is the family of vertex sets inducing acyclic
  subdigraphs. The writeup uses exactly this LP.
- **The intended open question is NP-hardness at p = 2, digons allowed.** The
  paper's conclusion says verbatim: "Theorem 3.20 furthermore does not treat
  the case p=2. Thus, we cannot rule out the possibility that there is some
  clever way to algorithmically decide whether a given digraph has fractional
  dichromatic number at most 2. Still, the authors strongly believe that using
  other techniques, it should be possible to prove NP-completeness also in this
  case." A proof of NP-completeness for multidigraph inputs is exactly what
  the authors asked for; a reasonable author of the paper would consider the
  problem resolved by this argument. (The writeup itself honestly flags that
  the oriented/digon-free variant is *not* settled — but that variant is not
  Problem 3.21.)

Why the authors' own techniques missed p = 2 is also visible in the source: their
p > 2 hardness reduces from χ_f(G) ≤ p for undirected graphs (NP-hard only for
p > 2; p = 2 is bipartiteness, polynomial), and their p ∈ (1,2) hardness pushes
values through the l-split operation, which maps any q > 1 to lq/((l−1)q+1) < 2.
Neither technique reaches exactly 2. The writeup's reduction from Bokal et al.'s
2-dichromatic hardness is precisely the kind of "other technique" the authors
anticipated.

## Caveats

- **Nonempty instance.** The forcing lemma needs V(D) ≠ ∅; the writeup assumes
  D nonempty. The empty digraph is a trivial yes-instance on both sides, so
  the reduction extends with a one-line remark. Cosmetic.
- **Loops.** The paper declares digraphs loopless, so the LP is always feasible;
  no issue.
- **Attainment of the LP optimum.** The argument tacitly uses that χ⃗_f ≤ 2
  yields an actual feasible solution of weight ≤ 2; true because χ⃗_f is a
  finite LP minimum (attained), consistent with the paper's own NP-membership
  proof. Not a gap.
- **Digons are essential** (flagged by the writeup itself): the complexity of
  deciding χ⃗_f ≤ 2 for *oriented* (digon-free) digraphs remains open. Problem
  3.21 explicitly allows multidigraphs, so this does not affect the verdict on
  the catalogued problem.
- **Surprising simplicity.** The reduction is elementary enough that its being
  missed for six years is mildly suspicious; this motivated the exhaustive
  hand re-derivation, the source-fetch of the paper, the literature search for
  prior resolution (none found), and the machine verification, all of which
  the argument survived. Residual risk is essentially limited to an unindexed
  prior appearance of the same trick (which would change the verdict to
  ALREADY_KNOWN, not to incorrect).
- **"Proved" semantics.** The catalog problem is a complexity question, not a
  yes/no conjecture; "proved" here means the open case p = 2 is resolved (as
  NP-complete, the outcome the authors predicted). The writeup's verdict block
  says exactly this.

## Referee summary

I set out assuming the writeup was wrong and failed to break it. The
interpretation is exactly the paper's: Problem 3.21 takes multidigraphs (the
source explicitly allows anti-parallel arcs and calls a digon a directed
2-cycle) and χ⃗_f is the acyclic-set covering LP, verbatim. The reduction —
two disjoint copies of D joined by a perfect matching of digons — is checked
step by step: the digon coverage/packing argument rigidly forces every support
set of a weight-2 fractional colouring to be a layer transversal, whose two
sides induce copies of D on complementary vertex sets, giving
χ⃗_f(R(D)) ≤ 2 ⟺ χ⃗(D) ≤ 2; combined with Bokal et al.'s 2004 NP-completeness
of 2-dichromatic colourability (confirmed) and the source paper's own NP
membership observation, NP-completeness of the p = 2 case follows. Independent
brute-force LP computation confirmed the equivalence on 125 instances,
including the digon-free Paley tournament (χ⃗_f(R(ST₇)) = 7/3) and bidirected
K₃ (χ⃗_f = 3). The paper's own techniques provably could not reach p = 2 (one
covers only p > 2, the other only p ∈ (1,2)), and its conclusion explicitly
solicits an NP-completeness proof "using other techniques," which this is. No
prior resolution was found in the literature. Verdict: CONFIRMED.
