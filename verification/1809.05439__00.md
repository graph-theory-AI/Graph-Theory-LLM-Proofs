# Referee report: 1809.05439__00

## Interpretation

The catalog statement is the informal remark following Theorem 5 of Dvořák–Hu,
*Fractional coloring of planar graphs of girth five* (arXiv:1809.05439): "It might
be possible to improve the ratio of extra colors assigned to the vertex $x$ in
Theorem 5 a bit; e.g., it could be true that there exists a coloring by subsets of
$\{1,\ldots,9\}$ such that all vertices get at least three colors and $x$ gets
five."

The decisive interpretation question is what Theorem 5 quantifies over, because the
writeup's counterexample is triangle-free but has girth 4. I downloaded the arXiv
TeX source (`nofrapl.tex`) and checked directly:

* All environments (`theorem`, `conjecture`, `corollary`, `lemma`, ...) share a
  single counter (`\newtheorem` block, lines 39–46).
* The numbered environments appear in order: Theorem 1 = Dvořák–Sereni–Volec
  (line 106), Conjecture 2 = `conj-main` (line 120), Theorem 3 = `frac-girth5`
  (line 128), Theorem 4 = `X-girth5` (line 137), **Theorem 5 = `x-tri-free`
  (line 147)**:

  > Let $G$ be a **triangle-free planar** graph. For any vertex $x\in V(G)$, the
  > graph $G$ has a set coloring $\varphi$ by subsets of $\{1,\ldots,6\}$ such that
  > $|\varphi(v)|\geq 2$ for $v\in V(G)$ and $|\varphi(x)|=3$.

* The informal conjecture in the source reads "improve the ratio of extra colors
  assigned to the vertex $x$ in Theorem~\ref{x-tri-free}", i.e. it is explicitly
  about the **triangle-free planar** theorem, not the girth-5 theorem (Theorem 4).

So the writeup's formalization — for every triangle-free planar $G$ and every
$x\in V(G)$, a set coloring by subsets of $[9]$ with all vertices $\geq 3$ colors
and $|\varphi(x)|=5$ — is exactly the intended reading. This is not interpretation
gaming: the authors themselves chose the triangle-free setting for Theorem 5 (and
note in the paper that girth 5 is only needed for the multi-vertex version). A
reasonable author would consider the concrete statement they suggested ("e.g., it
could be true that ...") refuted by this counterexample. The catalog title's
"girth-five wheels" phrasing is a slight misnomer inherited from the paper title;
the subdivided wheel obstruction happens to have girth 5, but the conjecture's
domain is triangle-free planar graphs. (See Caveats for what the counterexample
does *not* close.)

The implicit reduction that "$x$ gets five" may be taken as exactly five is
harmless: shrinking color sets preserves propriety, and the independent SAT check
below used $\lvert\varphi(x)\rvert\geq 5$ directly and still found no coloring.

## Computational check

Script: `/Users/viennot/dev/Graph-Theory-LLM-Proofs/verification/scripts/1809.05439__00/verify.py`
(Python 3.14, networkx 3.6.1, python-sat; runtime ~37 s). Results:

* **Structure of $G$**: 23 vertices, 35 edges, `check_planarity` = True, zero
  3-cycles, girth = 4. All as claimed.
* **Lemma rigidity (exhaustive)**: with $\varphi(x)=\{0,\dots,4\}$ fixed (WLOG by
  color relabeling) and every other vertex getting exactly 3 colors (WLOG by
  shrinking), $H$ admits exactly **28,800** proper colorings, and **all 28,800**
  satisfy Lemma claims (1), (2), (3) and the consequence
  $\varphi(u_i)\cup\varphi(u_{i+1})=Q$ — zero violations. In particular $H$ alone
  is colorable, so the gadget composition is genuinely needed.
* **Nonexistence for $G$ (exact decomposition)**: the two copies of the rooted
  gadget $H{+}z$ share only $x$ and interact only through the edge $z_1z_2$, so
  $G$ is colorable iff two achievable $\varphi(z)$ values are disjoint. Exhaustive
  enumeration of all **288,000** colorings of $H{+}z$ yields exactly **10**
  achievable $\varphi(z)$ values — precisely the $\binom{5}{3}=10$ triples inside
  $P$ (all $\subseteq P$, matching step C5) — and **no disjoint pair** exists.
  Hence $G$ has no valid coloring.
* **Independent SAT double-check (no WLOG reductions)**: direct CNF encoding of
  the original statement (adjacent vertices share no color; every vertex $\geq 3$
  colors; $x\geq 5$ colors, from 9 colors) on $G$: **UNSAT**. Controls: same
  encoding with $x\geq 4$ on $G$ is SAT, and $x\geq 5$ on a single copy $H$ is
  SAT — so the encoding is not vacuously unsatisfiable.

The failed-coloring claim is therefore verified by two independent methods.

## Caveats

1. **Girth-5 variant untouched.** $G$ has girth 4; the analogous statement for
   planar graphs of girth $\geq 5$ remains open. The writeup states this clearly
   in its caveats and Scope section.
2. **Only the concrete "e.g." statement (and its exact-$5/9$ scalings) is
   refuted.** The authors' remark has two layers: the vague "improve the ratio a
   bit" and the concrete example ($k=9$, all $\geq 3$, $x$ gets 5). The
   counterexample kills the concrete example, and the same counting argument
   scales to any $(k,\,k/3,\,5k/9)$ instance (the count $2a+5(k-a)=10b$ is tight
   exactly when $a/k=5/9$, $b=k/3$). It does **not** rule out intermediate
   improvements, e.g. $x$-ratio strictly between $1/2$ and $5/9$ (say $k=36$, all
   $\geq 12$, $x$ gets 19), where the counting bound has slack ($123>120$) and the
   rigidity lemma fails. Since the catalog's extracted statement is precisely the
   concrete example, the verdict "disproved" is correct for the target as
   cataloged, but the broader informal question of whether *any* improvement over
   the $1/2$ ratio of Theorem 5 is possible for triangle-free planar graphs is not
   settled by this construction.
3. **"$x$ gets five" exact vs at least.** Both readings are refuted: the SAT check
   used $\lvert\varphi(x)\rvert\geq 5$ directly, and shrinking reduces $\geq$ to
   $=$ in any case.
4. The writeup's prose embedding argument for planarity is somewhat informal
   (blocks in disjoint disks meeting at $x$), but planarity was confirmed
   computationally, so nothing rests on it.
5. The catalog page notes "the bound formula in the surrounding text may be
   garbled" — checked against the TeX source: it is not garbled
   ($k/3 + 2k/9 = 5k/9$), and the writeup's Lemma is exactly the tightness
   analysis of that bound.

## Referee summary

I set out assuming the writeup was wrong and failed to break it. The
interpretation is the intended one: the TeX source of arXiv:1809.05439 confirms
that Theorem 5 (label `x-tri-free`) quantifies over triangle-free planar graphs
and that the informal conjecture is attached to that theorem, so a triangle-free
girth-4 counterexample is fair play. Every step of the rigidity lemma and the
gluing argument checks out by hand, and the finite construction was verified by
brute force twice over: an exhaustive enumeration (28,800/28,800 colorings of the
gadget obey the lemma; 288,000 colorings of the rooted gadget yield only the 10
triples inside $P$ as possible $\varphi(z)$, no disjoint pair) and an independent
SAT encoding of the unreduced statement (UNSAT, with satisfiable controls). The
only genuine limitations — the girth-5 variant, and ratio improvements strictly
between $1/2$ and $5/9$, both of which remain open — are outside the cataloged
statement, and the first is already flagged by the writeup itself.
