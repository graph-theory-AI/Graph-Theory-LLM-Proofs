# Referee report: 2005.09767__00 (Conjecture 1.10 of "Many flows in the group connectivity setting")

Target claim: **proved**. The writeup claims exponentially many ($c^n$) $\Gamma$-flows
avoiding an arbitrary forbidden function $f$ on every 3-edge-connected oriented graph, for
every abelian $\Gamma$ with $|\Gamma|\ge 6$, by settling the two cases left open by
DeVos–Langhede–Mohar–Šámal (arXiv:2005.09767): $\mathbb Z_7$ and $\mathbb Z_6$.

## Interpretation

The writeup attacks exactly the catalog statement, which I confirmed verbatim against the
arXiv source (Conjecture 1.10 of arXiv:2005.09767): fixed $c>1$, all 3-edge-connected
oriented graphs of order $n$, all abelian $\Gamma$ with $|\Gamma|\ge6$, all
$f:E\to\Gamma$, at least $c^n$ flows $\phi$ with $\phi(e)\ne f(e)$ everywhere. There is no
interpretation gaming:

- $n$ is the number of vertices (order), used correctly throughout; the final bounds are
  $7^{n/4+1}$ for $\mathbb Z_7$ and $3^{n/12+2/3}$ for $\mathbb Z_6$, both of the required
  $c^n$ shape.
- The setting is multigraphs (as required in flow theory); the arguments nowhere assume
  simplicity, and my computational checks include parallel edges and a loop.
- The reduction to $\mathbb Z_6,\mathbb Z_7$ via the classification of abelian groups of
  order 6 and 7 is correct ($\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_3\cong\mathbb Z_6$; order 7 is
  prime), and group connectivity depends on $\Gamma$ only up to isomorphism.
- The degenerate one-vertex edgeless graph is explicitly excluded by the standard
  convention, which the writeup itself flags (its caveat 3). A reasonable author of the
  source paper would consider this the intended conjecture.

## Caveats

1. **Small-$n$ uniformity of $c_{\ge8}$** (step 12): Theorem 1.9's stated bound is $\le1$
   for some 3-edge-connected graphs with $n\le3$ (e.g. $n=2$, $\ell=1$, $k=8$), so the
   "uniform constant furnished by Theorem 1.9" needs a two-line patch for $n\le3$
   (shift along a parallel pair: $\ge|\Gamma|-2$ flows). Routine; also inherent in the
   source paper's own claim that Theorem 1.9 confirms the conjecture for $|\Gamma|\ge8$.
   The writeup's *new* bounds ($7^{n/4+1}$, $3^{n/12+2/3}$) have no such slack: they are
   $>1$ and of the form $c^n$ for every $n\ge2$.
2. **Degenerate conventions**: the one-vertex edgeless graph would falsify the literal
   statement ($N=1<c^1$); the writeup flags this itself, and the standard convention
   (3-edge-connected $\Rightarrow n\ge2$) excludes it. With $n\ge2$ everything goes
   through, loops and parallel edges included (checked).
3. **Attribution**: Lemma 1 is the Alon–Füredi/footprint bound and should be cited, not
   presented as new; Lemma 2 (Eulerian $\pm1$ lift) is folklore. Cosmetic.
4. **Extraordinariness**: if correct, this elementary argument (JLPT + Alon–Füredi for
   $\mathbb Z_7$; a short three-family CRT argument beating Theorem 1.11's
   $2^{\sqrt\ell/\log\ell}$ by an exponential margin for $\mathbb Z_6$) resolves a
   conjecture that four experts left open in 2020 and that remained open through the
   catalog's May 2026 review. The tools were fully available to the authors, which is a
   sociological red flag even though I found no mathematical error. Computational
   verification necessarily covers only small graphs; the general-case steps rest on my
   hand-verification. Independent expert review is strongly advised before announcing.
5. **Novelty is unverified**: I found no prior appearance of this argument or any
   resolution of Conjecture 1.10, but web/citation coverage of very recent preprints is
   imperfect.

## Referee summary

I set out assuming the writeup was wrong and failed to break it. The interpretation is
the intended one. The $\mathbb Z_7$ case is a five-line application of the (correct,
classical, machine-re-verified) Alon–Füredi footprint bound to the product polynomial
$\prod_e(L_e-f(e))$ on the $r$-dimensional $\mathbb F_7$-flow space, seeded by the JLPT
existence theorem: $N\ge7^{r-m/6}\ge7^{n/4+1}$. The $\mathbb Z_6$ case translates by one
admissible flow, splits $\mathbb Z_6\cong\mathbb F_2\times\mathbb F_3$, and produces
three families of admissible flows whose sizes cannot all be small, giving
$N\ge3^{(3m-4n+4)/6}\ge3^{n/12+2/3}$; I re-derived every inequality and verified the
construction member-by-member in code on ten graphs (including multigraphs and a loop),
with exhaustive minimization over all $f$ on eight graph/group pairs — zero violations,
and the bounds held with large margins. Both cited inputs (JLPT; Theorem 1.9 of the
source) check out against the arXiv text. The single defect found is the small-$n$
slack in deriving a uniform constant from Theorem 1.9 for $|\Gamma|\ge8$ (step 12),
which is trivially repairable and inherited from the source's own reading. Hence
MINOR_GAPS rather than CONFIRMED; confidence is medium only because the claim, if
correct, resolves a six-year-old expert conjecture with tools its authors possessed —
a base-rate warning that no local check can fully discharge.
