## Interpretation

**Verbatim source statement (checked against the arXiv v3 TeX source, Section 9
"Open Problems"):**

> Let $G$ be a graph on $n$ vertices with minimum vertex degree $\Omega(n)$.
> Then $G$ satisfies $\E[F] = \Theta(\log(n))$.

surrounded by: "A large family of examples of graphs on $n$ vertices with
$\E[F]=\Theta(n)$ are given in [CHMMS22]. However all of these examples have
maximum degree $O(1)$ ... We were unable to find any examples of dense graphs
with such a large number of average faces, which leads us to the next
conjecture." This matches the catalog text exactly.

**Simple vs multigraph.** The paper uses "multigraph" explicitly when it means
multigraphs (Theorems 1.11 vs 1.12) and says "However, we are mostly interested
in simple graphs." The writeup proves the conjecture for finite simple graphs
and additionally shows (Section 5, verified computationally below) that the
multigraph reading is trivially FALSE (a chain of $n$-edge dipoles joined by
bridges is a connected multigraph with $\delta \ge n$ and deterministically
$F \ge n/2+1$). Since the source authors certainly knew that dipoles/multistars
produce many faces, the simple-graph reading is unquestionably the intended one.
The writeup's interpretation is the intended interpretation, and a reasonable
author of the source paper would consider their conjecture resolved by the
writeup's theorem (two-sided bounds $\tfrac12\log n - O_c(1) \le \E F \le
(2/c^2+o_c(1))\log n$ uniformly over simple $G$ with $\delta(G)\ge cn$;
$c$-dependence of the $\Theta$-constants is inherent in an $\Omega(n)$
hypothesis).

**Connectivity.** The source defines random 2-cell embeddings for connected
graphs. $\delta\ge cn$ permits up to $\lfloor 1/c\rfloor$ components; the
writeup is silent on this, but its upper bound is per-dart and its lower bound
uses "each connected component contributes an eigenvalue 1", so both bounds
hold verbatim for disconnected $G$ (faces summed over components). Convention
gap only, not a mathematical one.

## Reference check

The proof is fully self-contained: it invokes no external theorem (its only
"standard" ingredient, the deferred-decision Lemma 2.1, is proved in the
writeup and verified here by exhaustive enumeration). Source-paper facts were
checked against the arXiv v3 TeX source (downloaded from
https://arxiv.org/e-print/2211.01032v3):

- **Conjecture 9.3**: confirmed verbatim in Section 9 (`\section{Open Problems}`),
  exactly as quoted in prompt.md, third conjecture of the section, with the
  surrounding context about dense graphs and the CHMMS22 $\Theta(n)$ sparse
  (multistar, i.e. multigraph) examples.
- **Theorem 1.8** (`thm:logBound`): confirmed — $\E[F(n)]\le3.65\ln n$ for
  $n\ge e^{e^{16}}$ (plus $5\ln n$ / $10\ln n+2$ regimes); lower bound
  $\tfrac12\ln n-2$ appears as the separate lower-bound theorem. So the
  catalog's "Theorem 1.8 confirms the conjecture for $K_n$" is accurate.
- **Mauk–Stahl conjecture** (restated in Section 9): $\E F(K_n)=2\ln n+O(1)$ —
  relevant because the writeup's $K_n$ specialization $(2+o(1))\ln n$ matches
  this conjectured optimal constant (a strong but not contradictory claim).
- **Model conventions**: the paper defines random embeddings via uniform local
  rotations of connected graphs — same model as the writeup; "graph" vs
  "multigraph" usage confirms the simple-graph reading of Conjecture 9.3.
- **Follow-up literature**: two web searches (Sept 2026) found no paper
  resolving or claiming Conjecture 9.3 for general dense graphs, consistent
  with the catalog's four-search audit of 2026-05. Not ALREADY_KNOWN.

## Caveats

1. **§3.2 off-by-one (the "minor gap").** "Lemma 2.1 leaves at least
   $d_u-\lfloor\theta d_u\rfloor$ candidate darts" should be
   $d_u-\lfloor\theta d_u\rfloor-1$; the displayed intermediate bound
   $1/((1-\theta)\delta)$ in (3.2) is a hair too strong as written, but the
   only bound used downstream, $2/\delta$, holds for all large $n$. Routine
   repair.
2. **Implicit large-$n$ thresholds.** Several steps need $n\ge n_0(c)$
   ($\theta\delta\ge2$, $(\tfrac12-\theta)\delta\ge1$, $K\ge2$, $\delta>1$ for
   the no-short-faces claim). Harmless for a $\Theta(\log n)$ statement, but
   the theorem's "uniformly over all simple graphs" should be read as "for
   $n\ge n_0(c)$".
3. **Connectivity.** The source's model is stated for connected graphs; the
   writeup silently covers disconnected $G$ too (both bounds go through), but
   never says so.
4. **$c$-dependent constants.** Upper constant $2/c^2$ blows up as $c\to0$;
   this is inherent to the $\Omega(n)$ hypothesis and consistent with the
   conjecture's $\Theta$.
5. **Strength of the byproducts.** If correct, the argument (a) improves the
   source's hard-won $K_n$ upper constant from $3.65$ to $2$, matching the
   Mauk–Stahl conjectured constant, and (b) improves the source's
   $\ln^2n+1/p$ bound for dense $G(n,p)$ to $O_p(\log n)$. That a ~4-page
   elementary argument beats a 55-page SODA paper's constants is the main
   reason for residual skepticism; however, the mechanism (light/heavy face
   split making the folklore $1/\delta^2$ closing heuristic rigorous, plus a
   spectral count of short simple facial cycles) is exactly the kind of clean
   idea that can be missed, every step survives detailed scrutiny, and all
   quantitative predictions match independent numerics.
6. **(4.5) is stated but never used** — cosmetic.
7. The writeup's own caveat that the multigraph statement is false is correct
   and computationally confirmed; it does not affect the verdict since the
   intended reading is simple graphs.

## Referee summary

I approached this as a rejection-seeking referee and failed to find a
substantive error. The formalization matches the source's Conjecture 9.3 under
its clearly intended simple-graph reading (verbatim statement confirmed from
the arXiv v3 TeX). Every lemma was re-derived in full: the exposure lemma and
the facial-cycle probability formula were verified *exactly* by exhaustive
enumeration; Lemma 3.1 — the crux of the upper bound, whose conditioning-on-
lightness step is the most delicate point — checks out analytically
(decomposition over compatible histories) and is exactly tight in small cases;
the heavy/short/long face bookkeeping and all arithmetic ($e/25$, $nK^2/\delta^2
\le1/16$, harmonic sums) re-derive correctly; the spectral lower bound handles
negative eigenvalues and bipartite components correctly via the even/odd
pairing. Monte Carlo at $n$ up to 800 matches all quantitative predictions,
including the striking one — $\E F(K_n)\approx2\ln n-0.9$ versus the claimed
upper bound $2\ln n+O(\sqrt{\log n})$. The only literal defects are a one-unit
admissible-count slip in §3.2 (conclusion unaffected for large $n$) and
implicit $n\ge n_0(c)$ thresholds, hence MINOR_GAPS rather than CONFIRMED.
Because the result would also improve the source paper's $K_n$ constant to the
conjectured Mauk–Stahl optimum, I recommend an independent expert pass before
treating the conjecture as resolved, but I could not refute the proof.
