## Interpretation

The catalog extraction of Question 1.7 reads "G is $H$-free but $G+e$ has an induced
subgraph isomorphic to $C_{2t-2}$", with $H$ never defined. I fetched arXiv:2505.24100v2
(PDF, page 4) and confirmed this is a **verbatim quote of the paper**: Question 1.7
(and Question 1.8) contain the same "$H$-free" phrasing, a leftover of the paper's
convention of fixing $t \ge 5$ and writing $H = C_{2t-2}$ throughout. The intended
reading is unambiguous from the paper: $G$ is $C_{2t-2}$-free (no *induced* $C_{2t-2}$,
per the paper's definition of $H$-free on page 1), has at least one non-edge, and
adding any non-edge creates an induced $C_{2t-2}$.

The writeup adopts exactly this interpretation ($H = C_{2t-2}$), flags the typo
honestly in its caveats, and proves the statement for **all** even cycles of length
$\ge 6$ (all $k \ge 3$, where the target cycle is $C_{2k}$ and $k = t-1$), which
covers $t \ge 6$ with room to spare. No interpretation gaming: a reasonable author of
the source paper would consider Question 1.7 answered affirmatively by the writeup's
theorem. `interpretation_ok: true`.

## Reference check

The writeup is fully self-contained: it invokes no external theorems, so there is
nothing internal to falsify. `references_ok: true`. However, the literature search
required by this review turned up decisive **priority** findings:

1. **Source question confirmed.** arXiv:2505.24100v2 (Fan, S. Hajebi, S. Hajebi,
   Spirkl), fetched as PDF: Question 1.7 on page 4 matches the catalog extraction
   verbatim (including the "$H$-free" typo). Theorem 1.6 (the deletion half) and the
   surrounding discussion (icosahedron for $C_4$, $C_5 \square C_5$ for $C_6$,
   dodecahedron for $C_8$, a 28-vertex graph for $C_{10}$) are as the catalog says.

2. **The question was already answered — in 2016, before it was posed.**
   Craig M. Tennenhouse, "Induced Subgraph Saturated Graphs", *Theory and
   Applications of Graphs* 3(2) (2016), Article 1
   (https://digitalcommons.georgiasouthern.edu/tag/vol3/iss2/1/). Its definition,
   quoted from the journal page: "A graph $G$ is said to be *induced H-saturated* if
   $G$ contains no induced subgraph isomorphic to $H$ and the addition of any edge to
   $G$ results in an induced copy of $H$" — precisely the property of Question 1.7 —
   and the paper constructs non-complete such graphs **for all cycles** (reported
   form: for any $k \ge 3$ and $n \ge 3(k-2)$ there is a non-complete induced
   $C_k$-saturated graph of order $n$).

3. **The source paper's own authors acknowledge this.** In their follow-up
   "Asymmetric induced saturation", arXiv:2606.24763 (Fan, S. Hajebi, S. Hajebi,
   Spirkl, June 23, 2026), Theorem 1.5 states "All even cycles are addition-normal"
   and is explicitly credited to Tennenhouse (2016). So the authors of Question 1.7
   themselves now treat the addition half as settled by prior work.

4. **The question is also subsumed by a newer, stronger result.** Ilkyoo Choi,
   "Induced-saturated graphs exist for even cycles", arXiv:2608.24202 (submitted
   August 25, 2026), Theorem 1.1: for every $q \ge 3$ there is a
   $C_{2q+2}$-induced-saturated graph (both halves simultaneously), via an $8q$-vertex
   block construction unrelated to the writeup's. This settles the full induced
   saturation problem for all even cycles of length $\ge 8$, hence in particular
   Question 1.7 for every $t \ge 6$.

I found no prior appearance of the specific construction $K_k \square C_{2k-1}$ for
the addition half; the writeup's construction appears to be new and is considerably
simpler (and gives a cleaner "every non-edge is an induced-$P_{2k}$ endpoint pair"
property) than what I could see of Tennenhouse's, but the *theorem* is not new.

## Caveats

- **$H$ undefined in the extracted statement.** This is a faithful transcription of a
  typo in the source paper itself; the writeup's interpretation $H = C_{2t-2}$ is the
  only sensible one and matches the paper's context. Not a defect of the writeup.
- **Only the addition half.** The writeup proves nothing about edge deletion and says
  so explicitly; $K_{t-1} \square C_{2t-3}$ is very unlikely to be
  $C_{2t-2}$-induced-saturated in the full sense (deleting a horizontal edge inside a
  clique level need not create an induced $C_{2t-2}$; not checked, not claimed).
- **Range of $t$.** Question 1.7 asks $t \ge 6$; the writeup proves all $k \ge 3$,
  i.e., all even cycles $C_{2k}$, $2k \ge 6$. Strictly stronger; no small-case gap.
  (For $2k = 4$ the construction degenerates: $m = 3$, and the claim is not made.)
- **Priority.** The writeup's caveat field says "makes no literature-priority claim",
  which turns out to be prescient: the statement proved was already a theorem of
  Tennenhouse (2016), posed as open in 2505.24100 apparently because the authors
  overlooked that paper — as they implicitly concede by crediting Tennenhouse for
  exactly this result in arXiv:2606.24763 (Theorem 1.5). The catalog's "open / high
  confidence" status (auto-review 2026-05-14) was therefore incorrect even at review
  time, and doubly so today given Choi's arXiv:2608.24202 (2026-08-25).
- The ALREADY_KNOWN sources are, respectively, a 2016 peer-reviewed journal paper
  (Tennenhouse) reaffirmed by the question's own authors, and an unrefereed 9-day-old
  preprint (Choi); the verdict rests on the former, with the latter as corroboration.

## Referee summary

The construction $K_{t-1} \square C_{2t-3}$ is correct: the induced-$C_{2t-2}$-freeness
argument (projection of vertical edges to a closed trail in the odd cycle, plus a
column-parity contradiction for length $m+1$) is airtight, the explicit induced-path
construction for every non-edge is feasible and induced exactly as claimed, and
computer verification for $k = 3,\dots,8$ (exhaustive for $k \le 6$,

Question 1.7) confirmed every claim, down to re-building the writeup's own
switch-level path recipe for all 13483 non-edges across the six graphs. The proof would
merit CONFIRMED on internal grounds. However, the question itself was not open:
Tennenhouse (2016) had already constructed non-complete induced $C_k$-saturated graphs
(in exactly the edge-addition sense of Question 1.7) for all cycles, a fact the source
paper's authors themselves now cite as Theorem 1.5 of arXiv:2606.24763, and Choi's
arXiv:2608.24202 (August 2026) has since resolved the full induced saturation problem
for all even cycles of length at least 8. The correct verdict is therefore
ALREADY_KNOWN, with the note that the writeup's construction is simpler than the known
ones and, to my knowledge, new as a construction.
