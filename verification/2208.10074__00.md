## Interpretation

The catalog statement (id 2208.10074__00) reads: every $n$-vertex graph in a hereditary
class with $O(n^{1-\epsilon})$ separators is a subgraph of $H \boxtimes K_{O(n^{1-\epsilon})}$
with $H$ of **bounded tree-depth**. This is a catalog extraction error. I fetched
arXiv:2208.10074 (Dvořák–Wood, v4) and confirmed:

- The paper's actual conjecture, **Open Problem 6**, asks for **bounded tree-width**:
  "For any hereditary graph class $\mathcal G$ with separation-number
  $\mathrm{sep}(\mathcal G) \in O(n^{1-\epsilon})$, does there exist a constant
  $c = c(\mathcal G)$ such that every $n$-vertex graph $G \in \mathcal G$ is contained in
  $H \boxtimes K_m$, where $\mathrm{tw}(H) \le c$ and $m \in O(n^{1-\epsilon})$."
- The paper's **Theorem 20** states: for fixed $d \ge 2$, if every $d$-dimensional grid
  graph $G^d_n$ is contained in $H \boxtimes K_s$ with $\mathrm{td}(H) \le t(n)$ and
  $s \le c\,n^{1-1/d}$, then $t(n) \in \Omega(\log\log n)$.
- Immediately after Theorem 20 the paper says, verbatim: "Note that Theorem 20 implies
  that the strengthening of Open Problem 6 with tree-width replaced by tree-depth is
  false."

The writeup takes the catalog statement literally and disproves the tree-depth version
via $N \times N$ grids — i.e., it re-proves Theorem 20 for $d = 2$. A reasonable author
of the source paper would absolutely **not** consider their conjecture resolved: they
themselves proved this lower bound (via Lemma 18, an isoperimetric argument on grids),
and their genuine open problem (tree-width) is explicitly left untouched by the writeup.
To its credit, the writeup itself flags this in its Section 5 and its caveats field
("the actual tree-width conjecture is not addressed here"), and does not claim to have
resolved the paper's real conjecture. Note also that even the catalog's own context
paragraph records that the $\mathrm{td}(H) \in O(\log\log n)$ limitation was "shown to
be best possible via isoperimetric inequalities for grid graphs" — i.e., the catalog
carried the refutation of its own (mis-extracted) headline statement as context.

So: the interpretation of the literal catalog text is accurate, but the catalog text is
not the intended open conjecture, hence `interpretation_ok: false`. The claimed
"disproved" verdict applies only to the mis-extracted statement, and that disproof is
already in the literature — in the very source paper.

## Caveats

1. **The headline caveat**: the disproved statement is a catalog mis-extraction. The
   source paper's actual conjecture (Open Problem 6) requires bounded **tree-width**
   and remains open; the writeup proves nothing about it (and says so).
2. **Not new**: the disproof is Theorem 20 of the source paper (d = 2 case), with
   essentially the same isoperimetric method; the paper draws the exact conclusion the
   writeup draws. The writeup presents it as a new refutation without noting the
   overlap — it noticed the tree-width/tree-depth discrepancy in the abstract but not
   that the paper had already proved this lower bound.
3. **S7 wording gap** (repairable in one line): Section 3's restatement of the
   colouring property omits "at least once", which is needed for the removed colours
   to be distinct; Lemma 1 as proved does supply it.
4. Minor conventions, all handled consistently: perimeter measured in $\mathbb Z^2$
   (so $p(V(Q_N)) = 4N$); tree-depth = height of the witnessing forest (depths
   $1..t$, hence $\le t$ colours); "sufficiently large $N$" thresholds depend only on
   $C, t$. Degenerate cases ($A \subseteq R$, disconnected remainders, $t = 1$) cause
   no trouble.
5. The writeup's quantitative constant is weaker than the paper's (Lemma 18 gives an
   explicit $n^{1/(d(d^t-1))}$-type bound; the writeup gives $\log_2\log N - O_C(1)$
   with an unspecified $O_C(1)$), but both yield $\Omega(\log\log n)$.
