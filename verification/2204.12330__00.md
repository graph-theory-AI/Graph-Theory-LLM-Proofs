# Referee report: 2204.12330__00

Target: conjecture of Bonnet–Geniet–Tessera–Thomassé, *Twin-width VII: groups*
(arXiv:2204.12330), Section 1.2: "uniform twin-width is strictly stronger than
twin-width, i.e. there are groups with finite twin-width and infinite uniform
twin-width", with the group `Sym_fin(Z)` of finitely supported permutations of
`Z` as the proposed candidate. The writeup claims **proved**, with witness
`Sym_fin(Z)` (and the finitary alternating group).

## Interpretation

I downloaded the full TeX source of arXiv:2204.12330v2 and compared the
writeup's formalization against the paper's definitions line by line.

* **The conjecture** (source lines 293–298): "We conjecture that uniform
  twin-width is strictly stronger than twin-width, i.e. there are groups with
  finite twin-width and infinite uniform twin-width. A candidate is the group
  of finitely supported permutations on Z." The body restates it for the
  lampshuffler `Sym_fin(Z) ⋊ T`, which has the same uniform twin-width as
  `Sym_fin(Z)` by the paper's `cor:semidirect-product`. The writeup's target
  is exactly the intended statement, with exactly the proposed witness.
* **Finite twin-width of a group** (paper, Section "Matrix characterisation"):
  a group `Γ` has finite twin-width iff its action on itself by product has
  finite twin-width w.r.t. some total order `<`, meaning
  `stww(bijection matrix of g)` is finite for **every** `g` — "but possibly
  arbitrarily large" (per-element bounds). This is verbatim the paper's
  alternative definition, explicitly stated to generalize to non-finitely
  generated groups (via `lem:grid-theorem-group`), so it applies to the
  locally finite, non-f.g. group `Sym_fin(Z)`. This matches the writeup's
  Section 1 bullet 1.
* **Uniform twin-width** (paper, Section 4.2):
  `utww(Γ) = min over orders < of sup over g of stww_{(Γ,<)}(g)`. So
  `utww(Γ) = ∞` iff for **every** order, the element widths are unbounded —
  exactly what the writeup's Section 5 proves. This matches bullet 2.
* **Grid vs twin-width**: the paper's `k`-grid is a `k×k` division (rows and
  columns each partitioned into `k` order-intervals) with all zones nonzero;
  `thm:grid-theorem-matrix` gives, for matrices of degree ≤ d (bijection
  matrices have degree 1), `stww(M) ≤ f(gn(M))` with `f(n) = 2^{O(n)}` and
  `gn(M) ≤ g(stww(M))` with `g(n) ~ 2n`, and for infinite matrices "stww
  finite iff grid number finite". Crucially `g` is a fixed function, so a
  uniform stww bound forces a uniform grid bound — which is what Section 5's
  contradiction needs — and a per-element grid bound gives a per-element stww
  bound — which is what Section 2 needs. Both directions are used with the
  correct quantifiers.
* **One convention mismatch, harmless**: the paper defines the self-action by
  *right* product; the writeup uses *left* multiplication and says "left and
  right multiplication are interchangeable throughout". This is true: the
  inversion map `x ↦ x^{-1}` is an order-isomorphism from `(G,≺')` to `(G,≺)`
  (where `a ≺' b ⇔ a^{-1} ≺ b^{-1}`) carrying the matrix of right-product by
  `g` to the matrix of left-product by `g^{-1}`, so the two sups over
  elements, and hence the two min-over-orders values, coincide. Routine but
  unproved in the writeup (noted as a minor gap below).
* **No interpretation gaming.** In fact the source paper itself
  (`lem:utww-finite-groups` + `lem:group-limit`/`cor:utww-infinite-gen`)
  reduces its conjecture to the question "is uniform twin-width unbounded over
  finite groups?", and the writeup answers precisely that question through the
  finite subgroups `A_n ≤ Sym_fin(Z)`. A reasonable author of the original
  paper would consider the conjecture resolved by this argument — and indeed
  the authors' question was resolved in the literature by exactly this route
  (see Reference check).

## Reference check

* **arXiv:2204.12330v2 (source paper)** — I downloaded the actual TeX source
  (`arxiv.org/e-print/2204.12330v2`) rather than trusting the writeup. All
  invoked facts exist and state what the writeup uses:
  * conjecture + candidate `Sym_fin(Z)` — intro, Section 1.2, as catalogued;
  * definition of (uniform) twin-width of group actions via ordered bijection
    matrices, `min` over orders / `sup` over elements — Section "Matrix
    characterisation", exactly the writeup's bullets (modulo left/right);
  * grid number and the grid theorem `thm:grid-theorem-matrix` (variant of
    Twin-width I, Theorem 10 / Guillemot–Marx), with `f(n) = 2^{O(n)}`,
    `g(n) ~ 2n`, degree-bounded matrices, infinite case by sup over finite
    submatrices — confirms both conversion directions the writeup needs;
  * heredity of grids under finite submatrices (`rmk:grid-witness`) —
    supports S5.2 verbatim;
  * `lem:subgroup`, `lem:group-limit`, `lem:utww-finite-groups` — confirm the
    paper's own reduction of the conjecture to unboundedness of `utww` over
    finite groups, i.e. the writeup attacks the intended core.
* **Gowers-type mixing (inequality (1))** — not cited by name in the writeup
  but proved from Schur orthogonality; it is the classical quasirandomness
  inequality (Gowers 2008; Babai–Nikolov–Pyber). Verified numerically.
* **Priority — the decisive finding**: **arXiv:2608.10150, G. Kontogeorgiou
  and B. Miraftab, "Quasirandomness and Uniform Twin-Width", submitted
  10 August 2026**, proves: quasirandom degree gives a polynomial lower bound
  `c·D_qr(G)^{1/4} ≤ utw(G)` for nontrivial finite groups (same exponent 1/4
  as the writeup's (3)), uniform twin-width is unbounded over finite groups
  (via alternating groups, their Corollary 22), and there exist groups —
  including finitely presented ones (Houghton groups `H_n`, `n ≥ 3`,
  Thompson's `V`; the lampshuffler is their `H_2`) and the finitary
  alternating group `FAlt(Z)` — with finite twin-width and infinite uniform
  twin-width, explicitly answering the Bonnet–Geniet–Tessera–Thomassé
  question. This is precisely the conjecture of this attack, resolved in the
  same direction by the same method. The catalog's literature review
  (auto-reviewed 2026-05-15) predates that paper, which is why the problem
  was still listed as open. The writeup's own caveat line ("literature
  priority is unchecked") anticipated this.

## Caveats

1. **Priority (the reason for the verdict).** The separation was published
   three weeks before this review in arXiv:2608.10150 (10 Aug 2026). Whether
   the writeup was generated before or after that date, the result is in the
   literature now, so it cannot be scored as a new resolution. Mathematically
   I found nothing wrong with the writeup — absent 2608.10150 my verdict
   would have been CONFIRMED (with the one minor left/right gap noted).
2. **Left vs right action.** The paper defines the self-action by right
   product; the writeup works with left multiplication and dismisses the
   difference in one sentence. The repair (inversion anti-isomorphism plus
   transported order) is standard and included above.
3. **Grid vs division conventions.** The writeup's `r`-grid (convex sets, not
   necessarily a full division) is weaker than the paper's (full `k×k`
   division); the writeup uses the weak form where it must *exclude* grids
   (safe) and completes to full divisions where it must *exhibit* them
   (correct). The ≥ r-ones-per-cell strengthening additionally covers
   mixed-minor and rank-division formulations, so no convention variant
   rescues a bounded order.
4. **Constants are not optimized** (acknowledged in the writeup): `D > 16r⁴`
   and `N ≥ 8r³` make the lemma vacuous below roughly `n ≈ 5200` at `r = 3`;
   the numerics confirm small cases genuinely need the hypothesis. Only
   finiteness vs infiniteness is claimed, so this is harmless.
5. **Novelty of the easy half.** Finite twin-width of `Sym_fin(Z)` was
   already proved in the source paper (lampshuffler argument + subgroup
   monotonicity); Section 2 of the writeup is an independent, correct
   re-derivation, and its general "locally finite ⇒ finite tww" claim also
   follows from the paper's `lem:group-limit` machinery.
6. **Edge cases.** n ≥ 5 is required for simplicity of `A_n` and is assumed
   where used; `r ≥ 2` in the mixed-grid remark is stated; the identity
   element (whose `L_g` has no 2-grid off-diagonal) plays no role since only
   existence of bad elements is needed; the countability of `Sym_fin(Z)` is
   used implicitly and holds.
7. The numeric value of `tww(Sym_fin(Z))` is not computed (only finiteness),
   consistent with the paper's remark that ordinary twin-width of a group is
   not a well-defined single number.

## Referee summary

I set out assuming the writeup was wrong and failed to break it. The
interpretation is exactly the intended conjecture — indeed the source paper's
own Proposition (`lem:utww-finite-groups`) reduces the conjecture to
unboundedness of uniform twin-width over finite groups, and the writeup proves
precisely that, via a correct Gowers-type mixing lemma
(`U(H) = Ω(D(H)^{1/4})`), a correct elementary bound `D(A_n) ≥ ⌊n/4⌋`, a
correct coset-convex order giving finite ordinary twin-width for locally
finite groups, and a correct heredity argument lifting grids from `A_n` to
`Sym_fin(Z)` under every order. Every step checked out by hand; every finite
ingredient checked out by machine; every citation to the source paper was
verified against its TeX source. However, the identical theorem — same
exponent 1/4, same alternating-group witnesses, same quasirandomness method —
appears in Kontogeorgiou–Miraftab, "Quasirandomness and Uniform Twin-Width",
arXiv:2608.10150 (10 August 2026), which explicitly answers the
Bonnet–Geniet–Tessera–Thomassé question. The result is therefore correct but
already known: verdict **ALREADY_KNOWN** (mathematically the writeup would
otherwise merit CONFIRMED, modulo the routine left/right-action remark).
