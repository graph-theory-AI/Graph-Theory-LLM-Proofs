## Interpretation

This is the decisive issue for this writeup, so I checked the source paper itself
(arXiv:2009.03418, Mohar, "On a conjecture by Anthony Hill"; PDF fetched and read
in full).

**What the paper actually says.** Section 2 defines, for integers n >= 5 and
0 <= t <= n/2, M_{n,t} = K_n minus a matching of size t. Theorem 3 is stated for
a set P of **k >= 3 points** on the sphere and the **n = 2k** points obtained by
adding antipodes; it gives a geodesic drawing D_n of M_n = M_{2k,k} with exactly
(1/4)k(k-1)(k-2)(k-3) crossings, each added antipodal half-circle contributing
(1/2)(k-1)(k-2) more. Corollary 4 (verbatim): "If a set P' ⊆ P has strength s and
t = |P \ P'|, then the drawing D_n(P) can be extended to a drawing of the graph
M_{n,t} with H(n) - (1/2)t(k-1)(k-2) + s crossings." Here, inescapably, **n = 2k**.
Conjecture 5 follows immediately (verbatim, in full): "The crossing number of
M_{n,t} is equal to H(n) - (1/2)t(k-1)(k-2)." The symbol k is **not** defined in
the conjecture statement; it inherits its meaning from the immediately preceding
Theorem 3 / Corollary 4, i.e. **n = 2k is even**. The clause "where
k = floor(n/2)" appearing in the catalog's extracted statement **is a catalog
addition, not in the paper**.

**Why the even-n reading is clearly the intended one.** Under the catalog's
floor(n/2) reading, the conjecture is already trivially false at the smallest
value in the paper's own range: for n = 5, t = 1 we get k = 2 and predicted value
H(5) - (1/2)(1)(1)(0) = 1, while K_5 - e is planar (cr = 0). It also contradicts
the value cr(K_7 - e) = 6, which has been in the literature since Zheng et al.
(2007) determined cr(K_n \ e) for n <= 8. No author — least of all Mohar, an
expert on crossing numbers — conjectures something refuted by a planar graph in
its first admissible case and by published tables. Moreover, Mohar's motivating
construction (Corollary 4 plus the abundance of strength-0 sets, Lemma 7) only
produces drawings of even-order graphs, so the "upper bound matching the
conjecture" that motivates it exists only for even n.

**Verdict on interpretation.** The writeup disproves the catalog's literal
extraction (odd n included), all of whose counterexamples are at odd n (n = 7,
and the infinite family n = 2k-1). It proves nothing about any even-n instance.
A reasonable author of the original paper would **not** consider Conjecture 5
resolved by this writeup: the intended conjecture — cr(M_{2k,t}) =
H(2k) - (1/2)t(k-1)(k-2) for n = 2k even, 0 <= t <= k — is untouched and remains
open (it contains the Hill conjecture itself as the case t = 0). The writeup's own
caveat line ("an intended restriction to even n ... would be a different
conjecture") shows awareness of this, yet it still declares "disproved" with high
confidence. This is precisely the interpretation-gaming failure mode: the refuted
statement is an artifact of catalog transcription.

## Reference check

- **arXiv:2009.03418 (Mohar, "On a conjecture by Anthony Hill")** — fetched the
  PDF and read all 6 pages. Theorem 3 and Corollary 4 exist and state exactly the
  upper bound the writeup uses in Section 4 (drawing of M_{n,t}, n = 2k, with
  H(n) - (1/2)t(k-1)(k-2) + s crossings; s = 0 available by Lemma 7). Conjecture 5

  with **no** "k = floor(n/2)" clause — confirming both that the writeup's quoted
  upper bound is genuine (references_ok) and that the extracted statement's
  odd-n scope is a catalog artifact (interpretation not ok).
- **H(n)** — the writeup's H(7) = 9 and the closed forms H(2k) =
  (1/4)k(k-1)^2(k-2), H(2k-1) = (1/4)(k-1)^2(k-2)^2 match the paper's eq. (1);
  re-verified numerically for k = 3..50.
- **cr(K_7 - e) = 6** — corroborated by the literature: Zheng, Lin, Yang et al.
  (2007) determined cr(K_n \ e) with equality for n <= 8, giving
  (1/4)*4*3*2*1 = 6 at n = 7 (also reported in Clancy–Haynes–Newcombe's survey
  of known crossing numbers, arXiv:1901.05155). So the writeup's centerpiece
  value is correct — and was already known, meaning the literal floor-reading
  statement was already contradicted by published work before this writeup.
- No other external results are invoked; the lower bounds are self-contained
  (Euler bound plus counting).
- Web searches found no published resolution of Conjecture 5 itself; the intended
  even-n conjecture appears to remain open (consistent with the catalog's status).

## Caveats

- **The refuted statement is not the paper's conjecture.** "k = floor(n/2)" was
  inserted during catalog extraction; in the paper, k is bound by the context
  n = 2k of Theorem 3 / Corollary 4. Every counterexample in the writeup has odd
  n; no even-n instance is addressed. The intended conjecture — for n = 2k even,
  0 <= t <= k — remains open.
- **The literal floor-reading was never seriously open.** It fails already at
  n = 5, t = 1 (K_5 - e is planar, prediction 1) and was contradicted by the
  published value cr(K_7 - e) = 6 (Zheng et al. 2007) well before 2020. Its
  "disproof" is therefore both a strawman kill and essentially already known.
- **The writeup half-acknowledges the problem** in its caveats field and Scope
  section ("an intended restriction to even n ... would be a different
  conjecture") but still outputs verdict "disproved" with high confidence and
  "no gap in the disproof". Under the intended reading the correct verdict
  would have been "not resolved".
- Ambient conventions are otherwise fine: good-drawing assumptions in S8/S13 are
  standard; the geodesic drawings of Theorem 3 have no adjacent-edge crossings
  (two shorter geodesic arcs sharing an endpoint meet only there, given general
  position), so the 2k - 4 survival count in S13 is sound; A_k need not be an
  integer for the averaging bound to hold (it is one anyway).
- A further unexplored reading: Section 2 of the paper is titled "On the
  geodesic crossing number of K_n - I_t"; if Conjecture 5 were about the
  spherical geodesic crossing number, the writeup's non-geodesic drawings would
  be irrelevant even at odd n. The plain reading of "the crossing number" in
  Conjecture 5 is the ordinary one, so I do not rest the verdict on this, but it
  reinforces that the writeup attacks the least charitable formalization.
- The t = 0 case of the intended conjecture is the Hill conjecture for even n
  itself, so a genuine full resolution was never plausible in either direction
  without resolving Hill's conjecture.

## Referee summary

The writeup's mathematics is flawless and fully verified: exact-arithmetic
realizations of its three octahedron-plus-apex drawings give exactly 3, 4, and 6
crossings, the Euler and vertex-deletion counting lower bounds are correct, so
cr(M_{7,1}) = 6, cr(M_{7,2}) = 4, cr(M_{7,3}) = 3, and the Section 4 algebra
(Q_k - A_k = (k-1)(k-2)/2 > 0) is an exact identity. But everything it refutes
lives at odd n, and the "k = floor(n/2)" clause that makes odd n admissible was
added by the catalog, not by Mohar: in the paper, Conjecture 5 directly follows
Theorem 3 and Corollary 4, where n = 2k is even, and the floor-reading is
trivially false at n = 5 (planar K_5 - e) and was already contradicted by the
published value cr(K_7 - e) = 6. No reasonable author would consider Conjecture 5
resolved by this writeup. The intended even-n conjecture is untouched and remains
open; the claimed disproof resolves a transcription artifact.
