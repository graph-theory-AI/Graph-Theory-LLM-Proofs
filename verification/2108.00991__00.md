## Interpretation

The catalog statement is Conjecture 3 of Conlon–Fox–Sudakov–Wei (arXiv:2108.00991, EJC 107 (2023)): for sufficiently large $k$, $m(P_k)=k!/2$ if $k$ is even and $m(P_k)=\frac{k-1}{4}(k-1)!$ if $k$ is odd, where $m(H)$ is the threshold Ramsey multiplicity — the minimum, over all 2-colorings of $K_{r(H)}$, of the number of monochromatic (unlabeled, not-necessarily-induced) copies of $H$, and $P_k$ is the path on $k$ vertices. I fetched the source paper (ar5iv rendering of 2108.00991) and confirmed the conjecture statement verbatim, the definition of $m(H)$, and the extremal colorings $\chi(a,b)$ (two blue cliques $A$, $B$ with all cross-edges red; $a_0=k-1$, $b_0=\lfloor k/2\rfloor-1$), for which the paper claims exactly $k!/2$ (even $k$, colorings $\chi(a_0+1,b_0)$ and $\chi(a_0,b_0+1)$) resp. $\frac{k-1}{4}(k-1)!$ (odd $k$, $\chi(a_0,b_0+1)$) monochromatic $P_k$.

The writeup interprets the conjecture exactly as intended: it exhibits, for every even $k\ge 4$ and every odd $k\ge 5$, an explicit 2-coloring of $K_{r(P_k)}$ with strictly fewer monochromatic $P_k$ than the conjectured value, so $m(P_k)$ is strictly below the conjectured value for all large $k$ of both parities. This is not a strawman literal reading: the "sufficiently large $k$" quantifier is met because the strict inequality holds for **every** $k$ beyond the base cases, and the copy-counting convention (unlabeled subgraph copies) is demonstrably the paper's own — my brute-force counter reproduces the paper's stated counts $k!/2$ and $\frac{k-1}{4}(k-1)!$ for the paper's colorings $\chi(a_0+1,b_0)$ and $\chi(a_0,b_0+1)$ under exactly this convention. A reasonable author of the original paper would consider the conjecture refuted — and indeed the actual literature now records it as disproved (see Reference check). Interpretation: OK.

## Reference check

- **Conjecture 3 statement** (arXiv:2108.00991v2, Conlon–Fox–Sudakov–Wei, *Threshold Ramsey multiplicity for paths and even cycles*): fetched via ar5iv. Verbatim: "For sufficiently large k, if k is even, then m(P_k)=k!/2 and if k is odd, then m(P_k)=(k−1)/4·(k−1)!." Matches the catalog extraction (including the line-break-reconstructed $\frac{k-1}{4}$). The paper's definition of $m(H)$ (minimum number of monochromatic copies over 2-colorings of $K_{r(H)}$) and its claim that $\chi(a_0+1,b_0)$, $\chi(a_0,b_0+1)$ achieve exactly the conjectured counts were both confirmed — and the latter counts were reproduced by my code, pinning down the copy-counting convention. CONFIRMED.
- **$r(P_k)=k+\lfloor k/2\rfloor-1$**: Gerencsér–Gyárfás 1967; restated as Theorem 1 in arXiv:2606.01996 ($r(P_t)=t-1+\lfloor t/2\rfloor$). CONFIRMED.
- **Hamiltonian-path counts in $K_m$** ($m!/2$ total, $(m-1)!$ through a fixed edge) and **edge-transitivity of $K_{2\ell,\ell}$**: standard; re-derived and verified numerically. CONFIRMED.
- **Prior art (decisive for the verdict)**: Ting Huang, Jiabao Yang, Yaojun Chen, *On the threshold Ramsey multiplicity conjectures for paths and even cycles*, arXiv:2606.01996, posted 1 June 2026. I fetched the PDF. Their Conjecture 2 is exactly this catalog conjecture (stated as $m(P_{2t+1})=\frac{t}{2}(2t)!$ and $m(P_{2t})=\frac{(2t)!}{2}$, equivalent to the catalog forms), and their abstract states "These results disprove two conjectures on the threshold Ramsey multiplicity for even cycles and paths, due to Conlon, Fox, Sudakov, and Wei." Their Theorem 5 gives, for **every** $t\ge3$, $m(P_{2t})\le \frac{(2t)!}{2}-\lfloor t^2/4\rfloor(2t-2)!$ (asymptotically $(\frac78+o(1))\frac{(2t)!}{2}$), strictly stronger than the writeup's even bound $\frac{(2t)!}{2}-(t-1)(2t-2)!$ for $t\ge4$ (they coincide at $t=3$: both give 312 for $k=6$). Their Theorem 4 gives $m(P_{2t+1})\le t^{-1/(1+\sqrt2)+o(1)}\frac{t}{2}(2t)!$, polynomially below the conjectured value, versus the writeup's $(1-O(1/t))$-factor saving. Their constructions are described as "the same Ramsey-critical split idea" with a local defect — the writeup's even-path perturbation is essentially the $t=1$ case of their deleted-blue-star defect. So the writeup's disproof is correct but subsumed. This paper postdates the catalog's auto-review (2026-05-15), which is why the catalog still listed the problem as open.

## Caveats

- **"Sufficiently large $k$" quantifier.** Small-$k$ counterexamples alone would not disprove the conjecture; the writeup correctly handles this by proving a strict deficit $-(\ell-1)(2\ell-2)!$ resp. $-(\ell-1)(2\ell-1)!$ for every $\ell\ge2$, i.e. for all even $k\ge4$ and odd $k\ge5$.
- **Copy-counting convention.** The disproof is convention-sensitive in principle (labeled vs unlabeled copies): under a labeled count the perturbation would *not* beat $k!/2$. The writeup flags this in its caveats, and the convention was verified to be the paper's own (the paper's stated extremal counts are reproduced under the unlabeled convention).
- **Improvement size.** The writeup's saving is only a $(1-\Theta(1/k))$ factor, far from the truth: Huang–Yang–Chen show a constant-factor ($7/8$) saving for even paths and a polynomial-factor saving for odd paths. The writeup honestly states it does not determine $m(P_k)$.
- **Base cases.** For even $\ell=2$ ($k=4$), $B$ is a single vertex and $K_B$ is empty; the argument still goes through (verified: total 10). For odd $\ell=2$ ($k=5$), $Z$ is a single vertex; likewise fine (verified: total 18). The constructions need $\ell\ge2$; $k\le3$ is outside the conjecture's scope anyway.
- **Staleness of the catalog.** The catalog's "open, high confidence" status was accurate as of its 2026-05-15 auto-review but is now outdated: the conjecture was disproved on 2026-06-01 (arXiv:2606.01996). The writeup does not cite this paper; whether it was produced independently cannot be determined from the writeup itself.

## Referee summary

I set out to break this disproof and could not. Every red and blue count, every closed-form simplification, and both example computations were re-derived by hand and confirmed by independent brute-force enumeration for $k=4$ through $8$; the two tersely justified structural claims (the run-pattern of blue paths in each construction) were reconstructed in full and hold. The interpretation is exactly the intended one — the copy-counting convention was pinned down by reproducing the source paper's own claimed extremal counts — and the "sufficiently large $k$" quantifier is properly handled by a for-all-$k$ deficit. Exhaustive search even shows the constructions are optimal at $k=4,5$ ($m(P_4)=10$, $m(P_5)=18$). The only reason this is not CONFIRMED is priority: Conjecture 3 of Conlon–Fox–Sudakov–Wei was already disproved by Huang, Yang and Chen (arXiv:2606.01996, 1 June 2026, after the catalog's last review), via the same critical-split-with-local-defect idea and with strictly stronger upper bounds ($(7/8+o(1))\,k!/2$ for even $k$; a $k^{-1/(1+\sqrt2)+o(1)}$ factor for odd $k$). The writeup is a correct, weaker, independent-looking rediscovery of a result now in the literature.
