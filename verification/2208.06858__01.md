# Referee report: 2208.06858__01

Target: Conjecture 2.2 of Alon–Friedgut–Kalai–Kindler, arXiv:2208.06858
("The success probability in Levine's hat problem, and independent sets in
graphs"): $p_{\mathrm{monotone}}(t) \to 0$ as $t \to \infty$.

Writeup's claim: **disproved** — a block-tribes construction gives balanced
monotone strategies with success probability arbitrarily close to $1/2$ for
every fixed $t$, so $p_{\mathrm{monotone}}(t) = 1/2$ for all $t$.

## Interpretation

I fetched the source paper (arXiv:2208.06858v2, PDF read in full for Sections
1–2) and compared the writeup's formalization against it verbatim.

The paper (Section 2, pp. 3–4) defines: a strategy for the $t$-player game is a
$t$-tuple $(f_1,\dots,f_t)$ with $f_i : B^{t-1} \to \mathcal{W}$; the winning
set of the strategy is $\{x : \forall i,\ x_i \in f_i(x^{-i})\}$;
$p(t,n) := \max_{W \in \mathcal{W}^{(t)}} \mu(W)$ and
$p(t) := \lim_{n\to\infty} p(t,n)$. For the monotone relaxation, $\mathcal{W}$
is "the set of all balanced monotone families in $\{0,1\}^n$, i.e. all $W$
containing precisely half the points in $\{0,1\}^n$ such that if $x \in W$ and
$y_i \ge x_i$ for all $i$ then $y \in W$." Conjecture 2.2 is exactly
"$p_{monotone}(t)$ tends to 0 as $t$ grows."

The writeup's formalization — player $i$, after seeing $x_{-i}$, selects an
arbitrary balanced upward-closed family $\mathcal A_i(x_{-i})$, succeeding when
$x_i \in \mathcal A_i(x_{-i})$; equivalently every $i$-fiber of the common
success set is contained in a balanced monotone family — **matches the paper's
definition exactly**. This is not a strawman reading: the family is allowed to
depend arbitrarily on the observed stacks in the paper's own definition, and
Conjecture 2.2 is stated for precisely this quantity. A reasonable author of
the source paper would consider Conjecture 2.2 resolved (negatively) by this
argument. The writeup correctly notes it leaves Conjectures 2.1
($p_{\mathrm{intersecting}}$) and 1.1 ($p_{\mathrm{dict}}$, Levine's original
conjecture) untouched, and it does not contradict anything proven in the source
paper (Theorem 1.2 concerns $p_{\mathrm{dict}}$; the paper's remark that
$p(t,n) \to 0$ for *fixed* $n$ is consistent, since the construction takes
$n \approx m(\log 2)2^{mt}$ growing with $t$).

One convention nuance: the paper defines $p(t)$ as $\lim_n p(t,n)$, the writeup
speaks of the supremum over stack lengths. These coincide:
$p_{\mathrm{monotone}}(t,n)$ is nondecreasing in $n$ (extend any balanced
monotone $W \subseteq \{0,1\}^n$ to $W \times \{0,1\} \subseteq \{0,1\}^{n+1}$,
which is again balanced and monotone, and let strategies ignore the extra bit),
and it is bounded above by $1/2$ for every $n$ (see step 2 below), so the limit
exists and equals the supremum. This one-line observation is not spelled out in
the writeup but is immediate; I verified it.

| 6. Fiber structure, eqs. ## Caveats

- **Limit vs. supremum over $n$.** The paper defines $p(t) = \lim_n p(t,n)$;
  the writeup argues via a supremum. The gap is closed by the standard
  observation that $p_{\mathrm{monotone}}(t,n)$ is nondecreasing in $n$
  (cylinder extension $W \mapsto W\times\{0,1\}$ preserves balance and
  monotonicity), which the writeup omits but which is immediate.
- **Enormous stacks.** The construction needs $n \approx m(\log 2)2^{mt}$ hats
  per player (double-exponential in $t$ if $m$ grows with $t$). This is
  entirely legitimate under the paper's definition (limit over $n$ for each
  fixed $t$), and is consistent with the paper's remark that $p(t,n) \to 0$
  for each *fixed* $n$.
- **Scope.** Only Conjecture 2.2 is refuted. Conjecture 2.1
  ($p_{\mathrm{intersecting}}(t) \to 0$) and Levine's Conjecture 1.1 remain
  open; the balanced monotone families produced are not intersecting, and the
  writeup says so explicitly.
- **Edge cases.** $t = 1$: trivially $p_{\mathrm{monotone}}(1) = 1/2$ (any
  balanced family), consistent with the theorem statement "for every
  $t \ge 1$". The construction itself is used for $t \ge 2$. Balancing to
  exactly $2^{n-1}$ points is always possible since $2^n$ is even for
  $n \ge 1$.
- **Sociological caveat.** The refuted conjecture is by Alon, Friedgut, Kalai
  and Kindler; that such a clean construction was missed is surprising but not
  implausible — the paper contains no lower-bound analysis of the monotone
  relaxation, and the degeneration of relaxed variants to $1/2$ has a recent
  independent echo in the continuous-stack game (arXiv:2508.01737,
  Theorem 32). This does not affect the mathematical verdict.

## Referee summary

I set out assuming the writeup was wrong and failed to find an error. The
formalization of $p_{\mathrm{monotone}}$ matches the source paper's Section 2
definitions verbatim (checked against the fetched PDF), so there is no
interpretation gaming: Conjecture 2.2 as the authors stated it is what is
refuted. Every step was re-derived: the balancing lemma, the synchronization
inequality, the tribes probability computations, the product-variance bound
$\mathrm{Var}(Q_i) \le Nsu \le 2u$, and the final estimate (7) are all correct;
the only unstated detail (monotonicity of $p_{\mathrm{monotone}}(t,n)$ in $n$,
needed to pass from large-$n$ constructions to the limit defining
$p_{\mathrm{monotone}}(t)$) is a one-line standard fact I verified. The
computational check confirmed Lemma 1 exhaustively on all 168 monotone families
of $\{0,1\}^4$, verified the entire construction end-to-end exhaustively at
$t=2$, $m=1$ (exact success $19/64$, all identities exact in rational
arithmetic), and evaluated the concentration bounds exactly for parameters up
to $N \sim 10^{60}$, with the rigorous lower bound on the success probability
reaching $0.4994$ ($t=2$) and $0.4968$ ($t=10$) at $m=20$ and converging to
$1/2$. A literature search found no prior resolution of Conjecture 2.2.
Verdict: **CONFIRMED** — the writeup correctly disproves Conjecture 2.2 of
arXiv:2208.06858, establishing $p_{\mathrm{monotone}}(t) = 1/2$ for every $t$,
while Conjectures 2.1 and 1.1 remain open.
