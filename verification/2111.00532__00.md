## Interpretation

**The extracted statement is a misquotation of Question 1.3, and the writeup proves only
the misquoted version.**

I downloaded the TeX source of arXiv:2111.00532v2 (file `paper-pure9.tex`). The actual
Question 1.3 (`\label{foxconj}`, the third `thm` environment of Section 1, so numbered
1.3) reads:

> **Question:** Do there exist $c,\varepsilon>0$ with the following property? Let
> $\mathcal{B}=(B_1,B_2,B_3)$ be a blockade in a graph $G$, and let $W$ be its width.
> If there is no $\mathcal{B}$-transversal triangle, then there exist distinct
> $i,j\in\{1,2,3\}$ and $X\subseteq B_i$ and $Y\subseteq B_j$, such that $X,Y$ is a
> pure pair, and **$|X|\ge\varepsilon W$, and $|Y|\ge \varepsilon W^c$**.

The catalog JSON (and hence prompt.md) replaced the asymmetric requirement
"$|X|\ge\varepsilon W$ and $|Y|\ge\varepsilon W^c$" by the symmetric, strictly weaker
"$|X|,|Y|\ge\varepsilon W^c$". The linear bound on one side is the entire content of
the question: immediately before posing it, the paper states the Erdős–Hajnal–Pach
theorem (its Theorem 1.2), which already gives, for **every** ordered graph $H$ on $k$
vertices and every blockade of length $k$ with no ordered $\mathcal{B}$-transversal
copy of $H$, a pure pair $X\subseteq B_i$, $Y\subseteq B_j$ ($i\ne j$) with
$|X|,|Y|\ge\varepsilon W^c$. Taking $H=K_3$ (for which ordered and unordered
transversal copies coincide), the extracted statement is literally a special case of
Theorem 1.2 and was known since Erdős–Hajnal–Pach (Geombinatorics 10 (2000), 64–68).
The paper's discussion makes the intent explicit: "for which $H$ can we get
$|X|\ge\varepsilon W$ and $|Y|\ge\varepsilon W^c$ in the second bullet? ... we cannot
get past the following open question." Also, Theorem 1.4 (`\label{cycle}`) is stated in
$(\varepsilon W,\varepsilon W^c)$-cohesive form, and its declared equivalence with
Question 1.3 at $k=3$ only makes sense for the asymmetric version.

No reasonable author of the source paper would consider Question 1.3 resolved by the
writeup. The writeup itself is commendably honest about this risk: it sets
`would_publish: false` and its caveat explicitly warns that "a stronger requirement may
be missing from the extracted statement" — that warning is exactly right.

Definitions check: the writeup's conventions (width = minimum block size; pure pair =
complete or anticomplete pair of disjoint sets; transversal triangle = induced $K_3$
with one vertex in each block) all match the paper's Section 1 verbatim. So the writeup
proves what the prompt literally asked, but the prompt does not state Question 1.3.

## Caveats

- The writeup proves only anticomplete pairs (never complete), which is allowed —
  a pure pair may be either — and is in fact the paper's own focus.
- Width convention: the writeup assumes $W=\min_i|B_i|$; this matches the paper
  exactly ("the width of $\mathcal B$ is the minimum cardinality of its blocks").
- Edge cases: blocks are nonempty by the paper's definition, so $W\ge1$ and $m\ge1$;
  the proof handles $m=1$ correctly (S4's inequality holds at $m=1$). Edges inside
  blocks and outside the blockade are irrelevant, as implicitly assumed.
- The writeup's own caveat ("a stronger requirement may be missing from the extracted
  statement") is correct and is the decisive point: the missing requirement is
  $|X|\ge\varepsilon W$.
- Gap between claim and proof: the verdict block says "proved" for a catalog entry
  representing Question 1.3; what is proved is a strictly weaker statement that the
  source paper itself presents as known (Theorem 1.2, EHP) before asking the real
  question. Nothing in the writeup bears on the open problem.
- The catalog entry itself should be fixed: the extracted statement drops
  "$|X|\ge\varepsilon W$" from Question 1.3, and any future attack on this id will be
  aimed at a trivial target until it is corrected.
