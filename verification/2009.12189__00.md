# Referee report: 2009.12189__00

Target claim: **disproof** of Conjecture 1.1 of Bonamy, Kardoš, Kelly, Postle
(arXiv:2009.12189), "Every planar graph has fractional vertex-arboricity at most
two", via the 25-vertex planar dual of the Tutte graph.

## Interpretation

The source paper defines va_f(G) measure-theoretically: a fractional
k-arborization maps each vertex to a measurable subset of (0,k) of Lebesgue
measure one such that every level set {v : α ∈ φ(v)} induces a forest; va_f(G)
is the infimum of such k (verbatim definition fetched from arXiv:2009.12189).
The writeup instead uses the LP covering formulation (minimize Σ x_F over
forest-inducing vertex sets subject to Σ_{F∋v} x_F ≥ 1) and its LP dual. For a
finite graph these are equal by the standard argument (the family of
forest-inducing sets is finite and hereditary: an LP solution yields interval
assignments of total length equal to the LP value; conversely a k-arborization
integrates to a feasible fractional cover of value k). So the formalization is
the intended one — no interpretation gaming. The writeup attacks exactly the
conjectured statement, for the class (planar graphs) it is stated for, and
produces a specific planar (indeed maximal planar) counterexample. A reasonable
author of the original paper would consider the conjecture resolved by this
argument — and in fact the authors' conjecture *has* been resolved in the
literature in exactly this spirit (see Reference check).

One point deserving suspicion was that the writeup's Proposition ("every
non-2-arborable plane triangulation has va_f ≥ 2 + 2/(2n−7)") plus the
classical existence of vertex-arboricity-3 triangulations would make the
conjecture fail by an elementary argument that four expert authors apparently
missed in 2020. That suspicion is resolved: the argument is correct (verified
computationally below), the authors did miss it, and the same refutation route
(Tutte's fragment / Wenger's graph) was found by Naserasr et al. in 2025.

## Reference check

1. **arXiv:2009.12189** (Bonamy, Kardoš, Kelly, Postle, *Fractional
   vertex-arboricity of planar graphs*): fetched. Conjecture 1.1 is verbatim
   "Every planar graph has fractional vertex-arboricity at most two", with the
   measure-theoretic definition quoted in the Interpretation section; classes
   must induce forests. Matches the writeup's usage (via the standard LP
   equivalence). The paper also states |V(G)|/a(G) ≤ va_f(G) ≤ va(G) and that
   planar graphs with vertex-arboricity three exist. Confirmed.
2. **W. T. Tutte, "On Hamiltonian circuits", J. London Math. Soc. 21 (1946),
   98–101**: confirmed (Journal of the LMS s1-21, issue 2, April 1946,
   pp. 98–101, DOI 10.1112/jlms/s1-21.2.98); it constructs the classical
   46-vertex non-Hamiltonian cubic 3-connected planar graph, refuting Tait's
   conjecture. The graph's properties (including non-Hamiltonicity) were also
   re-verified computationally here, so the disproof does not rest on the
   citation alone. Confirmed.
3. **Priority (the writeup explicitly did not check it)** — this is where the
   verdict lands on ALREADY_KNOWN:
   - **arXiv:2505.16808** (Naserasr, Pham, Pujol, Zhou, *Fractional balanced
     chromatic number and arboricity of planar (signed) graphs*, 22 May 2025).
     Fetched and read (PDF text extracted). Their "fractional arboricity"
     a_f(G) is the fractional **vertex**-arboricity (defined via (p,q)-colorings
     whose color classes are acyclic subsets of V(G)), and they state and refute
     "Conjecture 1 [Bonamy, Kardoš, Kelly, Postle]. Every planar graph satisfies
     a_f(G) ≤ 2", giving in Theorem 16 an explicit planar graph W_1 with
     a_f(W_1) = 2 + 2/25, built from Wenger's graph — the dual of Tutte's
     fragment, i.e. essentially the same circle of ideas as this writeup. The
     catalog's auto-review (2026-05-15) misclassified that paper as concerning
     edge arboricity of signed graphs only; that assessment is wrong, and the
     catalog's "open" status was therefore already incorrect at review time.
   - **Albertson–Berman is now also disproved**, which refutes Conjecture 1.1
     a fortiori (via n/a(G) ≤ va_f(G)): Makarov, arXiv:2608.13964 (Aug 14,
     2026; 39-vertex planar G with a(G) = 19 < n/2); Jung, arXiv:2608.17350
     (31-vertex triangulation with a = 15); Cames van Batenburg, Goedgebeur,
     Jooken, arXiv:2608.23260 (Aug 24, 2026; minimum counterexample order is
     29, and an infinite family with a(G) ≤ (25/52)n).

All external results invoked by the writeup exist and state what the writeup
uses. references_ok refers to the writeup's own citations; the missed prior
refutation is a priority issue, not a correctness issue.

## Computational check

Scripts (reproducible):
`/Users/viennot/dev/Graph-Theory-LLM-Proofs/verification/scripts/2009.12189__00/verify.py`
(log `run.log`) and `verify2.py` (log `run2.log`), run with Python 3.14 +
networkx 3.6.1 + scipy (HiGHS). Everything completes in seconds.

Exact numbers obtained:

- **Tutte graph H** (`networkx.tutte_graph()`): 46 vertices, 69 edges, cubic,
  planar, 3-connected. **No Hamiltonian cycle**: complete backtracking search
  over edge in/out states with forced-edge propagation (769 search nodes);
  searcher validated positively on K_4, the 3-prism and the dodecahedron and
  negatively on the Petersen graph.
- **Dual G**: 25 vertices, 69 = 3·25−6 edges, simple, planar, every face a
  triangle (checked from an embedding of G itself); min degree 4; degree
  sequence [4,4,4,4,4,4,5,…,5(×15),9,10,10,10]; Σ_v (d(v)−2) = **88** as
  claimed.
- **M := max induced-forest weight** under w(v) = d(v)−2: **M = 43** — exactly
  the writeup's requirement (2n−7 = 43; 2n−6 = 44 is not attained). Computed by
  exhaustive branch-and-bound (181,211 nodes, 0.1 s) and independently by
  MILP with lazily generated cycle constraints (HiGHS, 77 iterations); both give
  43. Hence the writeup's dual certificate z_v = (d(v)−2)/43 is feasible and
  **va_f(G) ≥ 88/43 ≈ 2.04651 > 2 is rigorously certified** (integer
  arithmetic throughout).
- **No partition of V(G) into two induced forests** (complete backtracking with
  undoable union-find; searcher validated on K_4, octahedron, icosahedron
  [partitions found] and K_5 [correctly none]). So va(G) = 3, independently of
  the writeup's duality argument.
- **True LP value**: column generation (restricted master LP + exact
  branch-and-bound pricing) converges with primal = verified dual =
  **va_f(G) = 2.0625 (= 33/16)**, 57 iterations, 81 columns. The claimed bound
  88/43 is a valid but non-tight lower bound. (33/16 is a numerical LP value
  with exact pricing verification at tolerance 1e-9; the rigorous exact
  statement used for the verdict is va_f(G) ≥ 88/43.)
- Side checks: a(G) = 15 (so G itself is *not* an Albertson–Berman
  counterexample, n/a = 5/3); a(icosahedron) = 6, confirming Section 1's bound
  is consistent with the tight vertex-transitive example va_f = 2.

No claimed property failed. Every computational assertion in the writeup is
correct.

## Caveats

- **Priority (the decisive one).** The writeup itself flags "publication
  priority has not been checked" and sets would_publish = false. Indeed the
  refutation of Conjecture 1.1 has been in the literature since May 2025
  (arXiv:2505.16808, Theorem 16: an explicit planar graph with fractional
  vertex-arboricity 2 + 2/25 = 2.08 > 2), and Albertson–Berman itself fell in
  August 2026. The writeup's specific 25-vertex example and the clean
  equivalence "for plane triangulations, va_f ≤ 2 ⟺ va ≤ 2" (its (4)) appear
  to be a nice, possibly novel packaging, but the headline claim is not new.
- The Proposition needs n ≥ 4 (2n−7 > 0); stated. K_3 (n = 3, va_f = 3/2) is
  correctly excluded; the "va_f ≥ 2 for triangulations" claim in Section 1 also
  needs n ≥ 4 (2n−6 > 0), which is stated.
- The LP "min" formulation vs the paper's infimum-over-k measure definition:
  equivalent for finite graphs; the writeup asserts rather than proves this,
  a routine but unstated step (GAP-level pedantry only; standard).
- The bipartite-planarity bound |δ(F)| ≤ 2n−4 uses N ≥ 3 vertices in the cut
  graph; degenerate cases are vacuous here (any nonempty F in a triangulation
  on n ≥ 4 vertices has |δ(F)| ≥ 3 or the bound holds trivially).
- The claimed bound 88/43 is not the true value (33/16); harmless, as only a
  lower bound > 2 is needed.
- The writeup's networkx code passes `seen` positionally to `traverse_face`;
  this binds to `mark_half_edges` and is correct in current networkx.
- The catalog metadata itself was wrong: its 2026-05-15 auto-review kept the
  problem "open" by misreading arXiv:2505.16808 as an edge-arboricity paper.
  Catalog maintainers should update the status to refuted.

## Referee summary

I set out expecting to find the flaw that four expert authors would surely have
caught, and instead confirmed every step. The writeup's argument — a
degree-weight dual certificate showing every plane triangulation has va_f ≥ 2,
an exact equality characterization showing the bound 2n−6 is attained iff the
triangulation splits into two induced trees (equivalently, iff its dual is
Hamiltonian), and the integrality jump to va_f ≥ 2 + 2/(2n−7) otherwise — is
correct in full detail, and every load-bearing finite claim was verified by
independent exhaustive computation: the Tutte graph is non-Hamiltonian, its
25-vertex dual triangulation has no two-forest partition, the maximum
(d−2)-weight of an induced forest is exactly 43, certifying va_f ≥ 88/43, and
the true LP value is 33/16 = 2.0625 > 2. The interpretation matches the source
paper's definition. The only reason this is not CONFIRMED is that the result is
already in the literature: Naserasr, Pham, Pujol and Zhou (arXiv:2505.16808,
May 2025) refuted Conjecture 1.1 with the same Tutte-fragment circle of ideas
(explicit planar graph with fractional vertex-arboricity 2 + 2/25), and the
August 2026 counterexamples to Albertson–Berman refute it a fortiori.
