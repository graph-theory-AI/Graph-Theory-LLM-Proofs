## Interpretation

I fetched arXiv:2209.09107v2 (the 2024 version) and read the relevant pages directly
(pp. 1-3 and the concluding Section 6, p. 14).

**Question 6.1, verbatim from the paper (p. 14):** "Does every graph G have a
spanning subgraph with an Alon-Tarsi orientation in which each vertex v has
out-degree at least ½(deg_G(v) − 1)?" There is **no floor** in the source, and the
catalog statement quotes it faithfully. So the writeup refutes exactly the printed
statement, not a strawman variant of its own construction.

Is the unfloored bound the intended one, or a typo for ⌊½(deg−1)⌋? Two pieces of
evidence say the unfloored bound is genuinely what the authors wrote *and needed*:

1. The paper's own derivation immediately after Question 6.1 (of the consequence
   AT(H) ≤ ⌊½Δ + 3/2⌋) reverses the orientation and computes out-degree at most
   ½deg_G(v) + ½, which requires the unfloored lower bound; the floored version at
   even-degree vertices would only give ½deg + 1 and lose the stated consequence.
2. The very next sentence of the paper reads: "If Question 6.1 has a negative
   answer, we can still ask the similar question where −1 is replaced with an
   arbitrary constant −C." The authors explicitly contemplated a negative answer
   and pre-announced the fallback question.

So a reasonable author of the source paper would, in my judgment, accept that
Question 6.1 as posed is answered negatively — while noting (as the writeup itself
does in its caveats) that the refutation exploits an integrality artifact at
even-degree vertices, and that the substantive program is untouched: the floored
variant d⁺(v) ≥ ⌊½(deg_G(v)−1)⌋, which still suffices to prove Conjecture 1.1 via
Theorem 1.4 (because |F(v)| is an integer, |F(v)| ≤ ½(deg−1) iff
|F(v)| ≤ ⌊½(deg−1)⌋), remains open, as does the authors' −C fallback (already for
C = 2 the odd-cycle obstruction evaporates: the empty spanning subgraph is
trivially Alon-Tarsi and meets the bound). The writeup states this limitation
honestly in its caveat block and sets would_publish: false, which is the right
level of modesty. Interpretation: fair.

## Reference check

- **Question 6.1** (arXiv:2209.09107v2, p. 14): confirmed verbatim, no floor, as
  quoted in the Interpretation section. The catalog page quotes it correctly.
- **Definition of Alon-Tarsi orientation** (paper, p. 3): "Given an orientation D
  of G, we let EE(D) and EO(D), respectively, denote the number of even and odd
  subgraphs of G that are Eulerian with respect to D. If G has an orientation D
  satisfying EE(D) ≠ EO(D), then we say that D is an Alon-Tarsi orientation."
  This is the definition the writeup uses (diff(D) = EE − EO ≠ 0). The
  empty-subgraph convention is discussed at step 1; the writeup's usage is the
  standard one and the one under which the paper's Theorem 1.4 and the Alon-Tarsi
  choosability theorem hold.
- **Theorem 1.4** (paper, p. 3): "Let G be a graph, let H be a spanning subgraph
  of G, and let F : V(G) → 2^N be a map. If there exists an Alon-Tarsi orientation
  D of H such that |F(v)| ≤ deg⁺_D(v) for every vertex v ∈ V(G), then G has an
  F-avoiding orientation." Confirmed; mentioned only as context by the writeup.
- **Conjecture 1.1** (paper, p. 2): |F(v)| ≤ ½(deg_G(v) − 1) for each v implies an
  F-avoiding orientation exists. Confirmed; the disproof of Question 6.1 does
  **not** disprove Conjecture 1.1 (see Caveats).
- Literature search for prior resolutions of Question 6.1 (web search, September
  2026): nothing found; the catalog's auto-review (2026-05-15) likewise found no
  follow-up. The ingredient facts (odd cycles have Alon-Tarsi number 3; Eulerian
  orientations of odd-size graphs have EE = EO) are classical, but their
  application to Question 6.1 does not appear in the literature, so this is not
  ALREADY_KNOWN as a resolution of the question.

## Caveats

1. **Scope of the disproof.** Only the unfloored statement d⁺(v) ≥ ½(deg_G(v)−1)
   is refuted. The floored variant d⁺(v) ≥ ⌊½(deg_G(v)−1)⌋ — which is all that is
   needed to prove Conjecture 1.1 via Theorem 1.4, since |F(v)| is an integer —
   is untouched and remains open. **Conjecture 1.1 is not disproved**, and the
   Theorem 1.4 route to it is not blocked.
2. **The authors' fallback survives.** The −C relaxation posed immediately after
   Question 6.1 remains open for every C ≥ 2: already at C = 2 the threshold at
   degree-2 vertices drops to 0 and the empty spanning subgraph (trivially AT) is
   a witness for odd cycles, so this counterexample family says nothing about it.
   Question 6.2 (matching removal, AT ≤ ½Δ + C) is likewise unaffected.
3. **Convention dependence.** The disproof needs the empty subdigraph counted
   among Eulerian subdigraphs (EE ≥ 1). This is the standard convention, forced by
   the graph-polynomial coefficient identity and by consistency of the Alon-Tarsi
   theorem itself; the paper's wording is compatible with it. Under the nonstandard
   empty-excluded reading the triangle argument would fail — but so would
   Theorem 1.4 and Alon-Tarsi's theorem, so that reading is untenable.
4. **Triviality.** The obstruction is an integrality-plus-parity triviality: at
   even-degree vertices the real-valued bound silently rounds up to deg/2, forcing
   an exact Eulerian orientation. The refutation is correct but says nothing deep;
   the writeup's would_publish: false is appropriate. The authors would most
   likely respond by re-posing the question with the floor or the −C constant.
5. **Degenerate cases.** Isolated vertices (threshold −½, i.e., 0) and
   disconnected even-degree graphs with odd |E| pose no problem for the argument;
   the counterexample family is nonempty (all odd cycles). The claim "every
   Eulerian graph with an odd number of edges" holds whether "Eulerian" means
   connected-even-degree or merely even-degree.
