## Interpretation

The catalog statement asks: "For which ordered graphs H can we get |X|, |Y| >= eps*W^c
(for some fixed c > 0 depending only on H) as the pure pair conclusion when there is no
ordered B-transversal copy of H in a blockade of width W?" The writeup answers "all H,
with c = 1/(|H|-1)", and itself flags in its caveats that "the catalog statement is
probably missing a stronger requirement".

That suspicion is correct. I downloaded the actual TeX source of arXiv:2111.00532v2
(Pure pairs. IX. Transversal trees, Scott–Seymour–Spirkl). The relevant passage
(introduction, around Theorem 1.2, `\label{EHPthm}`) reads:

- **Theorem 1.2** (attributed to Erdős, Hajnal and Pach, `\cite{EHP}`): "For every
  ordered graph H, there exist c, eps > 0, such that for every graph G, and every
  blockade B = (B_1,...,B_k) in G, where k = |H|, either there is an ordered
  B-transversal copy of H in G; or there are distinct i, j and X ⊆ B_i, Y ⊆ B_j such
  that X, Y is a pure pair, and |X|, |Y| >= eps*W^c, where W is the width of B."

- The text immediately after: "This is where we will start. We would particularly like
  to know, for which graphs H can we take **c = 1** in the second bullet? (Sadly, almost
  never: only for graphs H with at most two vertices.) And, more promising: for which H
  can we get **|X| >= eps*W and |Y| >= eps*W^c** in the second bullet?"

So the symmetric polynomial statement the writeup proves is not the open question — it
is the *already-known theorem* (Theorem 1.2, due to Erdős–Hajnal–Pach) that the open
question is a strengthening of. The actual "more promising" open question is the
**asymmetric** version: one side linear in W (|X| >= eps*W), the other side polynomial
(|Y| >= eps*W^c). The catalog extraction (flagged by the catalog itself as possibly
garbled: "the exact threshold formulas around this informal question may be partially
garbled") collapsed this to the symmetric polynomial form. The paper's Question 1.3
(the k = 3 transversal-triangle case of the asymmetric question, a variant of a
Conlon–Fox–Sudakov conjecture) is explicitly stated as open and as the authors'
unattained initial goal; the writeup's argument says nothing about it — its method
inherently degrades *both* sides to W^{1/(k-1)}.

The writeup's formalization of "ordered B-transversal copy" (v_i in B_i with adjacency
pattern equal to H, k = |H| blocks) matches the paper's definition exactly, so on that
point the interpretation is faithful. But no reasonable author of the source paper
would consider their question resolved by this writeup: it re-proves the theorem they
stated as the known starting point. Hence interpretation_ok: false, and the honest
classification of the (correct) proved statement is ALREADY_KNOWN.

## Caveats

- The fatal issue is not mathematical but bibliographical/interpretive: the proved
  statement is Theorem 1.2 of the very paper the question comes from, i.e. the
  Erdős–Hajnal–Pach theorem (Geombinatorics 10 (2000), 64–68). The writeup presents it
  as resolving an open problem ("verdict: proved", "confidence: high") when the actual
  open problem is the asymmetric strengthening (|X| >= eps*W, |Y| >= eps*W^c), which
  the writeup explicitly does not address and its method cannot address (the embedding
  argument shrinks both sides to W^{1/(k-1)}).
- The writeup deserves credit for honesty: its caveats field says the catalog statement
  is "probably missing a stronger requirement". That is exactly right. But it guessed
  the wrong repair candidates first ("a prescribed stronger exponent, a near-linear
  bound, a one-sided conclusion, ...") — the actual missing requirement is the
  one-sided linear condition, its third guess.
- Minor conventions, all handled correctly in the writeup: k = 1 (vacuous), k = 2
  (width-W pure pair), W < k-1 (bound < 1, singletons), blocks nonempty (paper's
  definition), pure pair allowed to be complete or anticomplete (paper allows both in
  Theorem 1.2's conclusion, though the paper's later cohesive-blockade results use only
  anticomplete pairs — irrelevant here).
- The exponent c = 1/(k-1) is not claimed optimal, correctly so; the paper's Theorem
  1.7 (ordered star counterexample) shows exponent limits of the same flavour
  (1/t for S_t^+) in the cohesive setting, so the writeup's bound is at least in a
  plausible regime, but no novelty over [EHP] is established.

sound induction (verified line-by-line and by an instrumented constructive
implementation on thousands of instances, plus one exhaustive tiny case), the XOR

answered the garbled version, which was never open. The intended open problem,
including its notorious k = 3 transversal-triangle case (Question 1.3, a variant of a
Conlon–Fox–Sudakov conjecture), remains open. Verdict: ALREADY_KNOWN.
