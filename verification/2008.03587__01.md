## Interpretation

The catalog page transcribes Question 4.2 of arXiv:2008.03587 as asking for
`z(G'_k) > z(G) + 1`, and the writeup builds its entire "disproved" verdict on
that strict inequality (an increase of at least two). The catalog prompt itself
warns, in its Notes: "the inequality z(G'_k) > z(G) + 1 may be garbled; it could
be z(G'_k) >= z(G) + 1 (a strict increase of at least 1)."

I settled the question definitively by downloading the arXiv v3 TeX source
(`https://arxiv.org/e-print/2008.03587`). Lines 429-433 of `main.tex` read:

```
\begin{question}\label{qu:petaluszombius}
For any graph $G$, is there an integer $k$ such that, for $G'_k$ the graph
obtained from $G$ by subdividing all edges $k$ times then adding the original
edges, $z(G'_k)\geq z(G)+1$?
\end{question}
```

The source is `\geq`, not `>`. The rendered PDF (page 4) likewise shows the
slanted `⩾`; the ">" in the catalog is a text-extraction artifact. So the
actual open problem asks whether subdivision-plus-original-edges forces an
increase of **at least one** for some k, and the authors' follow-up remark
("it could be that 5 is a valid answer to Question 4.2 in any graph") makes
sense exactly for this reading: k = 5 plausibly works for every graph, whereas
under the ">" reading K2 kills universality of any k trivially.

The writeup chose the literal ">" reading, exhibited G = K2 with
z(K2'_k) = z(C_{k+2}) <= 2 = z(K2) + 1, and declared the problem "disproved."
Under the real statement, K2 is not a counterexample at all: for every k >= 2,
z(K2'_k) = 2 = z(K2) + 1, i.e. **K2 satisfies Question 4.2 affirmatively**
(any k >= 2 works). The writeup itself concedes this ("it refutes the
displayed formulation but supports, rather than refutes, the likely intended
one-unit-increase formulation") and flags it in its caveats field — an honest
disclosure, but the headline verdict "disproved" is still claimed for an open
problem that this work does not resolve. No reasonable author of the source
paper would consider Question 4.2 settled by this. This is textbook
interpretation gaming: refuting a strawman produced by a PDF-to-text glitch.

Convention check: the writeup's game convention (zombies choose initial
vertices first, survivor places second, every zombie move must strictly
decrease distance to the survivor along a geodesic of the zombie's choice)
matches [FHMP16] as quoted in the source paper's introduction, and the paper
assumes all graphs connected. No convention mismatch.

## Caveats

- The entire verdict rests on the ">" vs ">=" reading; the prompt's Notes
  explicitly flagged the ">" as likely garbled, and the TeX source confirms
  ">=". The writeup acknowledged the ambiguity in its caveats field yet still
  claimed "disproved" as its headline verdict.
- Under the real (>=) statement, nothing in the writeup resolves the problem:
  the K2 computation and the tree partial result are both *positive* evidence
  for the conjecture (they verify it for K2 and all nontrivial trees with
  k >= 2, consistent with the authors' k = 5 suggestion). The general question
  remains open.
- The one-zombie lower bound for cycles and the initial-placement convention
  (zombies first, survivor second) follow [FHMP16]; under a survivor-first
  convention z(K2) would still be 1, so nothing hinges on this.
- k = 1 gives (K2)'_1 = C_3 with z = 1; the writeup's "z(G'_k) = 2 for every
  k >= 2" correctly excludes k = 1.
- K1 / disconnected graphs are excluded by the source paper's standing
  connectivity assumption; the writeup's K1 remark is harmless.
