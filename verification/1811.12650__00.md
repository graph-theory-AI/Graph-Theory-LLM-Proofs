## Interpretation

The target is the informal remark in arXiv:1811.12650 (Bonamy–Bousquet–Perarnau, v1),
in running prose immediately after Corollary 2: "The condition Δ = o(n) is probably
not necessary."

Exact source statements (read from the PDF, pages 3–4):

- **Theorem 1 (BBP).** For 3 ≤ Δ ≤ n−2 and G a *connected* graph on n vertices with
  maximum degree Δ, a uniform α ∈ Ω(G) satisfies P(α frozen) ≤ (6/7)^{n/(Δ+1)}.
- **Corollary 2 (BBP).** "Let G be a graph on n vertices and maximum degree Δ = o(n).
  Then Glauber dynamics for (Δ+1)-colourings of G starting at a uniformly random
  vertex, gives an almost uniform sampler for random (Δ+1)-colourings." The authors'
  own justification: "for Δ = o(n), the probability to start in a frozen colouring is
  o(1) and the contribution of frozen colourings to the stationary distribution is
  o(1)." Then the target remark.

So the entire role of Δ = o(n) is to make Theorem 1's bound (6/7)^{n/(Δ+1)} tend to 0
(it is only a constant when Δ = Θ(n)). The intended content of the remark is therefore:
*the frozen proportion is o(1) for connected graphs even without Δ = o(n)*, which is
exactly what the writeup formalizes and proves: F/C ≤ 4/(n+4) for every connected
G ≠ K_{Δ+1}, with no assumption on Δ whatsoever. This is the fair, intended reading —
not a strawman. The writeup also correctly identifies the two hypotheses that cannot
be dropped (completeness and connectedness) and states them as caveats rather than
using them to game the verdict; BBP themselves impose connectivity in Theorem 1 and
note that Δ ≤ n−2 excludes the (Δ+1)-clique, so a reasonable author of the source
paper would consider the remark resolved by this theorem (for the frozen-fraction
content; see the Section 6 caveat below for the sampler phrasing).

Two honest qualifications on interpretation:

1. The writeup's bound is polynomial (4/(n+4)), not exponential; it does not extend
   Theorem 1's *exponential* bound to Δ = Θ(n). The remark, however, is attached to
   Corollary 2, which needs only o(1); for that purpose 4/(n+4) fully suffices.
   (It cannot be improved to exponential in general: BBP's Proposition 4 gives graphs
   with P(frozen) ≥ e^{−(3log Δ/Δ)n}, which is only polynomially small when Δ = Θ(n) —
   consistent with, and nearly matching, the writeup's bound.)
2. Corollary 2 as literally printed has its own looseness (no connectivity hypothesis,
   no Δ ≥ 3, and "starting at a uniformly random vertex" makes the statement almost
   tautological since the Glauber kernel is symmetric). The writeup resolves the
   substantive content — P(frozen) = o(1) — rather than the literal sentence, which is
   the right call.

## Reference check

1. **BBP Theorem 1, Corollary 2, and the remark** (arXiv:1811.12650v1): fetched the
   PDF and read pages 1–6. All three exist and state exactly what is described above;
   the remark is verbatim "The condition Δ = o(n) is probably not necessary." The
   writeup's use of the surrounding context (frozen-fraction o(1) is the load-bearing
   content) matches the paper's own proof sketch of Corollary 2. CONFIRMED.
2. **Feghali–Johnson–Paulusma**, "A Reconfigurations Analogue of Brooks' Theorem and
   its Consequences", J. Graph Theory 83(4):340–358 (arXiv:1501.05800): exists;
   the relevant statement is that for a *connected* graph with **Δ ≥ 3**, the
   (Δ+1)-recolouring graph consists of a unique component containing all non-frozen
   colourings plus isolated frozen colourings (with exceptions precisely for complete
   graphs; cycles are excluded by Δ ≥ 3). This is also how BBP themselves quote it
   ("if k = Δ+1 and Δ ≥ 3, frozen colourings are actually the only possible
   obstruction for the irreducibility of the chain", p.2). The writeup's Section 6
   omits Δ ≥ 3 — see the GAP above. Otherwise CONFIRMED.
3. **Novelty screening**: web search found no follow-up paper removing the Δ = o(n)
   condition (consistent with the catalog's 2026-05 auto-review). The published CPC
   2020 version's abstract states the same exponential-in-n claim for connected
   graphs; I could not access its full text (paywalled), so I cannot fully exclude
   that the journal version strengthened Theorem 1, but the catalog review (with web
   search) reports the remark unaddressed in the literature. The switching argument
   itself is elementary but I found no prior source for the 4/(n+4) bound; not
   flagged as ALREADY_KNOWN.

## Caveats

1. **Δ ≥ 3 missing in Section 6.** The Glauber/TV consequence needs FJP's Δ ≥ 3;
   for connected Δ = 2 graphs (cycles divisible by 3 have frozen colourings and
   disconnected nonfrozen parts) the displayed ‖π* − π‖ bound is false. The main
   theorem (F/C ≤ 4/(n+4)) and the "random colouring is nonfrozen whp" statement
   hold for Δ = 2 as well (verified). Since BBP's own Corollary 2 lives in the
   Theorem 1 setting (3 ≤ Δ ≤ n−2, connected), this is a presentation gap, not a
   substantive one.
2. **Polynomial, not exponential.** The writeup proves o(1), not an extension of the
   exponential bound; this is all Corollary 2 needs, and by BBP Proposition 4 an
   exponential bound is impossible at Δ = Θ(n), but a stronger reading of the remark
   ("Theorem 1 itself holds without Δ = o(n)") is not established (and is false as
   stated).
3. **Complete graphs and disconnectedness** are unavoidable exceptions; the writeup
   states both, with correct witnesses (K_n; r·K_{Δ+1}).
4. **No mixing-time bound** — correctly disclaimed; "almost uniform sampler" is
   resolved only in the stationary/limiting sense used by BBP's own justification of
   Corollary 2.
5. Degenerate cases: K_1 and K_2 are K_{Δ+1} and are excluded by hypothesis; C > 0
   always since q = Δ+1 ≥ χ(G); the F = 0 case is handled trivially. No convention
   issues found (frozen = isolated vertex of the recolouring graph, matching BBP).
6. I could not read the paywalled CPC 2020 full text; the check of the source
   statements is against arXiv v1 (the only arXiv version), which is what the
   catalog targets.

## Referee summary

The writeup formalizes the intended content of BBP's informal remark correctly and
proves it by a clean, elementary switching argument: swapping the colours of the two
endpoints of any non-true-twin edge of a frozen colouring yields a nonfrozen proper
colouring, the switching map is at most 2-to-1, and connected non-complete graphs
with frozen colourings have at least n/2 non-true-twin edges, giving
F/C ≤ 4/(n+4) with no assumption on Δ. Every lemma, inequality, and the tightness of
the multiplicity bound survived exhaustive brute-force verification on all lifts of
K_4 and K_5 (the only graphs at these sizes with frozen colourings) and on cycles.
Both cited external results exist and are used with correct hypotheses, with one
exception: Section 6 quotes the Feghali–Johnson–Paulusma unique-component theorem
without its Δ ≥ 3 hypothesis, and the resulting TV-distance display is false for
cycles (computationally witnessed on C_9). This is a routine repair — insert Δ ≥ 3,
which is already a hypothesis of the source's Theorem 1/Corollary 2 setting — and it
does not affect the main theorem or the resolution of the remark's substantive
content. Verdict: MINOR_GAPS (correct modulo one repairable hypothesis omission in
the corollary section).
