# Referee report: 2310.04265__11 (Conjecture 6.1 of "Clique number of tournaments")

Target claim: **disproved** — the writeup constructs a hereditary, $\vec\chi$-bounded class of
digraphs $\mathcal C$ whose closure under substitution contains digraphs $G_n$ with
$\vec\omega(G_n)=2$ and $\vec\chi(G_n)=n+2$.

## Interpretation

I downloaded the arXiv source of **v2** (22 June 2026) of arXiv:2310.04265 and checked
every relevant definition against the writeup:

- **Conjecture 6.1** (label `conj:closed_subst`, in Section "conclusion"), verbatim from
  `main.tex` line 1292: *"If a class of digraphs $\mathcal C$ is $\vec\chi$-bounded, then so
  is its closure under substitution."* No restriction to bounded independence number, no
  added remark in v2 that the conjecture was resolved. It is stated immediately after the
  authors say they believe Theorem 3.9 (the tournament substitution theorem) "can be
  generalized to classes of digraphs".
- **Digraph conventions** (line ~240): no loops, no parallel arcs, **no digons**. The
  construction is an oriented graph — compliant.
- **Class** (line 330): in this paper a *class* is by definition **hereditary** (closed
  under induced subdigraphs). The writeup's $\mathcal C$ is explicitly the hereditary
  family of induced subdigraphs of the gadgets $S(H)$, so this convention is respected.
- **Backedge graph** $D^\prec$: edge $uv$ iff $u\prec v$ and $vu\in A(D)$ — identical to
  the writeup's convention ($xy\in E(D^\prec)$ iff $xy\in A(D)$ and $y\prec x$).
- **$\vec\chi$ and $\vec\omega$**: $\vec\chi(D)=\min_\prec \chi(D^\prec)$ is stated *in
  the paper itself* (line 258, for general digraphs), and
  $\vec\omega(D)=\min_\prec\omega(D^\prec)$ (line 264). $\vec\chi$-bounded = there is an
  increasing $f$ with $\vec\chi(D)\le f(\vec\omega(D))$ for all $D$ in the class; the
  writeup's class satisfies this with $f(w)=w+1$ (indeed $f=\mathrm{id}$ on nonempty members).
- **Substitution and closure** (lines 537–546): the paper's definition of substituting
  $D_2$ for a vertex $u$ (non-adjacency of $u,v$ becomes no arcs between $v$ and the
  copy) matches the writeup's $F_H(G)$ exactly (machine-checked, see below), and
  $\mathcal C^{subst}$ is "the closure of $\mathcal C$ under substitution", so iterated
  substitution of closure members into closure members is allowed — this is exactly how
  the paper's own proof of Theorem 3.9 uses it (line 569). Hence
  $G_{n+1}=F_{H_n}(G_n)\in\mathcal C^{subst}$ is legitimate.

**Is this the intended conjecture?** Yes. The refutation does not exploit a strawman
literal reading: it uses the paper's own definitions of digraph, class, clique number and
substitution. The only sense in which a defender could object is that the surrounding
discussion focuses on digraphs of *bounded independence number* (where a Ramsey analogue
of Theorem 3.3 holds), and the counterexample lives at the opposite extreme (huge
independent sets); but the authors deliberately stated the conjecture for all digraph
classes, so a reasonable author would consider Conjecture 6.1, as stated, refuted — and
would presumably re-pose it for classes of bounded independence number.

## Reference check

- **Conjecture 6.1** confirmed verbatim from the downloaded v2 TeX source
  (`main.tex` l.1292): "If a class of digraphs $\mathcal C$ is $\vec\chi$-bounded, then so
  is its closure under substitution." Still presented as an open conjecture in v2
  (June 2026); no retraction or resolution note anywhere in the file.
- **Definitions** ($\vec\chi$, $\vec\omega$, backedge graph, substitution, closure,
  hereditary classes, digon-free digraphs) all confirmed verbatim from the same source, as
  detailed above. The writeup's "Basic observations" are literally in the paper.
- **Theorem 3.9 / Theorem 3.3 context** (tournament substitution theorem; the
  $\chi/\omega$ backedge inequality failing for digraphs, replaced by a Ramsey version
  with independence number) confirmed — consistent with the catalog's framing and with
  why the construction (huge independence number) evades the tournament machinery.
- **Mycielski construction**: standard textbook material; also verified by computation.
- **Prior refutation search**: web searches for a counterexample to Conjecture 6.1 /
  substitution-closure of $\vec\chi$-bounded digraph classes found nothing; the most
  recent citing work found (arXiv:2608.09710, "Crossing tournaments are polynomially
  $\vec\chi$-bounded") does not mention the conjecture; v2 of the source paper itself
  (June 2026) keeps it open. Related known constructions (e.g. digraphs of large
  dichromatic number from triangle-free graphs, "Chordal directed graphs are not
  $\chi$-bounded") do not place such digraphs inside a substitution closure of a
  $\vec\chi$-bounded class, which is the whole point here. I found no source anticipating
  this refutation.

## Caveats

1. **Scope of refutation.** The conjecture as stated (v2, unchanged) is refuted. The
   morally interesting surviving question — the same statement for classes of digraphs
   with *bounded independence number*, the setting the concluding section actually
   develops tools for — is untouched: the counterexample has independence number
   $\Theta(|V|)$. The writeup does not claim otherwise, and its verdict line is accurate.
2. **Heredity convention.** The paper's "class" is hereditary; the writeup's $\mathcal C$
   complies. Under a non-hereditary reading the counterexample only gets easier.
3. **Digon-freeness.** The construction is an oriented graph, so it also refutes the
   conjecture under the paper's strict digon-free digraph convention; nothing hinges on
   allowing digons.
4. **Trivial cases.** Empty/one-vertex digraphs: $\vec\omega=\vec\chi=1$ conventions are
   respected; the writeup's "nonempty $J$" hedge is harmless, and only $\vec\chi\le2$ is
   load-bearing for $\vec\chi$-boundedness of $\mathcal C$ (with increasing
   $f(w)=w+1$, as the paper's definition requires an increasing $f$).
5. **Lemma 1 equality vs. inequality.** Only the exact value $\vec\chi(G_n)=n+2$ is used
   to feed the next induction step ($\chi(H_n)=n+3>\vec\chi(G_n)$ must hold, and each
   copy must be forced to use *all* $q$ colors); both directions of Lemma 1 are proven
   and were verified at the base level exhaustively.
6. **Level-2 lower bound** ($\vec\chi(G_2)\ge4$) was not brute-forced (infeasible at 411
   vertices); it rests on the Lemma 1 argument with both premises machine-verified. I
   consider this a complete verification of the argument, not a gap.
7. **Novelty.** I found no prior refutation in the indexed literature (searches
   September 2026), and v2 of the source paper (June 2026) still lists the conjecture as
   open — but absence of evidence in web search is not a guarantee; if the authors or
   others have an unposted refutation, the verdict would shift to ALREADY_KNOWN without
   affecting correctness.

## Referee summary

I set out to break this proof and could not. The construction is simple and robust: a
triangle gadget $S(H)$ turns any triangle-free graph $H$ into a 2-dicolorable oriented
graph whose edge-vertices, when substituted by a digraph $G$, transfer the chromatic
hardness of $H$ to dichromatic hardness one level up (Lemma 1), while a single
interleaved ordering keeps the backedge clique number at 2 because the backedge graph
decomposes as $H$ plus disjoint copies of $G$'s optimal backedge graph (Lemma 2).
Iterating with Mycielski graphs produces $G_n\in\mathcal C^{subst}$ with
$\vec\omega(G_n)=2$, $\vec\chi(G_n)=n+2$. Every definition and convention was checked
against the actual v2 TeX source of arXiv:2310.04265; every lemma was re-derived by hand;
the base cases were verified exhaustively by computer (including the critical exhaustive
proof that $G_1$ has no 2-dicoloring, and the clique-number-2 certificate for the
411-vertex $G_2$). The counterexample avoids the tournament machinery exactly where the
authors said it breaks (unbounded independence number), which is why it coexists with
their Theorem 3.9. Verdict: **CONFIRMED** — Conjecture 6.1, as stated, is disproved.
