# Referee report: 2508.08870__00 (Conjecture 1.5, Alon–Pinchasi, "Distinct Directions and Distinct Distances in R^d")

**Claim under review.** The writeup at `attacks/2508.08870__00/output.md` claims to **disprove** Conjecture 1.5 of arXiv:2508.08870: "For every fixed d, for all d-norms but a meagre set, for all n > n_0(d), any set of n points in R^d that do not all lie in an affine hyperplane determines at least (d-o(1))n distinct distances."

I approached this maximally skeptically — a trivial refutation of a conjecture by Alon and Pinchasi is a priori the least likely outcome, and misquotation of the conjecture was my leading hypothesis. That hypothesis fails: I pulled the TeX sources of **both** arXiv versions and the statement is quoted verbatim. The counterexample then survives every check I could throw at it.

## Interpretation

- **The catalog statement is a verbatim quote of the paper.** I downloaded the arXiv source of v1 (`ddd3.tex`, Aug 12 2025) and v2 (`ddd4.tex`, Nov 7 2025, the current version, revised after Duan–Ouyang–Wang found a gap in the proof of Theorem 1.3). The conjecture (TeX label `c14`, compiled as "Conjecture 1.5" — confirmed against the arXiv HTML of v2) is **identical in both versions** and identical to the catalog text, including the hypothesis "do not all lie in an affine hyperplane."
- **The hypothesis is the paper's own definition of d-dimensional.** The paper motivates the conjecture with: "this extremal example [an arithmetic progression on a line] ... is one-dimensional. It seems natural to consider the distinct distances problem for configurations of n points ... that are d-dimensional, that is, do not all lie in an affine hyperplane." There is no general-position or non-degeneracy condition anywhere; the writeup's formalization is exactly the intended statement, not a strawman.
- **The genericity clause cannot save the conjecture.** The counterexample's upper bound holds for *every* norm (it only uses ||v|| = ||-v||), so the set of norms with the conjectured property is empty; since the space of d-norms is a nonempty Baire space, "all but a meagre set" fails. The writeup makes this point correctly ("no norm — not merely no generic norm").
- **Would a reasonable author consider the conjecture resolved?** Yes, as posed. The refutation is not a technicality: it shows the conjectured leading coefficient d is wrong for every d >= 2, and pins the truth into the window [max(bd, known lower bounds), d-1+1/(2(d-1))]. Notably it is *consistent* with everything the paper proves and remarks: it lies above the (d-1-o(1))n bound the authors say Conjecture 1.1 would imply, and above the (2-o(1))n bound for d=3 they derive from Pach–Pinchasi–Sharir (2.25 > 2). So the paper's own concluding remarks bracket the truth from below and this example brackets it from above; the conjectured value d simply overshot. The paper's proposed extremal example (AP of n-d+1 points plus d-1 generic points, ~dn distances) is beaten by the parallel-translates construction (~1.5n in the plane). The interesting underlying question — the exact minimal coefficient — remains open, but Conjecture 1.5 as stated is dead.

## Caveats

1. **The refutation targets the conjecture exactly as published.** If Alon–Pinchasi had intended an extra hypothesis (e.g. general position, or excluding sets on few parallel translates of a common set), the statement would change — but no such hypothesis appears in v1 or v2, and the paper explicitly *defines* d-dimensional as "do not all lie in an affine hyperplane." The writeup itself flags this honestly in its caveats field.
2. **What survives**: the weaker bound (d-1-o(1))n conditional on Conjecture 1.1, the unconditional (bd-o(1))n of Theorem 1.6, and the question of the true minimal coefficient (now confined to at most d-1+1/(2(d-1))) all remain open and untouched. The refutation kills the constant d, not the research program; a repaired conjecture would assert some coefficient in [d-1, d-1+1/(2(d-1))].
3. **Asymptotic only**: the contradiction needs n large (eps(n) < 1/2 + 2/n); this is exactly how the conjecture is quantified, so it is not a gap.
4. The counterexample family lives on the arithmetic progression n = 2(d-1)m; since the conjecture asserts a bound for *all* n > n_0(d) and *all* d-dimensional sets, refuting it along one unbounded sequence suffices.
5. m >= 2 is needed for full affine dimension of the general construction (implicit in the writeup; irrelevant asymptotically).
6. The upper bound is norm-independent, so no measurability/genericity subtleties about "typical norms" arise; the Baire-space framing of the conjecture is refuted for the trivial reason that the conforming set of norms is empty.
7. It is startling that so simple an example refutes a conjecture of Alon and Pinchasi, which is why I checked the source of both arXiv versions, the compiled numbering, the precursor ABS paper, the citation graph, and the internal consistency with the paper's own partial results. All checks corroborate the refutation; the likely explanation is that the authors calibrated the conjecture against the AP-plus-(d-1)-points example (~dn) and overlooked configurations on parallel translates.

## Referee summary

The writeup disproves Conjecture 1.5 of arXiv:2508.08870 as stated. The argument is elementary and airtight: distinct distances are bounded by the number of {v,-v} classes of the difference set; two parallel arithmetic progressions give a noncollinear planar set with 3m-2 = (3/2)n - 2 such classes, and the product construction A_m + {±e_2,...,±e_d} gives a full-dimensional set in R^d with coefficient d-1+1/(2(d-1)) < d. The bound holds in every norm, so the meagre-set genericity cannot rescue the statement. I verified the conjecture is quoted verbatim from both arXiv versions, re-derived every count and inequality, confirmed all of it computationally with exact arithmetic, and found no prior appearance of the example and no citing literature. Verdict: CONFIRMED (claimed verdict "disproved" is correct), with the caveat that this resolves the conjecture as literally posed — the underlying question of the optimal coefficient, now trapped in [d-1, d-1+1/(2(d-1))] modulo Conjecture 1.1, remains open.
