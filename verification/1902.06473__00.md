# Referee report: 1902.06473__00

Target: `attacks/1902.06473__00/output.md`, claiming to **prove** the conjecture of
Cardinal, Joret, Roland (arXiv:1902.06473, conclusion): *"the two lower bounds

## Interpretation

The catalog flagged the conjecture statement as corrupted in the PDF. I downloaded
the **arXiv TeX source** (`arxiv.org/e-print/1902.06473`, files `paper.tex`,
`paper.bbl`) and found the conclusion intact. The conjecture is verbatim:

> "Our findings support the conjecture that the two lower bounds lb(P) and qlb(P)
> are within a constant factor of each other for all posets P."

with the table defining, for h(z) = -(1/n) Σ ln z_i on the chain polytope C(P):
H(P) = min_{z∈C(P)} h(z), QH(P) = E_{z∈C(P)}[h(z)], LB(P) = n(ln n − H(P)),
QLB(P) = n(H_n − QH(P)). The paper's Section 3 removes all ambiguity about the
averaging law: its Theorem 3 (`thm:improved`) proves
QLB(P) = E_{σ∈Δ(P)}[Σ_i H_{d_i(σ)−1}] = n(H_n − QH(P)) with QH the **uniform
(normalized Lebesgue) average over C(P)** — exactly the writeup's interpretation,
and the paper's d_i(σ) − 1 is exactly the writeup's r_σ(i). So the writeup's
Lemma 1 is literally the source paper's Theorem 3; there is no interpretation
gaming and no measure ambiguity. The writeup's own caveat about the averaging law
is thus moot: the interpretation is provably the intended one.

The writeup proves the strong two-sided form ln e(P) ≤ QLB(P) ≤ (1/ln 2) ln e(P),
and combines it with the known ITLB ≤ LB ≤ 2·ITLB (the paper's own Theorem 2,
Kahn–Kim + CFJJM) to get LB(P)/2 ≤ QLB(P) ≤ LB(P)/ln 2 for **all** finite posets.
A reasonable author of the source paper would consider the conjecture resolved by
this: it supplies exactly the "missing tool to analyze E_z[h(z)]" that the paper's
conclusion laments, and it strictly subsumes the paper's main theorem
(series-parallel and extended series-parallel posets). Interpretation: **OK**.

## Caveats

- **What QLB is.** QLB(P) is the source paper's *adversary lower bound quantity*,
  not the quantum query complexity itself. The conjecture as stated is about
  LB vs QLB, which the writeup addresses head-on. (Combined with the paper's
  Lemma 6, Adv ≥ c·QLB, the result also yields Yao's conjecture that the quantum
  query complexity of SORT_P is Ω(ITLB(P)) for all posets — a consequence the
  writeup does not explicitly claim.)
- **Corrupted catalog statement.** The catalog inferred the conjecture from a
  garbled PDF; the arXiv TeX source shows the inferred statement is verbatim
  correct, and the paper's Theorem 3 pins the averaging measure to uniform
  Lebesgue on C(P). The writeup's residual caveat about the averaging law can be
  discharged.
- **Degenerate cases.** For chains (and n ≤ 1), LB = QLB = 0; "within a constant
  factor" holds in the two-sided inequality sense 0 ≤ 0 ≤ 0. The writeup notes
  this. H_0 = 0 conventions are used consistently.
- **Novelty split.** Lemma 1 and inequality (18) are known results (the source
  paper's Theorems 3 and 2); the writeup presents them as such. The new content
  is Lemmas 2–4 and Proposition 5.
- **External load-bearing theorem.** The proof stands on Stanley's 1981
  Alexandrov–Fenchel log-concavity theorem — a deep but rock-solid classical
  result, used with exactly its standard statement.
- **Scale of brute force.** Exhaustive verification reaches n = 6 (all posets)
  plus random n = 7, 8; beyond that the guarantee is the (fully re-derived)
  induction, whose step reduces to the general Lemmas 2–3 plus Stanley.
- Since this resolves a stated open problem with a short proof, independent
  expert scrutiny is still warranted before treating it as literature-grade;
  this report found no flaw.
