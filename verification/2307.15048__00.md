## Interpretation

The writeup's verdict hinges entirely on an "as literally stated" reading, so the
interpretation audit is decisive here.

**What the paper literally says.** I fetched the source PDF (arXiv:2307.15048v1,
Dvořák–Yepremyan) and read the statements directly. Conjecture 1.4 reads, verbatim:

> **Conjecture 1.4.** *For every fixed positive p ≤ 1, Erdős-Rényi random graph
> G(n, 1−p) a.a.s. has correspondence chromatic number Θ(n/ log n).*

So the catalog extraction is faithful: the printed statement does include p = 1,
and at p = 1 the graph G(n, 0) is deterministically edgeless with correspondence
chromatic number 1, which is not Θ(n/log n). The writeup's counterexample is
arithmetically correct against the printed text.

**Why this is nevertheless not the intended conjecture.** Multiple signals in the
same paper make it unambiguous that the "positive p ≤ 1" quantifier is an endpoint
slip inherited from Theorem 1.1, not intended content:

1. Theorem 1.1 uses the identical quantifier ("For every fixed positive p ≤ 1 ...
   at most O(n/√log n)"), where p = 1 is harmless because the statement is only an
   *upper bound* (trivially true for the empty graph). Conjecture 1.4 copies this
   quantifier while upgrading O to Θ, which silently breaks the endpoint.
2. The paper's own lower-bound discussion is explicitly restricted away from the
   endpoint: "this also gives Ω(n/ log n) as a lower bound for the correspondence
   chromatic number of G(n, 1−p) **for any positive p < 1**." The authors knew the
   lower bound requires p < 1.
3. Immediately after Conjecture 1.4 the authors write: "It is our hope that this
   conjecture will actually turn out to be false, as this would give another
   interesting example of a difference of behavior between the list and
   correspondence chromatic numbers." For the edgeless graph, list and
   correspondence chromatic numbers are both 1 — the p = 1 "counterexample"
   exhibits no separation whatsoever, so it carries none of the content the
   authors care about.
4. The catalog page itself glosses the conjecture as "for every fixed p ∈ (0,1]"
   but its whole context (the O(n/√log n) vs Θ(n/log n) gap, the separation
   question) concerns the interior; the substantive open problem is the interior
   range.

Would a reasonable author of the original paper consider their conjecture
resolved by this observation? **No.** They would fix the quantifier to
0 < p < 1 in an erratum-level edit and the open problem would stand exactly as
before. The writeup itself admits this in its caveats field: "This only refutes
the literal endpoint-inclusive formulation; the intended conjecture for 0<p<1
remains open." That admission is honest but does not rescue the headline verdict
"disproved", which is precisely the interpretation-gaming failure mode: refuting
a strawman literal reading instead of the intended conjecture.

Every finite claim in the writeup checks out computationally. The failure is not
computational; it is the interpretation.

## Caveats

- **The entire verdict rests on the endpoint p = 1.** For every p in (0, 1) the
  writeup proves nothing: the gap between the known upper bound O(n/√log n) and
  the conjectured Θ(n/log n) remains exactly as open as before.
- The writeup's caveats field candidly states the intended conjecture remains
  open, yet the machine-readable verdict field says "disproved" with "high"
  confidence — downstream consumers reading only the verdict block would be
  misled into marking the catalog problem resolved.
- The refuted endpoint carries none of the paper's motivation: a genuine disproof
  was hoped to separate list and correspondence chromatic numbers, whereas
  chi_list(E_n) = chi_DP(E_n) = 1 gives no separation.
- Convention notes (none affect the outcome): at p = 1 the model is deterministic
  so "a.a.s." is trivially met; n = 1 has log n = 0 but Θ-notation is asymptotic;
  the paper's own lower-bound sentence "independence number Ω(log n), and thus
  χ = Ω(n/log n)" contains its own typo (it needs α = O(log n), which holds only
  for p < 1) — further evidence the endpoint was never considered.
- One could imagine crediting the writeup as a correct refutation "of the
  statement as printed" (its math is flawless as far as it goes), but under the
  verdict semantics of this review the claimed resolution of the *open problem*
  is what is being judged, and that claim fails irreparably: the same trivial
  observation was available to (and implicitly known by) the original authors,
  who restrict to p < 1 whenever the lower bound is at stake.

## Referee summary

The writeup does not resolve Conjecture 1.4. It observes that the printed
quantifier "for every fixed positive p ≤ 1" includes p = 1, at which
G(n, 1−p) = G(n, 0) is deterministically edgeless with correspondence chromatic
number 1, hence not Θ(n/log n). Every individual step is correct — I verified the
transcription against the arXiv PDF and verified chi_DP(edgeless) = 1 by an
independent brute-force DP-coloring checker with sanity checks on K_n and cycles —
but the refuted statement is a degenerate endpoint artifact copied from the
upper-bound Theorem 1.1's quantifier, not the intended conjecture. The paper
itself restricts to p < 1 exactly where the lower bound is discussed, and the
authors' stated motivation (a disproof should separate list and correspondence
chromatic numbers) is entirely unmet by an edgeless graph. The writeup's own
caveats concede that the intended conjecture for 0 < p < 1 "remains open". This
is interpretation gaming: verdict FATAL_ERROR with respect to the claimed
resolution of the open problem, with high confidence. The substantive open
problem — closing the gap between O(n/√log n) and Θ(n/log n) for fixed
p ∈ (0,1) — is untouched.
