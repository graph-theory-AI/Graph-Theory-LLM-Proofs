## Interpretation

**The catalog statement.** Problem 4.1 of arXiv:2511.02892 (posed by František Kardoš) asks:
"Is it true that every diamond-free claw-free cubic graph is strongly 6-edge-colorable?
In other words, is it true that χ'_s(T(G)) = 6 for every cubic graph G?" I downloaded the
paper's TeX source (`PROBLEMS25/Kardos.tex`, included fourth in `main.tex`, confirming the
"Problem 4.1" numbering) and confirmed the statement verbatim. Crucially, the paragraph
*immediately preceding* the problem says: "Lv, Li, and Zhang [LLZ] proved that if G is a
claw-free subcubic graph **other than the triangular prism**, then χ'_s(G) ≤ 8."

**The writeup's move.** The writeup observes that the triangular prism P is itself a
diamond-free claw-free cubic graph with χ'_s(P) = 9, and declares the problem "disproved"
(high confidence). All of that mathematics is correct (verified below). But the prism's
exceptional status is exactly the known fact the problem's own preamble cites: Kardoš and
every reader of the problem know χ'_s(prism) = 9. No reasonable author of the source paper
would consider Problem 4.1 resolved by pointing at the prism; the intended question is about
the nontrivial cases — under the T(G) formulation, truncations where the statement is not
already settled (the prism is T(G₀) only for the 3-dipole *multigraph* G₀, and is not T(G)
for any simple cubic G, as T of a simple cubic graph has ≥ 12 vertices). This is textbook
interpretation gaming: the literal first formulation is indeed sloppy (it forgot to except
the prism), but exposing a known editorial slip is not a resolution of the conjecture.

The writeup is, to its credit, honest about this: its own caveats field and its
"Why the claimed equivalence needs qualification" section concede that "the intended
conjecture for T(G) with G simple remains unresolved by this counterexample." That honesty
is incompatible with the top-level `"verdict": "disproved"` it nevertheless announces.

**Post-posting literature.** Notably, the intended conjecture *was* genuinely disproved
after the catalog snapshot: arXiv:2607.23462 (K. R. Tanwar, July 2026, "A diamond-free
claw-free cubic graph with strong chromatic index 7") constructs a connected simple
diamond-free claw-free cubic graph on 18 vertices with χ'_s = 7, explicitly answering
Problem 4.1 negatively "even after excluding the prism," and notes 18 vertices is minimum
for a non-prism example. This confirms the intended reading excludes the prism — the 2026
paper treats the prism as the trivial known exception, exactly as this review does — and it
means the writeup's coincidentally matching verdict is not established by the writeup's
argument.

## Caveats

- **The verdict/caveat contradiction is the central defect.** The JSON block says
  `"verdict": "disproved", "confidence": "high"` while the caveats field and the body
  concede the intended (simple-base / non-prism) conjecture is untouched. For the catalog's
  purposes the problem was not resolved by this writeup.
- **Known exception, not a discovery.** χ'_s(prism) = 9 is prior literature (LLZ 2022;
  the fact that all 9 prism edges are pairwise within distance 2 is classical), and the
  source problem's own preamble cites the prism exception two sentences before the problem.
  The literal first formulation is a minor editorial slip in the workshop compilation, not
  an open question.
- **Convention dependence.** The "disproof" of the second formulation requires reading
  "cubic graph" as cubic multigraph (G₀ = 3-dipole). Under the simple-graph convention the
  second formulation is not refuted at all; the writeup says so itself.
- **Scope of the positive results.** The local characterization (condition (1)) is proved
  only implicitly and verified here on two examples plus a general derivation; it is correct
  but for connected G with ≥ 2 vertices (for a single-vertex or looped base graph the
  truncation construction as described degenerates — irrelevant for cubic simple G). The
  K₄-homomorphism sufficient condition covers a thin class (it fails already for K₃,₃ and
  Q₃-like parity obstructions notwithstanding, the writeup itself flags the non-coverage).
- **Status update.** As of July 2026 the intended conjecture is false (arXiv:2607.23462,
  18-vertex non-prism counterexample with χ'_s = 7), so the catalog answer "no" is now
  literature-established — but on the strength of that paper, not of this writeup.

## Referee summary

The writeup contains no mathematical errors: every claim about the triangular prism (class
membership, L(P)² = K₉, χ'_s = 9), the multigraph truncation, the local rainbow/condition-(1)
characterization, and the K₄-labeling construction was independently re-derived and verified
by code. But its headline "disproved" rests entirely on the triangular prism — the very
exception the source problem's preceding paragraph cites from Lv–Li–Zhang (2022). This is a
refutation of a strawman literal reading; the problem's author would not (and, given the
July 2026 paper arXiv:2607.23462 that constructs a genuine 18-vertex non-prism counterexample
and explicitly treats the prism as the trivial exception, demonstrably did not) consider
Problem 4.1 settled by the prism. The writeup's own caveats admit the intended conjecture
"remains unresolved by this counterexample." Verdict: ALREADY_KNOWN (the proven content is
correct but prior literature), with interpretation_ok: false.
