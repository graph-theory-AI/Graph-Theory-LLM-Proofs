## Interpretation

The catalog extraction is itself confused, and the writeup inherits the confusion.

I downloaded the v3 LaTeX source of arXiv:1902.10878 (Chudnovsky, Hompe, Scott, Seymour,
Spirkl, "Concatenating bipartite graphs"). The relevant passage of the introduction reads
(verbatim, following Theorem 1.3 = `\ref{cayleybound}`):

> "We were originally motivated by the hope of extending Kneser's theorem from additive
> group theory to a general graph-theoretic setting, and a corresponding wild conjecture
> that the bound in 1.3 is always best possible, that is, that for all x,y in (0,1],
> there is an integer k>0 with phi(x,y) = (ceil(kx)+ceil(ky)-1)/k.
> **This turns out to be false**, but perhaps not ridiculously false; maybe something
> like it is true."

So the precise formula statement "for all x,y there is k with phi(x,y) =
(ceil(kx)+ceil(ky)-1)/k" is the **wild conjecture**, and the paper states it is false.
Immediately afterwards the paper *proves* it false: the blow-up graph of Figure 1
(`fig:exactcount`, 81 vertices) is (13/27, 1/9)-biconstrained with |N^2_A(v)| = 13 for
every v in C, giving psi(13/27, 1/9) = 13/27; since max(x,y) <= phi <= psi (paper
Theorems 1.2 and 1.5), also phi(13/27, 1/9) = 13/27, which is not of the ceiling form
for any k. The paper then modifies the example to be (13/27, 1/9)-exact and concludes:
"even for the supposedly nicest function xi of our three functions, there is not always
an integer k with xi(x,y) = (ceil(kx)+ceil(ky)-1)/k."

The genuinely **open** residual — what the catalog context correctly describes as
"leaving the correct characterisation of phi(x,y) open" — is the deliberately vague
"maybe something like it is true", i.e. finding the right analogue of Kneser's theorem.
The catalog's "Extracted statement" nevertheless typesets the exact formula version as
the conjecture, and the writeup refutes that literal formula. That is precisely the
"interpretation gaming" failure mode: a reasonable author of the source paper would
not consider anything resolved, because the authors themselves stated and demonstrated
in the same paper that this formula version is false. The writeup's caveat paragraph
("addresses exactly the one-sided definition of phi quoted in the question") does not
mention this at all, and even claims high-confidence "disproved" of an open conjecture.

To be fair to the writeup: it answered exactly the statement placed in front of it, and
its mathematics is flawless (see below). But the correct disposition of the result is
"already proved in the source paper's own introduction", not a resolution of an open
problem.

## Reference check

- **Definition of phi** (writeup's "Statement" section): confirmed against the downloaded
  v3 LaTeX source. The paper defines (x,y)-constrained via a tripartition (A,B,C of V(G),
  all stable, no A-C edges), and phi(x,y) as the attained sup (Theorem 1.1). The writeup's
  N_A^2(c) via a middle vertex in B agrees with the paper's N^2 (distance exactly 2) on
  the constructed graph, since A and C are non-adjacent there. Minor unflagged detail:
  the paper requires (A,B,C) to partition V(G) into stable sets with no A-C edges; the
  writeup's graph satisfies this, so no harm.
- **The conjectured form F_k**: matches the paper's Theorem 1.3 (`cayleybound`) upper
  bound and the wild-conjecture wording verbatim.
- **Writeup's "universal lower bound"**: is the paper's Theorem 1.2 (`maxbound`),
  phi(x,y) >= max(x,y), same proof. Not cited by the writeup, but correct.
- **Prior refutation (basis of ALREADY_KNOWN)**: the paper's introduction, Figure 1
  (`fig:exactcount`), caption "psi(13/27,1/9)=13/27", plus the surrounding text quoted
  above, including the explicit sentences "This turns out to be false" (about the wild
  conjecture for phi) and "This shows that there need not exist an integer k with
  psi(x,y) = (ceil(kx)+ceil(ky)-1)/k", and the exact-case version for xi. I verified
  this example computationally (below), including that it also pins phi(13/27,1/9) =
  13/27 via Theorem 1.2 and that 13/27 is not of the form F_k(13/27,1/9) for any k.
- Catalog claim that arXiv:1908.07453 (Hompe) was withdrawn into the source paper:
  consistent with the paper's line "Some of our results appear in [hompe] and

## Caveats

- The entire verdict of the writeup rests on the literal formula reading of an
  informal sentence ("maybe something like it is true"). The source paper's authors
  already knew and stated that the literal formula version is false; the open problem
  is the vague residual characterisation, which no explicit counterexample can refute.
  The writeup's caveats block does not disclose this.
- The writeup's caveat about the biconstrained variant being "a different problem" is
  moot: the paper's own example is biconstrained (indeed made exact), so the same
  refutation is already known for psi and xi as well.
- The writeup's lower bound section reproves paper Theorem 1.2 without attribution
  (harmless for correctness).
- The writeup silently assumes its tripartite construction is admissible under the
  paper's slightly stricter definition (tripartition of all of V(G), stability, no A-C
  edges); it is, but this is never checked in the writeup.
- Interpretation of "two-edge paths" (distance exactly 2 vs. walk through B) coincides
  on the constructed graph; no issue, but unremarked.
- The catalog metadata itself ("Catalog status: open ... lean disprove") is arguably
  the root cause: it presents as open a statement the source paper refutes on page 3.

## Referee summary

The mathematics of the writeup is entirely correct: the 5-5-4 graph is
(2/5,1/4)-constrained, every vertex of C reaches exactly 2/5 of A by two-edge paths,
the trivial averaging bound gives phi(2/5,1/4) >= 2/5, so phi(2/5,1/4) = 2/5, and
exact arithmetic confirms (ceil(2k/5)+ceil(k/4)-1)/k >= 1/2 > 2/5 for every k >= 1.
Every step checks out by hand and by machine. However, the statement thus refuted is
the paper's "wild conjecture", which the source paper (arXiv:1902.10878, introduction)
explicitly declares false and refutes with an 81-vertex (13/27,1/9) example of exactly
the same mechanism — I verified that example computationally as well. The actually open
problem is the informal residual "maybe something like it is true" (the correct
characterisation of phi), which the writeup does not address. The correct disposition
is therefore ALREADY_KNOWN (source: Chudnovsky, Hompe, Scott, Seymour, Spirkl,
"Concatenating bipartite graphs", arXiv:1902.10878, Section 1, Figure 1), not a
resolution of an open conjecture; the writeup's smaller 14-vertex example is a nice
simplification but not a new result.
