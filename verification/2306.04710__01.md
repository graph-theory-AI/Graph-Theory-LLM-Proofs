## Interpretation

The open problem (arXiv:2306.04710, prose after Theorem 1.6): determine for which
integers m, m' >= 1 the set {Δ(1,m,m'), F} is χ⃗-finite, where F is an oriented star
of degree 4. I fetched the source paper (ar5iv full text) and confirmed every
definition the writeup relies on, with no silent strengthening or weakening:

- **χ⃗-finite**: a family ℱ is χ⃗-finite if there is a constant c such that all
  ℱ-free digraphs have dichromatic number at most c; "ℱ-free" means no **induced**
  subdigraph isomorphic to a member of ℱ. The writeup's induced convention is the
  paper's convention.
- **Digraphs are digon-free** in the source paper ("between every two vertices u and
  v, at most one of uv and vu is present"), so the writeup's oriented graphs D_n lie
  in exactly the class the problem quantifies over.
- **Δ(1,m,m')** is the cyclic join of TT_1, TT_m, TT_{m'} (all arcs between blocks
  following the cyclic pattern (1,2),(2,3),(3,1)) — exactly the "cyclic-composition"
  definition the writeup uses.
- **Oriented star of degree 4** = an orientation of K_{1,4} (paper: "an oriented
  star is an orientation of K_{1,t}").
- Theorem 1.6 states exactly what prompt.md quotes, and the paper says verbatim:
  "the case when H = Δ(1,m,m') remains open."

The writeup answers the "determine for which (m,m')" question completely: for **no**
pair (m,m'), and for **every** orientation F of K_{1,4}, is {Δ(1,m,m'), F}
χ⃗-finite. That is a full, fair resolution of the intended question, not a strawman
reading; a reasonable author of 2306.04710 would consider the open problem settled
(negatively) by this statement. Combined with Theorems 1.4 and 1.6 it yields the
clean corollary that the heroes for degree-4-star-free digraphs are exactly the
transitive tournaments. Interpretation: OK.

- **arXiv:2602.08736** (Guillaume Aubian, Luis Kuffner, *"(Claw, C⃗_3)-free digraphs
  with unbounded dichromatic number"*, submitted 2026-02-09): fetched abstract and
  full text. They construct **deterministic** orientations of the N×N rook graph
  (= L(K_{N,N}), claw-free) with no directed C_3 and unbounded dichromatic number,

## Caveats

1. **Novelty (the decisive caveat).** The load-bearing theorem — orientations of
   rook graphs that are C⃗_3-free with claw-free underlying graph and unbounded
   dichromatic number — is Aubian-Kuffner, arXiv:2602.08736 (9 Feb 2026), which
   predates this writeup. The specialization to the 2306.04710 open problem is a
   two-line observation any expert would make, so the open problem should be
   regarded as resolved in the literature since Feb 2026, even though A-K do not
   name Δ(1,m,m'). The writeup itself disclaims novelty ("the supplied 2026
   preprint title may overlap"). The writeup's probabilistic proof (random local
   orders + counting acyclic orientations by indegree sequences) is different from
   A-K's deterministic construction and gives something A-K's Gallai-Witt argument
   does not: an explicit quantitative bound χ⃗ = Ω(√n) on n² vertices.
2. The bound is **asymptotic**: the union bound needs s = ⌈7n^{3/2}⌉ <= n², i.e.
   n >= 49, and √n/7 > c only for large n. This is harmless — only unboundedness
   is needed — and the writeup says "n sufficiently large".
3. The construction is **existential** (positive probability), acknowledged in the
   writeup; the deterministic properties (claw-free, C⃗_3-free) hold for every
   realization, so the positive-probability realization has all three properties.
4. Convention dependence is real but resolved: the result needs the induced
   (not subdigraph) meaning of F-free and digon-free digraphs; both are confirmed
   to be the source paper's conventions, so no interpretation gaming occurred.
5. The writeup's verdict token is "proved"; substantively the resolution is a
   *negative* determination (no (m,m') works), which, combined with Theorems 1.4
   and 1.6 of the source paper, gives: for F an oriented star of degree 4, the
   heroes of F-free digraphs are exactly the transitive tournaments.
6. Small-n behavior: for small n (and for adversarial order choices at any n) D_n
   can be globally acyclic, so nothing is claimed or needed about specific small
   digraphs; verified a(D_4)=16 for one seed, consistent with this.

## Referee summary

I set out to break this proof and could not. Every definition matches the source
paper exactly (induced F-free, digon-free digraphs, cyclic-join Δ, star = oriented
K_{1,4}); all thirteen steps check out, including the two nontrivial lemmas, which I
verified both by re-derivation and by exhaustive computation (indegree-sequence
injectivity on all 5-vertex graphs; exact equality of favorable local-order systems
with acyclic orientations of L(G_S) on dense cases with Pr < 1); the union-bound
arithmetic is correct with room to spare (2e³ = 40.17 < 49). The construction
resolves the open problem of arXiv:2306.04710 completely and negatively: no pair
(m,m') makes {Δ(1,m,m'), F} χ⃗-finite for any oriented star F of degree 4. However,
the essential theorem was already established months earlier by Aubian and Kuffner
(arXiv:2602.08736, Feb 2026), whose claw-free, C⃗_3-free rook-graph orientations of
unbounded dichromatic number imply the same resolution immediately; the catalog's
"open" status was based on a misreading of that paper's relevance. Hence:
ALREADY_KNOWN rather than CONFIRMED, with the note that the writeup's probabilistic
argument is correct, independent of A-K's method, and quantitatively stronger
(explicit Ω(√n) bound).
