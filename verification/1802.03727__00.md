## Interpretation

The target is Conjecture 1.3 of Esperet, Kang and Thomassé (arXiv:1802.03727, CPC 2019):
there is a function $x_1(d)\to\infty$ such that $\mathrm{ch}_{\mathrm{sep}}(G)\ge x_1(d)$
for any graph $G$ with minimum degree $d$. I checked the v2 PDF of the source paper: the
conjecture is stated there verbatim as in the catalog extraction, and the paper's
definition of separation (a $k$-list assignment has *maximum separation* if
$|L(u)\cap L(v)|\le 1$ for every edge $uv$; $\mathrm{ch}_{\mathrm{sep}}(G)$ is the least
$k$ such that every such assignment admits a proper $L$-colouring) is exactly the
definition the writeup uses. The writeup proves the intended statement — in fact a
quantitative strengthening, $\mathrm{ch}_{\mathrm{sep}}(G)=\Omega(\log d/\log\log d)$ for
all graphs — with no interpretation gaming, no weakened hypotheses, and no reliance on
the open Ramsey-type Conjectures 1.5/1.6 of the source paper. A reasonable author of the
original paper would consider this a genuine resolution of Conjecture 1.3.
Interpretation: OK.

## Reference check

- **Theorem 1.2 of arXiv:1802.03727 (the only external input claimed).** Confirmed
  against the v2 PDF: "There is a constant $C>0$ such that
  $\mathrm{ch}_{\mathrm{sep}}(G)\ge C\log d$ for any bipartite graph $G$ with minimum
  degree $d$." The writeup quotes it in the weaker $\Omega(\log D/\log\log D)$ form (the
  catalog page misquoted it that way), so the writeup relies on less than what is
  actually proven. Hypotheses match (bipartite, minimum degree); no silent strengthening.
- **Conjecture 1.3 of arXiv:1802.03727.** Confirmed verbatim from the PDF (p. 3).
- **Definition of separation.** Confirmed: maximum separation means
  $|L(u)\cap L(v)|\le1$ for every edge $uv$ (edgewise, as the writeup assumes).
- **Corroborating context found in the source paper.** Theorem 1.7 of the paper ("any
  graph with chromatic number at most $k$ and minimum degree $d$ has a bipartite induced
  subgraph of minimum degree at least $d/2k$") is the writeup's Lemma 3 up to a constant;
  the paper also records Kratochvíl–Tuza–Voigt's $\mathrm{ch}_{\mathrm{sep}}(K_{d+1})\sim\sqrt d$,
  of which the writeup's Lemma 2 is a chromatic-number generalization (same affine/
  polynomial construction; cf. also Füredi–Kostochka–Kumbhat, "Choosability with
  separation of complete multipartite graphs and hypergraphs", JGT 76 (2014) 129–137).
- **Priority (FATAL for novelty, not for correctness).** The source paper states
  explicitly (p. 3): "the following if true would imply Conjecture 1.3", introducing
  Conjecture 1.4 (every graph of min degree $d$ has a clique on $x_2(d)$ vertices or an
  induced bipartite subgraph of min degree $x_3(d)$). Kwan, Letzter, Sudakov and Tran,
  "Dense induced bipartite subgraphs in triangle-free graphs" (arXiv:1810.12144,
  Combinatorica 2020), Theorem 1.2, proves: for every graph $H$ there is $c_H>0$ such
  that every $H$-free graph of minimum degree $\ge d$ contains an induced bipartite
  subgraph of minimum degree $\ge c_H\log d/\log\log d$; their abstract says this
  "settle[s] Part (i) [= EKT Conjecture 1.4] in a strong form" / "proving another
  conjecture of Esperet, Kang and Thomassé". Chaining published results
  (KTV cliques + KLST with $H=K_{t(k)}$ + EKT Theorem 1.2 + induced monotonicity, exactly
  the implication EKT put in print) resolves Conjecture 1.3 qualitatively. The catalog
  auto-review's claim that arXiv:1810.12144 "does not directly resolve Conjecture 1.3"
  is therefore wrong. I found no paper stating the corollary explicitly, and no paper
  containing the writeup's quantitative $\Omega(\log d/\log\log d)$ bound for general
  graphs (the KLST route only yields roughly $\Omega(\sqrt{\log\log d})$ for general
  graphs because $c_{K_t}$ decays with $t$), nor its Lemma 2
  ($\mathrm{ch}_{\mathrm{sep}}=\Omega(\sqrt{\chi})$). Checked in addition: arXiv:2509.13913
  (Casselgren–Eriksson 2025) does not address the conjecture; Barbados 2019 open-problem
  list does not list it as solved.

## Caveats

1. **Priority.** The qualitative Conjecture 1.3 was already resolvable from the
   published literature (KLST 2020 + EKT's own printed implication); the writeup's own
   caveat ("literature priority has not been independently checked") is exactly where it
   fails. The proof method (chromatic-number dichotomy avoiding all Ramsey machinery) and
   the quantitative $\Omega(\log d/\log\log d)$ bound for general graphs do appear new.
2. **Monotonicity in list size.** The writeup silently uses that exhibiting one bad
   separated $k$-assignment gives $\mathrm{ch}_{\mathrm{sep}}>k$ under the "least $k$"
   definition; this needs the routine observation that separation $k$-choosability is
   monotone in $k$ (restrict $(k{+}1)$-lists to $k$-subsets; separation is preserved).
3. **Edge cases.** All handled or vacuous: $k\ge1$ throughout; $d>0$ forces $r\ge2$ so
   Lemma 3's denominator is positive; Lemma 2 needs $r>4k^2\ge4$ so $q\ge4$ and the field
   and the $k$-subset $X$ exist; the conjecture is asymptotic in $d$, so small $d$ is
   absorbed into $x_1(d)=1$. $x_1$ should use nondecreasing $T_k$ (trivial fix).
4. **Understated strength.** Since the real Theorem 1.2 gives $C\log d$ (not
   $C\log d/\log\log d$), the same argument in fact yields
   $\mathrm{ch}_{\mathrm{sep}}(G)\ge(1-o(1))\,C'\log d$-type growth for general graphs;
   claiming only $\Omega(\log d/\log\log d)$ is safe but suboptimal.
5. The proof is convention-robust: it uses only the edgewise separation condition, list
   colourings with arbitrary colour universes, and finite simple graphs.

## Referee summary

I set out assuming the writeup was wrong and could not break it. All twelve steps verify
in full detail; the two nontrivial ingredients — Lemma 2 (a separated $k$-assignment
built from affine functions over $\mathbb F_q$ that makes any graph with $\chi>4k^2$
uncolorable by a palette-counting argument, generalizing the Kratochvíl–Tuza–Voigt clique
construction from clique number to chromatic number) and Lemma 3 (two colour classes plus
degree peeling give an induced bipartite core of minimum degree $d/(2(r-1))$, essentially
the source paper's own Theorem 1.7) — were confirmed by exhaustive computation on
explicit instances, including a $\chi>\omega$ instance, and the quoted Theorem 1.2 was
confirmed against the source PDF in a form stronger than the writeup needs. The verdict
is nevertheless ALREADY_KNOWN rather than CONFIRMED: Kwan, Letzter, Sudakov and Tran
(arXiv:1810.12144, Combinatorica 2020) proved Esperet–Kang–Thomassé's Conjecture 1.4,
which the source paper explicitly states implies Conjecture 1.3, so the catalog's "open"
status was already stale (the catalog's auto-review misjudged KLST's relevance). That
said, the writeup's proof is independent of the Ramsey-type machinery, considerably
simpler, and quantitatively stronger for general graphs than the published route; its
Lemma 2 bound $\mathrm{ch}_{\mathrm{sep}}(G)=\Omega(\sqrt{\chi(G)})$ and the
$\Omega(\log d/\log\log d)$ bound for all graphs may be publishable observations in
their own right.
