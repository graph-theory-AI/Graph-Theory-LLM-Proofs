## Interpretation

The catalog statement (Problem 1.1 of arXiv:2512.17342) asks whether the reconfiguration
graph F(G,5) is connected for every 2-edge-connected graph G. I fetched the paper
(v4 HTML) and confirmed the paper's definition (Section 2.2): vertices of F(G,k) are the
nowhere-zero integer k-flows (values in {±1,...,±(k−1)} under a reference orientation),
and two flows f, g are adjacent iff the support of f−g is a **cycle**, defined as a
*2-regular connected subgraph* (a circuit), not an arbitrary element of the cycle space.

The writeup uses exactly this setting. Its adjacency phrasing ("agree outside the edge
set of one simple cycle") is equivalent to the paper's: a nonzero circulation supported
inside a circuit C must equal t·χ_C for t ≠ 0 (degree-1 vertices are impossible in the
support of a circulation), so its support is either empty or all of C. The writeup's
moves are therefore the paper's moves (plus trivial identity moves), and a flow isolated
under the writeup's relation is isolated under the paper's. **Interpretation is the
intended one; no strawman reading.**

The decisive interpretive issue is different: **the problem is no longer open.** The
prompt itself includes the v4 abstract, which states "The natural reconfiguration variant
of Tutte's 5-flow conjecture ... is false in the group and integer cases." I confirmed by
fetching the paper that Theorem 3.4 (present since v2, posted 21 April 2026) states:
"There exists a graph G with a nowhere-zero Z5-flow which forms an isolated vertex in
F(G, Z5). In particular, F(G, Z5) and F(G,5) are disconnected." The catalog status
"open" (auto-reviewed 2026-05-14) is stale — it reflects v1 (December 2025), before the
negative result was added. The writeup honestly flags this in its caveats field.

## Caveats

1. **Already known.** The headline claim ("F(G,5) is not always connected") is Theorem
   3.4 of the very source paper, present since v2 (21 April 2026). The catalog status
   "open" is stale; the prompt's own pasted abstract announces the negative result, and
   the writeup itself points this out. This is why the verdict is ALREADY_KNOWN rather
   than CONFIRMED, despite every step checking out.
2. **Possible novelty beyond the paper.** The writeup's counterexample is *planar* and
   cubic (3-connected, hence polyhedral), whereas the paper's Theorem 3.4 example appears
   non-planar and the paper's planar results only cover k ≥ 7. If the planar k = 5 (or 6)
   case is genuinely open in the literature, the writeup's example — equivalently, a
   5-coloring of a 21-vertex planar triangulation frozen under bond-shift recolorings —
   may have independent interest. I did not exhaustively survey the recoloring literature
   for this; it does not affect the verdict on Problem 1.1.
3. Move-definition equivalence: the writeup's "agree outside one simple cycle" equals the
   paper's "difference supported on a cycle" only because a circulation supported in a
   circuit has full support; this is stated implicitly (inside Lemma 1) and is correct.
4. The disconnection needs two flows in distinct components, i.e., F(G,5) non-empty with
   a second flow; both lifts f ≠ g were explicitly verified, so no vacuity issue (an
   isolated Z5-flow alone would not suffice for the integer statement without the lifts).
5. Lemma 2's "up to exchanging S and its complement and changing the sign of a" is terse
   (four cases compressed to one), but all four cases are covered by strong connectivity
   of all D_a; in any event the isolation was verified directly on G, bypassing Lemma 2
   and the duality bookkeeping entirely.
6. Convention dependence: the flow-value sign convention (which side of an H-edge the
   dual arc crosses) affects φ_c only up to sign per edge and cannot affect isolation or
   nowhere-zero-ness; my scripts fix one coherent orientation and verify conservation
   exactly, so no convention mismatch survives.
7. Small/degenerate cases (empty graph, bridges, k = 0,1) are irrelevant here: the claim
   is existential and the exhibited G is 3-connected and cubic.

nowhere-zero-5-flow reconfiguration graph for all 2-edge-connected graphs) via an explicit
counterexample: the 38-vertex cubic planar dual of two Z5-colored icosahedra glued along
