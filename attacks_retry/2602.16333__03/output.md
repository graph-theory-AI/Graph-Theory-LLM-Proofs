# A constant-factor bound for abelian-by-two Cayley digraphs

I do not resolve Question 4.4 in general. The partial result below extends the supplied attempt beyond its normal-chain hypothesis: the main construction explicitly accommodates a subgroup that need not be normal.

All digraphs below are finite and loopless; directed cycles of length two are allowed. Write
\[
p(D)=\max\{|V(P)|:P\text{ is a directed path in }D\},
\qquad
c(D)=\max\{|V(C)|:C\text{ is a directed cycle in }D\}.
\]
Bounds using this vertex-counting convention also imply the corresponding bounds when path length is counted in edges. We work with the intended connected, nontrivial formulation.

We use right Cayley digraphs:
\[
\operatorname{Cay}(G,S):\quad g\longrightarrow gs\qquad(s\in S).
\]

## 1. Partial results

### Theorem A

Let \(G\) be a finite group with an abelian subgroup \(A\) of index two. If \(S\subseteq G\setminus\{e\}\) generates \(G\), then, for
\[
D=\operatorname{Cay}(G,S),\qquad n=|G|,
\]
one has
\[
p(D)\ge \frac n2,
\qquad
c(D)\ge \frac n4+1.
\]
Consequently,
\[
p(D)<4c(D).
\]

There is also a sharper result under an additional subgroup-invariance assumption.

### Theorem B

Under the hypotheses of Theorem A, suppose every subgroup of \(A\) is normal in \(G\). Then \(D\) has a Hamilton path and
\[
c(D)\ge \frac n2+1.
\]
In particular,
\[
p(D)<2c(D).
\]

Theorem B applies, for every generating set \(S\), to:

- groups with a cyclic subgroup of index two;
- generalized dihedral groups;
- generalized dicyclic groups.

Indeed, every subgroup of a cyclic normal subgroup is normal in the whole group. In the latter two classes, conjugation by an element outside \(A\) acts on \(A\) by inversion, so it preserves every subgroup of \(A\).

The proofs are self-contained. The coset-stacking argument from the supplied attempt is checked and reused as a preliminary lemma.

## 2. Two elementary path constructions

### Lemma 2.1: normal-coset stacking

Let \(K\triangleleft G\), let \(q=|K|\), and suppose
\[
\operatorname{Cay}(K,S\cap K)
\]
has a Hamilton path. Let \(Q\) be the quotient Cayley digraph on \(G/K\), with loops discarded.

Then:

1. a directed path on \(a\) vertices in \(Q\) lifts to a directed path on \(qa\) vertices in \(\operatorname{Cay}(G,S)\);
2. a directed cycle of length \(b\ge2\) in \(Q\) yields a directed cycle of length at least
   \[
   q(b-1)+1
   \]
   in \(\operatorname{Cay}(G,S)\).

#### Proof

Translate a Hamilton path of \(K\) so that it starts at \(e\), and write it as
\[
P_K=(x_0=e,x_1,\ldots,x_{q-1}=h).
\]

Follow a directed path in the quotient, starting at \(K\), with arc labels \(s_0,s_1,\ldots\) chosen from \(S\). Recursively put
\[
a_0=e,\qquad a_{j+1}=a_jhs_j.
\]
Normality of \(K\) ensures that \(a_jK\) is the prescribed \(j\)-th quotient vertex.

In the coset \(a_jK\), traverse
\[
a_jP_K=(a_jx_0,\ldots,a_jx_{q-1}).
\]
Its last vertex has an \(s_j\)-arc to \(a_{j+1}\), the first vertex of the next block. Distinct quotient vertices give disjoint blocks, proving the path assertion.

For a quotient cycle of length \(b\), the final joining arc returns to some \(x_r\in K\), where \(0\le r\le q-1\). The suffix starting at \(x_r\), followed by that final arc, is a simple directed cycle of length
\[
bq-r\ge bq-(q-1)=q(b-1)+1.
\]
\(\square\)

### Lemma 2.2: an abelian base case, with a prescribed last generator

Let \(B\) be a finite abelian group generated by \(R\subseteq B\setminus\{e\}\).

1. \(\operatorname{Cay}(B,R)\) has a Hamilton path.
2. If \(u\in R\), a Hamilton path can be chosen whose last arc is labelled \(u\).
3. If \(|B|\ge2\), then
   \[
   c\bigl(\operatorname{Cay}(B,R)\bigr)\ge \frac{|B|}{2}+1.
   \]

The one-vertex group has a one-vertex Hamilton path.

#### Proof

Choose generators successively to obtain a strict chain
\[
\{e\}=B_0<B_1<\cdots<B_k=B,
\qquad
B_i=\langle r_1,\ldots,r_i\rangle.
\]
Every subgroup is normal, and each quotient \(B_i/B_{i-1}\) is cyclic, generated by \(r_iB_{i-1}\). Repeated use of Lemma 2.1 therefore constructs Hamilton paths.

To prescribe the last generator, choose \(r_1=u\). The Hamilton path
\[
e,u,\ldots,u^{|u|-1}
\]
in \(B_1=\langle u\rangle\) ends with a \(u\)-arc. At every subsequent stacking step, the final block is a translate of the preceding Hamilton path. Thus its last arc remains a \(u\)-arc.

At the final step, Lemma 2.1 gives
\[
c\bigl(\operatorname{Cay}(B,R)\bigr)
 \ge |B|-|B_{k-1}|+1
 \ge \frac{|B|}{2}+1,
\]
because \(B_{k-1}\) is proper. \(\square\)

## 3. A block-cycle lemma not requiring normality

The following observation is useful when a partition has controlled exits but is not a normal-coset partition.

### Lemma 3.1

Let \(B_0,\ldots,B_{\ell-1}\) be pairwise disjoint vertex sets in a finite digraph, where \(\ell\ge2\), and let \(X_i\subseteq B_i\). Suppose:

- from every vertex of \(B_i\), there is a simple directed path contained in \(B_i\), containing at least \(h\) vertices and ending in \(X_i\);
- every vertex of \(X_i\) has an outneighbor in \(B_{i+1}\), with indices modulo \(\ell\).

Then the digraph has a simple directed cycle of length at least
\[
(\ell-1)h+1.
\]

For a sequence of distinct blocks satisfying the analogous forward conditions, concatenation gives a simple directed path containing at least \(h\) vertices per block.

#### Proof

Starting in \(B_0\), repeatedly traverse one of the prescribed internal paths and take an arc to the next block. Continue until the first repeated vertex.

The segment between the two occurrences of that vertex is a simple directed cycle. The repeated vertex belongs to one block, and distinct visits to that block are separated by a positive multiple of \(\ell\) block visits. Hence this cycle contains at least \(\ell-1\) complete internal paths, each containing at least \(h\) vertices, as well as the repeated vertex counted once. Its length is therefore at least
\[
(\ell-1)h+1.
\]

For distinct blocks in a path sequence, no repetition between blocks is possible. \(\square\)

## 4. Proof of Theorem A

Let \(A\le G\) be abelian of index two. Choose
\[
t\in S\setminus A,
\]
which exists because \(S\) generates \(G\). Put
\[
z=t^2\in A,\qquad
\phi(a)=tat^{-1}\quad(a\in A).
\]
Since \(A\) is abelian and \(t^2\in A\),
\[
\phi^2=\mathrm{id}_A.
\]

Separate the generators into
\[
S_0=S\cap A,\qquad S_1=S\setminus A,
\]
and define
\[
U=\{st:s\in S_1\}\subseteq A,
\qquad
H=\langle S_0\cup U\rangle\le A.
\]
Notice that \(z\in U\), because \(t\in S_1\).

Write
\[
h=|H|,\qquad m=[A:H].
\]
Then
\[
n=2hm.
\]

### 4.1. The subgroup identity \(A=H\phi(H)\)

Every \(s\in S_1\) can be written as
\[
s=(st)t^{-1}\in Ht^{-1},
\]
so
\[
G=\langle H,t\rangle.
\]

Because \(A\) is abelian, \(L=H\phi(H)\) is a subgroup of \(A\). It is \(\phi\)-invariant and contains \(t^2\). Consequently \(L\cup Lt\) is a subgroup of \(G\) containing \(H\) and \(t\), and therefore equals \(G\). Intersecting with \(A\) gives
\[
A=H\phi(H). \tag{4.1}
\]

If \(H=\{e\}\), then \(t^2=e\) and \(G=\langle t\rangle\) has order two. The theorem is immediate in that case. Henceforth assume \(h\ge2\).

### 4.2. Paired blocks

For \(xH\in A/H\), define
\[
B_{xH}=xH\;\dot\cup\;xHt^{-1},
\qquad
X_{xH}=xHt^{-1}.
\]
These \(m\) blocks partition \(G\), each into an inside half and an exit half of size \(h\).

Crucially, \(H\) need not be normal in \(G\), and this partition need not be \(G\)-invariant.

Consider the auxiliary abelian Cayley digraph
\[
F=\operatorname{Cay}\bigl(H,(S_0\cup U)\setminus\{e\}\bigr).
\]
Its arcs have the following realizations inside a block:

- a generator \(k\in S_0\) gives the original one-arc step
  \[
  a\longrightarrow ak;
  \]
- a generator \(u=st\in U\), with \(s\in S_1\), gives the two-arc step
  \[
  a\longrightarrow as=(au)t^{-1}\longrightarrow au. \tag{4.2}
  \]

For a simple path or cycle in \(F\), these realizations introduce no unwanted repetitions: the inside vertices are the original vertices, and the intermediate outside vertex of a two-arc step is determined injectively by that step’s target, namely
\[
\text{target}\cdot t^{-1}.
\]

### 4.3. Long internal paths ending in the exit half

We claim that:

> From every vertex of \(B_{xH}\), there is a simple directed path contained in \(B_{xH}\), containing at least \(h\) vertices and ending in \(X_{xH}\).

First suppose \(U\) contains a nonidentity element \(u\). By Lemma 2.2, choose a Hamilton path in \(F\) whose last arc is labelled \(u\), and realize that last arc by (4.2). Lift the whole path and then delete its final arc and final inside vertex.

The resulting path ends in the exit half. It contains \(h-1\) inside vertices and at least one outside vertex, so it has at least \(h\) vertices.

This constructs the required path from any inside vertex, by translation. To start at an outside vertex \(a_0t^{-1}\), prepend the \(t\)-arc
\[
a_0t^{-1}\longrightarrow a_0.
\]
The prepended vertex is not any intermediate vertex of the lifted Hamilton path: those correspond to targets other than its starting vertex \(a_0\).

Now suppose \(U=\{e\}\). Then \(t^2=e\), \(S_1=\{t\}\), and \(H=\langle S_0\rangle\). Take a Hamilton path in \(\operatorname{Cay}(H,S_0)\), and append a \(t\)-arc to its final vertex. This ends in the exit half. For an outside starting vertex, also prepend its \(t\)-arc into the inside half. The two outside vertices are distinct because the Hamilton path has \(h\ge2\) vertices.

The claim follows.

### 4.4. An abelian quotient controlling the exits

Let
\[
Q=\operatorname{Cay}(A/H,T),
\qquad
T=\{\phi(r)H:r\in S_0\cup U\}\setminus\{H\}.
\]
By (4.1), these generators generate \(A/H\), so Lemma 2.2 applies to \(Q\).

We verify that every quotient arc can be followed from **every** vertex of the relevant exit half.

Let \(w=xat^{-1}\), where \(a\in H\).

For \(k\in S_0\),
\[
wk=xa\phi(k)t^{-1},
\]
which lies in the exit half of \(B_{x\phi(k)H}\).

For \(s\in S_1\), put \(u=st\in U\). Then
\[
ws
 =xat^{-1}ut^{-1}
 =xa\phi(u)z^{-1}.
\]
Because \(z\in H\), this lies in the inside half of \(B_{x\phi(u)H}\).

Thus the paired blocks and their exit halves satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma 3.1 along any directed path or cycle in \(Q\).

### 4.5. The bounds

If \(m\ge2\), Lemma 2.2 supplies a Hamilton path in \(Q\), and hence
\[
p(D)\ge mh=\frac n2.
\]
It also supplies a directed cycle in \(Q\) of length
\[
\ell\ge \frac m2+1.
\]
Lemma 3.1 gives
\[
c(D)\ge h(\ell-1)+1
      \ge \frac{hm}{2}+1
      =\frac n4+1.
\]

If \(m=1\), then \(H=A\). A longest cycle supplied by Lemma 2.2 in \(F\) has length at least
\[
\frac h2+1.
\]
Its realization by one- and two-arc steps is a simple directed cycle in \(D\), so
\[
c(D)\ge \frac h2+1=\frac n4+1.
\]
A lifted Hamilton path in \(F\) also gives \(p(D)\ge h=n/2\).

This proves Theorem A. \(\square\)

## 5. Proof of the sharper Theorem B

Assume every subgroup of \(A\) is normal in \(G\). Set
\[
K=\langle S\cap A\rangle,\qquad q=|K|.
\]
Then \(K\triangleleft G\), and the Cayley digraph on \(K\) with generators \(S\cap A\) has a Hamilton path by Lemma 2.2.

Pass to
\[
\overline G=G/K,\qquad B=A/K,
\]
and discard identity images of generators. All remaining generators \(T\) lie outside the index-two subgroup \(B\). Choose \(\tau\in T\), and put
\[
V=\{s\tau:s\in T\}\subseteq B.
\]

### 5.1. The two-step generators generate \(B\)

Let \(J=\langle V\rangle\). The subgroup-normality hypothesis passes to the quotient, so \(J\triangleleft\overline G\).

We have \(\tau^2\in J\), and every \(s\in T\) lies in \(J\tau^{-1}\). Therefore
\[
\overline G=\langle J,\tau\rangle,
\qquad
[\overline G:J]\le2.
\]
Since \(J\le B\) and \([\overline G:B]=2\), it follows that
\[
J=B.
\]

### 5.2. Lifting paths and cycles from \(B\)

A \(V\)-arc
\[
a\longrightarrow as\tau=b
\]
lifts to
\[
a\longrightarrow as=b\tau^{-1}\longrightarrow b.
\]
As before, different targets give different intermediate outside vertices.

Consequently:

- a directed cycle of length \(r\) in \(\operatorname{Cay}(B,V\setminus\{e\})\) lifts to a simple directed cycle of length \(2r\);
- a Hamilton path \(b_0,\ldots,b_{|B|-1}\) lifts to a Hamilton path in \(\operatorname{Cay}(\overline G,T)\) after prepending
  \[
  b_0\tau^{-1}\longrightarrow b_0.
  \]

If \(|B|\ge2\), Lemma 2.2 therefore gives a Hamilton path in the quotient digraph and
\[
c\bigl(\operatorname{Cay}(\overline G,T)\bigr)
 \ge 2\left(\frac{|B|}{2}+1\right)
 \ge |B|+1.
\]
If \(|B|=1\), the quotient is the directed two-cycle, and the same weaker bound holds.

Finally, Lemma 2.1 stacks Hamilton paths of \(K\) over the quotient Hamilton path, giving a Hamilton path in \(D\). Stacking over a quotient cycle gives
\[
c(D)
 \ge q\left(c\bigl(\operatorname{Cay}(\overline G,T)\bigr)-1\right)+1
 \ge q|B|+1
 =\frac{|G|}{2}+1.
\]
This proves Theorem B. \(\square\)

## 6. What remains open in this attack

Theorem A handles a non-normality obstruction left untreated by the supplied attempt: its subgroup \(H\) is generally not normal, but the paired blocks have enough uniformity at their exit halves to support the first-repetition construction.

The argument still uses two substantial restrictions:

1. the digraph is Cayley;
2. the group has an abelian subgroup of index two.

It gives neither a universal constant for arbitrary vertex-transitive digraphs nor an unbounded-ratio counterexample family. I have not independently checked whether these special-case bounds are already in the literature, so I make no novelty claim.
